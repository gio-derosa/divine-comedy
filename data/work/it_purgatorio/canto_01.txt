1|To run better waters now raises its sails
2|the little ship of my wit,
3|which leaves behind itself a sea so cruel;
4|and I will sing of that second realm
5|where the human spirit purges itself
6|and of rising to heaven becomes worthy.
7|But here let dead poetry rise again,
8|O holy Muses, since yours I am;
9|and here let Calliope somewhat rise,
10|accompanying my song with that sound
11|of which the wretched Magpies felt
12|the blow such, that they despaired of pardon.
13|Sweet color of oriental sapphire,
14|which was gathering in the serene aspect
15|of the middle air, pure as far as the first circle,
16|to my eyes began again delight,
17|as soon as I went out from the dead air
18|which had saddened my eyes and my breast.
19|The fair planet which to love comforts
20|was making all the orient laugh,
21|veiling the Fishes that were in its escort.
22|I turned to the right hand, and set my mind
23|on the other pole, and saw four stars
24|never seen except by the first people.
25|The sky seemed to rejoice in their little flames:
26|oh northern widowed site,
27|since you are deprived of gazing at those!
28|When I from their gaze had parted,
29|turning myself a little to the other pole,
30|there whence the Wain had already disappeared,
31|I saw near me an old man alone,
32|worthy of so much reverence in his look,
33|that no more does any son owe to a father.
34|Long his beard and with white hair mixed
35|he wore, like to his hair of the head,
36|of which fell to his breast a double strand.
37|The rays of the four holy lights
38|so adorned his face with light,
39|that I saw him as if the sun were before him.
40|"Who are you who against the blind river
41|have fled the eternal prison?",
42|said he, moving those honorable plumes.
43|"Who has guided you, or what was a lamp to you,
44|going forth out of the deep night
45|which ever makes black the infernal valley?
46|Are the laws of the abyss thus broken?
47|or is changed in heaven a new counsel,
48|that, damned, you come to my rocks?".
49|My leader then took hold of me,
50|and with words and with hands and with signs
51|made reverent my legs and my brow.
52|Then he answered him: "Of myself I did not come:
53|a lady came down from heaven, through whose prayers
54|with my company this one I succored.
55|But since it is your will that more be unfolded
56|of our condition, how it truly is,
57|it cannot be mine that to you it be denied.
58|This one never saw his last evening;
59|but through his folly he was so near to it,
60|that very little time was there to turn.
61|Just as I said, I was sent to him
62|to save him; and there was no other way
63|than this one by which I have set myself.
64|I have shown him all the guilty people;
65|and now I intend to show those spirits
66|that purge themselves under your authority.
67|How I have brought him, would be long to tell you;
68|from on high descends power which helps me
69|lead him to see you and to hear you.
70|Now may it please you to welcome his coming:
71|he goes seeking liberty, which is so dear,
72|as he knows who for her refuses life.
73|You know it, for to you for her it was not bitter
74|in Utica, death, where you left
75|the garment which on the great day will be so bright.
76|Not are the eternal edicts by us broken,
77|for this one lives and Minos binds not me;
78|but I am of the circle where are the chaste eyes
79|of your Marcia, who in her look still prays you,
80|O holy breast, that for yours you hold her:
81|for her love then to us incline yourself.
82|Let us go through your seven realms;
83|thanks I will carry back of you to her,
84|if to be mentioned down there you deign".
85|"Marcia pleased so much my eyes
86|while I was over there", said he then,
87|"that whatever favors she wished from me, I did.
88|Now that beyond the evil river she dwells,
89|she can move me no more, by that law
90|which was made when I went forth from there.
91|But if a lady of heaven moves and rules you,
92|as you say, there is no need of flatteries:
93|let it suffice well that for her you ask me.
94|Go then, and see that you gird this one
95|with a smooth rush and that you wash his face,
96|so that all filth from there you wipe away;
97|for it would not be fitting, the eye overtaken
98|by any mist, to go before the first
99|minister, who is of those of paradise.
100|This little island all around at its very base,
101|down there yonder where the wave beats it,
102|bears rushes upon the soft mud:
103|no other plant which might make leaves
104|or harden, can have life there,
105|because to the blows it does not yield.
106|Afterward let not your return be this way;
107|the sun will show you, which rises now,
108|to take the mountain by a gentler ascent".
109|Thus he vanished; and I up raised myself
110|without speaking, and wholly drew myself
111|to my leader, and my eyes to him I directed.
112|He began: "Son, follow my steps:
113|let us turn back, for this way slopes
114|this plain to its low bounds".
115|The dawn was vanquishing the morning hour
116|which fled before it, so that from afar
117|I recognized the trembling of the sea.
118|We were going over the solitary plain
119|like a man who returns to the lost road,
120|who until he reaches it seems to go in vain.
121|When we were there where the dew
122|fights with the sun, for being in a part
123|where, in the breeze, little it evaporates,
124|both hands spread out upon the little grass
125|gently my master placed:
126|whereupon I, who was aware of his art,
127|held out toward him my tearful cheeks;
128|there he made wholly uncovered on me
129|that color which hell had hidden on me.
130|We came then onto the desert shore,
131|which never saw sail upon its waters
132|a man, who of returning might afterward have experience.
133|There he girded me just as pleased another:
134|oh marvel! for such as he chose
135|the humble plant, such was reborn
136|suddenly there whence he had plucked it.
