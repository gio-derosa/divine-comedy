1|We were at the top of the stairway,
2|where a second time is cut back
3|the mountain which, by climbing, frees one from evil.
4|There likewise a cornice binds
5|around the hill, as the first one;
6|except that its arc bends more quickly.
7|Shade is not there nor figure that appears:
8|the bank looks and the road looks plain
9|with the livid color of the stone.
10|"If here to inquire we wait for people",
11|reasoned the poet, "I fear perhaps
12|that too much delay will our choice have".
13|Then fixedly at the sun he set his eyes;
14|he made of his right side the center for moving,
15|and the left part of himself he turned.
16|"O sweet light with trust in which I enter
17|on the new road, you lead us",
18|he said, "as one should be led in here.
19|You warm the world, you shine upon it;
20|if another reason does not urge to the contrary,
21|your rays must always be guides".
22|As much as here is counted for a mile,
23|so much there had we already gone,
24|in a short time, because of the ready will;
25|and toward us flying were heard,
26|not however seen, spirits speaking
27|to the table of love courteous invitations.
28|The first voice which passed flying
29|'They have no wine' loudly said,
30|and behind us went on repeating it.
31|And before it could be wholly no longer heard
32|through going away, another 'I am Orestes'
33|passed crying, and also did not stop.
34|"Oh!", said I, "father, what voices are these?".
35|And as I asked, behold the third
36|saying: 'Love those from whom you had evil'.
37|And the good master: "This circle scourges
38|the sin of envy, and therefore are
39|drawn from love the cords of the whip.
40|The bridle must be of the contrary sound;
41|I believe that you will hear it, in my opinion,
42|before you reach the pass of pardon.
43|But fix your eyes through the air very fixedly,
44|and you will see people before us sitting,
45|and each one is along the rock seated".
46|Then more than before I opened my eyes;
47|I looked before me, and saw shades with cloaks
48|from the color of the stone not different.
49|And when we were a little further on,
50|I heard crying: 'Mary pray for us':
51|crying 'Michael' and 'Peter' and 'All saints'.
52|I do not believe that on earth goes today
53|a man so hard, that he would not be pierced
54|by compassion for what I saw then;
55|for, when I had come so near to them,
56|that their features came to me distinctly,
57|through my eyes I was milked of heavy grief.
58|With vile haircloth they seemed to me covered,
59|and one supported the other with his shoulder,
60|and all were supported by the bank.
61|Thus the blind to whom livelihood fails,
62|stand at the pardons to beg for their needs,
63|and one lets his head sink upon the other,
64|so that in others pity may quickly be set,
65|not only by the sound of the words,
66|but by the sight which no less implores.
67|And as to the blind the sun does not reach,
68|so to the shades there, of whom I now speak,
69|light of heaven wills not to grant itself;
70|for in all an iron wire pierces the eyelids
71|and sews them, as to a wild sparrowhawk
72|is done because it does not stay quiet.
73|It seemed to me, going, to do an outrage,
74|seeing others, not being seen:
75|wherefore I turned to my wise counsel.
76|Well he knew what the mute wished to say;
77|and therefore he did not wait for my question,
78|but said: "Speak, and be brief and to the point".
79|Virgil was coming with me on that side
80|of the cornice from which one can fall,
81|because by no parapet is it garlanded;
82|on the other side of me were the devout
83|shades, who through the horrible seam
84|pressed so, that they bathed their cheeks.
85|I turned to them and: "O people certain",
86|I began, "of seeing the high light
87|which alone your desire has in its care,
88|so may grace soon dissolve the scum
89|of your conscience so that clear
90|through it may descend the river of the mind,
91|tell me, for it will be gracious and dear to me,
92|if there is a soul here among you which is Latin;
93|and perhaps for it it will be good if I learn it".
94|"O my brother, each one is a citizen
95|of one true city; but you mean
96|one who lived in Italy as a pilgrim".
97|This I seemed to hear as answer
98|somewhat further on than where I stood,
99|whereupon I made myself heard still further on.
100|Among the others I saw a shade that was waiting
101|in its look; and if anyone would say 'How?',
102|it was raising its chin upward in the manner of a blind man.
103|"Spirit", said I, "who to climb subdue yourself,
104|if you are the one who answered me,
105|make yourself known to me either by place or by name".
106|"I was Sienese", it answered, "and with these
107|others I mend here the guilty life,
108|weeping to him that he grant himself to us.
109|Wise I was not, although Sapia
110|I was called, and I was at others' harms
111|gladder by far than at my own good fortune.
112|And so that you may not believe that I deceive you,
113|hear if I was, as I tell you, foolish,
114|already descending the arc of my years.
115|My fellow citizens were near Colle
116|joined in the field with their adversaries,
117|and I was praying God for that which he willed.
118|Routed were they there and turned into the bitter
119|steps of flight; and seeing the chase,
120|I took a joy unequaled by all others,
121|so that I turned upward my bold face,
122|crying to God: 'Now I fear you no more!',
123|as did the blackbird for a little fair weather.
124|Peace I wished with God at the extreme
125|of my life; and still would not be
126|my debt by penitence diminished,
127|were it not for this, that in his memory held me
128|Pier Pettinaio in his holy prayers,
129|who out of charity grieved for me.
130|But you who are you, who our conditions
131|go asking about, and carry your eyes unbound,
132|as I believe, and breathing speak?".
133|"My eyes", said I, "will yet be taken from me here,
134|but for a short time, for small is the offense
135|done by their being turned with envy.
136|Far greater is the fear with which is suspended
137|my soul, of the torment below,
138|so that already the burden down there weighs on me".
139|And she to me: "Who then has led you
140|up here among us, if you believe you will return below?".
141|And I: "This one who is with me and says not a word.
142|And living I am; and therefore ask of me,
143|elect spirit, if you wish that I move
144|over there for you still my mortal feet".
145|"Oh, this is to hear so new a thing",
146|she answered, "that it is a great sign that God loves you;
147|therefore with your prayer help me sometimes.
148|And I beg you, by that which you most long for,
149|if ever you tread the land of Tuscany,
150|that to my kinsfolk you restore my good name.
151|You will see them among that vain people
152|which hopes in Talamone, and will lose there
153|more hope than in finding the Diana;
154|but more there will lose the admirals".
