1|As much as between the end of the third hour
2|and the beginning of the day appears of the sphere
3|which always in the manner of a child plays,
4|so much seemed already toward the evening
5|to be left to the sun of its course;
6|it was vespers there, and here midnight.
7|And the rays were striking us full on the nose,
8|because by us the mountain had been so circled,
9|that now we were going straight toward the sunset,
10|when I felt my brow weighed down
11|by the splendor much more than before,
12|and a cause of amazement to me were the things unknown;
13|whereupon I raised my hands toward the top
14|of my brows, and made myself a sunshade,
15|which of the excess of the visible files away.
16|As when from the water or from the mirror
17|the ray leaps to the opposite side,
18|rising up in the manner equal
19|to that in which it descends, and departs as much
20|from the fall of the stone in equal distance,
21|just as experiment and science show;
22|so it seemed to me by light reflected
23|there in front of me to be struck;
24|wherefore to flee my sight was swift.
25|"What is that, sweet father, from which I cannot
26|screen my sight so much that it avails me",
27|said I, "and seems toward us to be moving?".
28|"Do not marvel if still dazzles you
29|the family of heaven", he answered me:
30|"it is a messenger who comes to invite one to climb.
31|Soon it will be that to see these things
32|will not be heavy for you, but will be to you a delight
33|as much as nature disposed you to feel".
34|When we had reached the blessed angel,
35|with glad voice he said: "Enter here
36|to a stairway far less steep than the others".
37|We were mounting, already departed from there,
38|and 'Blessed are the merciful!' was
39|sung behind, and 'Rejoice you who conquer!'.
40|My master and I, both alone,
41|were going upward; and I thought, while going,
42|to gain profit from his words;
43|and I turned to him thus asking:
44|"What did the spirit from Romagna mean,
45|both 'exclusion' and 'partners' mentioning?".
46|Whereupon he to me: "Of his own greatest fault
47|he knows the harm; and therefore let it not be wondered
48|if he reproves it so that it be less wept for.
49|Because your desires are aimed
50|where by sharing the portion is lessened,
51|envy moves the bellows to sighs.
52|But if the love of the highest sphere
53|twisted upward your desire,
54|there would not be in your breast that fear;
55|for, the more there are who there say 'ours',
56|the more of good each one possesses,
57|and the more of charity burns in that cloister".
58|"I am of being content more fasting",
59|said I, "than if I had kept silent before,
60|and more doubt in my mind I gather.
61|How can it be that one good, distributed
62|among more possessors, makes more rich
63|with itself than if by few it is possessed?".
64|And he to me: "Because you fix again
65|your mind only on earthly things,
66|from true light you pluck darkness.
67|That infinite and ineffable good
68|which is up there, so runs to love
69|as to a shining body a ray comes.
70|So much it gives itself as it finds of ardor;
71|so that, however far charity extends,
72|there grows upon it the eternal worth.
73|And the more people up there are attuned,
74|the more there is to love well, and the more is loved there,
75|and as a mirror one to the other reflects.
76|And if my reasoning does not satisfy your hunger,
77|you will see Beatrice, and she fully
78|will take from you this and every other craving.
79|Only strive that soon be extinguished,
80|as are already the two, the five wounds,
81|which are closed again by being sorrowful".
82|As I was about to say 'You satisfy me',
83|I saw myself arrived upon the next circle,
84|so that my eager eyes made me keep silent.
85|There I seemed in a vision
86|ecstatic suddenly to be drawn,
87|and to see in a temple many persons;
88|and a woman, at the entrance, with the attitude
89|sweet of a mother, saying: "My son,
90|why have you thus done toward us?
91|Behold, sorrowing, your father and I
92|were seeking you". And as here she fell silent,
93|that which appeared at first, disappeared.
94|Then appeared to me another with those waters
95|down her cheeks which grief distills
96|when from great spite against others it is born,
97|saying: "If you are lord of the city
98|about whose name among the gods was such strife,
99|and whence every knowledge sparkles forth,
100|avenge yourself on those bold arms
101|which embraced our daughter, O Pisistratus".
102|And the lord seemed to me, benign and mild,
103|to answer her with tempered face:
104|"What shall we do to him who desires evil for us,
105|if he who loves us is by us condemned?".
106|Then I saw people kindled in the fire of wrath
107|with stones a youth killing, loudly
108|crying to one another only: "Kill, kill!".
109|And him I saw bending, because of death
110|which already weighed him down, toward the earth,
111|but of his eyes he always made gates to heaven,
112|praying to the high Lord, in such strife,
113|that he forgive his persecutors,
114|with that look which unlocks pity.
115|When my soul returned outward
116|to the things which are outside it real,
117|I recognized my not false errors.
118|My leader, who could see me
119|acting like a man who frees himself from sleep,
120|said: "What is the matter with you that you cannot hold yourself up,
121|but have come more than half a league
122|veiling your eyes and with your legs entangled,
123|in the manner of one whom wine or sleep bends?".
124|"O my sweet father, if you listen to me,
125|I will tell you", said I, "what appeared to me
126|when my legs were so taken from me".
127|And he: "If you had a hundred masks
128|upon your face, would not be closed to me
129|your thoughts, however small.
130|What you saw was so that you may not refuse
131|to open your heart to the waters of peace
132|which from the eternal fountain are poured.
133|I did not ask 'What is the matter?' for the reason he does
134|who looks only with the eye which does not see,
135|when unsouled the body lies;
136|but I asked to give strength to your foot:
137|thus must be goaded the lazy, slow
138|to use their wakefulness when it returns".
139|We were going through the evening, intent
140|forward as far as our eyes could reach
141|against the rays of evening and shining.
142|And behold little by little a smoke making itself
143|toward us dark as the night;
144|nor was there from it a place to shelter.
145|This took from us our eyes and the pure air.
