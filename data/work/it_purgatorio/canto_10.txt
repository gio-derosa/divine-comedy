1|When we were within the threshold of the gate
2|which the evil love of souls disuses,
3|because it makes the crooked way seem straight,
4|resounding I heard it being closed again;
5|and if I had turned my eyes to it,
6|what would have been for the fault a worthy excuse?
7|We were climbing through a cleft rock,
8|which was moving on one and on the other side,
9|just like the wave which flees and approaches.
10|"Here it is needful to use a little art",
11|began my leader, "in keeping close
12|now here, now there to the side which recedes".
13|And this made our steps scant,
14|so that sooner the waning part of the moon
15|reached again its bed to lie down again,
16|than we were out of that needle's eye;
17|but when we were free and in the open
18|up where the mountain gathers itself back,
19|I wearied and both uncertain
20|of our way, we stopped upon a plain
21|more solitary than roads through deserts.
22|From its edge, where it borders the void,
23|to the foot of the high bank which ever rises,
24|a human body would measure three times;
25|and as far as my eye could stretch its wings,
26|now on the left and now on the right side,
27|this cornice seemed to me such.
28|Up there our feet had not yet moved,
29|when I perceived that bank around
30|which of right ascent had lack,
31|to be of white marble and adorned
32|with carvings such, that not only Polycletus,
33|but nature there would have been put to shame.
34|The angel who came to earth with the decree
35|of the peace wept for through many years,
36|which opened heaven from its long ban,
37|before us appeared so true
38|there carved in a gentle attitude,
39|that it did not seem an image which is silent.
40|One would have sworn that he was saying 'Hail!';
41|because there was imaged she
42|who to open the high love turned the key;
43|and she had in her attitude impressed this speech
44|'Behold the handmaid of God', exactly
45|as a figure in wax is sealed.
46|"Do not keep your mind only on one place",
47|said the sweet master, who had me
48|on that side where people have the heart.
49|Whereupon I moved my sight, and saw
50|behind Mary, on that side
51|where was he who moved me,
52|another story in the rock set;
53|so I passed Virgil, and drew near,
54|so that it might be before my eyes displayed.
55|There was carved in the marble itself
56|the cart and the oxen, drawing the holy ark,
57|because of which one fears an office not entrusted.
58|In front appeared people; and all of them,
59|divided in seven choirs, of my two senses
60|made the one say 'No', the other 'Yes, they sing'.
61|Likewise at the smoke of the incense
62|which was imaged there, the eyes and the nose
63|both at the yes and at the no became discordant.
64|There preceded the blessed vessel,
65|dancing girt up, the humble psalmist,
66|and both more and less than king was he in that case.
67|Opposite, portrayed at a window
68|of a great palace, Michal was gazing
69|just like a woman scornful and sad.
70|I moved my feet from the place where I stood,
71|to observe up close another story,
72|which behind Michal gleamed white to me.
73|There was storied the high glory
74|of the Roman prince, whose worth
75|moved Gregory to his great victory;
76|I speak of Trajan the emperor;
77|and a poor widow was at his bridle,
78|in the attitude of tears and of sorrow.
79|Around him it seemed trampled and full
80|of horsemen, and the eagles in the gold
81|above them visibly in the wind were moving.
82|The poor wretch among all these
83|seemed to say: "Lord, give me vengeance
84|for my son who is dead, whereby I am heartbroken";
85|and he to her answer: "Now wait
86|until I return"; and she: "My lord",
87|like a person in whom grief is urgent,
88|"if you do not return?"; and he: "He who will be where I am,
89|will do it for you"; and she: "Another's good deed
90|to you what will it be, if you put your own in oblivion?";
91|whereupon he: "Now take comfort; for it is fitting
92|that I discharge my duty before I move:
93|justice wills it and pity holds me back".
94|He who never saw a new thing
95|produced this visible speech,
96|new to us because here it is not found.
97|While I was delighting in gazing at
98|the images of such great humilities,
99|and for their maker's sake dear to see,
100|"Behold on this side, but they make their steps slow",
101|murmured the poet, "many people:
102|these will direct us to the high steps".
103|My eyes, which were content with gazing
104|to see new things of which they are eager,
105|in turning toward him were not slow.
106|I do not wish, however, reader, that you be dismayed
107|from good purpose by hearing
108|how God wills that the debt be paid.
109|Do not heed the form of the torment:
110|think of what follows; think that at worst
111|beyond the great judgment it cannot go.
112|I began: "Master, what I see
113|moving toward us, do not seem to me persons,
114|and I do not know what, so in seeing I wander".
115|And he to me: "The heavy condition
116|of their torment to the ground crouches them,
117|so that my eyes at first had contention about it.
118|But look fixedly there, and disentangle
119|with your sight what comes under those stones:
120|already you can discern how each one beats his breast".
121|O proud Christians, wretched weary ones,
122|who, in the sight of the mind infirm,
123|have trust in backward steps,
124|do you not perceive that we are worms
125|born to form the angelic butterfly,
126|which flies to judgment without defenses?
127|Why does your mind float on high,
128|since you are like insects defective,
129|just like a worm in which formation fails?
130|As to support a ceiling or roof,
131|as a corbel sometimes a figure
132|is seen to join its knees to its breast,
133|which from the unreal makes real distress
134|be born in him who sees it; so made
135|saw I those, when I paid good heed.
136|True it is that more and less were they contracted
137|according as they had more and less on their backs;
138|and whoever had most patience in his bearing,
139|weeping seemed to say: 'I can no more'.
