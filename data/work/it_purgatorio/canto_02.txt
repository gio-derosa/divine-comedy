1|Already the sun had reached the horizon
2|whose meridian circle covers
3|Jerusalem with its highest point;
4|and the night, which opposite to it circles,
5|was coming forth out of the Ganges with the Scales,
6|which fall from her hand when she exceeds;
7|so that the white and the vermilion cheeks,
8|there where I was, of the fair Aurora
9|through too much age were becoming orange.
10|We were along the sea still,
11|like people who think of their road,
12|who go with the heart and with the body stay.
13|And behold, as, overtaken by the morning,
14|through the thick vapors Mars grows red
15|low in the west over the ocean floor,
16|such appeared to me, may I again see it,
17|a light coming over the sea so swift,
18|that its motion no flight equals.
19|From which when I had a little withdrawn
20|my eye to question my leader,
21|I saw it again made brighter and larger.
22|Then on each side of it appeared to me
23|a something white, I knew not what, and from below
24|little by little another came out of it.
25|My master still did not say a word,
26|while the first whites appeared as wings;
27|then when he well recognized the boatman,
28|he cried: "Quick, quick, bend your knees.
29|Behold the angel of God: fold your hands;
30|henceforth you will see such officials.
31|See how he scorns human instruments,
32|so that oar he wants not, nor other sail
33|than his wings, between shores so distant.
34|See how he has them straight toward the sky,
35|stirring the air with the eternal feathers,
36|which do not change like mortal hair".
37|Then, as more and more toward us came
38|the divine bird, brighter he appeared:
39|so that the eye close up could not bear him,
40|but I bent it down; and he came to the shore
41|with a vessel slender and light,
42|so much that the water swallowed nothing of it.
43|At the stern stood the celestial pilot,
44|such that he would make blessed merely described;
45|and more than a hundred spirits sat within.
46|'When Israel went out of Egypt'
47|they were singing all together with one voice
48|with as much of that psalm as is afterward written.
49|Then he made them the sign of the holy cross;
50|whereupon they all threw themselves on the shore:
51|and he went away, as he came, swift.
52|The crowd which remained there, strange
53|seemed to the place, gazing around
54|like one who tastes new things.
55|On all sides was shooting forth the day
56|the sun, which had with its bright arrows
57|from the middle of the sky chased Capricorn,
58|when the new people raised their brow
59|toward us, saying to us: "If you know,
60|show us the way to go to the mountain".
61|And Virgil answered: "You believe
62|perhaps that we are experienced in this place;
63|but we are pilgrims as you are.
64|We came just now, a little before you,
65|by another way, which was so rough and hard,
66|that the climbing now will seem to us play".
67|The souls, who had become aware of me,
68|by the breathing, that I was still alive,
69|marveling became pale.
70|And as to a messenger who carries an olive branch
71|the people crowd to hear news,
72|and of trampling no one shows himself shy,
73|so on my face fixed themselves those
74|fortunate souls, each and every one,
75|as if forgetting to go to make themselves fair.
76|I saw one of them draw himself forward
77|to embrace me, with such great affection,
78|that it moved me to do the like.
79|Oh shades empty, except in appearance!
80|three times behind it I clasped my hands,
81|and as many I returned with them to my breast.
82|With wonder, I think, I painted myself;
83|at which the shade smiled and drew back,
84|and I, following it, pushed myself forward.
85|Gently it said that I should stop;
86|then I recognized who it was, and I begged
87|that, to speak to me, it would stay a little.
88|It answered me: "Just as I loved you
89|in the mortal body, so I love you freed:
90|therefore I stop; but you, why do you go?".
91|"My Casella, to return another time
92|there where I am, I make this journey",
93|said I; "but from you how has so much time been taken?".
94|And he to me: "No wrong has been done to me,
95|if he who takes up when and whom he pleases,
96|several times has denied me this passage;
97|for from a just will his own is made:
98|truly for three months he has taken
99|whoever has wished to enter, in all peace.
100|Whereupon I, who was now turned to the sea-shore
101|where the water of the Tiber becomes salt,
102|kindly was by him gathered up.
103|To that river-mouth has he now directed his wing,
104|because always there are gathered
105|those who toward Acheron do not descend".
106|And I: "If a new law does not take from you
107|memory or practice of the amorous song
108|which used to quiet all my sorrows,
109|with that may it please you to console somewhat
110|my soul, which, with its body
111|coming here, is so wearied!".
112|'Love that in my mind discourses with me'
113|he began then so sweetly,
114|that the sweetness still within me sounds.
115|My master and I and those people
116|who were with him seemed so content,
117|as if to none anything else touched the mind.
118|We were all fixed and attentive
119|to his notes; and behold the honorable old man
120|crying: "What is this, slow spirits?
121|what negligence, what standing still is this?
122|Run to the mountain to strip yourselves of the slough
123|which does not let God be manifest to you".
124|As when, gathering wheat or tares,
125|the doves assembled at their feeding,
126|quiet, without showing their usual pride,
127|if something appears whereof they have fear,
128|suddenly leave the food alone,
129|because they are assailed by a greater care;
130|so I saw that fresh company
131|leave the song, and flee toward the slope,
132|like a man who goes, nor knows where he will come out;
133|nor was our departure less quick.
