1|The concubine of ancient Tithonus
2|was already whitening at the balcony of the east,
3|out of the arms of her sweet lover;
4|with gems her brow was shining,
5|set in the figure of the cold animal
6|which with its tail strikes people;
7|and the night, of the steps with which it climbs,
8|had made two in the place where we were,
9|and the third was already bending down its wings;
10|when I, who had with me something of Adam,
11|vanquished by sleep, on the grass reclined
12|there where already all five of us were sitting.
13|At the hour when begins her sad lays
14|the little swallow near the morning,
15|perhaps in memory of her first woes,
16|and when our mind, more a pilgrim
17|from the flesh and less by thoughts taken,
18|in its visions is almost divine,
19|in a dream I seemed to see suspended
20|an eagle in the sky with feathers of gold,
21|with wings open and intent on swooping down;
22|and I seemed to be there where were
23|abandoned his companions by Ganymede,
24|when he was snatched up to the highest consistory.
25|Within myself I thought: 'Perhaps this one strikes
26|only here by habit, and perhaps from another place
27|disdains to carry anyone up in its talons'.
28|Then it seemed to me that, having wheeled a little,
29|terrible as lightning it descended,
30|and snatched me upward as far as the fire.
31|There it seemed that it and I were burning;
32|and so the imagined fire scorched,
33|that it was necessary that my sleep be broken.
34|Not otherwise Achilles shook himself awake,
35|his awakened eyes turning around
36|and not knowing where he was,
37|when his mother from Chiron to Scyros
38|carried him off sleeping in her arms,
39|there whence afterward the Greeks took him away;
40|than I shook myself, as from my face
41|sleep fled, and I became pale,
42|as does the man who, frightened, freezes.
43|Beside me was only my comfort,
44|and the sun was high already more than two hours,
45|and my face was turned to the sea.
46|"Do not have fear", said my lord;
47|"make yourself sure, for we are at a good point;
48|do not restrain, but release all your vigor.
49|You have now arrived at purgatory:
50|see there the rampart which encloses it around;
51|see the entrance there where it seems disjoined.
52|Just now, in the dawn which precedes the day,
53|when your soul was sleeping within you,
54|upon the flowers with which down there is adorned
55|came a lady, and said: 'I am Lucia;
56|let me take this one who sleeps;
57|so I will speed him on his way'.
58|Sordello remained and the other noble forms;
59|she took you, and when the day was bright,
60|she came up; and I in her footsteps.
61|Here she laid you, but first showed me
62|her fair eyes that open entrance;
63|then she and your sleep together went away".
64|In the manner of a man who in doubt is reassured
65|and who changes into comfort his fear,
66|after the truth is disclosed to him,
67|I changed; and when free from care
68|my leader saw me, up along the rampart
69|he moved, and I behind toward the height.
70|Reader, you see well how I exalt
71|my subject matter, and therefore if with more art
72|I reinforce it, do not marvel.
73|We drew near, and were at a point
74|such that there where it seemed to me before a break,
75|just like a cleft which divides a wall,
76|I saw a gate, and three steps below
77|to go to it, of different colors,
78|and a gatekeeper who as yet did not say a word.
79|And as I opened my eye more and more on it,
80|I saw him sitting upon the highest step,
81|such in his face that I could not bear it;
82|and a naked sword he had in his hand,
83|which reflected the rays so toward us,
84|that I directed often my sight in vain.
85|"Say from there: what do you want?",
86|he began to say, "where is your escort?
87|Take care that the coming up does not harm you".
88|"A lady of heaven, aware of these things",
89|answered my master to him, "just now
90|said to us: 'Go there: there is the gate'".
91|"And may she advance your steps in good",
92|began again the courteous gatekeeper:
93|"Come then forward to our steps".
94|There we came; and the first step
95|white marble was so polished and clean,
96|that I mirrored myself in it as I appear.
97|The second was dyed darker than perse,
98|of a stone rough and scorched,
99|cracked lengthwise and crosswise.
100|The third, which above is massed,
101|porphyry it seemed to me, so flaming
102|as blood which spurts out of a vein.
103|Upon this one held both his soles
104|the angel of God sitting upon the threshold
105|which seemed to me stone of diamond.
106|Up the three steps with good will
107|drew me my leader, saying: "Ask
108|humbly that he undo the lock".
109|Devout I threw myself at the holy feet;
110|mercy I asked and that he open for me,
111|but three times on the breast first I struck myself.
112|Seven P's on my forehead he inscribed
113|with the point of his sword, and "See that you wash,
114|when you are inside, these wounds" he said.
115|Ashes, or earth which dry is dug,
116|of one color would be with his garment;
117|and from beneath that he drew two keys.
118|One was of gold and the other was of silver;
119|first with the white and afterward with the yellow
120|he did to the gate so, that I was content.
121|"Whenever one of these keys fails,
122|so that it does not turn right in the lock",
123|said he to us, "this passage does not open.
124|More precious is one; but the other requires much
125|of art and of wit before it unlocks,
126|because it is that which the knot unties.
127|From Peter I hold them; and he told me to err
128|rather in opening than in keeping it locked,
129|provided that the people at my feet prostrate themselves".
130|Then he pushed the door of the sacred gate,
131|saying: "Enter; but I make you aware
132|that outside returns he who looks behind".
133|And when were turned in their hinges
134|the pivots of that sacred portal,
135|which of metal are resounding and strong,
136|did not roar so nor show itself so harsh
137|Tarpeia, when was taken from it the good
138|Metellus, whereby afterward it remained lean.
139|I turned attentive to the first thunder,
140|and 'We praise thee, God' I seemed
141|to hear in a voice mingled with the sweet sound.
142|Such an image exactly gave me
143|what I heard, as one is used to receive
144|when to singing with organs one is standing;
145|for now yes now no the words are understood.
