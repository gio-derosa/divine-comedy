1|Remember, reader, if ever in the mountains
2|a fog caught you through which you saw
3|not otherwise than moles through skin,
4|how, when the vapors moist and thick
5|begin to thin out, the sphere
6|of the sun feebly enters through them;
7|and your imagination will be quick
8|in coming to see how I saw again
9|the sun at first, which was already at its setting.
10|So, matching mine with the trusty steps
11|of my master, I came out of such a cloud
12|to the rays already dead on the low shores.
13|O imagination which steals us
14|at times so from outside, that one does not notice
15|although around a thousand trumpets sound,
16|who moves you, if the sense does not offer to you?
17|A light moves you which in heaven is formed,
18|by itself or by a will which guides it down.
19|Of the impiety of her who changed her form
20|into the bird which most delights to sing,
21|in my imagination appeared the imprint;
22|and here my mind was so restricted
23|within itself, that from outside there came
24|nothing which was then by it received.
25|Then rained within the high fantasy
26|one crucified, scornful and fierce
27|in his look, and so he was dying;
28|around him was the great Ahasuerus,
29|Esther his wife and the just Mordecai,
30|who was in speech and in deed so upright.
31|And as this image broke
32|itself by itself, in the manner of a bubble
33|which lacks the water under which it was made,
34|there rose in my vision a maiden
35|weeping loudly, and she said: "O queen,
36|why through anger have you wished to be nothing?
37|You have killed yourself so as not to lose Lavinia;
38|now you have lost me! I am she who mourns,
39|mother, for yours before another's ruin".
40|As sleep is broken when suddenly
41|new light strikes the closed eyes,
42|which, broken, quivers before it wholly dies;
43|so my imagining fell down
44|as soon as a light struck my face,
45|greater by far than that which is in our use.
46|I was turning to see where I was,
47|when a voice said "Here one climbs",
48|which from every other intent removed me;
49|and it made my will so eager
50|to look at who it was that spoke,
51|as never rests, until it is face to face.
52|But as at the sun which weighs down our sight
53|and by excess veils its form,
54|so my power there was failing.
55|"This is a divine spirit, who in the
56|way to go up directs us without prayer,
57|and with his light hides himself.
58|He does with us, as a man does with himself;
59|for he who waits for a prayer and sees the need,
60|malignly already sets himself to denial.
61|Now let us accord to such an invitation our foot;
62|let us strive to climb before it grows dark,
63|for then one could not, until the day returns".
64|Thus said my leader, and I with him
65|turned our steps to a stairway;
66|and as soon as I was at the first step,
67|I felt near me as it were a moving of a wing
68|and fanning me in the face and saying: 'Blessed
69|are the peacemakers, who are without evil anger!'.
70|Already were above us so far raised
71|the last rays which the night follows,
72|that the stars were appearing on many sides.
73|'O my strength, why do you so melt away?',
74|within myself I said, for I felt
75|the power of my legs put in truce.
76|We were where no more was rising
77|the stairway up, and we were fixed,
78|just like a ship which arrives at the shore.
79|And I waited a little, if I might hear
80|anything in the new circle;
81|then I turned to my master, and said:
82|"My sweet father, say, what offense
83|is purged here in the circle where we are?
84|If the feet stand still, let not your speech stand still".
85|And he to me: "The love of good, falling short
86|of its duty, right here is restored;
87|here is plied again the ill-slackened oar.
88|But that you may understand yet more openly,
89|turn your mind to me, and you will gather
90|some good fruit from our stay".
91|"Neither creator nor creature ever",
92|he began, "son, was without love,
93|either natural or of the mind; and you know it.
94|The natural is always without error,
95|but the other can err through an evil object
96|or through too much or through too little vigor.
97|While it is on the first good directed,
98|and in the secondary ones measures itself,
99|it cannot be the cause of evil delight;
100|but when it twists to evil, or with more care
101|or with less than it should runs toward the good,
102|against the maker works his creature.
103|From this you can understand that love must be
104|the seed in you of every virtue
105|and of every action which deserves punishment.
106|Now, since never can from the welfare
107|of its subject love turn its face,
108|from self-hatred all things are safe;
109|and since one cannot conceive any being divided
110|from the first, and standing by itself,
111|from hating that every creature is cut off.
112|It remains, if by dividing I judge well,
113|that the evil which is loved is of one's neighbor; and this
114|love is born in three ways in your clay.
115|There is he who, by his neighbor being suppressed,
116|hopes for excellence, and only for this longs
117|that he be from his greatness brought low;
118|there is he who power, favor, honor and fame
119|fears to lose because another rises above,
120|whereupon he grows so sad that he loves the contrary;
121|and there is he who by injury seems to be so shamed,
122|that he becomes greedy of vengeance,
123|and such a one must contrive another's harm.
124|This threefold love down here below
125|is wept for: now I wish that you understand of the other,
126|which runs toward the good with corrupt order.
127|Each one confusedly apprehends a good
128|in which the mind may be at rest, and desires it;
129|wherefore to attain it each one strives.
130|If slow love draws you to see it
131|or to acquire it, this cornice,
132|after just repentance, torments you for it.
133|There is another good which does not make man happy;
134|it is not happiness, it is not the good
135|essence, of every good the fruit and root.
136|The love which to it too much abandons itself,
137|above us is wept for in three circles;
138|but how as tripartite it is reasoned,
139|I keep silent, so that you may search for it by yourself".
