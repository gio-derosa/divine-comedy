1|While my eyes through the green foliage
2|I was fixing just as he is wont to do
3|who behind little birds wastes his life,
4|he who was more than a father said to me: "Son,
5|come now, for the time which is allotted us
6|must be more usefully apportioned".
7|I turned my face, and my step no less quickly,
8|after the sages, who were speaking so,
9|that they made the going of no cost to me.
10|And behold weeping and singing were heard
11|'My lips, O Lord' in a manner
12|such, that it gave birth to delight and sorrow.
13|"O sweet father, what is that which I hear?",
14|I began; and he: "Shades which go
15|perhaps untying the knot of their debt".
16|Just as pensive pilgrims do,
17|overtaking on the road people not known,
18|who turn to them and do not stop,
19|so behind us, more quickly moving,
20|coming and passing by, gazed at us
21|a crowd of souls silent and devout.
22|In the eyes each one was dark and hollow,
23|pale in the face, and so wasted
24|that from the bones the skin took its shape.
25|I do not believe that so to the utmost rind
26|Erysichthon had been made dry,
27|through fasting, when he had most fear of it.
28|I said within myself thinking: 'Behold
29|the people who lost Jerusalem,
30|when Mary put her beak into her son!'.
31|The eye-sockets seemed rings without gems:
32|he who in the face of men reads 'omo'
33|would well have recognized there the M.
34|Who would believe that the scent of a fruit
35|could so govern, generating craving,
36|and that of a water, not knowing how?
37|Already I was marveling at what so famishes them,
38|because the cause was not yet manifest
39|of their leanness and of their sad scurf,
40|and behold from the depth of its head
41|a shade turned its eyes to me and looked fixedly;
42|then cried aloud: "What grace is this to me?".
43|Never would I have recognized him by his face;
44|but in his voice was made clear to me
45|that which his aspect had in itself destroyed.
46|This spark wholly rekindled for me
47|my knowledge of the changed features,
48|and I recognized the face of Forese.
49|"Ah, do not pay heed to the dry scab
50|which discolors", he begged, "my skin,
51|nor to lack of flesh which I may have;
52|but tell me the truth about yourself, tell me who are those
53|two souls who there escort you;
54|do not stay without speaking to me!".
55|"Your face, which I wept for once when dead,
56|gives me now no less grief for weeping",
57|I answered him, "seeing it so distorted.
58|Therefore tell me, by God, what so strips you;
59|do not make me speak while I marvel,
60|for ill can he speak who is full of another wish".
61|And he to me: "From the eternal counsel
62|falls a power into the water and into the plant
63|left behind, whereby I become so thin.
64|All these people who weeping sing
65|for having followed gluttony beyond measure,
66|in hunger and in thirst here make themselves holy again.
67|For drinking and for eating kindles desire in us
68|the scent which comes from the fruit and from the spray
69|which spreads up over its greenery.
70|And not only once, this floor
71|circling, is our pain renewed:
72|I say pain, and I ought to say solace,
73|for that will leads us to the trees
74|which led Christ gladly to say 'Eli',
75|when he freed us with his vein".
76|And I to him: "Forese, from that day
77|on which you changed world for a better life,
78|five years have not revolved up to now.
79|If sooner was the power in you ended
80|of sinning more, than there came the hour
81|of the good grief which weds us again to God,
82|how have you come up here already?
83|I believed I would find you down there below,
84|where time for time is repaid".
85|Whereupon he to me: "So quickly has led me
86|to drink the sweet wormwood of the torments
87|my Nella with her flooding tears.
88|With her devout prayers and with sighs
89|she has drawn me from the slope where one waits,
90|and has freed me from the other circles.
91|So much more dear to God and more beloved
92|is my little widow, whom I loved much,
93|as in good works she is more alone;
94|for the Barbagia of Sardinia by far
95|in its women is more modest
96|than the Barbagia where I left her.
97|O sweet brother, what do you wish me to say?
98|A future time is already in my sight,
99|for which this hour will not be very old,
100|in which it will be forbidden from the pulpit
101|to the shameless Florentine women
102|to go showing with their teats their breast.
103|What barbarian women were there ever, what Saracen,
104|for whom there was need, to make them go covered,
105|of either spiritual or other disciplines?
106|But if the shameless ones were certain
107|of what the swift heaven is preparing for them,
108|already to howl they would have their mouths open;
109|for, if foresight here does not deceive me,
110|they will be sad before his cheeks grow hair
111|he who now is soothed with lullabies.
112|Ah, brother, now see that you hide yourself from me no more!
113|see that not only I, but these people
114|all gaze there where you veil the sun".
115|Whereupon I to him: "If you bring back to mind
116|what you were with me, and what I was with you,
117|still will the present remembering be grievous.
118|From that life turned me this one
119|who goes before me, the other day, when round
120|showed herself to you the sister of that one",
121|and I pointed to the sun; "this one through the deep
122|night has led me of the truly dead
123|with this true flesh which follows him.
124|From there his comforts have drawn me up,
125|climbing and circling the mountain
126|which straightens you whom the world made crooked.
127|So long he says he will bear me company
128|until I shall be there where Beatrice will be;
129|there I must remain without him.
130|Virgil is this one who thus speaks to me",
131|and I pointed to him; "and this other is that shade
132|for whom just now shook every slope
133|your realm, which from itself releases him".
