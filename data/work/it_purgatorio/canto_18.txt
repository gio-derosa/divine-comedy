1|An end had put to his reasoning
2|the lofty teacher, and attentively he looked
3|into my face to see if I seemed content;
4|and I, whom a new thirst was still goading,
5|outwardly was silent, and within said: 'Perhaps
6|the too much asking which I do annoys him'.
7|But that true father, who perceived
8|the timid wish which did not open itself,
9|by speaking, gave me boldness to speak.
10|Whereupon I: "Master, my sight is so quickened
11|in your light, that I discern clearly
12|all that your reasoning distinguishes or describes.
13|Therefore I pray you, sweet dear father,
14|that you explain to me love, to which you reduce
15|every good action and its contrary".
16|"Direct", he said, "toward me the sharp eyes
17|of the intellect, and it will be manifest to you
18|the error of the blind who make themselves leaders.
19|The mind, which is created quick to love,
20|is mobile toward everything which pleases,
21|as soon as by pleasure it is roused into act.
22|Your apprehension from a real being
23|draws an image, and within you unfolds it,
24|so that it makes the mind turn to it;
25|and if, turned, toward it it inclines,
26|that inclining is love, that is nature
27|which through pleasure anew is bound in you.
28|Then, as fire moves upward
29|through its form which is born to rise
30|there where it lasts longest in its matter,
31|so the captured mind enters into desire,
32|which is a spiritual motion, and never rests
33|until the thing loved makes it rejoice.
34|Now it can appear to you how hidden is
35|the truth from the people who affirm
36|each love in itself a praiseworthy thing;
37|because perhaps its matter appears
38|always to be good, but not every imprint
39|is good, even though good be the wax".
40|"Your words and my following wit",
41|I answered him, "have revealed love to me,
42|but that has made me more pregnant with doubt;
43|for, if love is from outside offered to us
44|and the soul does not go with another foot,
45|if it goes straight or crooked, it is not its merit".
46|And he to me: "As much as reason sees here,
47|I can tell you; from there beyond wait
48|only for Beatrice, for it is a work of faith.
49|Every substantial form, which is distinct
50|from matter and is with it united,
51|has a specific power in itself collected,
52|which without operating is not perceived,
53|nor is it shown ever except by its effect,
54|as by green leaves life in a plant.
55|Therefore, whence may come the understanding
56|of the first notions, man does not know,
57|nor the inclination toward the first objects of appetite,
58|which are in you just as zeal in the bee
59|to make honey; and this first will
60|admits no merit of praise or of blame.
61|Now so that to this every other may be conformed,
62|innate in you is the power which counsels,
63|and which must hold the threshold of assent.
64|This is the principle whence is taken
65|the reason of deserving in you, according
66|as it gathers and winnows good and guilty loves.
67|Those who, reasoning, went to the bottom,
68|perceived this innate freedom;
69|therefore they left ethics to the world.
70|Whence, let us suppose that of necessity
71|arises every love which within you is kindled,
72|to restrain it is in you the power.
73|The noble power Beatrice understands
74|as free will, and therefore take care
75|that you have it in mind, if she takes to speaking to you of it".
76|The moon, belated almost to midnight,
77|made the stars appear to us fewer,
78|shaped like a bucket which is all burning;
79|and it ran against the sky along those roads
80|which the sun inflames when he of Rome
81|between the Sardinians and the Corsicans sees it when it sets.
82|And that noble shade through whom is named
83|Pietola more than any Mantuan town,
84|of my burden had laid down the load;
85|so that I, who the reasoning clear and plain
86|upon my questions had gathered,
87|stood like a man who drowsily rambles.
88|But this drowsiness was taken from me
89|suddenly by people who behind
90|our backs toward us had already turned.
91|And such as Ismenus once saw and Asopus
92|along their banks by night fury and throng,
93|whenever the Thebans had need of Bacchus,
94|such along that circle curves its step,
95|by what I saw of those, as they came,
96|whom good will and just love ride.
97|Soon they were upon us, because running
98|was moving all that great throng;
99|and two in front were crying weeping:
100|"Mary ran with haste to the mountain;
101|and Caesar, to subdue Ilerda,
102|stung Marseilles and then ran to Spain".
103|"Quick, quick, so that time be not lost
104|through little love", cried the others after,
105|"so that zeal for doing well may make grace green again".
106|"O people in whom keen fervor now
107|perhaps makes up for negligence and delay
108|by you through lukewarmness in doing good put,
109|this one who lives, and surely I do not lie to you,
110|wishes to go up, as soon as the sun shines again for us;
111|therefore tell us where the opening is near".
112|These were words of my leader;
113|and one of those spirits said: "Come
114|behind us, and you will find the gap.
115|We are with desire to move so full,
116|that we cannot stop; therefore pardon,
117|if you hold our justice to be rudeness.
118|I was abbot in San Zeno at Verona
119|under the rule of the good Barbarossa,
120|of whom still grieving Milan speaks.
121|And there is one who has already one foot in the grave,
122|who soon will weep for that monastery,
123|and will be sad to have had power there;
124|because his son, ill in his whole body,
125|and in his mind worse, and who was ill born,
126|he has put in place of its true pastor".
127|I do not know if he said more or if he was silent,
128|so far had he already run beyond us;
129|but this I heard, and it pleased me to retain.
130|And he who was for me in every need a help
131|said: "Turn here: see two of them
132|coming giving to sloth a bite".
133|Behind all they were saying: "First were
134|dead the people for whom the sea opened,
135|before the Jordan saw its heirs.
136|And those who did not endure the toil
137|until the end with the son of Anchises,
138|offered themselves to a life without glory".
139|Then when were from us so far separated
140|those shades, that they could no longer be seen,
141|a new thought placed itself within me,
142|from which many others were born and diverse;
143|and so much from one to another I wandered,
144|that my eyes for pleasure I closed,
145|and my thinking into dream I transformed.
