1|'O God, the nations have come', alternating
2|now three now four, a sweet psalmody,
3|the ladies began, and weeping;
4|and Beatrice, sighing and compassionate,
5|was listening to them so changed, that little
6|more at the cross was Mary changed.
7|But when the other virgins gave place
8|to her to speak, risen upright on her feet,
9|she answered, colored like fire:
10|'A little while, and you will not see me;
11|and again, my beloved sisters,
12|a little while, and you will see me'.
13|Then she put all seven before her,
14|and after her, only by beckoning, she moved
15|me and the lady and the sage who had remained.
16|So she went on; and I do not believe that
17|her tenth step had been set on the ground,
18|when with her eyes she struck my eyes;
19|and with tranquil aspect "Come more quickly",
20|she said to me, "so that, if I speak with you,
21|to listen to me you may be well placed".
22|As soon as I was, as I ought to be, with her,
23|she said to me: "Brother, why do you not venture
24|to ask me now as you come with me?".
25|As to those who too reverent
26|in speaking before their superiors are,
27|so that they do not bring the voice whole to their teeth,
28|it happened to me, that without full sound
29|I began: "My lady, my need
30|you know, and what is good for it".
31|And she to me: "From fear and from shame
32|I wish that you now disentangle yourself,
33|so that you no longer speak like a man who dreams.
34|Know that the vessel which the serpent broke,
35|was and is not; but let him who is to blame believe
36|that the vengeance of God fears no sops.
37|Not for all time will be without heir
38|the eagle which left its feathers on the chariot,
39|whereby it became a monster and then a prey;
40|for I see surely, and therefore I tell it,
41|stars already near to bring the time,
42|safe from every hindrance and from every barrier,
43|in which a five hundred ten and five,
44|sent by God, will kill the thief
45|with that giant who sins with her.
46|And perhaps my dark narration,
47|like Themis and Sphinx, persuades you less,
48|because in their manner it clouds the intellect;
49|but soon the facts will be the Naiads,
50|which will solve this hard enigma
51|without loss of sheep or of grain.
52|You take note; and just as by me they are uttered,
53|so mark these words for the living
54|of the life which is a running to death.
55|And keep in mind, when you write them,
56|not to conceal how you have seen the tree
57|which is now twice robbed here.
58|Whoever robs it or breaks it,
59|with blasphemy of deed offends God,
60|who for his own use alone created it holy.
61|For biting it, in pain and in desire
62|five thousand years and more the first soul
63|longed for him who punished the bite in himself.
64|Your wit sleeps, if it does not judge
65|that for a singular cause it is lofty
66|so much and so inverted at the top.
67|And if they had not been water of Elsa
68|the vain thoughts around your mind,
69|and their pleasure a Pyramus to the mulberry,
70|by so many circumstances alone
71|the justice of God, in the prohibition,
72|you would recognize in the tree morally.
73|But because I see you in your intellect
74|made of stone and, petrified, darkened,
75|so that the light of my word dazzles you,
76|I wish too, and if not written, at least painted,
77|that you carry it within you for that reason
78|for which the pilgrim's staff is brought back wreathed with palm".
79|And I: "Just as wax by a seal,
80|which does not change the figure impressed,
81|my brain is now stamped by you.
82|But why so far above my sight
83|does your longed-for word fly,
84|that the more it strives the more it loses it?".
85|"So that you may know", she said, "that school
86|which you have followed, and see how its doctrine
87|can follow my word;
88|and see your way from the divine
89|to be as distant, as is apart
90|from earth the heaven which highest hastens".
91|Whereupon I answered her: "I do not remember
92|that I ever estranged myself from you,
93|nor have I conscience of it which bites".
94|"And if you cannot remember it",
95|smiling she answered, "now remember
96|how you drank of Lethe today;
97|and if from smoke fire is inferred,
98|this forgetfulness clearly proves
99|a fault in your will intent elsewhere.
100|Truly henceforth will be naked
101|my words, as much as it will be fitting
102|to uncover them to your rude sight".
103|And more flashing and with slower steps
104|the sun was holding the circle of noon,
105|which here and there, as the aspects are, is made,
106|when there stopped, just as stops
107|one who goes before people as escort
108|if he finds something new or its traces,
109|the seven ladies at the edge of a pale shade,
110|such as under green leaves and black branches
111|over its cold streams the mountain bears.
112|In front of them Euphrates and Tigris
113|I seemed to see come out of one fountain,
114|and, like friends, part slowly.
115|"O light, O glory of the human race,
116|what water is this which here unfolds
117|from one source and from itself departs?".
118|For such a prayer it was said to me: "Pray
119|Matelda that she tell it to you". And here answered,
120|as does one who frees himself from blame,
121|the fair lady: "This and other things
122|have been told him by me; and I am sure
123|that the water of Lethe did not hide it from him".
124|And Beatrice: "Perhaps a greater care,
125|which often deprives of memory,
126|has made his mind in its eyes dark.
127|But see Eunoe which there flows forth:
128|lead him to it, and as you are accustomed,
129|revive his swooning power".
130|As a noble soul, which makes no excuse,
131|but makes its will of the will of another
132|as soon as it is by a sign disclosed;
133|so, after I was taken by her,
134|the fair lady moved, and to Statius
135|in a ladylike manner said: "Come with him".
136|If I had, reader, a longer space
137|for writing, I would still sing in part
138|the sweet drinking which would never have sated me;
139|but because full are all the pages
140|laid out for this second cantica,
141|the curb of art lets me go no further.
142|I returned from the most holy wave
143|remade just as new plants
144|renewed with new foliage,
145|pure and ready to rise to the stars.
