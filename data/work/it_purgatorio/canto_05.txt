1|I had already from those shades departed,
2|and was following the footprints of my leader,
3|when behind me, pointing his finger,
4|one cried: "See that it seems not to shine,
5|the ray on the left of that one below,
6|and like a living man he seems to conduct himself!".
7|My eyes I turned at the sound of this word,
8|and saw them gazing in wonder
9|just at me, just at me, and at the light which was broken.
10|"Why does your mind so entangle itself",
11|said the master, "that you slacken your going?
12|what does it matter to you what is whispered here?
13|Come behind me, and let the people talk:
14|stand like a firm tower, which does not shake
15|ever its top for blowing of winds;
16|for always the man in whom thought springs up
17|upon thought, removes from himself the mark,
18|because the ardor of the one softens the other".
19|What could I say back, if not "I come"?
20|I said it, somewhat sprinkled with the color
21|which makes a man of pardon sometimes worthy.
22|And meanwhile across the slope crosswise
23|came people a little before us,
24|singing 'Have mercy' verse by verse.
25|When they perceived that I gave no room
26|through my body to the passing of the rays,
27|they changed their song into an "oh!" long and hoarse;
28|and two of them, in the form of messengers,
29|ran to meet us and asked us:
30|"Of your condition make us aware".
31|And my master: "You can go away
32|and report to those who sent you
33|that the body of this one is true flesh.
34|If to see his shadow they stopped,
35|as I suppose, enough is answered them:
36|let them do him honor, and it can be dear to them".
37|Kindled vapors never did I see so quickly
38|at early night cleave the clear sky,
39|nor, at the setting sun, clouds of August,
40|as those returned up in less time;
41|and, arrived there, with the others to us turned back,
42|like a troop that runs without rein.
43|"These people who press on us are many,
44|and they come to pray you", said the poet:
45|"therefore keep going, and while going listen".
46|"O soul who goes to be glad
47|with those limbs with which you were born",
48|they came crying, "a little quiet your step.
49|Look if any of us you ever saw,
50|so that of him over there you may carry news:
51|ah, why do you go? ah, why do you not stop?
52|We were all once by violence killed,
53|and sinners until the last hour;
54|there light from heaven made us aware,
55|so that, repenting and forgiving, out
56|of life we went with God at peace,
57|who with the desire of seeing him afflicts us".
58|And I: "However much in your faces I gaze,
59|I do not recognize anyone; but if it pleases you
60|a thing which I can do, spirits well born,
61|you say, and I will do it by that peace
62|which, behind the feet of such a guide,
63|from world to world makes me seek it".
64|And one began: "Each one trusts
65|in your benefit without your swearing it,
66|provided that the will inability does not cut off.
67|Whereupon I, who alone before the others speak,
68|pray you, if ever you see that land
69|which sits between Romagna and that of Charles,
70|that you be to me of your prayers courteous
71|in Fano, so that well for me they may pray
72|so that I can purge the grave offenses.
73|From there was I; but the deep wounds
74|whence issued the blood in which I dwelt,
75|were made in me in the bosom of the Antenori,
76|there where I believed to be most safe:
77|he of Este had it done, who held me in anger
78|much beyond what justice would have.
79|But if I had fled toward the Mira,
80|when I was overtaken at Oriago,
81|still would I be over there where one breathes.
82|I ran to the marsh, and the reeds and the mire
83|entangled me so that I fell; and there I saw
84|of my veins made on the ground a lake".
85|Then said another: "Ah, so may that desire
86|be fulfilled which draws you to the high mountain,
87|with good pity help mine!
88|I was of Montefeltro, I am Bonconte;
89|Giovanna or others have no care for me;
90|wherefore I go among these with lowered brow".
91|And I to him: "What force or what chance
92|led you astray so far from Campaldino,
93|that never was known your burial place?".
94|"Oh!", answered he, "at the foot of the Casentino
95|crosses a water which has the name Archiano,
96|which above the Hermitage rises in the Apennines.
97|There where its name becomes void,
98|arrived I pierced in the throat,
99|fleeing on foot and bloodying the plain.
100|There I lost my sight and my speech;
101|in the name of Mary I ended, and there
102|I fell, and remained my flesh alone.
103|I will tell the truth, and you retell it among the living:
104|the angel of God took me, and he of hell
105|cried: 'O you from heaven, why do you deprive me?
106|You carry away with you of this one the eternal part
107|for one little tear which takes him from me;
108|but I will make of the other another handling!'.
109|Well you know how in the air is gathered
110|that moist vapor which into water returns,
111|as soon as it rises where the cold seizes it.
112|He joined that evil will which only evil seeks
113|with his intellect, and moved the mist and the wind
114|by the power which his nature gave.
115|Then the valley, when the day was spent,
116|from Pratomagno to the great ridge he covered
117|with fog; and the sky above he made so dense,
118|that the pregnant air into water turned;
119|the rain fell, and to the ditches came
120|of it what the earth did not endure;
121|and as it joined the great streams,
122|toward the royal river so swiftly
123|it rushed, that nothing held it back.
124|My body frozen at its mouth
125|found the swollen Archiano; and it pushed it
126|into the Arno, and loosed on my breast the cross
127|which I made of myself when the pain vanquished me;
128|it rolled me along the banks and along the bottom,
129|then with its spoil covered and girded me".
130|"Ah, when you will have returned to the world
131|and rested from the long way",
132|followed the third spirit after the second,
133|"remember me, who am la Pia;
134|Siena made me, Maremma unmade me:
135|he knows it who, having ringed me first,
136|had wedded me with his gem".
