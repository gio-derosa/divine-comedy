1|It was the hour when the climbing wanted no delay;
2|for the sun had the circle of noon
3|left to the Bull and the night to the Scorpion:
4|wherefore, as does the man who does not stop
5|but goes on his way, whatever may appear to him,
6|if the goad of need pierces him,
7|so we entered through the gap,
8|one before the other taking the stairway
9|which by its narrowness separates the climbers.
10|And like the little stork which lifts its wing
11|through desire to fly, and does not venture
12|to abandon the nest, and lowers it down;
13|such was I with desire kindled and quenched
14|to ask, coming as far as the act
15|which he makes who prepares himself to speak.
16|He did not leave off, though the going was swift,
17|my sweet father, but said: "Let fly
18|the bow of speech, which to the iron you have drawn".
19|Then confidently I opened my mouth
20|and began: "How can one become lean
21|there where the need of nourishment does not touch?".
22|"If you remembered how Meleager
23|was consumed at the consuming of a firebrand,
24|this would not be", he said, "so bitter for you;
25|and if you thought how, at your quivering,
26|quivers within the mirror your image,
27|what seems hard would seem to you soft.
28|But so that within your will you may be at ease,
29|behold here Statius; and I call on him and pray
30|that he be now the healer of your wounds".
31|"If I unfold to him the eternal view",
32|answered Statius, "there where you are,
33|let my excuse be that I cannot refuse you".
34|Then he began: "If my words,
35|son, your mind heeds and receives,
36|they will be a light to you on the how which you ask.
37|Perfect blood, which never is drunk
38|by the thirsty veins, and remains
39|like food which you take away from the table,
40|takes in the heart for all human limbs
41|an informing power, like that
42|which goes through the veins to become those.
43|Digested again, it descends where it is more seemly
44|to be silent than to speak; and thence afterward it drips
45|upon another's blood in the natural vessel.
46|There the one and the other gather together,
47|the one disposed to be passive, and the other to act
48|because of the perfect place whence it is pressed;
49|and, joined to it, it begins to work
50|coagulating first, and then quickens
51|that which as its matter it made consist.
52|The active power having become a soul
53|like that of a plant, in so far different,
54|that this one is on the way and that one is already at the shore,
55|it works so much then, that now it moves and feels,
56|like a sea sponge; and then it undertakes
57|to organize the faculties of which it is the seed.
58|Now unfolds, son, now extends
59|the power which is from the heart of the begetter,
60|where nature attends to all the limbs.
61|But how from animal it becomes a speaking being,
62|you do not see yet: this is such a point,
63|that it made one wiser than you once err,
64|so that by his doctrine he made separate
65|from the soul the possible intellect,
66|because he did not see an organ taken up by it.
67|Open to the truth which comes your breast;
68|and know that, as soon as in the fetus
69|the articulation of the brain is perfected,
70|the first mover turns to it glad
71|over such art of nature, and breathes
72|a new spirit, with power replete,
73|which what it finds active there, draws
74|into its substance, and becomes one single soul,
75|which lives and feels and turns itself upon itself.
76|And so that you may wonder less at the word,
77|look at the heat of the sun which becomes wine,
78|joined to the juice which flows from the vine.
79|When Lachesis has no more thread,
80|it frees itself from the flesh, and in potency
81|carries with it both the human and the divine:
82|the other faculties all mute;
83|memory, intelligence and will
84|in act much more than before keen.
85|Without stopping, of itself it falls
86|marvelously to one of the shores;
87|there it first knows its roads.
88|As soon as the place there circumscribes it,
89|the formative power rays around
90|in the same way and as much as in the living limbs.
91|And as the air, when it is very rainy,
92|through another's ray which is reflected in it,
93|with various colors becomes adorned;
94|so the neighboring air there sets itself
95|into that form which upon it stamps
96|by its power the soul which has stopped;
97|and like then to the little flame
98|which follows the fire wherever it moves,
99|follows the spirit its new form.
100|Because from this it then has its appearance,
101|it is called a shade; and from this it then organizes
102|every sense even to the sight.
103|By this we speak and by this we laugh;
104|by this we make the tears and the sighs
105|which about the mountain you may have heard.
106|According as the desires afflict us
107|and the other affections, the shade takes shape;
108|and this is the cause of that at which you wonder".
109|And already we had come to the last torment
110|and had turned to the right hand,
111|and we were intent on another care.
112|There the bank shoots flame outward,
113|and the cornice breathes a blast upward
114|which bends it back and keeps it away from it;
115|whence we had to go on the open side
116|one by one; and I feared the fire
117|on this side, and on that side I feared to fall down.
118|My leader said: "Along this place
119|one must keep on the eyes a tight rein,
120|because one could err by a little".
121|'God of supreme clemency' in the bosom
122|of the great burning then I heard singing,
123|which made me care no less to turn;
124|and I saw spirits going through the flame;
125|so that I looked at them and at my steps,
126|dividing my sight from time to time.
127|After the end which is made to that hymn,
128|they cried aloud: 'I know not a man';
129|then they began again the hymn in low voices.
130|Having finished it, they cried again: "To the woods
131|Diana kept, and Helice she drove away
132|who had felt the poison of Venus".
133|Then to singing they returned; then wives
134|they cried and husbands who were chaste
135|as virtue and matrimony enjoin on us.
136|And this manner I believe suffices them
137|for all the time that the fire burns them:
138|with such cure is it needful and with such food
139|that the wound at last be sewn up again.
