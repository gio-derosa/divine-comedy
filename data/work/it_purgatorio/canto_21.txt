1|The natural thirst which is never sated
2|except with the water whereof the poor woman
3|of Samaria asked the grace,
4|was tormenting me, and haste was goading me
5|along the encumbered way behind my leader,
6|and I was grieving at the just vengeance.
7|And behold, just as Luke writes for us
8|that Christ appeared to the two who were on the way,
9|already risen out of the sepulchral hollow,
10|there appeared to us a shade, and behind us it came,
11|looking down at the crowd which lies at its feet;
12|nor did we notice it, so it spoke first,
13|saying: "O my brothers, God give you peace".
14|We turned quickly, and Virgil
15|returned to it the sign which befits that.
16|Then he began: "In the blessed council
17|may you be placed in peace by the true court
18|which binds me in eternal exile".
19|"How!", said he, and meanwhile we were going swiftly:
20|"if you are shades whom God does not deem worthy of above,
21|who has escorted you so far up his stairs?".
22|And my teacher: "If you look at the marks
23|which this one bears and which the angel outlines,
24|well will you see that with the good he must reign.
25|But since she who day and night spins
26|had not yet drawn off for him the distaff
27|which Clotho loads for each one and packs,
28|his soul, which is your sister and mine,
29|coming up, could not come alone,
30|because it does not see in our manner.
31|Wherefore I was drawn out of the wide throat
32|of hell to show him the way, and I will show him
33|further, as far as my school can lead him.
34|But tell me, if you know, why such shakings
35|the mountain gave just now, and why all together
36|it seemed to cry down to its moist feet".
37|So he gave, by asking, through the needle's eye
38|of my desire, that just with the hope
39|my thirst became less fasting.
40|That one began: "There is nothing which without
41|order the religion of the mountain
42|feels, or which is outside of custom.
43|Free it is here from every alteration:
44|of that which heaven from itself into itself receives
45|there can be here, and of nothing else, the cause.
46|Wherefore neither rain, nor hail, nor snow,
47|nor dew, nor hoarfrost falls any higher
48|than the short little stairway of three steps;
49|clouds dense do not appear nor thin,
50|nor lightning, nor the daughter of Thaumas,
51|who over there often changes regions;
52|dry vapor does not rise any further
53|than to the top of the three steps of which I spoke,
54|where the vicar of Peter has his soles.
55|It trembles perhaps lower down little or much;
56|but through wind which in the earth is hidden,
57|I do not know how, up here it has never trembled.
58|It trembles here when some soul pure
59|feels itself, so that it may rise or move
60|to climb up; and such a cry accompanies it.
61|Of the purity the will alone gives proof,
62|which, wholly free to change its convent,
63|surprises the soul, and it rejoices in willing.
64|First it wills indeed, but the desire does not allow it
65|which divine justice, against the will,
66|as it was toward sinning, sets toward the torment.
67|And I, who have lain in this pain
68|five hundred years and more, only now felt
69|free will for a better threshold:
70|therefore you felt the earthquake and the pious
71|spirits throughout the mountain render praise
72|to that Lord, that soon he may send them up".
73|Thus he spoke to us; and because one enjoys
74|drinking as much as the thirst is great,
75|I could not say how much he did me good.
76|And the wise leader: "Now I see the net
77|which here entangles you and how one escapes from it,
78|why it trembles here and at what you rejoice together.
79|Now who you were, may it please you that I know,
80|and why so many centuries you have lain
81|here, in your words let it be contained for me".
82|"In the time when the good Titus, with the help
83|of the highest king, avenged the wounds
84|whence issued the blood by Judas sold,
85|with the name which most endures and most honors
86|was I over there", answered that spirit,
87|"famous enough, but not with faith yet.
88|So sweet was my vocal spirit,
89|that, a Toulousan, Rome drew me to herself,
90|where I deserved to adorn my temples with myrtle.
91|Statius the people still over there name me:
92|I sang of Thebes, and then of the great Achilles;
93|but I fell on the way with the second burden.
94|For my ardor were the seed the sparks,
95|which warmed me, of the divine flame
96|whereby more than a thousand have been kindled;
97|of the Aeneid I speak, which mother
98|was to me, and was to me nurse, in making poetry:
99|without it I did not fix the weight of a dram.
100|And to have lived over there when
101|Virgil lived, I would consent to one sun
102|more than I owe for my coming out of banishment".
103|These words turned Virgil to me
104|with a face which, silent, said 'Be silent';
105|but the power which wills cannot do everything;
106|for laughter and tears are such followers
107|of the passion from which each springs,
108|that they least follow the will in the most truthful.
109|I only smiled like a man who winks;
110|whereupon the shade fell silent, and looked at me
111|in the eyes where the expression most is fixed;
112|and "So may you bring so much labor to a good end",
113|it said, "why did your face just now
114|show me a flash of laughter?".
115|Now am I on one side and the other caught:
116|one makes me be silent, the other conjures me
117|that I speak; whereupon I sigh, and I am understood
118|by my master, and "Do not be afraid",
119|he says to me, "to speak; but speak and tell him
120|what he asks with such great care".
121|Whereupon I: "Perhaps you marvel,
122|ancient spirit, at the laughing which I did;
123|but I wish that more wonder seize you.
124|This one who guides on high my eyes,
125|is that Virgil from whom you took
126|strength to sing of men and of gods.
127|If you believed another cause for my laughing,
128|leave it as not true, and believe it to be
129|those words which you spoke of him".
130|Already he was bending down to embrace the feet
131|of my teacher, but he said to him: "Brother,
132|do not, for you are a shade and a shade you see".
133|And he rising: "Now you can the quantity
134|comprehend of the love which warms me toward you,
135|when I forget our emptiness,
136|treating the shades as a solid thing".
