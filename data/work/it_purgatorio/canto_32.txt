1|So fixed and attentive were my eyes
2|to satisfy their ten-year thirst,
3|that my other senses were all extinguished.
4|And they on this side and that had a wall
5|of indifference - so the holy smile
6|drew them to itself with the ancient net! -;
7|when by force my face was turned
8|toward my left by those goddesses,
9|because I heard from them a "Too fixedly!";
10|and the condition for seeing which is
11|in eyes just now struck by the sun,
12|made me be without sight for a while.
13|But when to the lesser my sight readjusted itself
14|(and I say 'to the lesser' in respect to the great
15|sensible object from which by force I had withdrawn),
16|I saw on the right arm had wheeled
17|the glorious army, and was returning
18|with the sun and with the seven flames in its face.
19|As under the shields to save itself
20|a troop turns, and wheels with the standard,
21|before it can wholly change its front;
22|that soldiery of the celestial kingdom
23|which was in front, all passed by us
24|before the chariot turned its pole.
25|Then to the wheels the ladies returned,
26|and the griffin moved the blessed burden
27|so, that yet no feather of it was shaken.
28|The fair lady who drew me to the ford
29|and Statius and I were following the wheel
30|which made its orbit with the smaller arc.
31|So walking through the high wood empty,
32|by fault of her who believed the serpent,
33|an angelic melody measured our steps.
34|Perhaps in three flights as much space would cover
35|an unleashed arrow, as we were
36|removed, when Beatrice descended.
37|I heard all murmur "Adam";
38|then they circled a tree stripped
39|of flowers and of other foliage on each branch.
40|Its crown, which spreads so much
41|the more the higher it is, would be by the Indians
42|in their woods for its height marveled at.
43|"Blessed are you, griffin, who do not tear
44|with your beak of this tree sweet to the taste,
45|since the belly is ill twisted by it".
46|So around the sturdy tree
47|cried the others; and the twofold animal:
48|"So is preserved the seed of all justice".
49|And turned to the pole which he had drawn,
50|he drew it to the foot of the widowed bough,
51|and what came from it to it he left bound.
52|As our plants, when falls
53|down the great light mingled with that
54|which rays behind the heavenly Carp,
55|become swollen, and then is renewed
56|with its color each one, before the sun
57|yokes his coursers under another star;
58|less than of roses and more than of violets
59|opening a color, the tree was renewed,
60|which before had its branches so bare.
61|I did not understand it, nor here is sung
62|the hymn which those people then sang,
63|nor did I endure the melody to its end.
64|If I could portray how fell asleep
65|the pitiless eyes hearing of Syrinx,
66|the eyes to which keeping watch cost so dear;
67|like a painter who paints from a model,
68|I would draw how I fell asleep;
69|but let him be whoever will who can well depict falling asleep.
70|Therefore I pass on to when I awoke,
71|and I say that a splendor tore for me the veil
72|of sleep, and a calling: "Rise: what are you doing?".
73|As, to see some of the blossoms of the apple tree
74|which makes the angels greedy of its fruit
75|and makes perpetual wedding feast in heaven,
76|Peter and John and James were led
77|and overcome, came back at the word
78|by which greater sleeps were broken,
79|and saw their company diminished
80|both of Moses and of Elijah,
81|and on their master his robe changed;
82|so I came back, and saw that pious one
83|standing over me who had been the guide
84|of my steps along the river before.
85|And all in doubt I said: "Where is Beatrice?".
86|Whereupon she: "See her under the new
87|foliage sitting upon its root.
88|See the company which surrounds her:
89|the others after the griffin go up
90|with a sweeter song and more profound".
91|And if her speech was more prolonged,
92|I do not know, because already in my eyes was
93|she who to any other attention had closed me.
94|Alone she sat upon the bare ground,
95|like a guard left there of the wagon
96|which I saw the biform beast bind.
97|In a circle made of themselves a cloister for her
98|the seven nymphs, with those lights in hand
99|which are safe from Aquilo and from Auster.
100|"Here you will be a short time a forest-dweller;
101|and you will be with me without end a citizen
102|of that Rome whereof Christ is a Roman.
103|Therefore, for the good of the world which lives ill,
104|on the chariot keep now your eyes, and what you see,
105|having returned over there, see that you write".
106|Thus Beatrice; and I, who wholly at the feet
107|of her commandments was devoted,
108|my mind and my eyes where she willed I gave.
109|Never descended with so swift a motion
110|fire from a dense cloud, when it rains
111|from that region which is most remote,
112|as I saw swoop the bird of Jove
113|down through the tree, breaking of the bark,
114|to say nothing of the flowers and the new leaves;
115|and it struck the chariot with all its force;
116|whereupon it reeled like a ship in a storm,
117|overcome by the waves, now to starboard, now to larboard.
118|Then I saw leap into the cradle
119|of the triumphal vehicle a fox
120|which of every good food seemed fasting;
121|but, reproving it for foul sins,
122|my lady turned it to such flight
123|as the bones without flesh allowed.
124|Then from there whence it had first come,
125|I saw the eagle descend down into the body
126|of the chariot and leave it feathered with itself;
127|and such as comes from a heart which laments,
128|such a voice came from heaven and thus it said:
129|"O my little ship, how ill you are laden!".
130|Then it seemed to me that the earth opened
131|between both the wheels, and I saw come out of it a dragon
132|which up through the chariot fixed its tail;
133|and like a wasp which draws back its sting,
134|drawing to itself its malignant tail,
135|it drew out part of the floor, and went away wandering.
136|What remained, as with couch grass
137|lively soil, with the plumage, offered
138|perhaps with intention sound and kind,
139|covered itself again, and with it were covered
140|both the one and the other wheel and the pole, in so short a time
141|that a sigh keeps the mouth open longer.
142|Transformed thus the holy structure
143|put forth heads through its parts,
144|three over the pole and one at each corner.
145|The first were horned like an ox,
146|but the four had a single horn on the forehead:
147|a similar monster was never yet seen.
148|Secure, like a fortress on a high mountain,
149|sitting upon it an ungirt whore
150|appeared to me with her eyes darting around;
151|and as if so that she would not be taken from him,
152|I saw beside her standing a giant;
153|and they kissed each other sometimes.
154|But because her eye lustful and wandering
155|she turned to me, that fierce lover
156|scourged her from her head to her soles;
157|then, full of suspicion and cruel with anger,
158|he loosed the monster, and dragged it through the wood,
159|so far that of that alone he made a shield for me
160|from the whore and from the strange beast.
