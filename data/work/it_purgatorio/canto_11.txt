1|"O our Father, who in the heavens dwell,
2|not circumscribed, but through the greater love
3|which for the first effects up there you have,
4|praised be your name and your power
5|by every creature, as it is fitting
6|to render thanks to your sweet breath.
7|May come to us the peace of your kingdom,
8|for we to it cannot of ourselves,
9|if it does not come, with all our wit.
10|As of their will your angels
11|make sacrifice to you, singing hosanna,
12|so may men do of theirs.
13|Give today to us the daily manna,
14|without which through this harsh desert
15|backward goes he who most strives to go on.
16|And as we the wrong which we have suffered
17|forgive to everyone, you too forgive
18|kindly, and do not look at our merit.
19|Our strength which easily is subdued,
20|do not test with the ancient adversary,
21|but free it from him who so spurs it.
22|This last prayer, dear lord,
23|indeed is not made for us, for there is no need,
24|but for those who behind us remained".
25|Thus for themselves and us good speed
26|those shades praying, went under the weight,
27|like that which sometimes is dreamed,
28|unequally tormented all around
29|and weary along the first cornice,
30|purging away the fog of the world.
31|If there always good for us is said,
32|here what can be said and done for them
33|by those who have for their will a good root?
34|Well must one help them wash away the marks
35|which they carried from here, so that, clean and light,
36|they can go out to the starry wheels.
37|"Ah, so may justice and pity unburden you
38|soon, so that you can move the wing,
39|which according to your desire may lift you,
40|show on which hand toward the stairway
41|one goes shortest; and if there is more than one pass,
42|show us that which less steeply descends;
43|for this one who comes with me, because of the burden
44|of the flesh of Adam with which he is clothed,
45|in climbing up, against his will, is slow".
46|Their words, which they returned to these
47|which he had said whom I was following,
48|were not manifest from whom they came;
49|but it was said: "To the right hand along the bank
50|come with us, and you will find the pass
51|possible for a living person to climb.
52|And if I were not hindered by the stone
53|which my proud neck subdues,
54|whereby I must carry my face low,
55|this one, who still lives and is not named,
56|I would look at, to see if I know him,
57|and to make him pitiful toward this burden.
58|I was Latin and born of a great Tuscan:
59|Guglielmo Aldobrandesco was my father;
60|I do not know if his name was ever with you.
61|The ancient blood and the gallant deeds
62|of my forebears made me so arrogant,
63|that, not thinking of the common mother,
64|every man I held in scorn so far,
65|that I died of it, as the Sienese know,
66|and every child in Campagnatico knows it.
67|I am Omberto; and not only to me harm
68|pride does, for all my kinsmen
69|has it dragged with it into calamity.
70|And here I must this weight carry
71|for it, until God be satisfied,
72|since I did not do it among the living, here among the dead".
73|Listening I bent down my face;
74|and one of them, not this one who was speaking,
75|twisted himself under the weight which encumbers him,
76|and saw me and knew me and was calling,
77|keeping his eyes with effort fixed
78|on me who all bent over with them was going.
79|"Oh!", said I to him, "are you not Oderisi,
80|the honor of Gubbio and the honor of that art
81|which illuminating is called in Paris?".
82|"Brother", said he, "more smile the pages
83|which Franco Bolognese brushes;
84|the honor is all his now, and mine in part.
85|Truly I would not have been so courteous
86|while I lived, because of the great desire
87|for excellence on which my heart was set.
88|For such pride here one pays the fee;
89|and still I would not be here, were it not
90|that, being able to sin, I turned to God.
91|Oh vain glory of human powers!
92|how short a time the green on the top lasts,
93|if it is not overtaken by dull ages!
94|Cimabue believed in painting
95|to hold the field, and now Giotto has the cry,
96|so that the fame of the other is dim.
97|So has taken the one from the other Guido
98|the glory of the language; and perhaps is born
99|he who the one and the other will chase from the nest.
100|Worldly noise is nothing but a breath
101|of wind, which now comes from here and now comes from there,
102|and changes name because it changes side.
103|What fame will you have more, if old you strip
104|from yourself the flesh, than if you had died
105|before you left off the 'pap' and the 'jingle-jingle',
106|before a thousand years pass? which is a shorter
107|span to the eternal, than a movement of the brows
108|to the circle which slowest in heaven is turned.
109|He who of the road takes so little
110|in front of me, all Tuscany resounded with;
111|and now hardly in Siena is he whispered of,
112|where he was lord when was destroyed
113|the Florentine rage, which proud
114|was at that time just as now it is a whore.
115|Your renown is the color of grass,
116|which comes and goes, and he discolors it
117|through whom it comes out of the earth unripe".
118|And I to him: "Your true speech puts in my heart
119|good humility, and a great swelling you flatten in me;
120|but who is he of whom you were speaking now?".
121|"That is", he answered, "Provenzan Salvani;
122|and he is here because he was presumptuous
123|to bring Siena wholly into his hands.
124|He has gone thus and goes, without rest,
125|since he died; such coin pays back
126|in satisfaction he who is over there too bold".
127|And I: "If that spirit who waits,
128|before he repents, for the edge of life,
129|down here dwells and up here does not ascend,
130|unless good prayer helps him,
131|before as much time passes as he lived,
132|how was the coming granted to him?".
133|"When he lived most gloriously", he said,
134|"freely in the Campo of Siena,
135|all shame laid aside, he planted himself;
136|and there, to draw his friend out of pain,
137|which he was suffering in the prison of Charles,
138|he brought himself to tremble in every vein.
139|I will say no more, and I know that I speak darkly;
140|but little time will pass, before your neighbors
141|will act so that you will be able to gloss it.
142|This deed removed for him those confines".
