1|Just as when it darts its first rays
2|there where its maker shed his blood,
3|the Ebro falling under the high Scales,
4|and the waves in the Ganges scorched by noon,
5|so stood the sun; so that the day was departing,
6|when the angel of God, glad, appeared to us.
7|Outside the flame he stood upon the bank,
8|and was singing 'Blessed are the pure in heart!'
9|in a voice far more living than ours.
10|Then "No further does one go, unless first it bites,
11|holy souls, the fire: enter into it,
12|and to the singing beyond do not be deaf",
13|he said to us when we were near him;
14|whereupon I became such, when I heard him,
15|as is he who is put into the pit.
16|Over my clasped hands I stretched forward,
17|looking at the fire and imagining vividly
18|human bodies once seen burning.
19|Turned toward me the good escorts;
20|and Virgil said to me: "My son,
21|here there can be torment, but not death.
22|Remember, remember! And if I
23|upon Geryon guided you safe,
24|what shall I do now nearer to God?
25|Believe for certain that if within the belly
26|of this flame you stayed fully a thousand years,
27|it could not make you bald of one hair.
28|And if you perhaps believe that I deceive you,
29|go toward it, and make trial of it for yourself
30|with your hands on the hem of your garments.
31|Put down now, put down every fear;
32|turn this way and come: enter secure!".
33|And I still fixed and against conscience.
34|When he saw me stand still fixed and hard,
35|troubled a little he said: "Now see, son:
36|between Beatrice and you is this wall".
37|As at the name of Thisbe opened his eyes
38|Pyramus at the point of death, and looked at her,
39|then when the mulberry became vermilion;
40|so, my hardness made soft,
41|I turned to the wise leader, hearing the name
42|which in my mind always springs up.
43|Whereupon he shook his head and said: "How!
44|do we want to stay on this side?"; then he smiled
45|as one does at a child who is won by an apple.
46|Then into the fire in front of me he put himself,
47|asking Statius to come behind,
48|who before for a long way had divided us.
49|As soon as I was inside, into boiling glass
50|I would have thrown myself to cool myself,
51|so beyond measure was the burning there.
52|My sweet father, to comfort me,
53|went on talking only of Beatrice,
54|saying: "Her eyes I seem already to see".
55|There guided us a voice which was singing
56|on the other side; and we, attentive only to it,
57|came out there where the ascent began.
58|'Come, you blessed of my Father',
59|sounded from within a light which was there,
60|such that it overcame me and I could not look at it.
61|"The sun goes away", it added, "and the evening comes;
62|do not stop, but hasten your step,
63|while the west does not grow dark".
64|Straight rose the way through the rock
65|toward such a direction that I cut off the rays
66|in front of me of the sun which was already low.
67|And of few steps had we made trial,
68|when the sun's setting, by the shadow which vanished,
69|we perceived behind us, both I and my sages.
70|And before in all its immense parts
71|the horizon had become of one aspect,
72|and night had all her dispensations,
73|each of us made of a step his bed;
74|for the nature of the mountain broke in us
75|the power to climb more, rather than the delight.
76|As stay ruminating tame
77|the goats, which have been swift and wanton
78|upon the peaks before they have fed,
79|silent in the shade, while the sun burns,
80|watched by the shepherd, who upon his staff
81|has leaned and on it tends them as they rest;
82|and as the herdsman who lodges outdoors,
83|beside his flock quietly spends the night,
84|watching that a wild beast may not scatter it;
85|such were we all three then,
86|I like a goat, and they like shepherds,
87|hemmed in on this side and that by the high rock.
88|Little could appear there of the outside;
89|but, through that little, I saw the stars
90|than their wont both brighter and larger.
91|Thus ruminating and thus gazing at them,
92|sleep seized me; the sleep which often,
93|before the fact happens, knows the news of it.
94|At the hour, I believe, when from the east
95|first shone on the mountain Cytherea,
96|who with fire of love seems always burning,
97|young and beautiful in a dream I seemed
98|to see a lady going through a meadow
99|gathering flowers; and singing she said:
100|"Let whoever asks my name know
101|that I am Leah, and I go moving around
102|my fair hands to make myself a garland.
103|To please myself at the mirror, here I adorn myself;
104|but my sister Rachel never departs
105|from her mirror, and sits all day.
106|She is to see her fair eyes eager
107|as I am to adorn myself with my hands;
108|her seeing, and me doing satisfies".
109|And already through the splendors before dawn,
110|which rise so much the more welcome to pilgrims,
111|as, returning, they lodge less far away,
112|the darkness was fleeing on all sides,
113|and my sleep with it; whereupon I rose,
114|seeing the great masters already risen.
115|"That sweet fruit which through so many branches
116|the care of mortals goes seeking,
117|today will put at peace your hungers".
118|Virgil toward me these very
119|words used; and never were there gifts
120|which were in pleasure equal to these.
121|So much will upon will came to me
122|to be above, that at every step thereafter
123|for flight I felt my feathers grow.
124|When the stairway all beneath us
125|had been run and we were upon the highest step,
126|on me Virgil fixed his eyes,
127|and said: "The temporal fire and the eternal
128|you have seen, son; and you have come to a place
129|where I by myself discern no further.
130|I have brought you here with wit and with art;
131|your own pleasure take now as your leader;
132|out you are of the steep ways, out you are of the narrow.
133|See the sun which shines on your forehead;
134|see the little grasses, the flowers and the shrubs
135|which here the earth by itself alone produces.
136|Until come glad the fair eyes
137|which, weeping, made me come to you,
138|you can sit and you can go among them.
139|Do not wait for my word any more nor my sign;
140|free, upright and sound is your will,
141|and it would be a fault not to do as it bids:
142|wherefore I crown and miter you over yourself".
