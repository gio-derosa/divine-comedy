1|It was already the hour which turns back the desire
2|of seafarers and softens the heart
3|the day they have said to sweet friends farewell;
4|and which the new pilgrim with love
5|pierces, if he hears a bell from afar
6|which seems to weep for the day that is dying;
7|when I began to render vain
8|my hearing and to gaze at one of the souls
9|risen, who was asking with its hand to be heard.
10|It joined and raised both its palms,
11|fixing its eyes toward the east,
12|as if it said to God: 'For nothing else I care'.
13|'Thee before the light' so devoutly
14|came out of its mouth and with such sweet notes,
15|that it made me go out of my own mind;
16|and the others then sweetly and devoutly
17|followed it through all the hymn entire,
18|having their eyes on the supernal wheels.
19|Sharpen here, reader, well your eyes to the truth,
20|for the veil is now indeed so thin,
21|surely that passing within is easy.
22|I saw that noble army
23|silent afterward look upward,
24|as if waiting, pale and humble;
25|and I saw come out from on high and descend below
26|two angels with two flaming swords,
27|broken off and deprived of their points.
28|Green as little leaves just now born
29|were they in garments, which by green feathers
30|struck they drew behind and fanned.
31|One came to stand a little above us,
32|and the other descended on the opposite bank,
33|so that the people in the middle were contained.
34|Well I discerned in them the blond head;
35|but in the face the eye was lost,
36|like a faculty which by excess is confounded.
37|"Both come from the bosom of Mary",
38|said Sordello, "to guard the valley,
39|because of the serpent which will come right away".
40|Whereupon I, who did not know by which path,
41|turned around, and closely drew near,
42|all frozen, to the trusted shoulders.
43|And Sordello again: "Now let us go down now
44|among the great shades, and we will speak to them;
45|gracious will it be to them to see you much".
46|Only three steps I think that I descended,
47|and I was below, and saw one who was gazing
48|only at me, as if he wished to know me.
49|It was already the time when the air was darkening,
50|but not so that between his eyes and mine
51|it did not make clear what before it locked.
52|Toward me he came, and I toward him came:
53|noble judge Nino, how much it pleased me
54|when I saw you not to be among the guilty!
55|No fair greeting between us was left unsaid;
56|then he asked: "How long is it since you came
57|to the foot of the mountain over the distant waters?".
58|"Oh!", said I to him, "through the sad places
59|I came this morning, and I am in the first life,
60|although the other, thus going, I may gain".
61|And when my answer was heard,
62|Sordello and he drew themselves back
63|like people suddenly bewildered.
64|The one to Virgil and the other to one turned
65|who was sitting there, crying: "Up, Currado!
66|come to see what God by grace has willed".
67|Then, turned to me: "By that singular gratitude
68|which you owe to him who so hides
69|his first why, that there is no ford to it,
70|when you will be beyond the wide waves,
71|tell my Giovanna that for me she call
72|there where to the innocent answer is given.
73|I do not believe that her mother loves me any more,
74|since she changed the white wimples,
75|which it must be that, wretched one!, she will yet long for.
76|Through her very easily is understood
77|how long in woman fire of love lasts,
78|if the eye or the touch often does not kindle it.
79|Will not make for her so fair a burial
80|the viper which the Milanese encamps,
81|as would have made the cock of Gallura".
82|Thus he spoke, marked with the stamp,
83|in his aspect, of that righteous zeal
84|which in measure in the heart blazes.
85|My greedy eyes were going only to the sky,
86|just there where the stars are slowest,
87|as a wheel nearest to the axle.
88|And my leader: "Son, what do you gaze at up there?".
89|And I to him: "At those three torches
90|with which the pole here is all ablaze".
91|Whereupon he to me: "The four bright stars
92|which you saw this morning, are over there low,
93|and these have risen where those were".
94|As he was speaking, lo Sordello drew him to himself
95|saying: "See there our adversary";
96|and he pointed his finger so that he would look there.
97|On that side where there is no barrier
98|to the little valley, was a snake,
99|perhaps such as gave to Eve the bitter food.
100|Through the grass and the flowers came the evil streak,
101|turning from time to time its head, and its back
102|licking like a beast that sleeks itself.
103|I did not see, and therefore cannot say,
104|how the celestial hawks moved;
105|but I saw well both the one and the other moving.
106|Hearing the air cleft by the green wings,
107|the serpent fled, and the angels turned back,
108|up to their posts flying back alike.
109|The shade which had drawn close to the judge
110|when he called, through all that assault
111|was not for a moment released from gazing at me.
112|"So may the lamp which leads you on high
113|find in your free will as much wax
114|as is needful as far as the highest enamel",
115|it began, "if true news
116|of Val di Magra or of a neighboring part
117|you know, tell it to me, for once I was great there.
118|I was called Currado Malaspina;
119|I am not the elder, but from him I descended;
120|to my own I bore the love which here is refined".
121|"Oh!", said I to him, "through your lands
122|never have I been; but where do people dwell
123|in all Europe that they are not known?
124|The fame which honors your house,
125|proclaims the lords and proclaims the region,
126|so that he knows of it who has not yet been there;
127|and I swear to you, so may I go above,
128|that your honored people does not strip itself
129|of the glory of the purse and of the sword.
130|Custom and nature so privilege it,
131|that, although the guilty head twists the world,
132|alone it goes straight and scorns the evil way".
133|And he: "Now go; for the sun will not lie again
134|seven times in the bed which the Ram
135|with all four feet covers and straddles,
136|before this courteous opinion
137|will be nailed into the middle of your head
138|with greater nails than others' speech,
139|if the course of judgment does not stop".
