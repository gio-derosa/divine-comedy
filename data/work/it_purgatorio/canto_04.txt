1|When through delights or else through pains,
2|which some faculty of ours takes in,
3|the soul wholly gathers itself to it,
4|it seems that to no other power it attends;
5|and this is against that error which believes
6|that one soul above another in us is kindled.
7|And therefore, when a thing is heard or seen
8|which holds the soul strongly turned to it,
9|the time goes away and the man does not notice it;
10|for one power is that which listens,
11|and another is that which holds the soul entire:
12|this one is as if bound and that one is loose.
13|Of this I had true experience,
14|hearing that spirit and marveling;
15|for fully fifty degrees had climbed
16|the sun, and I had not noticed, when
17|we came where those souls with one voice
18|cried to us: "Here is what you asked".
19|A larger opening many times hedges with thorns
20|with a small forkful of his thorns
21|the man of the farm when the grape darkens,
22|than was the gap through which climbed
23|my leader, and I after, alone,
24|when from us the troop had parted.
25|One goes to Sanleo and descends to Noli,
26|one mounts up Bismantova and Cacume
27|with just the feet; but here one must fly;
28|I mean with the swift wings and with the plumes
29|of great desire, behind that guide
30|who gave me hope and made light for me.
31|We were climbing within the broken rock,
32|and on each side the edge pressed us,
33|and feet and hands the ground below required.
34|After we were upon the uppermost rim
35|of the high bank, on the open slope,
36|"My master", said I, "what way shall we take?".
37|And he to me: "Let no step of yours fall back;
38|only up the mountain behind me gain ground,
39|until some wise escort appear to us".
40|The summit was so high it vanquished the sight,
41|and the slope steeper by far
42|than from mid-quadrant to center a line.
43|I was weary, when I began:
44|"O sweet father, turn yourself, and look
45|how I remain alone, if you do not stop".
46|"My son", he said, "as far as there drag yourself",
47|pointing out to me a ledge a little above
48|which on that side circles all the hill.
49|So spurred me on his words,
50|that I forced myself crawling after him,
51|until the ledge was beneath my feet.
52|To sit we set ourselves there both
53|turned to the east whence we had climbed,
54|for to look back is wont to help one.
55|My eyes first I directed to the low shores;
56|then I raised them to the sun, and I marveled
57|that from the left we were struck by it.
58|Well the poet perceived that I was standing
59|all astonished at the chariot of the light,
60|where between us and Aquilo it was entering.
61|Whereupon he to me: "If Castor and Pollux
62|were in company with that mirror
63|which up and down leads with its light,
64|you would see the ruddy Zodiac
65|revolve still closer to the Bears,
66|unless it went out of its old path.
67|How this may be, if you wish to be able to think it,
68|collected within, imagine Zion
69|with this mountain to stand on the earth
70|so, that both have one sole horizon
71|and different hemispheres; whence the road
72|which to his harm Phaethon did not know how to drive,
73|you will see how to this one it must go
74|on one, when to that one on the other side,
75|if your intellect attends very clearly".
76|"Certainly, my master", said I, "never
77|did I see clear as I discern
78|there where my wit seemed lacking,
79|that the middle circle of the supernal motion,
80|which is called the Equator in some art,
81|and which always remains between the sun and the winter,
82|for the reason which you tell, from here departs
83|toward the north, as far as the Hebrews
84|saw it toward the hot part.
85|But if it pleases you, gladly would I know
86|how far we have to go; for the hill rises
87|more than my eyes can rise".
88|And he to me: "This mountain is such,
89|that always at the beginning below it is hard;
90|and the more one goes up, the less it hurts.
91|Therefore, when it will seem to you gentle
92|so much, that going up will be for you as light
93|as going downstream by ship,
94|then you will be at the end of this path;
95|there to rest your weariness expect.
96|No more I answer, and this I know for true".
97|And when he had his word spoken,
98|a voice close by sounded: "Perhaps
99|of sitting before then you will have need!".
100|At the sound of it each of us turned,
101|and we saw on the left a great boulder,
102|of which neither I nor he before had noticed.
103|There we drew ourselves; and there were persons
104|who were staying in the shade behind the rock
105|as a man through laziness sets himself to stay.
106|And one of them, who seemed to me weary,
107|was sitting and embracing his knees,
108|holding his face down between them low.
109|"O my sweet lord", said I, "eye
110|that one who shows himself more negligent
111|than if laziness were his sister".
112|Then he turned to us and paid heed,
113|moving his face only up along his thigh,
114|and said: "Now you go up, you who are valiant!".
115|I recognized then who he was, and that anguish
116|which still quickened a little my breath,
117|did not hinder my going to him; and after
118|I had reached him, he raised his head barely,
119|saying: "Have you well seen how the sun
120|on your left shoulder drives its chariot?".
121|His lazy acts and his short words
122|moved my lips a little to a smile;
123|then I began: "Belacqua, I do not grieve
124|for you now; but tell me: why seated
125|right here are you? do you await an escort,
126|or has only your usual manner taken you again?".
127|And he: "O brother, going up, what does it matter?
128|for he would not let me go to the torments,
129|the angel of God who sits upon the gate.
130|First it is needful that the sky circle around me
131|outside of it, as long as it did in life,
132|because I delayed to the end the good sighs,
133|unless prayer first help me
134|which rises up from a heart that lives in grace;
135|the other what avails it, which in heaven is not heard?".
136|And already the poet was climbing ahead of me,
137|and was saying: "Come now; see that touched is
138|the meridian by the sun, and on the shore
139|night covers already with its foot Morocco".
