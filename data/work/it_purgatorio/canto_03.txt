1|Although the sudden flight
2|scattered those over the plain,
3|turned to the mountain where reason searches us,
4|I drew close to my faithful companion:
5|and how would I have run without him?
6|who would have drawn me up the mountain?
7|He seemed to me by himself remorseful:
8|O dignified conscience and clean,
9|how to you is a small fault a bitter bite!
10|When his feet left off the haste,
11|which takes from every act its dignity,
12|my mind, which before was restricted,
13|widened its attention, as if eager,
14|and I turned my face toward the hill
15|which toward heaven rises highest from the sea.
16|The sun, which behind was flaming red,
17|was broken before me in the figure,
18|for it had in me the obstacle to its rays.
19|I turned to the side with fear
20|of being abandoned, when I saw
21|only before me the ground darkened;
22|and my comfort: "Why still do you distrust?",
23|began to say to me, wholly turned around;
24|"do you not believe me with you and that I guide you?
25|It is already evening there where is buried
26|the body within which I made a shadow;
27|Naples has it, and from Brindisi it was taken.
28|Now, if before me nothing is shadowed,
29|do not marvel more than at the heavens
30|of which one does not block the ray to the other.
31|To suffer torments, heats and frosts
32|similar bodies the Power disposes
33|which, how it does it, wills not that to us be revealed.
34|Mad is he who hopes that our reason
35|can traverse the infinite way
36|which holds one substance in three persons.
37|Be content, human race, with the 'that';
38|for, if you had been able to see everything,
39|there was no need for Mary to give birth;
40|and you have seen desire without fruit
41|such ones that their desire would have been quieted,
42|which eternally is given them for grief:
43|I speak of Aristotle and of Plato
44|and of many others"; and here he bent his brow,
45|and said no more, and remained troubled.
46|We came meanwhile to the foot of the mountain;
47|there we found the rock so steep,
48|that in vain would be there legs ready.
49|Between Lerici and Turbia the most deserted,
50|the most broken landslide is a stairway,
51|compared to that, easy and open.
52|"Now who knows on which hand the slope descends",
53|said my master halting his step,
54|"so that he can climb who goes without wing?".
55|And while he, keeping his face low,
56|was examining in his mind the road,
57|and I was gazing up around the rock,
58|on the left hand appeared to me a crowd
59|of souls, who were moving their feet toward us,
60|and it did not seem so, so slowly were they coming.
61|"Raise", said I, "master, your eyes:
62|behold on this side those who will give us counsel,
63|if you from yourself cannot have it".
64|He looked then, and with a frank expression
65|answered: "Let us go there, for they come slowly;
66|and you make firm your hope, sweet son".
67|Still was that people far away,
68|I mean after our thousand steps,
69|as far as a good thrower would cast with his hand,
70|when they all pressed close to the hard rocks
71|of the high bank, and stood still and close
72|as, to look, one who goes doubting stops.
73|"O you well ended, O spirits already elect",
74|Virgil began, "by that peace
75|which I believe by you all is awaited,
76|tell us where the mountain slopes,
77|so that possible be the going upward;
78|for to lose time to him who knows most most displeases".
79|As the little sheep come out of the fold
80|by one, by two, by three, and the others stand
81|timid, lowering eye and muzzle to the ground;
82|and what the first does, the others do too,
83|huddling against her, if she stops,
84|simple and quiet, and the why they do not know;
85|so I saw move to come the head
86|of that fortunate flock then,
87|modest in face and in walking dignified.
88|When those in front saw broken
89|the light on the ground at my right side,
90|so that the shadow was from me to the rock,
91|they stopped, and drew themselves back somewhat,
92|and all the others who were coming after,
93|not knowing the why, did as much.
94|"Without your asking I confess to you
95|that this is a human body which you see;
96|whereby the light of the sun on the ground is cleft.
97|Do not marvel, but believe
98|that not without power which from heaven comes
99|does he seek to surmount this wall".
100|Thus the master; and that worthy people
101|"Turn back", said, "go ahead before us then",
102|with the backs of the hands making a sign.
103|And one of them began: "Whoever
104|you are, thus going, turn your face:
105|consider if over there you ever saw me".
106|I turned toward him and looked at him fixedly:
107|blond he was and handsome and of noble aspect,
108|but one of his brows a blow had split.
109|When I had humbly disclaimed
110|ever having seen him, he said: "Now see";
111|and he showed me a wound at the top of his breast.
112|Then smiling he said: "I am Manfred,
113|grandson of Constance the empress;
114|wherefore I pray you that, when you return,
115|you go to my fair daughter, mother
116|of the honor of Sicily and of Aragon,
117|and tell the truth to her, if other is said.
118|After I had my body broken
119|by two mortal stabs, I gave myself up,
120|weeping, to him who willingly pardons.
121|Horrible were my sins;
122|but the infinite goodness has such great arms,
123|that it takes whatever turns itself to it.
124|If the pastor of Cosenza, who to the hunt
125|of me was set by Clement then,
126|had in God well read this page,
127|the bones of my body would be still
128|at the head of the bridge near Benevento,
129|under the guard of the heavy cairn.
130|Now the rain bathes them and the wind moves them
131|outside the kingdom, almost along the Verde,
132|where he transferred them with candles extinguished.
133|By their curse is not so lost,
134|that it cannot return, the eternal love,
135|as long as hope has a flower of green.
136|True it is that whoever in contumacy dies
137|of Holy Church, even if at the end he repents,
138|must stay outside this bank,
139|for every period that he has been, thirty,
140|in his presumption, if such decree
141|shorter through good prayers does not become.
142|See now if you can make me glad,
143|revealing to my good Constance
144|how you have seen me, and also this ban;
145|for here through those over there much is advanced".
