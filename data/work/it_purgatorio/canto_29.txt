1|Singing like a lady in love,
2|she continued at the end of her words:
3|'Blessed are they whose sins are covered!'.
4|And like nymphs who went alone
5|through the woodland shades, desiring
6|one to see, one to flee the sun,
7|then she moved against the stream, walking
8|up along the bank; and I abreast of her,
9|small step with small step following.
10|There were not a hundred between her steps and mine,
11|when the banks equally made a turn,
12|in such a way that to the east I faced again.
13|Nor yet was our way thus long,
14|when the lady wholly turned to me,
15|saying: "My brother, look and listen".
16|And behold a sudden brightness ran
17|on all sides through the great forest,
18|such that it put me in doubt whether it was lightning.
19|But since lightning, as it comes, stops,
20|and that, lasting, shone more and more,
21|in my thought I said: 'What thing is this?'.
22|And a sweet melody ran
23|through the luminous air; whereupon good zeal
24|made me reprove the boldness of Eve,
25|who there where earth and heaven obeyed,
26|a woman, alone and only just formed,
27|did not endure to stay under any veil;
28|under which if she had been devout,
29|I would have those ineffable delights
30|felt sooner and for a longer time.
31|While I went among so many first fruits
32|of the eternal pleasure all in suspense,
33|and desirous still of more joys,
34|before us, just like a kindled fire,
35|the air became for us under the green branches;
36|and the sweet sound was now heard as singing.
37|O most holy Virgins, if hungers,
38|colds or vigils ever for you I suffered,
39|a cause spurs me that I call on you for reward.
40|Now it is fitting that Helicon pour forth for me,
41|and Urania help me with her choir
42|to put into verse things hard to think.
43|A little further on, seven trees of gold
44|were falsely shown in appearance by the long stretch
45|of the space which was still between us and them;
46|but when I had come so near to them,
47|that the common object, which deceives the sense,
48|did not lose through distance any of its features,
49|the faculty which provides reason with discourse,
50|as they were candlesticks perceived,
51|and in the voices of the singing 'Hosanna'.
52|Above flamed the fair array
53|brighter by far than the moon in the clear sky
54|at midnight in her mid-month.
55|I turned full of wonder
56|to the good Virgil, and he answered me
57|with a look laden with amazement no less.
58|Then I turned back my gaze to the high things
59|which were moving toward us so slowly,
60|that they would have been outdone by new brides.
61|The lady chided me: "Why do you only burn
62|so in the affection for the living lights,
63|and what comes behind them you do not look at?".
64|People I saw then, as if following their leaders,
65|coming after, clothed in white;
66|and such whiteness here never was.
67|The water was gleaming on my left side,
68|and gave back to me my left side,
69|if I looked into it, like a mirror too.
70|When I on my bank had such a position,
71|that only the river kept me distant,
72|to see better I gave pause to my steps,
73|and I saw the little flames go before,
74|leaving behind them the air painted,
75|and they had the semblance of drawn brushes;
76|so that there above it remained marked
77|with seven stripes, all in those colors
78|of which the Sun makes his bow and Delia her girdle.
79|These banners to the rear were longer
80|than my sight; and, as far as I could judge,
81|ten paces apart were the outer ones.
82|Under so fair a sky as I describe,
83|twenty-four elders, two by two,
84|came crowned with fleur-de-lis.
85|All were singing: "Blessed are you
86|among the daughters of Adam, and blessed
87|be in eternity your beauties!".
88|After the flowers and the other fresh grasses
89|opposite me on the other bank
90|were free of those chosen people,
91|just as light follows light in the sky,
92|there came after them four animals,
93|crowned each with green leaves.
94|Each one was feathered with six wings;
95|the feathers full of eyes; and the eyes of Argus,
96|if they were living, would be such.
97|To describe their forms I scatter no more
98|rhymes, reader; for other expense constrains me,
99|so much that in this I cannot be lavish;
100|but read Ezekiel, who depicts them
101|as he saw them from the cold region
102|come with wind and with cloud and with fire;
103|and such as you will find them in his pages,
104|such were they here, except that as to the wings
105|John is with me and from him departs.
106|The space within the four of them contained
107|a chariot, upon two wheels, triumphal,
108|which at the neck of a griffin came drawn.
109|It stretched upward the one and the other wing
110|between the middle and the three and three stripes,
111|so that to none, by cleaving, did it harm.
112|So high they rose that they were not seen;
113|his limbs were of gold as far as he was bird,
114|and white the others, with vermilion mixed.
115|Not only did Rome with a chariot so fair
116|never gladden Africanus, or indeed Augustus,
117|but that of the Sun would be poor beside it;
118|that of the Sun which, straying, was burned up
119|at the prayer of the devout Earth,
120|when Jove was mysteriously just.
121|Three ladies in a circle at the right wheel
122|came dancing; the one so red
123|that hardly would she have been noticed within a fire;
124|the other was as if her flesh and bones
125|had been made of emerald;
126|the third seemed snow just fallen;
127|and now they seemed led by the white,
128|now by the red; and from the song of this one
129|the others took their going both slow and swift.
130|On the left four were making festival,
131|clad in purple, following the measure
132|of one of them who had three eyes in her head.
133|After all the group described
134|I saw two old men unlike in dress,
135|but like in bearing both dignified and firm.
136|One showed himself one of the disciples
137|of that highest Hippocrates whom nature
138|made for the creatures which she holds most dear;
139|the other showed the contrary care
140|with a sword shining and sharp,
141|such that on this side of the stream it made me afraid.
142|Then I saw four of humble appearance;
143|and behind all an old man alone
144|coming, sleeping, with keen face.
145|And these seven like the first troop
146|were clothed, but of lilies
147|around their heads they did not make a garland,
148|but rather of roses and of other vermilion flowers;
149|one would have sworn with a view not far off
150|that all were burning above their brows.
151|And when the chariot was opposite me,
152|a thunderclap was heard, and those worthy people
153|seemed to have further going forbidden,
154|stopping there with the first ensigns.
