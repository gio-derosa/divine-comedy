1|Side by side, like oxen which go in a yoke,
2|I went with that burdened soul,
3|as long as the sweet tutor allowed it.
4|But when he said: "Leave him and pass on;
5|for here it is good with sails and with oars,
6|as much as he can, for each to push his boat";
7|upright, as one should walk, I made myself again
8|with my body, although my thoughts
9|remained in me both bowed down and diminished.
10|I had moved, and was following willingly
11|my master's steps, and both of us
12|already were showing how light we were;
13|and he said to me: "Turn your eyes downward:
14|good it will be for you, to ease the way,
15|to see the bed of your soles".
16|As, so that of them there may be memory,
17|over the buried the tombs in the ground
18|bear engraved what they were before,
19|whereby there many times one weeps again
20|at the prick of remembrance,
21|which only to the pious gives the spur;
22|so saw I there, but of better semblance
23|according to the craftsmanship, figured
24|all that as a road juts out from the mountain.
25|I saw him who was created noble
26|more than any other creature, down from heaven
27|like lightning descending, on one side.
28|I saw Briareus pierced by the bolt
29|celestial lying, on the other part,
30|heavy on the earth with the mortal chill.
31|I saw Thymbraeus, I saw Pallas and Mars,
32|armed still, around their father,
33|gazing at the limbs of the Giants scattered.
34|I saw Nimrod at the foot of the great work
35|as if bewildered, and looking at the people
36|who in Shinar with him were proud.
37|O Niobe, with what sorrowing eyes
38|did I see you portrayed upon the road,
39|between seven and seven of your children slain!
40|O Saul, how upon your own sword
41|you appeared there dead in Gilboa,
42|which afterward felt neither rain nor dew!
43|O mad Arachne, so did I see you
44|already half spider, sad upon the shreds
45|of the work which to your harm was made.
46|O Rehoboam, indeed it does not seem to threaten
47|there, your image; but full of terror
48|a chariot carries it away, without anyone pursuing it.
49|It showed also, the hard pavement,
50|how Alcmaeon made to his mother seem
51|costly the ill-fated ornament.
52|It showed how the sons threw themselves
53|upon Sennacherib within the temple,
54|and how, he dead, there they left him.
55|It showed the ruin and the cruel slaughter
56|which Tomyris made, when she said to Cyrus:
57|"Blood you thirsted for, and I with blood fill you".
58|It showed how in rout fled
59|the Assyrians, after Holofernes was killed,
60|and also the remains of the slaughter.
61|I saw Troy in ashes and in ruins;
62|O Ilion, how low and abject
63|showed you the image which there is discerned!
64|What master was there of brush or of stylus
65|who could portray the shadings and the lines which there
66|would make a subtle genius marvel?
67|Dead the dead and the living seemed living:
68|he did not see better than I who saw the reality,
69|all that I trod upon, while bent over I went.
70|Now be proud, and on with haughty face,
71|children of Eve, and do not bend your face
72|so that you may see your evil path!
73|More of the mountain had already been circled by us
74|and of the course of the sun much more spent
75|than the mind, not free, was reckoning,
76|when he who always intent ahead
77|was going, began: "Lift up your head;
78|it is no longer time to go so absorbed.
79|See there an angel who is getting ready
80|to come toward us; see that returns
81|from the service of the day the sixth handmaid.
82|With reverence adorn your face and your bearing,
83|so that it may please him to send us upward;
84|think that this day never dawns again!".
85|I was well used to his admonition
86|never to lose time, so that on that
87|matter he could not speak to me obscurely.
88|To us came the beautiful creature,
89|white-robed and in his face such
90|as seems, trembling, the morning star.
91|His arms he opened, and then he opened his wings;
92|he said: "Come: here near are the steps,
93|and easily now one climbs.
94|To this invitation come very few:
95|O human race, born to fly upward,
96|why at so little wind do you thus fall?".
97|He led us where the rock was cut;
98|there he struck me with his wings on the forehead;
99|then he promised me a safe journey.
100|As on the right hand, to climb the hill
101|where sits the church which dominates
102|the well-guided city above Rubaconte,
103|is broken the bold steepness of the ascent
104|by the stairs which were made in an age
105|when the ledger and the stave were safe;
106|so is eased the bank which falls
107|there very steeply from the next circle;
108|but on this side and that the high rock grazes.
109|As we were turning there our persons,
110|'Blessed are the poor in spirit!' voices
111|sang in such a way, that speech could not tell it.
112|Ah how different are those passages
113|from the infernal ones! for here with songs
114|one enters, and down there with fierce laments.
115|Already we were mounting up the holy stairs,
116|and I seemed to be far lighter
117|than on the level I had seemed before.
118|Whereupon I: "Master, say, what heavy thing
119|has been lifted from me, that almost no
120|fatigue, while walking, is felt by me?".
121|He answered: "When the P's which have remained
122|still on your face almost effaced,
123|will be, as is the one, wholly erased,
124|your feet will be by good will so overcome,
125|that not only will they not feel fatigue,
126|but it will be a delight to them to be pushed up".
127|Then I did like those who go
128|with something on their head unknown to them,
129|except that others' signs make them suspect;
130|whereupon the hand helps itself to make certain,
131|and seeks and finds and fulfills that office
132|which cannot be accomplished by the sight;
133|and with the fingers of my right hand spread
134|I found only six the letters which had carved
135|he of the keys on me above my temples:
136|at which, watching, my leader smiled.
