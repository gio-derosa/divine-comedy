1|Neither the speaking the going, nor the going it more slow
2|made, but talking we went swiftly,
3|just like a ship driven by a good wind;
4|and the shades, which seemed things twice dead,
5|through the pits of their eyes wonder
6|drew at me, aware of my living.
7|And I, continuing my discourse,
8|said: "He goes up perhaps more slowly
9|than he would do, for another's sake.
10|But tell me, if you know, where is Piccarda;
11|tell me if I see any person worthy of note
12|among these people who so gaze at me".
13|"My sister, who between beautiful and good
14|I do not know which she was more, triumphs glad
15|in high Olympus already in her crown".
16|So he said first; and then: "Here it is not forbidden
17|to name each one, since so milked
18|is our semblance away by the diet.
19|This one", and he showed with his finger, "is Bonagiunta,
20|Bonagiunta of Lucca; and that face
21|beyond him more than the others pierced
22|had the Holy Church in his arms:
23|from Tours he was, and purges by fasting
24|the eels of Bolsena and the vernaccia wine".
25|Many others he named to me one by one;
26|and at the naming they all seemed content,
27|so that I did not see for this one dark look.
28|I saw through hunger using their teeth on emptiness
29|Ubaldin da la Pila and Boniface
30|who shepherded with his crozier many people.
31|I saw messer Marchese, who had leisure
32|once to drink at Forlì with less dryness,
33|and yet was such, that he never felt sated.
34|But as one does who looks and then values
35|more one than another, I did with him of Lucca,
36|who seemed most to have knowledge of me.
37|He was murmuring; and something like "Gentucca"
38|I heard there, where he felt the wound
39|of the justice which so plucks them.
40|"O soul", said I, "who seems so eager
41|to speak with me, act so that I may understand you,
42|and both you and me with your speech satisfy".
43|"A woman is born, and does not yet wear the wimple",
44|he began, "who will make pleasing to you
45|my city, however men may reproach it.
46|You will go away with this foreknowledge:
47|if from my murmuring you took an error,
48|the real events will yet make it clear to you.
49|But tell me if I see here him who brought
50|forth the new rhymes, beginning
51|'Ladies who have understanding of love'".
52|And I to him: "I am one who, when
53|Love inspires me, take note, and in that manner
54|which he dictates within I go expressing".
55|"O brother, now I see", said he, "the knot
56|which the Notary and Guittone and me held back
57|on this side of the sweet new style which I hear!
58|I see well how your pens
59|behind the dictator go close,
60|which with ours certainly did not happen;
61|and whoever sets himself to look further,
62|sees nothing more between the one and the other style";
63|and, as if satisfied, he fell silent.
64|As the birds which winter along the Nile,
65|sometimes in the air form a flock,
66|then fly with more haste and go in a line,
67|so all the people who were there,
68|turning their face, quickened again their pace,
69|light both through leanness and through will.
70|And as the man who is weary of trotting,
71|lets his companions go, and walks
72|until he eases the heaving of his chest,
73|so let the holy flock pass by
74|Forese, and behind with me he came,
75|saying: "When will it be that I see you again?".
76|"I do not know", I answered him, "how long I shall live;
77|but indeed my return will not be so soon,
78|that I will not be with my will sooner at the shore;
79|because the place where I was set to live,
80|from day to day is more stripped of good,
81|and seems disposed to sad ruin".
82|"Now go", said he; "for him who has most guilt for it,
83|I see at the tail of a beast dragged
84|toward the valley where never is sin purged.
85|The beast at every step goes faster,
86|increasing always, until it strikes him,
87|and leaves the body vilely undone.
88|Those wheels have not long to turn",
89|and he directed his eyes to heaven, "before it will be clear to you
90|that which my speech can declare no further.
91|You stay behind now; for time is precious
92|in this realm, so that I lose too much
93|coming with you thus side by side".
94|As comes out sometimes at a gallop
95|a horseman from a troop which is riding,
96|and goes to win himself the honor of the first clash,
97|so he departed from us with greater strides;
98|and I remained on the way with the two
99|who were of the world such great marshals.
100|And when he had gone so far in front of us,
101|that my eyes became followers of him,
102|as my mind was of his words,
103|there appeared to me the branches laden and lively
104|of another fruit tree, and not very far
105|because I had only then turned there.
106|I saw people under it raise their hands
107|and cry I know not what toward the leaves,
108|like greedy little children and vain
109|who beg, and he who is begged does not answer,
110|but, to make their longing well keen,
111|holds high what they desire and does not hide it.
112|Then they departed as if undeceived;
113|and we came to the great tree at once,
114|which so many prayers and tears refuses.
115|"Pass on beyond without drawing near:
116|there is a tree higher up which was bitten by Eve,
117|and this plant was raised from it".
118|So among the branches I know not who was saying;
119|wherefore Virgil and Statius and I, drawn close together,
120|went onward on the side which rises.
121|"Remember", it said, "the cursed ones
122|formed in the clouds, who, sated,
123|fought Theseus with their double breasts;
124|and the Hebrews who at drinking showed themselves soft,
125|for which Gideon would not have them as companions,
126|when toward Midian he descended the hills".
127|So keeping close to one of the two edges
128|we passed, hearing sins of gluttony
129|followed once by wretched gains.
130|Then, spread out again along the empty road,
131|fully a thousand steps and more carried us on,
132|each contemplating without a word.
133|"What are you going thinking so, you three alone?",
134|a sudden voice said; whereupon I started
135|as do beasts frightened and lazy.
136|I raised my head to see who it was;
137|and never were seen in a furnace
138|glass or metals so shining and red,
139|as I saw one who said: "If it pleases you
140|to mount up, here one must turn;
141|this way goes he who wishes to go for peace".
142|His aspect had taken away my sight;
143|whereupon I turned behind my teachers,
144|like a man who goes according as he hears.
145|And as, herald of the dawns,
146|the breeze of May moves and is fragrant,
147|all impregnated by the grass and by the flowers;
148|such I felt a wind strike through the middle
149|of my forehead, and well I felt the feather move,
150|which made me feel the breath of ambrosia.
151|And I heard say: "Blessed are those whom illumines
152|so much grace, that the love of taste
153|in their breast does not fume with too much desire,
154|hungering always as much as is just!".
