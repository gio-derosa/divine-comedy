1|Against a better will a will fights poorly;
2|whence against my pleasure, to please him,
3|I drew from the water the sponge not sated.
4|I moved; and my leader moved through the
5|free places only along the rock,
6|as one goes on a wall close to the battlements;
7|for the people who melt drop by drop
8|through their eyes the evil which occupies all the world,
9|on the other side come too near the edge.
10|Cursed be you, ancient she-wolf,
11|who more than all the other beasts have prey
12|because of your hunger endlessly deep!
13|O heaven, in whose revolving it seems to be believed
14|that conditions down here are transformed,
15|when will he come through whom she will depart?
16|We were going with steps slow and scant,
17|and I attentive to the shades, whom I heard
18|piteously weeping and lamenting;
19|and by chance I heard "Sweet Mary!"
20|before us called out thus in the weeping
21|as does a woman who is in labor;
22|and continue: "Poor you were so much,
23|as can be seen by that inn
24|where you laid down your holy burden".
25|Next I heard: "O good Fabricius,
26|with poverty you wished rather virtue
27|than great riches to possess with vice".
28|These words had so pleased me,
29|that I drew myself forward to have knowledge
30|of that spirit from whom they seemed to have come.
31|It was speaking still of the largess
32|which Nicholas made to the maidens,
33|to lead to honor their youth.
34|"O soul who speaks so much good,
35|tell me who you were", I said, "and why alone
36|you renew these worthy praises.
37|Your word will not be without reward,
38|if I return to complete the short road
39|of that life which flies to its end".
40|And he: "I will tell you, not for comfort
41|which I expect from over there, but because so much
42|grace shines in you before you are dead.
43|I was the root of the evil plant
44|which overshadows all the Christian land,
45|so that good fruit is rarely plucked from it.
46|But if Douai, Lille, Ghent and Bruges
47|could, soon there would be vengeance for it;
48|and I beg it of him who judges all.
49|I was called over there Hugh Capet;
50|from me were born the Philips and the Louis
51|by whom of late France is ruled.
52|I was the son of a butcher of Paris:
53|when the ancient kings had died out
54|all, except one who had turned to gray garments,
55|I found tight in my hands the rein
56|of the government of the kingdom, and so much power
57|of new acquisition, and so full of friends,
58|that to the widowed crown promoted
59|was the head of my son, from whom
60|began the consecrated bones of these.
61|As long as the great Provençal dowry
62|had not taken from my blood its shame,
63|it was worth little, but still it did no harm.
64|There began with force and with lies
65|its rapine; and afterward, for amends,
66|Ponthieu and Normandy it took and Gascony.
67|Charles came into Italy and, for amends,
68|made a victim of Conradin; and then
69|thrust back to heaven Thomas, for amends.
70|A time I see, not long after today,
71|which draws another Charles out of France,
72|to make better known both himself and his own.
73|Without arms he goes forth and only with the lance
74|with which Judas jousted, and that he thrusts
75|so, that he makes the belly of Florence burst.
76|From it not land, but sin and shame
77|he will gain, for himself so much the heavier,
78|as the lighter he counts such harm.
79|The other, who once came out captured from a ship,
80|I see sell his daughter and bargain over her
81|as corsairs do with other slave women.
82|O avarice, what more can you do to us,
83|since you have my blood so drawn to you,
84|that it does not care for its own flesh?
85|So that the evil to come and the done may seem less,
86|I see into Anagni enter the fleur-de-lis,
87|and in his vicar Christ be made captive.
88|I see him another time be mocked;
89|I see renewed the vinegar and the gall,
90|and between living thieves be killed.
91|I see the new Pilate so cruel,
92|that this does not sate him, but without decree
93|he carries into the Temple his greedy sails.
94|O my Lord, when shall I be glad
95|to see the vengeance which, hidden,
96|makes sweet your wrath in your secret?
97|What I was saying of that only bride
98|of the Holy Spirit and which made you
99|turn toward me for some gloss,
100|so long is it the response to all our prayers
101|as the day lasts; but when it becomes night,
102|a contrary sound we take up in its stead.
103|We repeat Pygmalion then,
104|whom traitor and thief and parricide
105|made his will greedy for gold;
106|and the misery of the avaricious Midas,
107|which followed on his greedy request,
108|at which always one must laugh.
109|Of the foolish Achan each one then remembers,
110|how he stole the spoils, so that the wrath
111|of Joshua here seems still to bite him.
112|Then we accuse with her husband Sapphira;
113|we praise the kicks which Heliodorus had;
114|and in infamy all the mountain circles
115|Polymnestor who killed Polydorus;
116|lastly here is cried: 'Crassus,
117|tell us, for you know it: of what flavor is gold?'.
118|Sometimes one speaks loud and another low,
119|according to the feeling which spurs us to go
120|now at a greater and now at a lesser pace:
121|therefore at the good which by day is spoken of here,
122|just now I was not alone; but here near by
123|no other person was raising his voice".
124|We had already departed from him,
125|and were striving to overcome the road
126|as much as to our power was permitted,
127|when I felt, like a thing which falls,
128|the mountain tremble; whereupon a chill seized me
129|such as is wont to seize him who goes to death.
130|Certainly Delos did not shake so strongly,
131|before Latona made on it her nest
132|to give birth to the two eyes of heaven.
133|Then began on all sides a cry
134|such, that the master turned toward me,
135|saying: "Do not fear, while I guide you".
136|'Glory in the highest' all 'to God'
137|were saying, from what I understood from those near,
138|where the cry could be understood.
139|We stood motionless and in suspense
140|like the shepherds who first heard that song,
141|until the trembling ceased and it was completed.
142|Then we took up again our holy road,
143|looking at the shades which lay on the ground,
144|already returned to their usual weeping.
145|No ignorance ever with so much strife
146|made me desirous of knowing,
147|if my memory in this does not err,
148|as I seemed then, thinking, to have;
149|nor because of the haste was I bold to ask,
150|nor by myself there could I see anything:
151|so I went on timid and pensive.
