1|While thus along the edge, one before the other,
2|we went on, and often the good master
3|said to me: "Take care: let it help that I warn you";
4|the sun was striking me on the right shoulder,
5|which already, raying, all the west
6|was changing from sky-blue into a white aspect;
7|and I was making with my shadow more glowing
8|the flame appear; and just at such a sign
9|I saw many shades, as they went, pay heed.
10|This was the cause which gave a beginning
11|to them to speak of me; and they began
12|to say: "That one does not seem a fictitious body";
13|then toward me, as far as they could come,
14|certain ones came, always with care
15|not to come out where they would not be burned.
16|"O you who go, not for being slower,
17|but perhaps out of reverence, after the others,
18|answer me who in thirst and in fire burn.
19|Nor to me alone is your answer a need;
20|for all these have greater thirst for it
21|than for cold water Indian or Ethiopian.
22|Tell us how it is that you make of yourself a wall
23|to the sun, just as if you had not yet
24|entered within the net of death".
25|So one of them was speaking to me; and I would have
26|already made myself known, if I had not been intent
27|on another new thing which appeared then;
28|for through the middle of the burning road
29|came people with their faces toward these,
30|which made me gaze in suspense.
31|There I see on every side hurrying
32|each shade and kissing one with one
33|without stopping, content with a brief greeting;
34|so within their dark troop
35|one ant nuzzles with another,
36|perhaps to spy out their way and their fortune.
37|As soon as they end the friendly greeting,
38|before the first step there passes on,
39|each one strives to outcry the others:
40|the new people: "Sodom and Gomorrah";
41|and the other: "Into the cow enters Pasiphae,
42|so that the young bull may run to her lust".
43|Then, like cranes which to the Riphaean mountains
44|might fly in part, and in part toward the sands,
45|these shy of the frost, those of the sun,
46|the one people goes away, the other comes on;
47|and they return, weeping, to their first songs
48|and to the cry which most befits them;
49|and drew close to me again, as before,
50|the same ones who had begged me,
51|attentive to listen in their looks.
52|I, who twice had seen their wish,
53|began: "O souls certain
54|of having, whenever it may be, a state of peace,
55|have not remained unripe nor ripe
56|my limbs over there, but are here with me
57|with their blood and with their joints.
58|From here I go up so as to be blind no more;
59|there is a lady above who wins grace for me,
60|whereby my mortal part through your world I bring.
61|But so may your greatest wish sated
62|soon become, so that that heaven may lodge you
63|which is full of love and most amply spreads,
64|tell me, so that I may yet rule pages with it,
65|who you are, and who is that crowd
66|which goes away behind your backs".
67|Not otherwise stupefied is troubled
68|the mountaineer, and gazing becomes mute,
69|when rough and wild he comes to the city,
70|than each shade did in its appearance;
71|but when they were unburdened of amazement,
72|which in lofty hearts is soon quelled,
73|"Blessed are you, who of our borders",
74|began again the one who first questioned me,
75|"to die better, take on board experience!
76|The people who do not come with us, offended
77|in that for which once Caesar, triumphing,
78|heard himself called 'Queen' against him:
79|therefore they go away crying 'Sodom',
80|reproaching themselves as you have heard,
81|and they help the burning by their shame.
82|Our sin was hermaphrodite;
83|but because we did not observe human law,
84|following like beasts our appetite,
85|in opprobrium of ourselves, by us is read,
86|when we part, the name of her
87|who made herself a beast in the beast-shaped planks.
88|Now you know our acts and of what we were guilty:
89|if perhaps by name you wish to know who we are,
90|there is no time to tell, and I would not know how.
91|I will indeed make your wish for me wanting nothing:
92|I am Guido Guinizzelli, and already I purge myself
93|for having well repented before the end".
94|As in the sorrow of Lycurgus
95|became the two sons on seeing again their mother,
96|such did I become, but I do not rise so far,
97|when I hear name himself the father
98|of me and of the others my betters who ever
99|used rhymes of love sweet and graceful;
100|and without hearing and speaking I went pensive
101|a long time gazing at him,
102|nor, because of the fire, did I draw nearer there.
103|When I was fed full with gazing,
104|I wholly offered myself ready for his service
105|with the affirmation which makes others believe.
106|And he to me: "You leave such a trace,
107|from what I hear, in me, and so clear,
108|that Lethe cannot take it away nor make it dim.
109|But if your words just now swore truth,
110|tell me what is the cause for which you show
111|in speech and in look that you hold me dear".
112|And I to him: "Your sweet verses,
113|which, as long as modern usage lasts,
114|will make dear still their inks".
115|"O brother", he said, "this one whom I point out to you
116|with my finger", and he pointed to a spirit ahead,
117|"was a better craftsman of the mother tongue.
118|Verses of love and proses of romances
119|he surpassed all; and let the fools talk
120|who believe that he of Limoges excels.
121|To rumor more than to truth they turn their faces,
122|and thus they fix their opinion
123|before art or reason is listened to by them.
124|So did many of old with Guittone,
125|from cry to cry giving the prize to him alone,
126|until the truth has vanquished him with most persons.
127|Now if you have so ample a privilege,
128|that it is lawful for you to go to the cloister
129|in which Christ is abbot of the college,
130|say to him for me a saying of an Our Father,
131|as much as we need of it in this world,
132|where the power to sin is no longer ours".
133|Then, perhaps to give place to another second
134|who was near him, he disappeared through the fire,
135|as through the water the fish going to the bottom.
136|I moved a little forward to the one pointed out,
137|and said that for his name my desire
138|was preparing a gracious place.
139|He began freely to say:
140|"So much pleases me your courteous request,
141|that I cannot nor wish to hide myself from you.
142|I am Arnaut, who weep and go singing;
143|pensive I see the past folly,
144|and I see rejoicing the joy which I hope for, ahead.
145|Now I pray you, by that power
146|which guides you to the top of the stairway,
147|be mindful in time of my pain!".
148|Then he hid himself in the fire which refines them.
