1|When the seven stars of the first heaven,
2|which neither setting ever knew nor rising
3|nor veil of other mist than of sin,
4|and which made there each one aware
5|of his duty, as the lower one does
6|for whoever turns the helm to come to port,
7|stopped fixed: the truthful people,
8|who had come first between the griffin and it,
9|turned themselves to the chariot as to their peace;
10|and one of them, as if sent from heaven,
11|'Come, bride, from Lebanon' singing
12|cried three times, and all the others after.
13|As the blessed at the last summons
14|will rise quickly each from his cavern,
15|with reclothed voice singing alleluia,
16|such upon the divine chariot
17|rose a hundred, at the voice of so great an elder,
18|ministers and messengers of eternal life.
19|All were saying: 'Blessed are you who come!',
20|and throwing flowers both above and around,
21|'Oh, give lilies with full hands!'.
22|I have seen before at the beginning of the day
23|the eastern part all rosy,
24|and the rest of the sky adorned with fair clearness;
25|and the face of the sun rise shaded,
26|so that through the tempering of vapors
27|the eye could bear it for a long time:
28|so within a cloud of flowers
29|which from the angelic hands was rising
30|and falling back down within and without,
31|over a white veil girded with olive
32|a lady appeared to me, under a green mantle
33|clothed in the color of living flame.
34|And my spirit, which now so long
35|a time had been since at her presence
36|it had not been, trembling, broken with awe,
37|without having more knowledge through the eyes,
38|through hidden power which moved from her,
39|of ancient love felt the great strength.
40|As soon as on my sight struck
41|the high power which had already pierced me
42|before I was out of boyhood,
43|I turned to the left with the trust
44|with which the little child runs to its mother
45|when it is afraid or when it is afflicted,
46|to say to Virgil: 'Less than a dram
47|of blood has remained in me which does not tremble:
48|I recognize the signs of the ancient flame'.
49|But Virgil had left us deprived
50|of himself, Virgil sweetest father,
51|Virgil to whom for my salvation I gave myself;
52|nor all that the ancient mother lost,
53|availed for my cheeks cleansed with dew
54|that, weeping, they should not turn dark again.
55|"Dante, because Virgil goes away,
56|do not weep yet, do not weep yet;
57|for you must weep for another sword".
58|Like an admiral who at stern and at bow
59|comes to see the people who serve
60|on the other ships, and encourages them to do well;
61|upon the left side of the chariot,
62|when I turned at the sound of my name,
63|which of necessity is here recorded,
64|I saw the lady who first appeared to me
65|veiled under the angelic festival,
66|direct her eyes toward me on this side of the stream.
67|Although the veil which descended from her head,
68|encircled with the leaves of Minerva,
69|did not let her appear plainly,
70|royally in her bearing still haughty
71|she continued like one who speaks
72|and keeps the hottest speech in reserve:
73|"Look at us well! Indeed I am, indeed I am Beatrice.
74|How did you deign to approach the mountain?
75|did you not know that here man is happy?".
76|My eyes fell down into the clear fountain;
77|but seeing myself in it, I drew them to the grass,
78|such shame weighed on my brow.
79|So the mother to her son seems harsh,
80|as she seemed to me; because bitter
81|tastes the flavor of stern pity.
82|She was silent; and the angels sang
83|suddenly 'In you, Lord, I have hoped';
84|but beyond 'my feet' they did not pass.
85|Just as snow among the living rafters
86|along the back of Italy freezes,
87|blown and packed by the Slavonian winds,
88|then, melted, trickles into itself,
89|provided that the land which loses shadow breathes,
90|so that it seems fire melting the candle;
91|so was I without tears and sighs
92|before the singing of those who always sing
93|following the notes of the eternal circles;
94|but after I heard in the sweet harmonies
95|their compassion for me, as if they had
96|said: 'Lady, why do you so undo him?',
97|the ice which was tight around my heart,
98|became breath and water, and with anguish
99|through the mouth and through the eyes came out from my breast.
100|She, still standing firm on the said side
101|of the chariot, to the pious beings
102|turned her words thus then:
103|"You keep watch in the eternal day,
104|so that neither night nor sleep steals from you
105|a step which the world may take on its ways;
106|wherefore my answer is with more care
107|that he may understand me who over there weeps,
108|so that guilt and grief be of one measure.
109|Not only through the work of the great wheels,
110|which direct each seed to some end
111|according as the stars are its companions,
112|but through the largess of divine graces,
113|which have such high vapors for their rain,
114|that our sight does not go near there,
115|this one was such in his new life
116|potentially, that every right disposition
117|would have made in him a marvelous proof.
118|But so much the more malign and more wild
119|becomes the ground with bad seed and untilled,
120|as it has more of good earthly vigor.
121|For some time I sustained him with my face:
122|showing my youthful eyes to him,
123|I led him with me turned in the right direction.
124|As soon as I was upon the threshold
125|of my second age and changed life,
126|this one took himself from me, and gave himself to others.
127|When from flesh to spirit I had risen,
128|and beauty and virtue had grown in me,
129|I was to him less dear and less pleasing;
130|and he turned his steps along a way not true,
131|following false images of good,
132|which pay no promise in full.
133|Nor did obtaining inspirations avail me,
134|with which both in dream and otherwise
135|I called him back: so little did he care for them!
136|So low he fell, that all means
137|for his salvation were already short,
138|except to show him the lost people.
139|For this I visited the threshold of the dead,
140|and to him who has led him up here,
141|my prayers, weeping, were offered.
142|The high decree of God would be broken,
143|if Lethe were passed and such food
144|were tasted without any payment
145|of repentance which pours out tears".
