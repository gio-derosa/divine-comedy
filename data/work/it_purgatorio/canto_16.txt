1|Darkness of hell and of a night deprived
2|of every planet, under a poor sky,
3|as much as it can be by cloud darkened,
4|did not make for my sight so thick a veil
5|as that smoke which there covered us,
6|nor to the feeling of so harsh a texture,
7|for the eye did not endure to stay open;
8|whereupon my escort wise and trusty
9|drew near me and offered me his shoulder.
10|Just as a blind man goes behind his guide
11|so as not to go astray and not to knock
12|against something which may hurt him, or perhaps kill him,
13|so went I through the air bitter and foul,
14|listening to my leader who was saying
15|continually: "Take care that from me you are not cut off".
16|I heard voices, and each one seemed
17|to pray for peace and for mercy
18|to the Lamb of God who takes away sins.
19|Only 'Lamb of God' were their beginnings;
20|one word was in all and one measure,
21|so that there seemed among them full concord.
22|"Are those spirits, master, which I hear?",
23|said I. And he to me: "You grasp the truth,
24|and of anger they are untying the knot".
25|"Now who are you who cleave our smoke,
26|and speak of us just as if you
27|still divided time by calends?".
28|Thus by one voice was said;
29|whereupon my master said: "Answer,
30|and ask if this way one goes up".
31|And I: "O creature who cleanse yourself
32|to return beautiful to him who made you,
33|a marvel you will hear, if you follow me".
34|"I will follow you as far as is permitted to me",
35|it answered; "and if seeing the smoke does not allow,
36|hearing will keep us joined in its stead".
37|Then I began: "With that swathing
38|which death dissolves I go upward,
39|and I came here through the infernal anguish.
40|And if God has enclosed me in his grace,
41|so much that he wills that I see his court
42|in a manner wholly outside modern usage,
43|do not hide from me who you were before death,
44|but tell it to me, and tell me if I go right to the pass;
45|and your words will be our escorts".
46|"Lombard I was, and was called Marco;
47|of the world I knew, and that worth I loved
48|at which everyone has now unbent his bow.
49|For mounting up you go rightly".
50|Thus he answered, and added: "I pray you
51|that for me you pray when you are up above".
52|And I to him: "By my faith I bind myself to you
53|to do what you ask me; but I am bursting
54|within with a doubt, if I do not free myself from it.
55|First it was single, and now it is made double
56|by your statement, which makes me certain,
57|here, and elsewhere, of that with which I couple it.
58|The world is indeed thus wholly deserted
59|of every virtue, as you sound to me,
60|and with wickedness pregnant and covered;
61|but I pray that you point out to me the cause,
62|so that I may see it and that I may show it to others;
63|for one places it in the heavens, and another down here".
64|A deep sigh, which grief wrung into "alas!",
65|he let out first; and then began: "Brother,
66|the world is blind, and you indeed come from it.
67|You who are living refer every cause
68|only up to the heavens, just as if everything
69|they moved with themselves of necessity.
70|If it were so, in you would be destroyed
71|free will, and it would not be justice
72|for good to have joy, and for evil to have mourning.
73|The heavens initiate your movements;
74|I do not say all, but, supposing that I say it,
75|light is given you for good and for evil,
76|and free will; which, if it endures fatigue
77|in the first battles with the heavens,
78|afterward conquers all, if it is well nourished.
79|To a greater power and to a better nature
80|free you are subject; and that creates
81|the mind in you, which the heavens have not in their charge.
82|Therefore, if the present world goes astray,
83|in you is the cause, in you let it be sought;
84|and I will be now to you a true informant about it.
85|There comes forth from the hand of him who fondly gazes on it
86|before it exists, in the manner of a little girl
87|who weeping and laughing plays childishly,
88|the simple little soul which knows nothing,
89|except that, moved by a glad maker,
90|willingly it turns to what delights it.
91|Of a small good at first it tastes the flavor;
92|there it is deceived, and runs after it,
93|if guide or bridle does not bend its love.
94|Whence it was needful to set law as a bridle;
95|it was needful to have a king, who could discern
96|of the true city at least the tower.
97|The laws exist, but who puts his hand to them?
98|No one, because the shepherd who leads,
99|can chew the cud, but has not the hooves cloven;
100|wherefore the people, who see their guide
101|strike only at that good for which they are greedy,
102|feed on that, and further do not ask.
103|Well can you see that the bad guidance
104|is the cause which has made the world wicked,
105|and not nature which in you may be corrupt.
106|Rome, which made the good world, used
107|to have two suns, which the one and the other road
108|made visible, both of the world and of God.
109|The one has extinguished the other; and joined is the sword
110|with the crook, and the one with the other together
111|by main force must go ill;
112|because, joined, the one does not fear the other:
113|if you do not believe me, pay heed to the ear of grain,
114|for every plant is known by its seed.
115|In the land which the Adige and the Po water,
116|valor and courtesy used to be found,
117|before Frederick had strife;
118|now it can safely be passed through
119|by whoever would have ceased, out of shame,
120|to speak with the good or to draw near them.
121|True there are three old men still in whom rebukes
122|the ancient age the new, and it seems long to them
123|until God to a better life removes them:
124|Currado da Palazzo and the good Gherardo
125|and Guido da Castel, who is better named,
126|in the French manner, the simple Lombard.
127|Say henceforth that the Church of Rome,
128|through confounding in itself two governments,
129|falls in the mire, and fouls itself and its burden".
130|"O my Marco", said I, "you reason well;
131|and now I discern why from the inheritance
132|the sons of Levi were exempt.
133|But which Gherardo is that one who you as a sample
134|say has remained of the extinct people,
135|in reproach of the savage age?".
136|"Either your speech deceives me, or it tempts me",
137|he answered me; "for, speaking to me Tuscan,
138|it seems that of the good Gherardo you know nothing.
139|By other surname I do not know him,
140|unless I took it from his daughter Gaia.
141|God be with you, for I come no further with you.
142|See the brightness which through the smoke rays
143|already whitening, and I must depart
144|(the angel is there) before I appear to him".
145|Thus he turned back, and would not hear me further.
