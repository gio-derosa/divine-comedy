1|Silent, alone, without company
2|we went, the one in front and the other after,
3|as Friars Minor go along the road.
4|Turned was upon the fable of Aesop
5|my thought because of the present brawl,
6|where he spoke of the frog and of the mouse;
7|for 'mo' and 'issa' are not more alike
8|than the one is to the other, if one couples well
9|beginning and end with an attentive mind.
10|And as one thought bursts out of another,
11|so was born from that one another then,
12|which made my first fear double.
13|I was thinking thus: 'These because of us
14|are mocked with harm and with a jest
15|such, that I believe it greatly annoys them.
16|If anger is wound upon ill will,
17|they will come after us more cruel
18|than the dog to that hare which it snaps up'.
19|Already I felt all my hairs bristling
20|from fear and I was intent on what was behind,
21|when I said: "Master, if you do not hide
22|yourself and me quickly, I have dread
23|of the Malebranche. We have them already behind us;
24|I imagine them so, that already I hear them".
25|And he: "If I were of leaded glass,
26|your outer image I would not draw
27|more quickly to me, than I receive the inner one.
28|Just now your thoughts were coming among mine,
29|with similar act and with similar face,
30|so that of both I made a single counsel.
31|If it is that the right slope so lies,
32|that we can descend into the next pouch,
33|we will flee the imagined chase".
34|He had not yet finished giving such counsel,
35|when I saw them coming with wings spread
36|not very far off, wanting to take us.
37|My leader suddenly took me,
38|like the mother who is woken by the noise
39|and sees near her the kindled flames,
40|who takes her son and flees and does not stop,
41|having more care for him than for herself,
42|so much that she puts on only a shift;
43|and down from the neck of the hard bank
44|supine he gave himself to the sloping rock,
45|which blocks one of the sides of the next pouch.
46|Never did water run so fast through a sluice
47|to turn the wheel of a land mill,
48|when it most approaches the paddles,
49|as my master down that border,
50|carrying me away upon his chest,
51|as his son, not as a companion.
52|Hardly had his feet arrived at the bed
53|of the bottom below, when they were upon the height
54|right above us; but there was no fear there:
55|for the high providence which willed to
56|place them as ministers of the fifth ditch,
57|takes from all of them the power to depart from there.
58|Down there we found a painted people
59|who went around with very slow steps,
60|weeping and in semblance tired and vanquished.
61|They had capes with hoods low
62|before their eyes, made in the cut
63|that is made for the monks in Cluny.
64|Outside they are gilded, so that it dazzles;
65|but inside all lead, and so heavy,
66|that Frederick put them on of straw.
67|Oh mantle wearisome in eternity!
68|We turned again still to the left hand
69|together with them, intent on the sad weeping;
70|but because of the weight that tired people
71|came so slowly, that we were new
72|in company at every movement of the hip.
73|Wherefore I to my leader: "See that you find
74|someone who by deed or by name may be known,
75|and move your eyes around, while going so".
76|And one who understood the Tuscan word,
77|behind us cried: "Hold your feet,
78|you who run so through the dusky air!
79|Perhaps you will have from me what you ask".
80|Whereupon the leader turned and said: "Wait,
81|and then proceed according to his pace".
82|I stopped, and saw two show great haste
83|of mind, in their face, to be with me;
84|but the load and the narrow way slowed them.
85|When they had arrived, long with a squinting eye
86|they gazed at me without making a word;
87|then they turned to each other, and said among themselves:
88|"This one seems alive by the action of his throat;
89|and if they are dead, by what privilege
90|do they go uncovered by the heavy stole?".
91|Then they said to me: "O Tuscan, who to the college
92|of the sad hypocrites have come,
93|do not hold in scorn to tell who you are".
94|And I to them: "I was born and grew up
95|on the beautiful river of Arno in the great town,
96|and I am with the body that I have always had.
97|But you, who are you, down whose cheeks distills
98|so much pain as I see?
99|and what punishment is in you that so glitters?".
100|And one answered me: "The orange capes
101|are of lead so thick, that the weights
102|make their scales creak thus.
103|Jovial Friars we were, and Bolognese;
104|I Catalano and this one Loderingo
105|named, and by your city together taken
106|as usually one man alone is chosen,
107|to preserve its peace; and we were such,
108|that it still appears around the Gardingo".
109|I began: "O friars, your evils...";
110|but I said no more, for there struck my eye
111|one, crucified on the ground with three stakes.
112|When he saw me, he writhed all over,
113|blowing into his beard with sighs;
114|and Friar Catalano, who perceived this,
115|said to me: "That transfixed one at whom you gaze,
116|counseled the Pharisees that it was fitting
117|to put one man to torments for the people.
118|He is laid across, naked, in the way,
119|as you see, and it is needful that he feel
120|whoever passes, how much he weighs, first.
121|And in such manner the father-in-law is racked
122|in this ditch, and the others of the council
123|which was for the Jews an evil seed".
124|Then I saw Virgil marvel
125|over him who was stretched on a cross
126|so vilely in the eternal exile.
127|Then he directed to the friar such words:
128|"May it not displease you, if it is permitted you, to tell us
129|whether on the right hand lies some opening
130|by which we both can get out from here,
131|without compelling some of the black angels
132|to come from this bottom to take us away".
133|He answered then: "Nearer than you hope
134|approaches a rock which from the great circle
135|moves and crosses all the cruel valleys,
136|except that at this one it is broken and does not cover it;
137|you can climb up along the ruin,
138|which lies on the slope and heaps up at the bottom".
139|The leader stood a little with his head bowed;
140|then he said: "Badly did he recount the business
141|who hooks the sinners over there".
142|And the friar: "I once heard tell at Bologna
143|of the devil's many vices, among which I heard
144|that he is a liar and the father of lies".
145|After that the leader went off with great strides,
146|troubled a little with anger in his semblance;
147|whereupon I departed from the burdened ones
148|behind the prints of the dear feet.
