1|I have seen before horsemen move camp,
2|and begin the attack and make their muster,
3|and sometimes depart for their escape;
4|coursers I have seen through your land,
5|O Aretines, and I have seen raiding parties go,
6|tournaments struck and jousts run;
7|now with trumpets, and now with bells,
8|with drums and with signals from castles,
9|and with things of ours and with foreign ones;
10|but never with so strange a pipe
11|have I seen horsemen move nor foot soldiers,
12|nor ship by sign of land or of star.
13|We were going with the ten demons.
14|Ah fierce company! but in the church
15|with the saints, and in the tavern with the gluttons.
16|Still on the pitch was my attention,
17|to see of the pouch every condition
18|and of the people who were burned inside it.
19|As the dolphins, when they make a sign
20|to the sailors with the arch of their back
21|that they should contrive to save their vessel,
22|at times so, to lighten the pain,
23|some of the sinners showed his back
24|and hid it in less time than it takes to flash.
25|And as at the edge of the water of a ditch
26|the little frogs stay with just their muzzle out,
27|so that they hide their feet and the other bulk,
28|so were the sinners on every side;
29|but as Barbariccia approached,
30|so they drew back under the boilings.
31|I saw, and still my heart shudders at it,
32|one waiting so, as it happens
33|that one frog remains and the other darts off;
34|and Graffiacane, who was most opposite him,
35|hooked him by his pitch-smeared hair
36|and drew him up, so that he seemed to me an otter.
37|I already knew the name of all of them,
38|so well I noted them when they were chosen,
39|and when they called each other, I listened how.
40|"O Rubicante, see that you put
41|your claws on him, so that you flay him!",
42|all the cursed ones were shouting together.
43|And I: "My master, contrive, if you can,
44|that you learn who is the wretch
45|come into the hands of his adversaries".
46|My leader drew up beside him;
47|he asked him where he was from, and he answered:
48|"I was born in the kingdom of Navarre.
49|My mother placed me as servant to a lord,
50|for she had begotten me of a scoundrel,
51|destroyer of himself and of his things.
52|Then I was of the household of the good king Thibault;
53|there I set myself to practice barratry,
54|for which I render account in this heat".
55|And Ciriatto, from whose mouth came out
56|on each side a tusk as on a boar,
57|made him feel how one of them rips.
58|Among evil cats the mouse had come;
59|but Barbariccia locked him in his arms
60|and said: "Stand back there, while I fork him".
61|And to my master he turned his face;
62|"Ask", he said, "further, if you desire more
63|to know from him, before another undoes him".
64|The leader then: "Now say: of the other guilty ones
65|do you know any who is Italian
66|under the pitch?". And he: "I departed,
67|a short while ago, from one who was a neighbor to it.
68|Would that I were still covered with him,
69|for I would fear neither claw nor hook!".
70|And Libicocco: "We have endured too much",
71|he said; and he took his arm with the grapple,
72|so that, tearing, he carried off a muscle.
73|Draghignazzo too wanted to grab him
74|down at the legs; whereupon their decurion
75|turned around and around with an evil look.
76|When they were a little calmed,
77|of him, who was still gazing at his wound,
78|my leader asked without delay:
79|"Who was that one from whom an evil parting
80|you say you made to come to shore?".
81|And he answered: "It was Friar Gomita,
82|he of Gallura, vessel of every fraud,
83|who had his lord's enemies in hand,
84|and did so to them, that each one praises himself for it.
85|Money he took and let them off smoothly,
86|as he says; and in the other offices too
87|he was a barrator not small, but sovereign.
88|Keeps company with him Don Michel Zanche
89|of Logudoro; and in speaking of Sardinia
90|their tongues do not feel themselves tired.
91|Alas, see the other who gnashes his teeth;
92|I would say more, but I fear that he
93|is preparing to scratch my scurf".
94|And the great provost, turned to Farfarello
95|who was rolling his eyes to strike,
96|said: "Get over there, wicked bird!".
97|"If you want to see or to hear",
98|began again the frightened one afterward,
99|"Tuscans or Lombards, I will make them come;
100|but let the Malebranche stand a little apart,
101|so that they may not fear their vengeance;
102|and I, sitting in this same place,
103|for one that I am, will make seven come
104|when I will whistle, as is our custom
105|to do when someone gets himself out".
106|Cagnazzo at such a word raised his muzzle,
107|shaking his head, and said: "Hear the malice
108|that he has thought up to throw himself down!".
109|Whereupon he, who had snares in great abundance,
110|answered: "Too malicious am I,
111|when I procure for my own greater sorrow".
112|Alichino did not hold back and, in opposition
113|to the others, said to him: "If you go down,
114|I will not come after you at a gallop,
115|but I will beat my wings above the pitch.
116|Let the ridge be left, and let the bank be a shield,
117|to see if you alone are worth more than us".
118|O you who read, you will hear a new game:
119|each turned his eyes to the other side,
120|he first, who had been most reluctant to do it.
121|The Navarrese chose his time well;
122|he planted his soles on the ground, and in an instant
123|he jumped and freed himself from their purpose.
124|At which each was stung with guilt,
125|but he most who was cause of the fault;
126|therefore he moved and cried: "You are caught!".
127|But it availed him little: for the wings could not
128|outstrip the fear; that one went under,
129|and this one straightened his chest flying upward:
130|not otherwise the duck suddenly,
131|when the falcon approaches, plunges down,
132|and he returns up angered and defeated.
133|Calcabrina, angry at the trick,
134|flying kept behind him, eager
135|that that one might escape so as to have the scuffle;
136|and as the barrator had disappeared,
137|so he turned his talons on his companion,
138|and was grappled with him above the ditch.
139|But the other was indeed a fierce sparrowhawk
140|to claw him well, and both
141|fell into the middle of the boiling pond.
142|The heat was an ungrappler at once;
143|but still to rise up there was no way,
144|so had they smeared their wings.
145|Barbariccia, grieving with the others of his,
146|made four of them fly to the other side
147|with all their hooks, and very quickly
148|here and there they descended to their post;
149|they stretched out the hooks toward the limed ones,
150|who were already cooked inside the crust.
151|And we left them thus entangled.
