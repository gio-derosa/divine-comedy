1|In the middle of the path of our life
2|I found myself again through a dark forest,
3|for the straight way was lost.
4|Ah how hard a thing it is to say what it was,
5|this forest savage and harsh and strong
6|which in the thought renews the fear!
7|So bitter it is that death is little more;
8|but to treat of the good that I found there,
9|I will speak of the other things that I have seen there.
10|I do not know well how to retell how I entered there,
11|so full of sleep was I at that point
12|when I abandoned the true way.
13|But after I had arrived at the foot of a hill,
14|there where that valley ended
15|which had pierced my heart with fear,
16|I looked on high and saw its shoulders
17|clothed already with the rays of the planet
18|that leads others straight along every path.
19|Then the fear was a little quieted,
20|which in the lake of the heart had lasted in me
21|the night that I passed with so much anguish.
22|And like one who with laboring breath,
23|come out of the sea to the shore,
24|turns to the perilous water and gazes,
25|so my mind, which was still fleeing,
26|turned back to look again at the pass
27|which never yet left a person alive.
28|After I had rested a little the weary body,
29|I took up the way again along the deserted slope,
30|so that the firm foot was always the lower.
31|And behold, almost at the beginning of the ascent,
32|a leopard light and very quick,
33|which was covered with spotted fur;
34|and it did not depart from before my face,
35|but rather impeded so much my path,
36|that I was turned many times to go back.
37|The time was the beginning of the morning,
38|and the sun was mounting up with those stars
39|that were with him when the divine love
40|first moved those beautiful things;
41|so that for me to hope well was cause
42|about that beast with the gay-colored skin
43|the hour of the time and the sweet season;
44|but not so that fear was not given me
45|by the sight that appeared to me of a lion.
46|This one seemed that against me he was coming
47|with the head high and with rabid hunger,
48|so that it seemed that the air trembled at it.
49|And a she-wolf, who with all cravings
50|seemed laden in her leanness,
51|and has already made many peoples live wretched,
52|this one brought me so much heaviness
53|with the fear that came out of her sight,
54|that I lost the hope of the height.
55|And like he is who willingly acquires,
56|and the time comes that makes him lose,
57|who in all his thoughts weeps and grows sad;
58|such did the beast without peace make me,
59|which, coming against me, little by little
60|was pushing me back there where the sun is silent.
61|While I was falling down into the low place,
62|before my eyes there was offered to me
63|one who through long silence seemed faint.
64|When I saw this one in the great desert,
65|"Have mercy on me", I cried to him,
66|"whatever you are, either shade or real man!".
67|He answered me: "Not man, man once I was,
68|and my parents were Lombards,
69|Mantuans by homeland both.
70|I was born under Julius, although it was late,
71|and I lived in Rome under the good Augustus
72|in the time of the false and lying gods.
73|Poet I was, and I sang of that just
74|son of Anchises who came from Troy,
75|after proud Ilion was burned.
76|But you, why do you return to so much trouble?
77|why do you not climb the delightful mountain
78|which is beginning and cause of all joy?".
79|"Now are you that Virgil and that fountain
80|which spreads so wide a river of speech?",
81|I answered him with ashamed forehead.
82|"O of the other poets honor and light,
83|may the long study and the great love avail me
84|which have made me search your volume.
85|You are my master and my author,
86|you are the only one from whom I took
87|the beautiful style that has done me honor.
88|See the beast for which I turned;
89|help me from her, famous sage,
90|for she makes my veins and pulses tremble".
91|"It is fitting for you to hold another journey",
92|he answered, after he saw me weeping,
93|"if you want to escape from this savage place;
94|for this beast, for which you cry out,
95|does not let others pass along her way,
96|but so much she impedes him that she kills him;
97|and she has a nature so wicked and evil,
98|that she never fills the greedy will,
99|and after the meal she has more hunger than before.
100|Many are the animals with which she mates,
101|and more there will be still, until the greyhound
102|will come, who will make her die with pain.
103|This one will not feed on land nor pewter,
104|but wisdom, love and virtue,
105|and his nation will be between felt and felt.
106|Of that humble Italy he will be salvation
107|for which died the virgin Camilla,
108|Euryalus and Turnus and Nisus of wounds.
109|This one will hunt her through every town,
110|until he will have put her back into Hell,
111|there whence envy first sent her forth.
112|Whence I for your best think and discern
113|that you follow me, and I will be your guide,
114|and I will draw you from here through an eternal place;
115|where you will hear the desperate shrieks,
116|you will see the ancient sorrowing spirits,
117|who each cry out for the second death;
118|and you will see those who are content
119|in the fire, because they hope to come
120|whenever it may be to the blessed people.
121|To whom then if you will want to ascend,
122|a soul will be for that more worthy than I:
123|with her I will leave you at my departing;
124|for that emperor who reigns up there,
125|because I was rebellious to his law,
126|does not want that into his city through me one should come.
127|In all parts he governs and there he rules;
128|there is his city and the high seat:
129|oh happy the one whom he chooses there!".
130|And I to him: "Poet, I entreat you
131|by that God whom you did not know,
132|so that I may flee this evil and worse,
133|that you lead me there where you now said,
134|so that I may see the gate of Saint Peter
135|and those whom you make so sorrowful".
136|Then he moved, and I kept behind him.
