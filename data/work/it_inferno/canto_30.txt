1|In the time when Juno was angered
2|because of Semele against the Theban blood,
3|as she showed one and another time,
4|Athamas became so insane,
5|that seeing his wife with two sons
6|going laden on each hand,
7|he cried: "Let us spread the nets, so that I may catch
8|the lioness and the lion cubs at the pass";
9|and then he stretched out his pitiless talons,
10|taking the one who had the name Learchus,
11|and whirled him and dashed him against a rock;
12|and she drowned herself with the other burden.
13|And when fortune turned downward
14|the loftiness of the Trojans which dared all,
15|so that together with the kingdom the king was destroyed,
16|Hecuba sad, miserable and captive,
17|after she saw Polyxena dead,
18|and of her Polydorus on the shore
19|of the sea the sorrowful one became aware,
20|out of her senses barked like a dog;
21|so much did the pain twist her mind.
22|But neither furies of Thebes nor Trojan ones
23|were ever seen in anyone so cruel,
24|not in goading beasts, much less human limbs,
25|as I saw in two shades pale and naked,
26|who biting were running in that manner
27|that the pig does when it is let out of the sty.
28|One came at Capocchio, and on the knot
29|of his neck sank its tusks, so that, dragging,
30|it made his belly scrape on the hard bottom.
31|And the Aretine who remained, trembling
32|said to me: "That goblin is Gianni Schicchi,
33|and he goes rabid treating others so".
34|"Oh", I said to him, "so may the other not fix
35|its teeth on you, may it not be a labor for you
36|to tell who it is, before it darts away from here".
37|And he to me: "That is the ancient soul
38|of wicked Myrrha, who became
39|to her father, beyond rightful love, a lover.
40|She came to sin with him thus,
41|falsifying herself in another's form,
42|as the other who goes away there, undertook,
43|to gain the lady of the herd,
44|to falsify in himself Buoso Donati,
45|making a will and giving the will legal form".
46|And after the two rabid ones had passed
47|on whom I had kept my eye,
48|I turned it back to look at the other ill-born.
49|I saw one, made in the shape of a lute,
50|if only he had had his groin
51|cut off from the part where man is forked.
52|The heavy dropsy, which so unmatches
53|the limbs with the humor that it ill converts,
54|that the face does not correspond to the belly,
55|made him hold his lips open
56|as the consumptive does, who for thirst
57|turns one toward the chin and the other upward.
58|"O you who are without any punishment,
59|and I do not know why, in the grim world",
60|he said to us, "look and attend
61|to the misery of Master Adam;
62|I had, alive, plenty of what I wanted,
63|and now, alas!, a drop of water I crave.
64|The little brooks which from the green hills
65|of the Casentino descend down into the Arno,
66|making their channels cool and moist,
67|always stand before me, and not in vain,
68|for their image dries me up far more
69|than the illness by which I waste away in my face.
70|The rigid justice that searches me
71|draws occasion from the place where I sinned
72|to put my sighs more to flight.
73|There is Romena, there where I falsified
74|the alloy sealed with the Baptist;
75|for which I left my body burned up there.
76|But if I saw here the sad soul
77|of Guido or of Alessandro or of their brother,
78|for Fonte Branda I would not give the sight.
79|Inside here is one already, if the raging
80|shades that go around say true;
81|but what does it avail me, who have my limbs bound?
82|If I were only still so light
83|that I could in a hundred years go an inch,
84|I would already have set out on the path,
85|seeking him among these disfigured people,
86|although it circles eleven miles,
87|and is not less than half a mile across.
88|I am because of them among such a family;
89|they induced me to strike the florins
90|which had three carats of dross".
91|And I to him: "Who are the two wretches
92|who smoke like wet hands in winter,
93|lying close to your right borders?".
94|"Here I found them - and since then they have not turned -",
95|he answered, "when I rained down into this cleft,
96|and I do not believe that they will turn in all eternity.
97|One is the false woman who accused Joseph;
98|the other is the false Sinon, Greek from Troy:
99|from acute fever they throw off such a stench".
100|And one of them, who took it ill
101|perhaps to be named so darkly,
102|with his fist struck him on his stiff paunch.
103|It sounded as if it were a drum;
104|and Master Adam struck him on the face
105|with his arm, which did not seem less hard,
106|saying to him: "Although there is taken from me
107|the moving by my limbs which are heavy,
108|I have an arm free for such a need".
109|Whereupon he answered: "When you were going
110|to the fire, you did not have it so ready;
111|but so and more you had it when you were coining".
112|And the dropsical one: "You say true in this:
113|but you were not so true a witness
114|there where you were asked for the truth at Troy".
115|"If I spoke false, you too falsified the coin",
116|said Sinon; "and I am here for one fault,
117|and you for more than any other demon!".
118|"Remember, perjurer, the horse",
119|answered he who had the swollen paunch;
120|"and let it be bitter to you that the whole world knows it!".
121|"And to you be bitter the thirst by which cracks",
122|said the Greek, "your tongue, and the putrid water
123|which so hedges your belly before your eyes!".
124|Then the coiner: "Thus gapes open
125|your mouth for your ill as it is accustomed;
126|for, if I have thirst and humor stuffs me,
127|you have the burning and the head that aches you,
128|and to lick the mirror of Narcissus,
129|you would not want many words of invitation".
130|To listening to them I was wholly fixed,
131|when the master said to me: "Now just keep looking,
132|for a little more and I will quarrel with you!".
133|When I heard him speak to me with anger,
134|I turned toward him with such shame,
135|that still it circles through my memory.
136|As is he who dreams of his harm,
137|who dreaming desires to be dreaming,
138|so that what is, as if it were not, he craves,
139|such I became, not being able to speak,
140|for I desired to excuse myself, and was excusing
141|myself all the while, and did not believe I was doing it.
142|"Greater fault less shame washes away",
143|said the master, "than yours has been;
144|therefore unburden yourself of all sadness.
145|And reckon that I am always at your side,
146|if again it happens that fortune brings you
147|where there are people in a similar dispute:
148|for to want to hear that is a base desire".
