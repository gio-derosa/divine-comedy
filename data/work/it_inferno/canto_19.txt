1|O Simon Magus, O miserable followers
2|who the things of God, which to goodness
3|should be brides, and you, rapacious,
4|for gold and for silver prostitute,
5|now it is fitting that for you the trumpet sound,
6|because in the third pouch you stay.
7|Already we had, at the next tomb,
8|climbed onto the part of the crag
9|which exactly over the middle of the ditch hangs plumb.
10|O highest wisdom, how great is the art
11|that you show in heaven, on earth and in the evil world,
12|and how justly your power distributes!
13|I saw along the sides and along the bottom
14|the livid stone full of holes,
15|all of one width, and each was round.
16|They did not seem to me less wide nor greater
17|than those which are in my beautiful San Giovanni,
18|made as places for the baptizers;
19|one of which, not many years ago,
20|I broke for one who was drowning inside it:
21|and let this be a seal that undeceives every man.
22|Out of the mouth of each protruded
23|a sinner's feet and of the legs
24|up to the thick part, and the rest stayed inside.
25|The soles were on fire for all of them, both;
26|wherefore so strongly the joints were writhing,
27|that they would have broken withes and ropes.
28|As the flaming of oily things is accustomed
29|to move only along the outer skin,
30|such was it there from the heels to the tips.
31|"Who is that one, master, who torments himself
32|writhing more than his other fellows",
33|I said, "and whom a redder flame sucks?".
34|And he to me: "If you want me to carry you
35|down there along that bank which lies lower,
36|from him you will learn of himself and of his wrongs".
37|And I: "As much is pleasing to me as pleases you:
38|you are lord, and you know that I do not depart
39|from your will, and you know what is left unsaid".
40|Then we came upon the fourth bank;
41|we turned and descended on the left hand
42|down there into the bottom full of holes and narrow.
43|The good master did not yet from his hip
44|set me down, until he brought me to the hole
45|of him who was lamenting so with his shanks.
46|"O whoever you are who hold your upper part below,
47|sad soul, planted like a stake",
48|I began to say, "if you can, make a word".
49|I was standing like the friar who confesses
50|the perfidious assassin, who, after he is fixed in,
51|calls him back, whereby death is delayed.
52|And he cried: "Are you already standing there,
53|are you already standing there, Boniface?
54|By several years the writing lied to me.
55|Are you so soon sated with that wealth
56|for which you did not fear to take by deceit
57|the beautiful lady, and then to make havoc of her?".
58|Such I became, as are those who stand,
59|through not understanding what is answered them,
60|as if mocked, and do not know how to answer.
61|Then Virgil said: "Tell him quickly:
62|'I am not he, I am not he whom you believe'";
63|and I answered as was imposed on me.
64|Whereupon the spirit twisted both his feet;
65|then, sighing and with a voice of weeping,
66|he said to me: "Then what do you ask of me?
67|If to know who I am concerns you so much,
68|that you have for that come down the bank,
69|know that I was clothed with the great mantle;
70|and truly I was a son of the she-bear,
71|so eager to advance the bear cubs,
72|that up there wealth, and here myself I put in the purse.
73|Beneath my head are the others dragged
74|who preceded me in simony,
75|flattened through the fissures of the stone.
76|Down there I will fall likewise when
77|he will come whom I believed you were,
78|when I made the sudden question.
79|But longer already is the time that I have cooked my feet
80|and that I have been thus upside down,
81|than he will stay planted with red feet:
82|for after him will come, of fouler deed,
83|from toward the west, a shepherd without law,
84|such that he must cover both him and me.
85|A new Jason he will be, of whom one reads
86|in Maccabees; and as to that one was pliant
87|his king, so to him will be he who rules France".
88|I do not know if I was here too rash,
89|for I simply answered him in this measure:
90|"Ah, now tell me: how much treasure did
91|Our Lord want first from Saint Peter
92|before he placed the keys in his keeping?
93|Surely he asked nothing but 'Follow me'.
94|Nor did Peter nor the others take from Matthias
95|gold or silver, when he was chosen by lot
96|for the place that the guilty soul lost.
97|Therefore stay there, for you are well punished;
98|and guard well the ill-gotten money
99|which made you bold against Charles.
100|And were it not that still forbids me
101|the reverence for the highest keys
102|that you held in the glad life,
103|I would use words still more grave;
104|for your avarice saddens the world,
105|trampling the good and raising up the wicked.
106|Of you shepherds the Evangelist was aware,
107|when she who sits upon the waters
108|was seen by him whoring with the kings;
109|she who was born with the seven heads,
110|and from the ten horns had strength,
111|as long as virtue pleased her husband.
112|You have made yourselves a god of gold and of silver;
113|and what other difference is there between you and the idolater,
114|except that he one, and you pray to a hundred?
115|Ah, Constantine, of how much evil was mother,
116|not your conversion, but that dowry
117|which the first rich father took from you!".
118|And while I was singing him such notes,
119|whether anger or conscience bit him,
120|he kicked hard with both his soles.
121|I believe indeed that it pleased my leader,
122|with so contented a look he listened all along
123|to the sound of the true words expressed.
124|Therefore with both his arms he took me;
125|and when he had me wholly up on his chest,
126|he climbed back by the way by which he had descended.
127|Nor did he tire of having me clasped to himself,
128|until he carried me over the top of the arch
129|which from the fourth to the fifth bank is a crossing.
130|There gently he set down the load,
131|gently because of the crag rough and steep
132|which would be for goats a hard passage.
133|From there another valley was uncovered to me.
