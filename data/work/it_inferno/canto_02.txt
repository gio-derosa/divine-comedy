1|The day was going away, and the dark air
2|was taking the animals that are on earth
3|from their labors; and I alone
4|was preparing myself to sustain the war
5|both of the journey and of the pity,
6|which the mind that does not err will portray.
7|O muses, O high genius, now help me;
8|O memory that wrote down what I saw,
9|here your nobility will be shown.
10|I began: "Poet who guides me,
11|look at my power, whether it is strong enough,
12|before to the high pass you trust me.
13|You say that of Silvius the parent,
14|still corruptible, to the immortal
15|world went, and was there bodily.
16|However, if the adversary of every evil
17|was courteous to him, thinking of the high effect
18|that was to come out of him, and the who and the what,
19|it does not seem unworthy to a man of intellect;
20|for he was of noble Rome and of its empire
21|in the empyrean heaven chosen as father:
22|the which and the which, to want to say the truth,
23|were established for the holy place
24|where sits the successor of the greater Peter.
25|Through this going for which you give him praise,
26|he understood things that were the cause
27|of his victory and of the papal mantle.
28|There went later the Vessel of election,
29|to bring from there comfort to that faith
30|which is the beginning of the way of salvation.
31|But I, why come there? or who allows it?
32|I am not Aeneas, I am not Paul;
33|me worthy of this neither I nor others believe it.
34|Wherefore, if to the coming I abandon myself,
35|I fear that the coming may be mad.
36|You are wise; you understand better than I reason".
37|And like he is who unwants what he wanted
38|and through new thoughts changes purpose,
39|so that from the beginning he withdraws entirely,
40|such I made myself on that dark slope,
41|because, thinking, I consumed the enterprise
42|which was in the beginning so quick.
43|"If I have well understood your word",
44|answered that shade of the magnanimous one,
45|"your soul is offended by cowardice;
46|which many times encumbers man
47|so that from an honored enterprise it turns him back,
48|as false seeing does a beast when it shies.
49|So that you may free yourself from this fear,
50|I will tell you why I came and what I heard
51|at the first point that I grieved for you.
52|I was among those who are suspended,
53|and a lady called me, blessed and beautiful,
54|such that I asked her to command.
55|Her eyes were shining more than the star;
56|and she began to say to me, gentle and soft,
57|with angelic voice, in her speech:
58|"O courteous Mantuan soul,
59|of whom the fame still in the world lasts,
60|and will last as long as the world, far,
61|my friend, and not of fortune,
62|on the deserted slope is impeded
63|so on the path, that he has turned back for fear;
64|and I fear that he may be already so lost,
65|that I may have risen late to the rescue,
66|from what I have heard of him in heaven.
67|Now move, and with your ornate word
68|and with what is needed for his escaping,
69|help him so that I may be consoled by it.
70|I am Beatrice who makes you go;
71|I come from the place where I desire to return;
72|love moved me, which makes me speak.
73|When I will be before my lord,
74|I will praise myself of you often to him".
75|She fell silent then, and then I began:
76|"O lady of virtue, alone through whom
77|the human species exceeds all that is contained
78|by that heaven which has its circles smallest,
79|so much your command pleases me,
80|that the obeying, if it were already done, is late for me;
81|there is no more need for you but to open to me your wish.
82|But tell me the reason why you do not beware
83|of the descending down here into this center
84|from the ample place where you burn to return".
85|"Since you want to know so far within,
86|I will tell you briefly", she answered me,
87|"why I do not fear to come in here.
88|One must fear only those things
89|that have power to do others harm;
90|of the others no, for they are not fearful.
91|I am made by God, his mercy, such,
92|that your misery does not touch me,
93|nor does flame of this fire assail me.
94|There is a gentle lady in heaven who grieves
95|over this impediment where I send you,
96|so that she breaks hard judgment up there.
97|This one called Lucia in her request
98|and said: - Now has need your faithful one
99|of you, and I to you commend him -.
100|Lucia, enemy of everyone cruel,
101|moved, and came to the place where I was,
102|where I was sitting with the ancient Rachel.
103|She said: - Beatrice, true praise of God,
104|why do you not help him who loved you so much,
105|that he came out for you from the common crowd?
106|Do you not hear the anguish of his weeping,
107|do you not see the death that fights him
108|on the flood over which the sea has no boast? -.
109|In the world there were never persons swift
110|to do their profit or to flee their harm,
111|as I, after such words were made,
112|came down here from my blessed seat,
113|trusting myself to your honest speech,
114|which honors you and those who have heard it".
115|After she had spoken this to me,
116|her shining eyes weeping she turned,
117|wherefore she made me quicker in coming.
118|And I came to you just as she wished:
119|from before that beast I took you away
120|which from the beautiful mountain took from you the short going.
121|So: what is it, why, why do you stay,
122|why do you harbor such cowardice in the heart,
123|why do you not have daring and boldness,
124|since three such blessed ladies
125|care for you in the court of heaven,
126|and my speech promises you so much good?".
127|As little flowers by the nightly frost
128|bent and closed, after the sun whitens them,
129|straighten themselves all open on their stem,
130|such I made myself with my tired power,
131|and so much good daring ran to my heart,
132|that I began like a person freed:
133|"Oh compassionate she who helped me!
134|and you courteous who obeyed quickly
135|the true words that she offered you!
136|You have with desire disposed my heart
137|so to the coming with your words,
138|that I have returned to the first purpose.
139|Now go, for one single will is of both:
140|you leader, you lord and you master".
141|So I said to him; and after he had moved,
142|I entered along the path deep and wild.
