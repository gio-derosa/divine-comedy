1|I say, continuing, that well before
2|we were at the foot of the high tower,
3|our eyes went up to the top
4|because of two little flames that we saw placed there,
5|and another from far away give back a signal,
6|so far that the eye could hardly catch it.
7|And I turned to the sea of all wisdom;
8|I said: "This one, what does it say? and what answers
9|that other fire? and who are those who made it?".
10|And he to me: "Over the filthy waves
11|already you can discern that which is awaited,
12|if the smoke of the marsh does not hide it from you".
13|Bowstring never shot from itself an arrow
14|that ran so swiftly away through the air,
15|as I saw a very little boat
16|come through the water toward us at that moment,
17|under the steering of a single boatman,
18|who was shouting: "Now you have arrived, wicked soul!".
19|"Phlegyas, Phlegyas, you shout in vain",
20|said my lord, "this time:
21|you will have us no longer than only crossing the mud".
22|As is he who a great deceit hears
23|that was done to him, and then resents it,
24|so became Phlegyas in his gathered anger.
25|My leader went down into the boat,
26|and then he made me enter after him;
27|and only when I was inside did it seem laden.
28|As soon as the leader and I were in the vessel,
29|cutting goes off the ancient prow
30|more of the water than it is used to with others.
31|While we were running the dead channel,
32|before me there placed himself one full of mud,
33|and said: "Who are you who come before the hour?".
34|And I to him: "If I come, I do not remain;
35|but you, who are you, who have become so foul?".
36|He answered: "You see that I am one who weeps".
37|And I to him: "With weeping and with mourning,
38|cursed spirit, may you remain;
39|for I know you, even if you are all filthy".
40|Then he stretched to the vessel both his hands;
41|wherefore the wary master pushed him off,
42|saying: "Away there with the other dogs!".
43|My neck then with his arms he embraced;
44|he kissed my face and said: "Disdainful soul,
45|blessed she who was pregnant with you!
46|That one was in the world an arrogant person;
47|there is no goodness that adorns his memory:
48|so is his shade here furious.
49|How many now hold themselves up there great kings
50|who here will stay like pigs in the mire,
51|leaving of themselves horrible contempt!".
52|And I: "Master, I would be very eager
53|to see him ducked in this broth
54|before we went out of the lake".
55|And he to me: "Before the shore
56|lets itself be seen by you, you will be satisfied:
57|of such a desire it is fitting that you enjoy".
58|A little after that I saw such a rending
59|made of this one by the muddy people,
60|that for it I still praise and thank God.
61|All were shouting: "At Filippo Argenti!";
62|and the Florentine spirit, irascible,
63|turned on himself with his teeth.
64|There we left him, so that I tell no more of him;
65|but in my ears there struck me a wailing,
66|wherefore I open wide forward my intent eye.
67|The good master said: "Now, son,
68|draws near the city that has the name Dis,
69|with the grave citizens, with the great host".
70|And I: "Master, already its mosques
71|there within clearly in the valley I discern,
72|vermilion as if out of fire come
73|they were". And he said to me: "The eternal fire
74|that inside burns them shows them red,
75|as you see in this lower hell".
76|We came at last within the deep moats
77|that fortify that disconsolate land:
78|the walls seemed to me as if they were iron.
79|Not without first making a great circuit,
80|we came to a part where the boatman loudly
81|"Get out", shouted: "here is the entrance".
82|I saw more than a thousand upon the gates
83|from heaven rained down, who angrily
84|were saying: "Who is this one who without death
85|goes through the kingdom of the dead people?".
86|And my wise master made a sign
87|of wanting to speak to them secretly.
88|Then they shut in a little their great disdain
89|and said: "You come alone, and let that one go away
90|who so daringly entered through this kingdom.
91|Alone let him return along the mad road:
92|let him try, if he knows how; for you will remain here,
93|who have escorted him through so dark a region".
94|Think, reader, if I was discomforted
95|at the sound of the cursed words,
96|for I did not believe I would ever return here.
97|"O my dear leader, who more than seven
98|times have given me back safety and drawn me
99|from deep peril that stood against me,
100|do not leave me", I said, "so undone;
101|and if the passing further on is denied us,
102|let us retrace our footsteps together quickly".
103|And that lord who had led me there,
104|said to me: "Do not fear; for our passage
105|no one can take from us: by such a one is it given us.
106|But wait for me here, and your weary spirit
107|comfort and feed with good hope,
108|for I will not leave you in the low world".
109|So he goes away, and there abandons me
110|the sweet father, and I remain in doubt,
111|for yes and no in my head contend.
112|I could not hear what he offered them;
113|but he did not stay there with them long,
114|for each one inside ran back in haste.
115|They closed the gates, those adversaries of ours,
116|in the chest of my lord, who remained outside
117|and turned back to me with slow steps.
118|His eyes on the ground and his brows he had shaved
119|of all boldness, and he said amid sighs:
120|"Who has denied me the sorrowful houses!".
121|And to me he said: "You, because I am angered,
122|do not be dismayed, for I will win the trial,
123|whoever inside may bustle about for the defense.
124|This insolence of theirs is not new;
125|for they already used it at a less secret gate,
126|which without bolt is still found.
127|Over it you saw the dead writing:
128|and already on this side of it descends the slope,
129|passing through the circles without escort,
130|such a one that by him the city will be opened to us".
