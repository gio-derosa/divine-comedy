1|Since the love of my native place
2|constrained me, I gathered the scattered leaves
3|and gave them back to him, who was already faint.
4|Then we came to the border where is divided
5|the second ring from the third, and where
6|is seen a horrible art of justice.
7|To make well manifest the new things,
8|I say that we arrived at a plain
9|which from its bed removes every plant.
10|The sorrowful forest is a garland to it
11|around, as the sad moat is to that;
12|there we stopped our steps at the very edge.
13|The ground was a sand arid and thick,
14|not made in another fashion than that
15|which was once trodden by the feet of Cato.
16|O vengeance of God, how much you must
17|be feared by each one who reads
18|what was manifest to my eyes!
19|Of naked souls I saw many herds
20|who were all weeping very miserably,
21|and there seemed imposed on them a different law.
22|Supine some people were lying on the ground,
23|some were sitting all huddled up,
24|and others were walking continuously.
25|Those who went around were the more numerous,
26|and those fewer who lay in the torment,
27|but they had their tongue more loosened to lament.
28|Over all the sand, with a slow falling,
29|were raining broad flakes of fire,
30|as of snow in the mountains without wind.
31|As Alexander in those hot parts
32|of India saw upon his host
33|flames falling solid to the ground,
34|wherefore he provided for trampling the soil
35|with his troops, so that the vapor
36|might be better extinguished while it was alone:
37|so was descending the eternal burning;
38|whereby the sand was kindled, like tinder
39|under flint, to double the pain.
40|Without rest ever was the dance
41|of the miserable hands, now there now here
42|shaking off from themselves the fresh burning.
43|I began: "Master, you who overcome
44|all things, except the hard demons
45|who at the entering of the gate came out against us,
46|who is that great one who does not seem to care about
47|the fire and lies disdainful and scowling,
48|so that the rain does not seem to torment him?".
49|And that same one, who had perceived
50|that I was asking my leader about him,
51|cried: "What I was alive, such I am dead.
52|Though Jove weary his smith from whom
53|angered he took the sharp thunderbolt
54|with which on the last day I was struck;
55|or though he weary the others in turn after turn
56|in Mongibello at the black forge,
57|calling 'Good Vulcan, help, help!',
58|just as he did at the fight of Phlegra,
59|and shoot at me with all his force:
60|he could not have from it glad vengeance".
61|Then my leader spoke with force
62|so much, that I had not heard him so loud:
63|"O Capaneus, in that is not quenched
64|your pride, you are more punished;
65|no torment, except your rage,
66|would be to your fury a pain complete".
67|Then he turned back to me with a better face,
68|saying: "That one was one of the seven kings
69|who besieged Thebes; and he had and seems to have
70|God in disdain, and little it seems that he prizes him;
71|but, as I said to him, his scorns
72|are for his chest quite fitting ornaments.
73|Now come behind me, and see that you do not put,
74|yet, your feet on the burning sand;
75|but always keep your feet close to the wood".
76|Silent we came there where gushes
77|out of the forest a little stream,
78|whose redness still makes me shudder.
79|As from the Bulicame comes out a brook
80|which the sinful women then share among themselves,
81|so through the sand down went that one.
82|Its bottom and both the slopes
83|were made of stone, and the margins at the side;
84|wherefore I perceived that the passage was there.
85|"Among all the rest that I have shown you,
86|since we entered through the gate
87|whose threshold is denied to no one,
88|nothing has been discerned by your eyes
89|as notable as is the present river,
90|which above itself quenches all flames".
91|These words were of my leader;
92|wherefore I begged him that he grant me the food
93|for which he had granted me the desire.
94|"In the middle of the sea sits a ruined land",
95|he said then, "which is called Crete,
96|under whose king the world was once chaste.
97|A mountain is there which once was glad
98|with water and with leaves, which was called Ida;
99|now it is deserted like an outworn thing.
100|Rhea chose it once for a trusty cradle
101|of her son, and to hide him better,
102|when he cried, she had shouts made there.
103|Inside the mountain stands upright a great old man,
104|who keeps his shoulders turned toward Damietta
105|and looks at Rome as at his mirror.
106|His head is formed of fine gold,
107|and pure silver are the arms and the chest,
108|then he is of copper as far as the fork;
109|from there downward he is all choice iron,
110|except that the right foot is baked clay;
111|and he stands on that one, more than on the other, erect.
112|Each part, except the gold, is broken
113|by a fissure that drips tears,
114|which, gathered, pierce that cave.
115|Their course into this valley falls from rock to rock;
116|they make Acheron, Styx and Phlegethon;
117|then they go away down through this narrow channel,
118|until, there where one descends no more,
119|they make Cocytus; and what that pool is
120|you will see it, therefore here it is not told".
121|And I to him: "If the present rivulet
122|derives thus from our world,
123|why does it appear to us only at this border?".
124|And he to me: "You know that the place is round;
125|and although you have come far,
126|always to the left, going down toward the bottom,
127|you have not yet turned through the whole circle;
128|wherefore, if something new appears to us,
129|it should not bring wonder to your face".
130|And I again: "Master, where is found
131|Phlegethon and Lethe? for of the one you are silent,
132|and the other you say is made by this rain".
133|"In all your questions surely you please me",
134|he answered, "but the boiling of the red water
135|should well solve the one that you make.
136|Lethe you will see, but outside this pit,
137|there where the souls go to wash themselves
138|when the repented guilt is removed".
139|Then he said: "Now it is time to move away
140|from the wood; see that you come behind me:
141|the margins make a way, for they are not burned,
142|and above them every vapor is quenched".
