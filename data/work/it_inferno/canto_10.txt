1|Now goes away along a secret path,
2|between the wall of the city and the torments,
3|my master, and I behind his shoulders.
4|"O highest virtue, who through the impious circles
5|turn me", I began, "as it pleases you,
6|speak to me, and satisfy me in my desires.
7|The people who lie in the sepulchres
8|could they be seen? already are raised
9|all the lids, and no one keeps guard".
10|And he to me: "All will be locked
11|when from Jehoshaphat they will return here
12|with the bodies that they have left up there.
13|Their cemetery on this side have
14|with Epicurus all his followers,
15|who make the soul dead with the body.
16|Therefore to the question that you make me
17|in here you will be satisfied soon,
18|and also to the desire which you keep silent from me".
19|And I: "Good leader, I do not keep hidden
20|from you my heart except in order to speak little,
21|and you have not only now disposed me to that".
22|"O Tuscan who through the city of fire
23|go your way alive, speaking thus decorously,
24|may it please you to stop in this place.
25|Your speech makes you manifest
26|as native of that noble fatherland,
27|to which perhaps I was too troublesome".
28|Suddenly this sound came out
29|of one of the chests; therefore I drew near,
30|fearing, a little more to my leader.
31|And he said to me: "Turn around! What are you doing?
32|See there Farinata who has stood upright:
33|from the waist up you will see him whole".
34|I had already my gaze fixed on his;
35|and he was rising up with his chest and with his brow
36|as if he held hell in great contempt.
37|And the spirited and ready hands of the leader
38|pushed me among the sepulchres to him,
39|saying: "Let your words be measured".
40|As soon as I was at the foot of his tomb,
41|he looked at me a little, and then, almost disdainful,
42|he asked me: "Who were your ancestors?".
43|I, who was desirous to obey,
44|did not hide it from him, but opened it all to him;
45|whereupon he raised his brows a little upward;
46|then he said: "Fiercely were they adverse
47|to me and to my forebears and to my party,
48|so that two times I scattered them".
49|"If they were chased out, they returned from every side",
50|I answered him, "the one and the other time;
51|but yours did not learn well that art".
52|Then there rose at the uncovered opening
53|a shade, beside this one, up to the chin:
54|I believe that it had risen on its knees.
55|It looked around me, as if desire
56|it had to see whether another was with me;
57|and after the suspecting was all quenched,
58|weeping it said: "If through this blind
59|prison you go by height of genius,
60|my son where is he? and why is he not with you?".
61|And I to him: "Of myself I do not come:
62|he who waits there, leads me through here,
63|perhaps to her whom your Guido held in disdain".
64|His words and the manner of the punishment
65|had already read to me the name of this one;
66|therefore was the answer so full.
67|Suddenly straightened up he cried: "How?
68|did you say 'he held'? does he not live still?
69|does the sweet light not strike his eyes?".
70|When he perceived some delay
71|that I was making before the answer,
72|supine he fell back and did not appear outside anymore.
73|But that other magnanimous one, at whose request
74|I had stopped, did not change aspect,
75|nor move his neck, nor bend his side;
76|and continuing himself on his first speech,
77|"If they have that art", he said, "badly learned,
78|that torments me more than this bed.
79|But not fifty times will be rekindled
80|the face of the lady who rules here,
81|before you will know how much that art weighs.
82|And so may you ever return to the sweet world,
83|tell me: why is that people so pitiless
84|against mine in each of its laws?".
85|Whereupon I to him: "The slaughter and the great havoc
86|which made the Arbia colored in red,
87|cause such prayer to be made in our temple".
88|After he had, sighing, shaken his head,
89|"In that I was not alone", he said, "nor surely
90|without cause would I have moved with the others.
91|But I was alone, there where it was agreed
92|by everyone to do away with Florence,
93|the one who defended her with open face".
94|"Ah, so may your seed ever find rest",
95|I prayed him, "untie for me that knot
96|which here has entangled my judgment.
97|It seems that you see, if I hear well,
98|beforehand that which time brings with it,
99|and in the present you hold another manner".
100|"We see, like one who has bad light,
101|the things", he said, "that are far from us;
102|so much still does the highest leader shine on us.
103|When they draw near or are, all empty is
104|our intellect; and if another does not bring us news,
105|we know nothing of your human state.
106|Therefore you can comprehend that wholly dead
107|will be our knowledge from that point
108|when of the future the door will be closed".
109|Then, as if stung by my fault,
110|I said: "Now you will tell then to that fallen one
111|that his son is still joined with the living;
112|and if I was, before, to the answer mute,
113|make him know that I did it because I was thinking
114|already in the error which you have solved for me".
115|And already my master was calling me back;
116|wherefore I begged the spirit more hastily
117|that he tell me who was with him.
118|He said to me: "Here with more than a thousand I lie:
119|in here is the second Frederick
120|and the Cardinal; and of the others I am silent".
121|Then he hid himself; and I toward the ancient
122|poet turned my steps, thinking again
123|of that speech which seemed hostile to me.
124|He moved; and then, going thus,
125|he said to me: "Why are you so bewildered?".
126|And I satisfied him in his question.
127|"Let your mind preserve what you have heard
128|against yourself", that sage commanded me;
129|"and now attend here", and he raised his finger:
130|"when you will be before the sweet ray
131|of her whose beautiful eye sees all,
132|from her you will learn of your life the journey".
133|After that he moved his foot to the left hand:
134|we left the wall and went toward the middle
135|by a path that strikes into a valley,
136|which even up there made its stench displeasing.
