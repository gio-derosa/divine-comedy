1|Thus I descended from the first circle
2|down into the second, which girds less space
3|and so much more pain, which goads to wailing.
4|There stands Minos horribly, and snarls:
5|he examines the faults at the entrance;
6|he judges and sends according as he entwines.
7|I say that when the ill-born soul
8|comes before him, it confesses itself entirely;
9|and that discerner of the sins
10|sees what place of hell is for it;
11|he girds himself with his tail as many times
12|as the grades he wants that it be put down.
13|Always before him there stand many of them:
14|they go by turns each one to the judgment,
15|they speak and hear and then are turned down.
16|"O you who come to the sorrowful hospice",
17|said Minos to me when he saw me,
18|leaving the act of so great an office,
19|"watch how you enter and in whom you trust;
20|let not the width of the entering deceive you!".
21|And my leader to him: "Why do you still cry out?
22|Do not impede his fated going:
23|it is willed so there where one can do
24|what one wills, and ask no more".
25|Now begin the sorrowful notes
26|to make themselves heard by me; now I have come
27|there where much weeping strikes me.
28|I came into a place mute of all light,
29|which bellows as the sea does in a tempest,
30|if by contrary winds it is fought.
31|The infernal hurricane, which never rests,
32|leads the spirits with its rapine;
33|turning and striking it molests them.
34|When they arrive before the ruin,
35|there the shrieks, the moaning, the lament;
36|they blaspheme there the divine power.
37|I understood that to such a torment
38|are damned the carnal sinners,
39|who subject reason to desire.
40|And as their wings carry the starlings
41|in the cold time, in a band broad and full,
42|so that blast the evil spirits
43|here, there, down, up it leads them;
44|no hope comforts them ever,
45|not of rest, but of lesser pain.
46|And as the cranes go singing their lays,
47|making in the air of themselves a long line,
48|so I saw coming, uttering wails,
49|shades carried by the said strife;
50|wherefore I said: "Master, who are those
51|people whom the black air so chastises?".
52|"The first of those of whom news
53|you want to know", he said to me then,
54|"was empress of many tongues.
55|To the vice of lust she was so abandoned,
56|that she made lust licit in her law,
57|to take away the blame into which she was led.
58|She is Semiramis, of whom one reads
59|that she succeeded Ninus and was his wife:
60|she held the land that the Sultan rules.
61|The other is she who killed herself, loving,
62|and broke faith with the ashes of Sychaeus;
63|then is Cleopatra lustful.
64|See Helen, for whom so much evil
65|time revolved, and see the great Achilles,
66|who with love at the end fought.
67|See Paris, Tristan"; and more than a thousand
68|shades he showed me and named me with his finger,
69|whom love from our life had parted.
70|After I had heard my teacher
71|name the ancient ladies and the knights,
72|pity came over me, and I was almost bewildered.
73|I began: "Poet, willingly
74|I would speak to those two who go together,
75|and seem so light upon the wind".
76|And he to me: "You will see when they will be
77|closer to us; and you then entreat them
78|by that love which leads them, and they will come".
79|As soon as the wind bends them to us,
80|I raised my voice: "O weary souls,
81|come to speak to us, if another does not forbid it!".
82|As doves by desire called
83|with wings raised and steady to the sweet nest
84|come through the air, by the will carried;
85|such came out of the band where Dido is,
86|coming to us through the malignant air,
87|so strong was the affectionate cry.
88|"O living being gracious and benign
89|who go visiting through the dark air
90|us who stained the world with blood,
91|if the king of the universe were a friend,
92|we would pray him for your peace,
93|since you have pity on our perverse ill.
94|Of what to hear and to speak pleases you,
95|we will hear and will speak to you,
96|while the wind, as it does, is silent for us.
97|The land where I was born sits
98|on the shore where the Po descends
99|to have peace with its followers.
100|Love, which in the gentle heart quickly takes hold,
101|seized this one with the beautiful person
102|that was taken from me; and the manner still offends me.
103|Love, which exempts no one loved from loving,
104|seized me with this one's charm so strongly,
105|that, as you see, it still does not abandon me.
106|Love led us to one death.
107|Caina awaits him who quenched our life".
108|These words were offered us by them.
109|When I heard those offended souls,
110|I bowed my face, and so long I held it low,
111|until the poet said to me: "What are you thinking?".
112|When I answered, I began: "Oh alas,
113|how many sweet thoughts, how much desire
114|led these ones to the sorrowful pass!".
115|Then I turned back to them and I spoke,
116|and I began: "Francesca, your torments
117|to weeping make me sad and compassionate.
118|But tell me: at the time of the sweet sighs,
119|by what and how did love grant
120|that you knew the doubtful desires?".
121|And she to me: "No greater sorrow
122|than to remember the happy time
123|in misery; and that your teacher knows.
124|But if to know the first root
125|of our love you have so much longing,
126|I will do as one who weeps and speaks.
127|We were reading one day for delight
128|of Lancelot, how love bound him;
129|alone we were and without any suspicion.
130|Many times our eyes were urged together
131|by that reading, and it drained our face of color;
132|but only one point was that which vanquished us.
133|When we read of the desired smile
134|being kissed by so great a lover,
135|this one, who never from me will be divided,
136|kissed my mouth all trembling.
137|A Gallehault was the book and he who wrote it:
138|that day we read in it no further".
139|While the one spirit said this,
140|the other was weeping; so that from pity
141|I fainted just as if I were dying.
142|And I fell as a dead body falls.
