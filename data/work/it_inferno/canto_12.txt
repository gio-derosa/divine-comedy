1|The place where to descend the bank
2|we came was alpine and, because of what was there besides,
3|such that every sight would shun it.
4|As is that landslide which in the flank
5|on this side of Trento struck the Adige,
6|either by earthquake or by failing support,
7|so that from the top of the mountain, whence it moved,
8|to the plain the rock is so tumbled down,
9|that it would give some way to one who was above:
10|such was the descent of that ravine;
11|and on the edge of the broken chasm
12|the infamy of Crete was stretched out
13|which was conceived in the false cow;
14|and when he saw us, he bit himself,
15|like one whom anger breaks within.
16|My sage toward him cried: "Perhaps
17|you believe that here is the duke of Athens,
18|who up in the world dealt you death?
19|Depart, beast, for this one does not come
20|instructed by your sister,
21|but he goes to see your punishments".
22|As is that bull which breaks loose at the moment
23|that it has received already the mortal blow,
24|which cannot go, but here and there leaps,
25|I saw the Minotaur do likewise;
26|and that wary one cried: "Run to the passage;
27|while he is in fury, it is good that you go down".
28|So we took our way down over the heap
29|of those stones, which often moved
30|under my feet because of the new load.
31|I went thinking; and he said: "You are thinking
32|perhaps of this ruin, which is guarded
33|by that bestial anger which I just quenched.
34|Now I want you to know that the other time
35|that I descended down here into the lower hell,
36|this rock had not yet fallen.
37|But certainly a little before, if I discern well,
38|that he came who the great prey
39|took away from Dis from the uppermost circle,
40|on all sides the deep foul valley
41|trembled so, that I thought that the universe
42|felt love, by which there are some who believe
43|the world has many times been turned into chaos;
44|and at that moment this old rock,
45|here and elsewhere, made such an overturning.
46|But fix your eyes below, for there approaches
47|the river of blood in which boils
48|whoever by violence harms others".
49|Oh blind greed and mad anger,
50|which so spur us in the short life,
51|and in the eternal then so evilly steep us!
52|I saw a wide moat bent in an arc,
53|as one that embraces the whole plain,
54|according to what my escort had said;
55|and between the foot of the bank and it, in file
56|were running centaurs, armed with arrows,
57|as they used in the world to go hunting.
58|Seeing us come down, each one stopped,
59|and from the band three departed
60|with bows and shafts first chosen;
61|and one cried from far away: "To what torment
62|are you coming, you who descend the slope?
63|Say it from there; if not, I draw the bow".
64|My master said: "The answer
65|we will make to Chiron there close by:
66|ill for you was your will always so hasty".
67|Then he touched me, and said: "That one is Nessus,
68|who died for the beautiful Deianira,
69|and made for himself the vengeance he himself.
70|And that one in the middle, who gazes at his chest,
71|is the great Chiron, who nursed Achilles;
72|that other is Pholus, who was so full of anger.
73|Around the moat they go by thousands and thousands,
74|shooting whatever soul pulls itself out
75|of the blood more than its guilt has allotted it".
76|We drew near to those swift beasts:
77|Chiron took an arrow, and with the notch
78|he put his beard back from his jaws.
79|When he had uncovered his great mouth,
80|he said to his companions: "Are you aware
81|that the one behind moves what he touches?
82|So the feet of the dead are not accustomed to do".
83|And my good leader, who was already at his chest,
84|where the two natures are consorted,
85|answered: "Indeed he is alive, and so all alone
86|it is fitting for me to show him the dark valley;
87|necessity leads him here, and not delight.
88|Such a one departed from singing alleluia
89|who committed to me this new office:
90|he is not a robber, nor I a thieving soul.
91|But by that power through which I move
92|my steps along so savage a road,
93|give us one of yours, to whom we may be close,
94|and who may show us where one fords,
95|and who may carry this one on his back,
96|for he is not a spirit who goes through the air".
97|Chiron turned upon his right breast,
98|and said to Nessus: "Go back, and so guide them,
99|and make them turn aside if another band encounters you".
100|Now we moved with the trusty escort
101|along the edge of the vermilion boiling,
102|where the boiled ones were making loud shrieks.
103|I saw people under up to the brow;
104|and the great centaur said: "They are tyrants
105|who laid hold on blood and on possessions.
106|Here they lament their pitiless harms;
107|here is Alexander, and fierce Dionysius
108|who made Sicily have sorrowful years.
109|And that forehead which has the hair so black,
110|is Azzolino; and that other who is blond,
111|is Obizzo of Este, who in truth
112|was killed by his stepson up in the world".
113|Then I turned to the poet, and he said:
114|"Let this one now be first to you, and I second".
115|A little further on the centaur stopped
116|over a people who up to the throat
117|seemed to come out of that boiling stream.
118|He showed us a shade on one side alone,
119|saying: "That one cleft in the bosom of God
120|the heart that on the Thames is still honored".
121|Then I saw people who out of the river
122|held the head and also all the chest;
123|and of these I recognized many.
124|Thus more and more became shallow
125|that blood, so that it cooked only the feet;
126|and there was our passage of the moat.
127|"Just as you on this side see
128|the boiling stream that always diminishes",
129|said the centaur, "I want you to believe
130|that on this other more and more down presses
131|its bottom, until it rejoins
132|where tyranny must groan.
133|The divine justice here goads
134|that Attila who was a scourge on earth,
135|and Pyrrhus and Sextus; and in eternity milks
136|the tears, which with the boiling it unlocks,
137|from Rinier of Corneto, from Rinier Pazzo,
138|who made on the roads so much war".
139|Then he turned back and crossed again the ford.
