1|Of new punishment I must make verses
2|and give matter to the twentieth canto
3|of the first canticle, which is of the submerged.
4|I was already disposed entirely
5|to look into the uncovered bottom,
6|which was bathed with anguished weeping;
7|and I saw people through the round valley
8|coming, silent and weeping, at the pace
9|that the litanies make in this world.
10|As my sight descended lower on them,
11|each appeared marvelously to be twisted
12|between the chin and the beginning of the chest,
13|for toward the loins the face was turned,
14|and backward it was fitting for them to come,
15|because the seeing ahead was taken from them.
16|Perhaps by force of palsy once
17|someone was twisted thus completely;
18|but I have not seen it, nor do I believe it is.
19|So may God let you, reader, take fruit
20|from your reading, now think for yourself
21|how I could keep my face dry,
22|when our image close by
23|I saw so twisted, that the weeping of the eyes
24|bathed the buttocks along the cleft.
25|Truly I was weeping, leaning on one of the rocks
26|of the hard crag, so that my escort
27|said to me: "Are you too among the other fools?
28|Here pity lives when it is quite dead;
29|who is more wicked than he
30|who brings passion to the divine judgment?
31|Raise your head, raise it, and see him for whom
32|the earth opened before the eyes of the Thebans;
33|whereupon they all were shouting: 'Where are you rushing,
34|Amphiaraus? why do you leave the war?'.
35|And he did not stop rushing down
36|as far as Minos who seizes each one.
37|Look how he has made a chest of his shoulders;
38|because he wanted to see too far ahead,
39|he looks behind and makes a backward path.
40|See Tiresias, who changed semblance
41|when from male he became female,
42|his limbs changing all of them;
43|and first, afterward, he had to strike again
44|the two entwined serpents, with the rod,
45|before he could have again his male plumes.
46|Aruns is he who to his belly backs up,
47|who in the mountains of Luni, where hoes
48|the man of Carrara who lodges below,
49|had among the white marbles the cave
50|for his dwelling; whence to watch the stars
51|and the sea his view was not cut off.
52|And she who covers her breasts,
53|which you do not see, with her loose tresses,
54|and has on that side all her hairy skin,
55|was Manto, who searched through many lands;
56|then she settled there where I was born;
57|whereof it pleases me a little that you listen to me.
58|After her father went out of life
59|and the city of Bacchus became a slave,
60|she went a long time through the world.
61|Up in beautiful Italy lies a lake,
62|at the foot of the Alps which lock in Germany
63|above Tyrol, which has the name Benaco.
64|By a thousand springs, I believe, and more is bathed
65|between Garda and Val Camonica and Pennino
66|with the water that stagnates in the said lake.
67|There is a place in the middle where the Trentine
68|shepherd and he of Brescia and the Veronese
69|could bless, if he made that journey.
70|There sits Peschiera, beautiful and strong fortress
71|to face the Brescians and the Bergamasques,
72|where the shore around is lowest.
73|There it is fitting that all fall
74|that which in the lap of Benaco cannot stay,
75|and it becomes a river down through green pastures.
76|As soon as the water begins to run,
77|no longer Benaco, but Mincio it is called
78|as far as Governolo, where it falls into the Po.
79|Not far has it run, when it finds a lowland,
80|in which it spreads out and makes it a marsh;
81|and it is accustomed in summer sometimes to be noxious.
82|Passing there the cruel virgin
83|saw land, in the middle of the swamp,
84|without cultivation and bare of inhabitants.
85|There, to flee all human society,
86|she stayed with her servants to practice her arts,
87|and lived, and left there her empty body.
88|The men then who were scattered around
89|gathered at that place, which was strong
90|because of the swamp that it had on all sides.
91|They made the city over those dead bones;
92|and for her who first chose the place,
93|they called it Mantua without other lot.
94|Once were its people within more numerous,
95|before the folly of Casalodi
96|received deceit from Pinamonte.
97|Therefore I instruct you that, if you ever hear
98|my city originate otherwise,
99|let no lie defraud the truth".
100|And I: "Master, your reasonings
101|are so certain to me and take so my faith,
102|that the others would be to me extinguished coals.
103|But tell me, of the people who proceed,
104|if you see any worthy of note;
105|for only on that my mind strikes back".
106|Then he said to me: "That one who from his cheek
107|stretches his beard onto his dark shoulders,
108|was - when Greece was empty of males,
109|so that they hardly remained in the cradles -
110|an augur, and gave the moment with Calchas
111|in Aulis to cut the first cable.
112|Eurypylus was his name, and so sings him
113|my high tragedy in a certain place:
114|well you know it, who know it all.
115|That other who in the flanks is so slight,
116|was Michael Scot, who truly
117|of magical frauds knew the game.
118|See Guido Bonatti; see Asdente,
119|who to have attended to the leather and to the thread
120|now would wish, but late he repents.
121|See the wretched women who left the needle,
122|the shuttle and the spindle, and made themselves diviners;
123|they made spells with herbs and with images.
124|But come now, for already holds the border
125|of both the hemispheres and touches the wave
126|below Seville Cain and the thorns;
127|and already last night the moon was round:
128|well you should remember it, for it did not harm you
129|at times in the deep forest".
130|So he spoke to me, and we went on meanwhile.
