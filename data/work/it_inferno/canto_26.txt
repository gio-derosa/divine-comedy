1|Rejoice, Florence, since you are so great
2|that over sea and over land you beat your wings,
3|and through hell your name spreads!
4|Among the thieves I found five such
5|citizens of yours, whereby shame comes to me,
6|and you do not rise to great honor from it.
7|But if near morning one dreams of the truth,
8|you will feel, before long,
9|what Prato, not to speak of others, craves for you.
10|And if it were already, it would not be too early.
11|Would that it were, since indeed it must be!
12|for it will weigh on me more, the more I age.
13|We departed, and up along the stairs
14|which the jutting stones had made for us to descend before,
15|my leader climbed back and drew me;
16|and pursuing the solitary way,
17|among the splinters and among the rocks of the crag
18|the foot without the hand did not make its way.
19|Then I grieved, and now I grieve again
20|when I direct my mind to what I saw,
21|and I rein in my genius more than I am accustomed,
22|so that it may not run where virtue does not guide it;
23|so that, if a good star or a better thing
24|has given me the good, I myself may not begrudge it to myself.
25|As many as the peasant who rests on the hill,
26|in the time when he who lights the world
27|keeps his face less hidden from us,
28|when the fly yields to the mosquito,
29|sees fireflies down along the valley,
30|perhaps there where he harvests grapes and plows:
31|with so many flames all was shining
32|the eighth pouch, as I perceived
33|as soon as I was there where the bottom appeared.
34|And as he who avenged himself with the bears
35|saw the chariot of Elijah at its departing,
36|when the horses rose steeply to heaven,
37|for he could not so follow it with his eyes,
38|that he saw anything but the flame alone,
39|like a little cloud, rising upward:
40|so each moves along the gullet
41|of the ditch, for none shows the theft,
42|and every flame steals away a sinner.
43|I was standing on the bridge, risen up to see,
44|so that if I had not taken hold of a rock,
45|I would have fallen down without being pushed.
46|And the leader, who saw me so intent,
47|said: "Within the fires are the spirits;
48|each swathes himself with that by which he is burned".
49|"My master", I answered, "by hearing you
50|I am more certain; but already it seemed to me
51|that it was so, and already I wanted to say to you:
52|who is in that fire which comes so divided
53|at the top, that it seems to rise from the pyre
54|where Eteocles with his brother was placed?".
55|He answered me: "In there are tormented
56|Ulysses and Diomedes, and so together
57|they go to vengeance as they went to wrath;
58|and within their flame is lamented
59|the ambush of the horse which made the gate
60|from which came out the noble seed of the Romans.
61|Within it is wept the art whereby, dead,
62|Deidamia still grieves for Achilles,
63|and for the Palladium punishment is borne there".
64|"If they can within those sparks
65|speak", I said, "master, much I pray you
66|and pray again, so that the prayer may be worth a thousand,
67|that you do not make me denial of waiting
68|until the horned flame comes here;
69|see that from desire I bend toward it!".
70|And he to me: "Your prayer is worthy
71|of much praise, and I therefore accept it;
72|but see that your tongue restrains itself.
73|Let me speak, for I have conceived
74|what you want; for they would be disdainful,
75|because they were Greeks, perhaps of your speech".
76|After the flame had come there
77|where it seemed to my leader the time and place,
78|in this form I heard him speak:
79|"O you who are two within one fire,
80|if I deserved of you while I lived,
81|if I deserved of you much or little
82|when in the world I wrote the high verses,
83|do not move; but let one of you tell
84|where, through his own doing, lost he went to die".
85|The greater horn of the ancient flame
86|began to shake itself murmuring,
87|just like that which wind wearies;
88|then moving the tip here and there,
89|as if it were the tongue that spoke,
90|it threw out a voice and said: "When
91|I departed from Circe, who withheld
92|me more than a year there near Gaeta,
93|before Aeneas so named it,
94|neither sweetness of son, nor the piety
95|toward my old father, nor the due love
96|which should have made Penelope glad,
97|could conquer within me the ardor
98|that I had to become expert of the world
99|and of human vices and of worth;
100|but I put myself out on the deep open sea
101|with only one ship and with that company
102|small by which I was not deserted.
103|The one shore and the other I saw as far as Spain,
104|as far as Morocco, and the island of the Sardinians,
105|and the others which that sea bathes around.
106|I and my companions were old and slow
107|when we came to that narrow strait
108|where Hercules marked his boundary signs
109|so that man should not venture further;
110|on the right hand I left Seville,
111|on the other I had already left Ceuta.
112|'O brothers', I said, 'who through a hundred thousand
113|perils have arrived at the west,
114|to this so little vigil
115|of our senses which is of what remains
116|do not wish to deny the experience,
117|following the sun, of the world without people.
118|Consider your origin:
119|you were not made to live like brutes,
120|but to follow virtue and knowledge'.
121|My companions I made so keen,
122|with this little oration, for the journey,
123|that hardly afterward would I have held them back;
124|and having turned our stern toward the morning,
125|of the oars we made wings for the mad flight,
126|always gaining on the left side.
127|All the stars already of the other pole
128|the night saw, and ours so low,
129|that it did not rise out of the ocean floor.
130|Five times rekindled and as many quenched
131|was the light beneath the moon,
132|since we had entered on the deep passage,
133|when there appeared to us a mountain, dark
134|from the distance, and it seemed to me as high
135|as I had never seen any.
136|We rejoiced, and soon it turned to weeping;
137|for from the new land a whirlwind was born
138|and struck the ship's forward part.
139|Three times it made it whirl with all the waters;
140|at the fourth it made the stern lift up
141|and the prow go down, as pleased another,
142|until the sea was closed again over us".
