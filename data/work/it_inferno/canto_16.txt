1|Already I was in a place where one heard the roar
2|of the water that fell into the next circle,
3|similar to that hum which the beehives make,
4|when three shades together departed,
5|running, from a troop that was passing
6|under the rain of the harsh torment.
7|They came toward us, and each was crying:
8|"Stop, you who by your dress seem to us
9|to be someone from our depraved city".
10|Alas, what wounds I saw on their limbs,
11|recent and old, burned in by the flames!
12|It still pains me only that I remember it.
13|To their cries my teacher attended;
14|he turned his face toward me, and "Now wait",
15|he said, "to these one should be courteous.
16|And were it not for the fire that shoots
17|the nature of the place, I would say
18|that the haste would better suit you than them".
19|They began again, as we stopped,
20|the ancient verse; and when they had come to us,
21|they made a wheel of themselves all three.
22|As champions are accustomed to do, naked and oiled,
23|watching for their hold and their advantage,
24|before they are exchanging blows and thrusts,
25|so wheeling, each his face
26|directed to me, so that contrary to the neck
27|the feet made a continuous journey.
28|And "If the misery of this loose place
29|brings into contempt us and our prayers",
30|began one, "and the stained and peeled aspect,
31|let our fame incline your mind
32|to tell us who you are, who your living feet
33|so securely rub through hell.
34|This one, whose footprints you see me tread,
35|although he goes naked and hairless,
36|was of higher rank than you believe:
37|he was grandson of the good Gualdrada;
38|Guido Guerra was his name, and in his life
39|he did much with wisdom and with the sword.
40|The other, who behind me treads the sand,
41|is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose voice
42|in the world above should have been welcome.
43|And I, who am placed with them on the cross,
44|was Iacopo Rusticucci, and certainly
45|my fierce wife more than anything harms me".
46|If I had been sheltered from the fire,
47|I would have thrown myself among them below,
48|and I believe that the teacher would have allowed it;
49|but because I would have been burned and cooked,
50|fear overcame my good will
51|which made me greedy to embrace them.
52|Then I began: "Not contempt, but grief
53|your condition fixed within me,
54|so great that late will it be all stripped off,
55|as soon as this my lord said to me
56|words by which I thought
57|that such as you are, such people were coming.
58|Of your city I am, and always
59|your deeds and the honored names
60|with affection I have retold and heard.
61|I leave the gall and go for sweet fruits
62|promised to me by the truthful leader;
63|but as far as the center first I must fall".
64|"So may the soul long guide
65|your limbs", answered that one again,
66|"and so may your fame shine after you,
67|say whether courtesy and valor dwell
68|in our city as they used to,
69|or whether they have altogether gone out of it;
70|for Guglielmo Borsiere, who laments
71|with us but a short while and goes there with the companions,
72|greatly grieves us with his words".
73|"The new people and the sudden gains
74|have generated pride and excess,
75|Florence, in you, so that already you weep for it".
76|So I cried with my face lifted;
77|and the three, who took that for answer,
78|looked at each other as one gazes at the truth.
79|"If other times it costs you so little",
80|they all answered, "to satisfy others,
81|happy you if you speak thus at your pleasure!
82|Therefore, if you escape from these dark places
83|and return to see again the beautiful stars,
84|when it will please you to say 'I was',
85|see that you speak of us to people".
86|Then they broke the wheel, and in fleeing
87|their swift legs seemed wings.
88|An amen could not have been said
89|as quickly as they had vanished;
90|wherefore it seemed good to the master to depart.
91|I was following him, and we had gone a little,
92|when the sound of the water was so near us,
93|that for speaking we would hardly have been heard.
94|As that river which has its own course
95|first from Monte Viso toward the east,
96|on the left slope of the Apennine,
97|which is called Acquacheta above, before
98|it descends down into the low bed,
99|and at Forlì is empty of that name,
100|resounds there above San Benedetto
101|of the Alps by falling in one descent
102|where it should have been received by a thousand;
103|so, down from a steep bank,
104|we found resounding that dark water,
105|so that in a short time it would have harmed the ear.
106|I had a cord girded around me,
107|and with it I thought at one time
108|to catch the leopard with the painted skin.
109|After I had loosed it all from me,
110|just as the leader had commanded me,
111|I held it out to him knotted and coiled.
112|Whereupon he turned toward the right side,
113|and somewhat far from the edge
114|he threw it down into that deep ravine.
115|'It must be that something new answers',
116|I was saying within myself, 'the new signal
117|which the master so follows with his eye'.
118|Ah how cautious men should be
119|near those who do not see only the deed,
120|but look into the thoughts with their wisdom!
121|He said to me: "Soon will come up above
122|what I await and what your thought dreams;
123|soon must it be uncovered to your sight".
124|Always to that truth which has the face of a lie
125|a man should close his lips as long as he can,
126|because without fault it brings shame;
127|but here I cannot be silent about it; and by the notes
128|of this comedy, reader, I swear to you,
129|so may they not be empty of lasting favor,
130|that I saw through that air thick and dark
131|come swimming upward a figure,
132|marvelous to every steady heart,
133|just as he returns who goes down
134|at times to loosen the anchor that grapples
135|either a reef or something else that is hidden in the sea,
136|who stretches upward and draws in his feet.
