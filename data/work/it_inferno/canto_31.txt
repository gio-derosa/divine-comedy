1|One same tongue first bit me,
2|so that it tinged the one and the other cheek,
3|and then offered me the medicine again;
4|so I hear that the lance used to do,
5|of Achilles and of his father, to be the cause
6|first of a sad and then of a good gift.
7|We turned our back on the miserable valley
8|up along the bank which girds it around,
9|crossing without any speech.
10|There it was less than night and less than day,
11|so that my sight went little ahead;
12|but I heard sound a loud horn,
13|so much that it would have made every thunder faint,
14|which, following its way back against itself,
15|directed my eyes wholly to one place.
16|After the sorrowful rout, when
17|Charlemagne lost the holy company,
18|Roland did not sound so terribly.
19|Short while I kept my head turned there,
20|when it seemed to me I saw many high towers;
21|whereupon I: "Master, say, what city is this?".
22|And he to me: "Because you run
23|through the darkness too far off,
24|it happens that then in imagining you go astray.
25|You will see well, if you get there,
26|how much the sense is deceived from far away;
27|therefore spur yourself somewhat more".
28|Then dearly he took me by the hand
29|and said: "Before we are further ahead,
30|so that the fact may seem less strange to you,
31|know that they are not towers, but giants,
32|and they are in the well around the bank
33|from the navel downward all of them".
34|As when the fog dissipates,
35|the gaze little by little makes out
36|what the vapor conceals which crowds the air,
37|so piercing the air thick and dark,
38|more and more approaching toward the edge,
39|error fled from me and fear grew in me;
40|for, as upon its round circle of walls
41|Montereggioni crowns itself with towers,
42|so the brink which surrounds the well
43|was towered with half their person
44|by the horrible giants, whom Jove still
45|threatens from heaven when he thunders.
46|And I already discerned the face of one,
47|the shoulders and the chest and a great part of the belly,
48|and down along the sides both his arms.
49|Nature surely, when she left the art
50|of such animals, did very well
51|to take away such executors from Mars.
52|And if she of elephants and of whales
53|does not repent, whoever looks subtly,
54|holds her for it more just and more discerning;
55|for where the reasoning of the mind
56|is joined to evil will and to power,
57|people can make no defense there.
58|His face seemed to me long and thick
59|as the pine cone of Saint Peter at Rome,
60|and in its proportion were the other bones;
61|so that the bank, which was a loincloth
62|from the middle down, showed of him so much
63|above, that of reaching to his hair
64|three Frisians would have made a vain boast;
65|for I saw thirty great spans of him
66|from the place down where a man clasps his mantle.
67|"Raphèl maì amècche zabì almi",
68|began to shout the fierce mouth,
69|to which sweeter psalms were not fitting.
70|And my leader toward him: "Foolish soul,
71|keep to your horn, and with that vent yourself
72|when anger or other passion touches you!
73|Search at your neck, and you will find the strap
74|which holds it tied, O confused soul,
75|and see it which hoops your great chest".
76|Then he said to me: "He accuses himself;
77|this is Nimrod through whose evil thought
78|one single language is not used in the world.
79|Let us leave him be and not speak in vain;
80|for so is every language to him
81|as his to others, which is known to no one".
82|We made then a longer journey,
83|turned to the left; and at a crossbow's shot
84|we found the next one much fiercer and bigger.
85|To gird him whoever the master was,
86|I cannot say, but he held girt
87|in front the other and behind the right arm
88|with a chain which held him bound
89|from the neck down, so that on the uncovered part
90|it wound around as far as the fifth turn.
91|"This proud one wanted to make trial
92|of his power against the highest Jove",
93|said my leader, "whence he has such reward.
94|Ephialtes is his name, and he made the great attempts
95|when the giants made the gods afraid;
96|the arms that he plied, he never moves".
97|And I to him: "If it can be, I would wish
98|that of the immeasurable Briareus
99|my eyes might have experience".
100|Whereupon he answered: "You will see Antaeus
101|near here, who speaks and is unbound,
102|who will set us at the bottom of all guilt.
103|The one that you want to see is much further on
104|and is bound and made like this one,
105|except that he seems more ferocious in the face".
106|There was never an earthquake so violent,
107|that shook a tower so strongly,
108|as Ephialtes was quick to shake himself.
109|Then I feared death more than ever,
110|and there was no need of more than the fear,
111|if I had not seen the bonds.
112|We proceeded further on then,
113|and came to Antaeus, who a full five ells,
114|without the head, came out of the cavern.
115|"O you who in the fortunate valley
116|which made Scipio heir of glory,
117|when Hannibal with his men turned his back,
118|once brought a thousand lions as prey,
119|and who, if you had been at the high war
120|of your brothers, it still seems some believe
121|that the sons of the earth would have won:
122|set us down, and let it not be disdainful to you,
123|where the cold locks up Cocytus.
124|Do not make us go to Tityus nor to Typhon:
125|this one can give of that which is craved here;
126|therefore bend down and do not twist your snout.
127|He can still give you fame in the world,
128|for he lives, and long life he still expects
129|if grace does not call him to itself before his time".
130|So said the master; and that one in haste
131|stretched out his hands, and took my leader,
132|from which Hercules once felt a great grip.
133|Virgil, when he felt himself taken,
134|said to me: "Come here, so that I may take you";
135|then he made it so that one bundle were he and I.
136|As the Garisenda seems to one looking
137|from under the leaning side, when a cloud goes
138|over it so, that it hangs against it:
139|such seemed Antaeus to me who stood waiting
140|to see him bend, and it was such a moment
141|that I would have wanted to go by another road.
142|But lightly on the bottom which devours
143|Lucifer with Judas, he set us down;
144|nor, so bent, did he make delay there,
145|and like a mast on a ship he rose.
