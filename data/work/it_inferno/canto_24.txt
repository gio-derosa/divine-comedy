1|In that part of the youthful year
2|when the sun tempers its locks beneath Aquarius
3|and already the nights go off toward half the day,
4|when the hoarfrost copies on the ground
5|the image of her white sister,
6|but little lasts the temper of its pen,
7|the little peasant whose fodder is lacking,
8|rises, and looks, and sees the countryside
9|all whitening; whereupon he beats his hip,
10|returns into the house, and here and there laments,
11|like the wretch who does not know what to do;
12|then he comes back, and puts hope back in the basket,
13|seeing the world has changed face
14|in a little time, and he takes his crook
15|and drives out the little sheep to graze.
16|So the master made me dismayed
17|when I saw him so troubling his forehead,
18|and so quickly to the ill came the plaster;
19|for, as we came to the ruined bridge,
20|the leader turned to me with that look
21|sweet that I saw first at the foot of the mountain.
22|He opened his arms, after some counsel
23|chosen with himself, looking first
24|well at the ruin, and took hold of me.
25|And like one who works and estimates,
26|who always seems to provide beforehand,
27|so, lifting me up toward the top
28|of one great boulder, he was eyeing another splinter
29|saying: "Onto that one next grapple yourself;
30|but test first whether it is such that it will hold you".
31|It was not a way for one clothed in a cape,
32|for we hardly, he light and I pushed,
33|could climb up from ledge to ledge.
34|And were it not that on that precinct
35|more than on the other the slope was short,
36|I do not know about him, but I would have been quite vanquished.
37|But because Malebolge toward the opening
38|of the lowest well all slopes,
39|the site of each valley brings it
40|that one side rises and the other descends;
41|we came nevertheless at last onto the point
42|from which the last stone breaks off.
43|The breath was so milked from my lungs
44|when I was up, that I could go no further,
45|rather I sat down at the first arrival.
46|"Now it is fitting that you thus shake off sloth",
47|said the master; "for, sitting on down,
48|one does not come into fame, nor under covers;
49|without which whoever consumes his life,
50|such a trace of himself on earth leaves,
51|as smoke in air and in water the foam.
52|And therefore get up; conquer the breathlessness
53|with the mind which wins every battle,
54|if with its heavy body it does not sink down.
55|A longer stair it is necessary to climb;
56|it is not enough to have departed from these.
57|If you understand me, now make it so that it avails you".
58|Then I rose, showing myself furnished
59|better with breath than I felt myself,
60|and said: "Go, for I am strong and bold".
61|Up along the crag we took the way,
62|which was rugged, narrow and difficult,
63|and steep much more than that before.
64|Speaking I went so as not to seem feeble;
65|whereupon a voice came out of the next ditch,
66|unsuited to forming words.
67|I do not know what it said, although upon the back
68|I was already of the arch that crosses there;
69|but he who spoke seemed moved to anger.
70|I was turned downward, but the living eyes
71|could not go to the bottom because of the dark;
72|wherefore I: "Master, see that you arrive
73|at the next belt and let us descend the wall;
74|for, as I hear from here and do not understand,
75|so down I look and make out nothing".
76|"Other answer", he said, "I do not give you
77|than doing it; for the honest request
78|should be followed by the deed in silence".
79|We descended the bridge from the head
80|where it joins with the eighth bank,
81|and then the pouch was manifest to me:
82|and I saw within it a terrible mass
83|of serpents, and of such strange kind
84|that the memory still drains my blood.
85|Let Libya boast no more with her sand;
86|for if chelydri, jaculi and phareae
87|she produces, and cenchri with amphisbaena,
88|neither so many plagues nor so evil
89|did she ever show with all Ethiopia
90|nor with what is above the Red Sea.
91|Among this cruel and most dismal abundance
92|were running people naked and terrified,
93|without hope of a hole or heliotrope:
94|with serpents they had their hands tied behind;
95|these thrust through the loins the tail
96|and the head, and were knotted in front.
97|And behold at one who was by our bank,
98|a serpent darted which transfixed him
99|there where the neck is knotted to the shoulders.
100|Neither O so quickly nor I was ever written,
101|as he caught fire and burned, and all ash
102|it was fitting that falling he became;
103|and after he was on the ground so destroyed,
104|the dust gathered itself of itself
105|and into that same one returned suddenly.
106|So by the great sages it is professed
107|that the phoenix dies and then is reborn,
108|when to the five-hundredth year it approaches;
109|neither grass nor grain in its life does it feed on,
110|but only on tears of incense and of amomum,
111|and nard and myrrh are its last swaddling bands.
112|And as is he who falls, and does not know how,
113|by force of a demon that drags him to the ground,
114|or of another obstruction that binds a man,
115|when he rises, who gazes around himself
116|all bewildered by the great anguish
117|that he has suffered, and looking sighs:
118|such was the sinner risen afterward.
119|Oh power of God, how severe it is,
120|which showers such blows for vengeance!
121|The leader then asked him who he was;
122|whereupon he answered: "I rained from Tuscany,
123|a short time ago, into this fierce gullet.
124|Bestial life pleased me and not human,
125|like the mule that I was; I am Vanni Fucci
126|beast, and Pistoia was a worthy den to me".
127|And I to the leader: "Tell him not to slip away,
128|and ask what fault thrust him down here;
129|for I saw him a man of blood and of rages".
130|And the sinner, who heard, did not feign,
131|but directed toward me his mind and his face,
132|and with sad shame he painted himself;
133|then he said: "It grieves me more that you have caught me
134|in the misery where you see me,
135|than when I was taken from the other life.
136|I cannot deny what you ask;
137|I am put so far down because I was
138|a thief in the sacristy of the beautiful ornaments,
139|and falsely it was once imputed to another.
140|But so that you may not enjoy such a sight,
141|if ever you will be outside the dark places,
142|open your ears to my announcement, and hear.
143|Pistoia first is thinned of the Blacks;
144|then Florence renews people and ways.
145|Mars draws a vapor from Val di Magra
146|which is wrapped in turbid clouds;
147|and with a tempest impetuous and bitter
148|over Campo Piceno it will be fought;
149|whence it will suddenly rend the mist,
150|so that every White will be wounded by it.
151|And I have said it so that it must grieve you!".
