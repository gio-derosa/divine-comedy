1|There is a place in hell called Malebolge,
2|all of stone of iron color,
3|like the circle that turns around it.
4|In the very middle of the malign field
5|yawns a well quite wide and deep,
6|of which in its place I will tell the structure.
7|That belt which remains then is round
8|between the well and the foot of the high hard bank,
9|and has its bottom divided into ten valleys.
10|As, where for guard of the walls
11|more and more moats gird the castles,
12|the part where they are presents a figure,
13|such an image there those made;
14|and as at such fortresses from their thresholds
15|to the outer bank there are little bridges,
16|so from the base of the cliff crags
17|moved which cut across the banks and the ditches
18|as far as the well which truncates and gathers them.
19|In this place, shaken off from the back
20|of Geryon, we found ourselves; and the poet
21|held to the left, and I moved behind.
22|On the right hand I saw new anguish,
23|new torment and new scourgers,
24|with which the first pouch was replete.
25|At the bottom the sinners were naked;
26|from the middle on this side they came toward our face,
27|beyond, with us, but with greater steps,
28|as the Romans because of the great army,
29|in the year of the jubilee, over the bridge
30|have taken a way to make the people pass,
31|so that on one side all have their forehead
32|toward the castle and go to Saint Peter,
33|on the other side they go toward the mount.
34|Here, there, along the gloomy rock
35|I saw horned demons with great whips,
36|who beat them cruelly from behind.
37|Ah how they made them lift their heels
38|at the first blows! already none
39|waited for the second nor the third.
40|While I was going, my eyes on one
41|were met; and I at once said:
42|"Already of seeing this one I am not fasting".
43|Wherefore I stopped my feet to make him out;
44|and the sweet leader stopped with me,
45|and allowed that I go somewhat back.
46|And that whipped one believed he hid himself
47|by lowering his face; but it availed him little,
48|for I said: "O you who cast your eye to the ground,
49|if the features that you bear are not false,
50|you are Venedico Caccianemico.
51|But what brings you to such stinging sauces?".
52|And he to me: "Unwillingly I say it;
53|but your clear speech forces me,
54|which makes me remember the ancient world.
55|I was the one who Ghisolabella
56|led to do the will of the marquis,
57|however the foul story may sound.
58|And not only I weep here as a Bolognese;
59|rather this place is so full of them,
60|that not so many tongues are now taught
61|to say 'sipa' between Savena and Reno;
62|and if of this you want faith or testimony,
63|bring to mind our avaricious breast".
64|As he spoke thus a demon struck him
65|with his lash, and said: "Away,
66|pimp! here there are no women to coin".
67|I rejoined my escort;
68|then with a few steps we came
69|there where a crag came out of the bank.
70|Quite easily we climbed that;
71|and turned to the right along its ridge,
72|from those eternal circlings we departed.
73|When we were there where it yawns
74|underneath to give passage to the scourged,
75|the leader said: "Stop, and let strike
76|on you the sight of these other ill-born,
77|whose face you have not yet seen
78|because they have gone together with us".
79|From the old bridge we looked at the file
80|which was coming toward us from the other side,
81|and which the whip similarly drives.
82|And the good master, without my asking,
83|said to me: "Look at that great one who comes,
84|and for pain does not seem to shed tears:
85|how much regal aspect he still retains!
86|That one is Jason, who by heart and by wisdom
87|made the Colchians deprived of the ram.
88|He passed by the island of Lemnos
89|after the bold pitiless women
90|had given all their males to death.
91|There with signs and with ornate words
92|he deceived Hypsipyle, the young girl
93|who before had deceived all the others.
94|He left her there, pregnant, all alone;
95|such guilt condemns him to such torment;
96|and also for Medea vengeance is made.
97|With him go away those who deceive in such a way;
98|and let this suffice of the first valley
99|to know and of those whom it holds in its fangs".
100|Already we were there where the narrow path
101|crosses with the second bank,
102|and makes of that shoulders for another arch.
103|From there we heard people who whimper
104|in the next pouch and who snort with their snouts,
105|and themselves strike with their palms.
106|The banks were encrusted with a mold,
107|from the breath from below that sticks there,
108|which with the eyes and with the nose made a fight.
109|The bottom is so deep, that no place suffices us
110|to see without climbing to the back
111|of the arch, where the crag most overhangs.
112|There we came; and from there down in the ditch
113|I saw people plunged in an excrement
114|that seemed to have come from human privies.
115|And while I search down there with my eye,
116|I saw one with his head so filthy with shit,
117|that it did not appear whether he was layman or cleric.
118|He shouted at me: "Why are you so greedy
119|to look more at me than at the other filthy ones?".
120|And I to him: "Because, if I remember well,
121|I have seen you before with your hair dry,
122|and you are Alessio Interminei of Lucca:
123|therefore I eye you more than all the others".
124|And he then, beating his pumpkin:
125|"Down here have submerged me the flatteries
126|with which I never had my tongue cloyed".
127|After this the leader: "See that you push",
128|said to me, "your sight a little further forward,
129|so that you reach well with your eye the face
130|of that filthy and disheveled wench
131|who there scratches herself with shitty nails,
132|and now crouches and now is standing on her feet.
133|It is Thais, the whore who answered
134|her lover when he said 'Do I have thanks
135|great with you?': 'Rather marvelous ones!'.
136|And with this let our sights be satisfied".
