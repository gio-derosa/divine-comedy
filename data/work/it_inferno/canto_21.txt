1|So from bridge to bridge, speaking of other things
2|which my comedy does not care to sing,
3|we came; and we were holding the summit, when
4|we stopped to see the next cleft
5|of Malebolge and the other vain laments;
6|and I saw it marvelously dark.
7|As in the arsenal of the Venetians
8|boils in winter the sticky pitch
9|to caulk again their unsound vessels,
10|for they cannot sail - in place of that
11|one makes his ship new and one plugs
12|the ribs of that which made more voyages;
13|one hammers at the prow and one at the stern;
14|another makes oars and another twists ropes;
15|one patches the jib and the mainsail -:
16|so, not by fire but by divine art,
17|was boiling down there a thick pitch,
18|which smeared the bank on every side.
19|I saw it, but did not see in it
20|anything but the bubbles that the boiling raised,
21|and all of it swell, and settle again compressed.
22|While I was gazing fixedly down there,
23|my leader, saying "Look out, look out!",
24|drew me to himself from the place where I was standing.
25|Then I turned like the man who longs
26|to see that which it behooves him to flee
27|and whom sudden fear unmans,
28|so that, for seeing, he does not delay the departing:
29|and I saw behind us a black devil
30|come running up along the crag.
31|Ah how fierce he was in aspect!
32|and how cruel he seemed to me in bearing,
33|with his wings open and light upon his feet!
34|His shoulder, which was sharp and high,
35|a sinner loaded with both his haunches,
36|and he held gripped the sinew of his feet.
37|From our bridge he said: "O Malebranche,
38|behold one of the elders of Santa Zita!
39|Put him under, for I return for more
40|to that city, which is well furnished with them:
41|every man there is a barrator, except Bonturo;
42|of no, for money, one makes there yes".
43|Down there he threw him, and along the hard crag
44|he turned; and never was a mastiff unleashed
45|with so much haste to pursue the thief.
46|That one plunged, and came back up doubled;
47|but the demons who had cover from the bridge,
48|shouted: "Here the Holy Face has no place!
49|here one swims otherwise than in the Serchio!
50|Therefore, if you do not want our grapples,
51|do not make yourself stick above the pitch".
52|Then they bit him with more than a hundred hooks,
53|they said: "Covered it is fitting that here you dance,
54|so that, if you can, you may grab secretly".
55|Not otherwise do the cooks to their scullions
56|make them plunge into the middle of the cauldron
57|the meat with the hooks, so that it does not float.
58|The good master: "So that it may not appear
59|that you are here", said to me, "crouch down
60|behind a jag, so that it may give you some shelter;
61|and for no offense that may be done to me,
62|do not fear, for I have the things known,
63|because another time I was in such a scuffle".
64|Then he passed beyond the head of the bridge;
65|and as he arrived upon the sixth bank,
66|he had need to have a secure forehead.
67|With that fury and with that storm
68|with which the dogs rush out upon the poor wretch
69|who suddenly begs where he stops,
70|came out those from under the little bridge,
71|and turned against him all the hooks;
72|but he cried: "Let none of you be wicked!
73|Before your hook takes me,
74|let one of you come forward who may hear me,
75|and then take counsel about hooking me".
76|All cried: "Let Malacoda go!";
77|whereupon one moved - and the others stood still -
78|and came to him saying: "What good does it do him?".
79|"Do you believe, Malacoda, to see me here
80|to have come", said my master,
81|"secure already from all your defenses,
82|without divine will and favorable fate?
83|Let us go, for in heaven it is willed
84|that I show another this wild path".
85|Then his pride was so fallen,
86|that he let the hook drop at his feet,
87|and said to the others: "Now let him not be struck".
88|And my leader to me: "O you who sit
89|among the splinters of the bridge crouching, crouching,
90|securely now return to me".
91|Wherefore I moved and came to him quickly;
92|and the devils all came forward,
93|so that I feared that they would not keep the pact;
94|thus I once saw the foot soldiers fear
95|who came out under pact from Caprona,
96|seeing themselves among so many enemies.
97|I drew close with my whole person
98|alongside my leader, and did not turn my eyes
99|from their semblance, which was not good.
100|They lowered their hooks and "Do you want me to touch him",
101|said one to the other, "on the rump?".
102|And they answered: "Yes, see that you notch it for him".
103|But that demon who was holding speech
104|with my leader, turned very quickly
105|and said: "Stop, stop, Scarmiglione!".
106|Then he said to us: "To go further along this
107|crag is not possible, because lies
108|all shattered at the bottom the sixth arch.
109|And if going forward still pleases you,
110|go away along this bank;
111|near is another crag that makes a way.
112|Yesterday, five hours later than this hour,
113|one thousand two hundred and sixty-six
114|years were completed since here the way was broken.
115|I am sending toward there some of these of mine
116|to see whether anyone airs himself out;
117|go with them, for they will not be wicked".
118|"Step forward, Alichino, and Calcabrina",
119|he began to say, "and you, Cagnazzo;
120|and let Barbariccia lead the ten.
121|Let Libicocco come too and Draghignazzo,
122|tusked Ciriatto and Graffiacane
123|and Farfarello and mad Rubicante.
124|Search around the boiling glue;
125|let these be safe as far as the next crag
126|which goes all unbroken over the dens".
127|"Alas, master, what is that which I see?",
128|I said, "ah, without escort let us go alone,
129|if you know how to go; for I for myself do not ask for it.
130|If you are as perceptive as you usually are,
131|do you not see that they gnash their teeth
132|and with their brows threaten us with pains?".
133|And he to me: "I do not want you to be afraid;
134|let them gnash as they please,
135|for they do that for the suffering boiled ones".
136|Along the left bank they turned;
137|but first each had his tongue pressed
138|between his teeth, toward their leader, as a signal;
139|and he had made a trumpet of his ass.
