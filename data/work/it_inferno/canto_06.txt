1|At the return of the mind, which closed
2|before the pity of the two in-laws,
3|which with sadness wholly confounded me,
4|new torments and new tormented ones
5|I see around me, however I move
6|and however I turn, and however I gaze.
7|I am at the third circle, of the rain
8|eternal, cursed, cold and heavy;
9|measure and quality are never new to it.
10|Hail thick, dirty water and snow
11|through the dark air pour down;
12|the ground that receives this stinks.
13|Cerberus, beast cruel and monstrous,
14|with three throats doglike barks
15|over the people who there are submerged.
16|He has his eyes red, the beard greasy and black,
17|and the belly wide, and clawed the hands;
18|he scratches the spirits and flays and quarters them.
19|The rain makes them howl like dogs;
20|with one of their sides they make for the other a shield;
21|they turn often, the miserable profane ones.
22|When Cerberus, the great worm, spotted us,
23|he opened his mouths and showed us the fangs;
24|he had no limb that he held still.
25|And my leader spread out his palms,
26|took up earth, and with full fists
27|he threw it into the greedy gullets.
28|As is that dog which barking craves,
29|and grows quiet again after it bites the food,
30|for only on devouring it he is intent and struggles,
31|such became those filthy faces
32|of the demon Cerberus, who thunders at
33|the souls so, that they would like to be deaf.
34|We were passing over the shades whom subdues
35|the heavy rain, and we were placing our soles
36|upon their emptiness which seems a person.
37|They were lying on the ground all of them,
38|except one who rose to sit, as soon
39|as she saw us passing before her.
40|"O you who are through this hell led",
41|she said to me, "recognize me, if you can:
42|you were, before I was unmade, made".
43|And I to her: "The anguish that you have
44|perhaps draws you out of my memory,
45|so that it does not seem that I ever saw you.
46|But tell me who you are who in so sorrowful
47|a place are put, and have such a punishment,
48|that, if another is greater, none is so displeasing".
49|And he to me: "Your city, which is full
50|of envy so that already the sack overflows,
51|held me with it in the serene life.
52|You citizens called me Ciacco;
53|for the damning fault of gluttony,
54|as you see, I am broken in the rain.
55|And I, sad soul, am not alone,
56|for all these are in similar punishment
57|for similar fault". And no more did he make word.
58|I answered him: "Ciacco, your distress
59|weighs on me so, that it invites me to weep;
60|but tell me, if you know, to what will come
61|the citizens of the divided city;
62|if anyone there is just; and tell me the reason
63|why so much discord has assailed it".
64|And he to me: "After long contention
65|they will come to blood, and the rustic party
66|will chase out the other with much offense.
67|Then after it is fated that this one fall
68|within three suns, and that the other rise above
69|with the force of one who now is tacking.
70|High will it hold for a long time its foreheads,
71|holding the other under heavy weights,
72|however much it may weep at this or be ashamed of it.
73|Just are two, and they are not heeded there;
74|pride, envy and avarice are
75|the three sparks that have set the hearts on fire".
76|Here he put an end to the lamentable sound.
77|And I to him: "Still I want you to teach me
78|and that of more speech you make me a gift.
79|Farinata and Tegghiaio, who were so worthy,
80|Iacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo and Mosca
81|and the others who to doing good set their minds,
82|tell me where they are and make me know them;
83|for great desire presses me to know
84|whether heaven sweetens them or hell poisons them".
85|And he: "They are among the blacker souls;
86|different faults weigh them down to the bottom:
87|if you descend so far, there you will be able to see them.
88|But when you will be in the sweet world,
89|I pray you that you bring me to others' mind:
90|no more do I tell you and no more do I answer you".
91|His straight eyes he twisted then into squinting;
92|he looked at me a little and then bowed his head:
93|he fell with it level with the other blind ones.
94|And the leader said to me: "No more does he wake
95|this side of the sound of the angelic trumpet,
96|when the hostile power will come:
97|each will see again the sad tomb,
98|will take again his flesh and his figure,
99|will hear that which in eternity resounds".
100|So we passed through the foul mixture
101|of the shades and of the rain, with slow steps,
102|touching a little on the future life;
103|wherefore I said: "Master, these torments
104|will they grow after the great sentence,
105|or will they be lesser, or will they be as burning?".
106|And he to me: "Return to your science,
107|which holds that, the more the thing is perfect,
108|the more it feels the good, and so the pain.
109|Although this cursed people
110|to true perfection never may go,
111|beyond then more than here it expects to be".
112|We circled around that road,
113|speaking much more than I repeat;
114|we came to the point where it descends:
115|there we found Plutus, the great enemy.
