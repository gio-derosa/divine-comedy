1|If I had rhymes harsh and grating,
2|as would befit the sad hole
3|upon which all the other rocks bear down,
4|I would press out of my concept the juice
5|more fully; but because I do not have them,
6|not without fear do I bring myself to speak;
7|for it is not an enterprise to take up in jest
8|to describe the bottom of the whole universe,
9|nor for a tongue that calls mamma or daddy.
10|But may those ladies help my verse
11|who helped Amphion to enclose Thebes,
12|so that from the fact the telling may not be different.
13|Oh above all ill-created rabble
14|who are in the place of which to speak is hard,
15|better had you been here sheep or goats!
16|When we were down in the dark well
17|beneath the feet of the giant much lower,
18|and I was still gazing at the high wall,
19|I heard said to me: "Watch how you pass:
20|go so, that you do not tread with your soles
21|the heads of the miserable weary brothers".
22|Wherefore I turned, and saw before me
23|and under my feet a lake which through frost
24|had the semblance of glass and not of water.
25|Never made for its course so thick a veil
26|in winter the Danube in Austria,
27|nor the Don there under the cold sky,
28|as there was here; for if Tambernicchi
29|had fallen on it, or Pietrapana,
30|it would not even at the edge have made a creak.
31|And as to croak the frog stays
32|with its muzzle out of the water, when dreams
33|often of gleaning the peasant woman,
34|livid, up to where shame appears
35|were the sorrowful shades in the ice,
36|setting their teeth to the note of the stork.
37|Each held her face turned downward;
38|from the mouth the cold, and from the eyes the sad heart
39|among them procures testimony of itself.
40|When I had looked around a while,
41|I turned to my feet, and saw two so pressed together,
42|that they had the hair of their heads mingled.
43|"Tell me, you who so press your chests together",
44|I said, "who are you?". And they bent their necks;
45|and after they had raised their faces to me,
46|their eyes, which before were moist only within,
47|dripped over the lips, and the frost bound
48|the tears between them and locked them again.
49|Clamp never girded wood with wood
50|so strongly; whereupon they like two goats
51|butted together, such anger overcame them.
52|And one who had lost both his ears
53|to the cold, still with his face downward,
54|said: "Why do you mirror yourself so much in us?
55|If you want to know who these two are,
56|the valley from which the Bisenzio descends
57|was their father Alberto's and theirs.
58|From one body they came out; and all Caina
59|you can search, and you will not find a shade
60|more worthy to be fixed in jelly:
61|not he whose breast and shadow were broken
62|with one blow by the hand of Arthur;
63|not Focaccia; not this one who encumbers me
64|with his head so, that I see no further,
65|and was named Sassol Mascheroni;
66|if you are Tuscan, you know well now who he was.
67|And so that you do not put me to more speeches,
68|know that I was Camiscion de' Pazzi;
69|and I await Carlino who will exculpate me".
70|Then I saw a thousand faces made doglike
71|by the cold; whence a shudder comes over me,
72|and always will come, at frozen fords.
73|And while we were going toward the middle
74|at which all heaviness gathers,
75|and I was trembling in the eternal chill;
76|whether it was will or destiny or fortune,
77|I do not know; but, walking among the heads,
78|I struck my foot hard in the face of one.
79|Weeping it shouted at me: "Why do you trample me?
80|if you do not come to increase the vengeance
81|of Montaperti, why do you molest me?".
82|And I: "My master, now wait for me here,
83|so that I may get out of a doubt through this one;
84|then you will make me hurry as much as you wish".
85|The leader stopped, and I said to that one
86|who was still cursing harshly:
87|"Who are you who so reproach others?".
88|"Now who are you who go through Antenora,
89|striking", he answered, "the cheeks of others,
90|so that, if you were alive, it would be too much?".
91|"Alive I am, and it can be dear to you",
92|was my answer, "if you ask for fame,
93|that I put your name among the other notes".
94|And he to me: "Of the contrary I have craving.
95|Take yourself away from here and give me no more trouble,
96|for badly do you know how to flatter in this lowland!".
97|Then I took him by the scruff
98|and said: "It will be necessary that you name yourself,
99|or that not a hair remain on you up here".
100|Whereupon he to me: "Though you strip me of hair,
101|I will not tell you who I am, nor show it to you
102|if a thousand times you fall upon my head".
103|I had already his hair wound in my hand,
104|and had pulled out more than one tuft of it,
105|he barking with his eyes held downward,
106|when another cried: "What is the matter with you, Bocca?
107|is it not enough for you to sound with your jaws,
108|if you do not bark? what devil touches you?".
109|"Now", I said, "I do not want you to speak more,
110|wicked traitor; for to your shame
111|I will carry true news of you".
112|"Go away", he answered, "and tell what you want;
113|but do not be silent, if you get out from in here,
114|about him who had just now his tongue so ready.
115|He weeps here the silver of the French:
116|'I saw', you can say, 'him of Duera
117|there where the sinners stay cool'.
118|If you were asked 'Who else was there?',
119|you have beside you him of Beccheria
120|whose gorget Florence sawed through.
121|Gianni de' Soldanieri I believe is
122|further on with Ganelon and Tebaldello,
123|who opened Faenza when it was sleeping".
124|We had already departed from him,
125|when I saw two frozen in one hole,
126|so that one head was a hat to the other;
127|and as bread is chewed for hunger,
128|so the upper one set his teeth in the other
129|there where the brain joins with the nape:
130|not otherwise Tydeus gnawed
131|the temples of Menalippus in disdain,
132|than this one was doing the skull and the other things.
133|"O you who show by so bestial a sign
134|hatred upon him whom you devour,
135|tell me the why", I said, "on this condition,
136|that if you with reason complain of him,
137|knowing who you are and his sin,
138|in the world above I may still repay you for it,
139|if that with which I speak does not dry up".
