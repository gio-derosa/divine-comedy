1|Upon the edge of a high bank
2|which great broken stones made in a circle,
3|we came above a more cruel crowd;
4|and there, because of the horrible excess
5|of the stench that the deep abyss throws out,
6|we drew back, behind, to a lid
7|of a great tomb, where I saw an inscription
8|which said: 'I hold Pope Anastasius,
9|whom Photinus drew from the straight way'.
10|"Our descending must be slow,
11|so that the sense may get used a little first
12|to the sad breath; and then there will be no heed of it".
13|So the master; and I: "Some compensation",
14|I said to him, "find so that the time does not pass
15|lost". And he: "You see that I am thinking of that".
16|"My son, inside these stones",
17|he then began to say, "are three little circles
18|from grade to grade, like those that you are leaving.
19|All are full of cursed spirits;
20|but so that afterward the sight alone may suffice you,
21|understand how and why they are confined.
22|Of every malice that gains hatred in heaven,
23|injury is the end, and every such end
24|either with force or with fraud afflicts another.
25|But because fraud is man's own evil,
26|it displeases God more; and therefore below stand
27|the fraudulent, and more pain assails them.
28|Of the violent the first circle is entirely;
29|but because force is done to three persons,
30|into three rings it is divided and constructed.
31|To God, to oneself, to one's neighbor one can
32|do force, I mean to them and to their things,
33|as you will hear with open reasoning.
34|Death by force and painful wounds
35|are given to the neighbor, and to his possessions
36|ruins, fires and damaging extortions;
37|whence homicides and each one who strikes wrongfully,
38|wasters and plunderers, all are tormented
39|by the first ring in different bands.
40|A man can have in himself a violent hand
41|and in his goods; and therefore in the second
42|ring it is fitting that without profit repent
43|whoever deprives himself of your world,
44|gambles away and melts down his wealth,
45|and weeps there where he should be joyful.
46|One can do force against the deity,
47|with the heart denying and blaspheming it,
48|and despising nature and its goodness;
49|and therefore the smallest ring seals
50|with its sign both Sodom and Cahors
51|and him who, despising God in his heart, speaks.
52|Fraud, by which every conscience is bitten,
53|a man can use on him who trusts in him
54|and on him who does not purse up trust.
55|This latter mode seems to cut
56|only the bond of love that nature makes;
57|whence in the second circle nest
58|hypocrisy, flatteries and whoever bewitches,
59|falsity, thievery and simony,
60|panders, barratry and similar filth.
61|By the other mode that love is forgotten
62|which nature makes, and that which is added afterward,
63|from which the special trust is created;
64|whence in the smallest circle, where is the point
65|of the universe upon which Dis sits,
66|whoever betrays is in eternity consumed".
67|And I: "Master, quite clearly proceeds
68|your reasoning, and quite well distinguishes
69|this chasm and the people that it possesses.
70|But tell me: those of the thick marsh,
71|whom the wind drives, and whom the rain beats,
72|and who meet each other with such harsh tongues,
73|why not inside the red city
74|are they punished, if God has them in wrath?
75|and if he does not have them, why are they in such a fashion?".
76|And he to me: "Why does it so stray",
77|he said, "your wit from what it is accustomed?
78|or else your mind, where else is it gazing?
79|Do you not remember those words
80|with which your Ethics treats thoroughly
81|the three dispositions that heaven does not want,
82|incontinence, malice and mad
83|bestiality? and how incontinence
84|less offends God and less blame incurs?
85|If you look well at this judgment,
86|and bring to your mind who are those
87|who up outside sustain penance,
88|you will see well why from these wicked ones
89|they are separated, and why less angered
90|the divine vengeance hammers them".
91|"O sun that heals every troubled sight,
92|you content me so when you resolve,
93|that, no less than knowing, doubting pleases me.
94|Still turn back a little",
95|I said, "there where you say that usury offends
96|the divine goodness, and untie the knot".
97|"Philosophy", he said to me, "to one who understands it,
98|notes, not only in one single part,
99|how nature takes its course
100|from the divine intellect and from its art;
101|and if you note well your Physics,
102|you will find, not after many pages,
103|that your art follows that, as much as it can,
104|as the disciple does the master;
105|so that your art is to God almost grandchild.
106|From these two, if you bring to mind
107|Genesis from the beginning, it is fitting
108|for people to take their life and to advance;
109|and because the usurer holds another way,
110|nature in itself and in its follower
111|he despises, since he puts his hope in something else.
112|But follow me now, for the going pleases me;
113|for the Fishes are quivering on the horizon,
114|and the Wain lies all over Caurus,
115|and the cliff farther on there is descended".
