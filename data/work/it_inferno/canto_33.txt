1|His mouth he lifted from the fierce meal,
2|that sinner, wiping it on the hair
3|of the head that he had ruined behind.
4|Then he began: "You want me to renew
5|desperate grief which presses my heart
6|already just thinking, before I speak of it.
7|But if my words are to be seed
8|that may bear infamy as fruit for the traitor whom I gnaw,
9|you will see speaking and weeping together.
10|I do not know who you are nor by what means
11|you have come down here; but Florentine
12|you truly seem to me when I hear you.
13|You must know that I was Count Ugolino,
14|and this one is the Archbishop Ruggieri:
15|now I will tell you why I am such a neighbor to him.
16|That by the effect of his evil thoughts,
17|trusting myself to him, I was taken
18|and then killed, there is no need to say;
19|therefore what you cannot have heard,
20|that is how cruel my death was,
21|you will hear, and you will know whether he has wronged me.
22|A narrow slit within the Mew,
23|which because of me has the title of hunger,
24|and in which it is fitting that others still be shut,
25|had shown me through its opening
26|several moons already, when I had the bad dream
27|which rent for me the veil of the future.
28|This one seemed to me master and lord,
29|hunting the wolf and the whelps on the mountain
30|because of which the Pisans cannot see Lucca.
31|With lean bitches, eager and trained
32|Gualandi with Sismondi and with Lanfranchi
33|he had put in front of him at the head.
34|After a short run they seemed to me tired,
35|the father and the sons, and with the sharp fangs
36|I seemed to see their flanks torn open.
37|When I was awake before the morning,
38|I heard weeping in their sleep my sons
39|who were with me, and asking for bread.
40|Truly you are cruel, if you do not already grieve
41|thinking of what my heart was foretelling to itself;
42|and if you do not weep, at what are you accustomed to weep?
43|Already they were awake, and the hour was approaching
44|when the food used to be brought to us,
45|and because of his dream each was in fear;
46|and I heard nailing up the door below
47|of the horrible tower; whereupon I looked
48|into the face of my sons without making a word.
49|I did not weep, so did I turn to stone within:
50|they wept; and my little Anselm
51|said: 'You look so, father! what is the matter with you?'.
52|Yet I did not shed tears nor did I answer
53|all that day nor the night after,
54|until the next sun came out upon the world.
55|As a little ray had made its way
56|into the sorrowful prison, and I discerned
57|in four faces my own aspect,
58|both my hands I bit for grief;
59|and they, thinking that I did it from desire
60|to eat, suddenly rose
61|and said: 'Father, much less pain it will be for us
62|if you eat of us: you clothed us
63|in this miserable flesh, and you strip it off'.
64|I calmed myself then so as not to make them sadder;
65|that day and the next we all stayed mute;
66|ah hard earth, why did you not open?
67|After we had come to the fourth day,
68|Gaddo threw himself stretched out at my feet,
69|saying: 'My father, why do you not help me?'.
70|There he died; and as you see me,
71|I saw the three fall one by one
72|between the fifth day and the sixth; whereupon I gave myself,
73|already blind, to groping over each,
74|and for two days I called them, after they were dead.
75|Then, more than grief, fasting had power".
76|When he had said this, with his eyes twisted
77|he took again the wretched skull with his teeth,
78|which were strong on the bone, like a dog's.
79|Ah Pisa, reproach of the peoples
80|of the beautiful country where the sì sounds,
81|since your neighbors are slow to punish you,
82|let Capraia and Gorgona move,
83|and make a hedge for the Arno at its mouth,
84|so that it may drown in you every person!
85|For if Count Ugolino had the reputation
86|of having betrayed you in your castles,
87|you should not have put the sons to such a cross.
88|Innocent made them their young age,
89|new Thebes, Uguiccione and Brigata
90|and the other two whom the song names above.
91|We passed further on, where the frost
92|roughly swathes another people,
93|not turned downward, but all upturned.
94|The weeping itself there does not let them weep,
95|and the grief which finds upon the eyes a barrier,
96|turns inward to make the anguish grow;
97|for the first tears form a knot,
98|and like visors of crystal,
99|fill beneath the brow the whole cup.
100|And although, as from a callus,
101|because of the cold every feeling
102|had ceased to have its dwelling in my face,
103|already I seemed to feel some wind;
104|wherefore I: "My master, who moves this?
105|is not down here every vapor quenched?".
106|Whereupon he to me: "Soon you will be where
107|of this your eye will give you the answer,
108|seeing the cause which rains down the blast".
109|And one of the wretches of the cold crust
110|cried to us: "O souls so cruel
111|that the last station is given to you,
112|lift from my face the hard veils,
113|so that I may vent the grief that swells my heart,
114|a little, before the weeping freezes again".
115|Wherefore I to him: "If you want me to help you,
116|tell me who you are, and if I do not free you,
117|may I have to go to the bottom of the ice".
118|He answered then: "I am Friar Alberigo;
119|I am he of the fruits of the evil garden,
120|who here receive back a date for a fig".
121|"Oh", I said to him, "now are you already dead?".
122|And he to me: "How my body fares
123|in the world above, I bear no knowledge.
124|Such advantage has this Ptolomea,
125|that many times the soul falls here
126|before Atropos gives it motion.
127|And so that you more willingly may scrape
128|the glassy tears from my face,
129|know that, as soon as the soul betrays
130|as I did, its body is taken from it
131|by a demon, who then governs it
132|until its time has all revolved.
133|It falls headlong into such a cistern;
134|and perhaps still appears above the body
135|of the shade that here behind me winters.
136|You must know it, if you come only now down here:
137|he is ser Branca Doria, and many years
138|have passed since he was so enclosed".
139|"I believe", I said to him, "that you deceive me;
140|for Branca Doria has not died yet,
141|and he eats and drinks and sleeps and puts on clothes".
142|"In the ditch above", he said, "of the Malebranche,
143|there where the sticky pitch boils,
144|Michel Zanche had not yet arrived,
145|when this one left a devil in his place
146|in his body, and one of his kinsmen
147|who did the treachery together with him.
148|But stretch out now your hand here;
149|open my eyes". And I did not open them for him;
150|and it was courtesy to be rude to him.
151|Ah Genoese, men estranged
152|from every custom and full of every vice,
153|why are you not scattered from the world?
154|For with the worst spirit of Romagna
155|I found one of you such, that for his deeds
156|in soul in Cocytus already he bathes,
157|and in body he seems still alive above.
