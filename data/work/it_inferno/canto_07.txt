1|"Pape Satàn, pape Satàn aleppe!",
2|began Plutus with the clucking voice;
3|and that gentle sage, who knew everything,
4|said to comfort me: "Let it not harm you,
5|your fear; for, whatever power he may have,
6|he will not take from us the descending of this rock".
7|Then he turned back to that swollen face,
8|and said: "Be silent, cursed wolf!
9|consume yourself inside with your rage.
10|Not without cause is the going to the depth:
11|it is willed on high, there where Michael
12|took vengeance for the proud rape".
13|As by the wind the swollen sails
14|fall tangled, after the mast breaks,
15|so fell to earth the cruel beast.
16|Thus we descended into the fourth hollow,
17|taking more of the sorrowful bank
18|which bags up the evil of the whole universe.
19|Ah justice of God! who crams so many
20|new travails and pains as I saw?
21|and why does our guilt so waste us?
22|As the wave does there above Charybdis,
23|which breaks itself against that which it runs into,
24|so it is fated that here the people dance the round.
25|Here I saw people, more than elsewhere, too many,
26|both on one side and on the other, with great howls,
27|rolling weights by force of chest.
28|They struck against each other; and then right there
29|each turned back, rolling backward,
30|shouting: "Why do you hoard?" and "Why do you squander?".
31|So they returned along the gloomy circle
32|from each hand to the opposite point,
33|shouting at each other again their shameful refrain;
34|then each turned, when he had arrived,
35|through his half circle to the other joust.
36|And I, who had my heart almost pierced,
37|said: "My master, now show me
38|what people this is, and whether all were clerics
39|these tonsured ones on our left".
40|And he to me: "All of them were squint-eyed
41|so in the mind in the first life,
42|that with measure they made no spending there.
43|Quite clearly their voice barks it out,
44|when they come to the two points of the circle
45|where contrary fault unpairs them.
46|These were clerics, who have no covering
47|of hair on the head, and popes and cardinals,
48|in whom avarice practices its excess".
49|And I: "Master, among these such ones
50|I should indeed recognize some
51|who were unclean with these evils".
52|And he to me: "A vain thought you gather:
53|the undiscerning life that made them foul,
54|to every recognition now makes them dark.
55|In eternity they will come to the two clashes:
56|these will rise again from the sepulchre
57|with the fist closed, and these with hair cropped.
58|Ill giving and ill keeping the beautiful world
59|have taken from them, and set them to this scuffle:
60|what it is, I do not embellish words for it.
61|Now you can, son, see the short mockery
62|of the goods that are entrusted to fortune,
63|for which the human race quarrels;
64|for all the gold that is beneath the moon
65|and that ever was, of these weary souls
66|could not make one of them rest".
67|"My master", I said, "now tell me also:
68|this fortune of which you touch on to me,
69|what is it, which has the goods of the world so in its clutches?".
70|And he to me: "Oh foolish creatures,
71|how great an ignorance is that which harms you!
72|Now I want you to take in my judgment on it.
73|He whose knowledge transcends all,
74|made the heavens and gave them one who guides them
75|so, that every part shines to every part,
76|distributing equally the light.
77|Similarly for the worldly splendors
78|he ordained a general minister and guide
79|who should change in time the vain goods
80|from people to people and from one blood to another,
81|beyond the resistance of human wits;
82|wherefore one people rules and the other languishes,
83|following the judgment of this one,
84|which is hidden like the snake in the grass.
85|Your knowledge has no opposition to her:
86|she provides, judges, and pursues
87|her reign as the other gods do theirs.
88|Her changes have no truces:
89|necessity makes her be swift;
90|so often comes one who obtains his turn.
91|This is she who is so much put on the cross
92|even by those who should give her praise,
93|giving her blame wrongly and ill repute;
94|but she is blessed and does not hear this:
95|with the other first creatures glad
96|she turns her sphere and blessed enjoys herself.
97|Now let us descend at last to greater anguish;
98|already every star is falling that was rising
99|when I set out, and the too long staying is forbidden".
100|We crossed the circle to the other bank
101|above a spring that boils and pours over
102|through a ditch that derives from it.
103|The water was dark much more than purple-black;
104|and we, in the company of the gray waves,
105|entered down by a strange way.
106|Into the marsh that has the name Styx goes
107|this sad stream, when it has descended
108|to the foot of the malign gray slopes.
109|And I, who was intent on gazing,
110|saw muddy people in that bog,
111|naked all, with offended semblance.
112|These were striking each other not only with the hand,
113|but with the head and with the chest and with the feet,
114|tearing each other with their teeth piece by piece.
115|The good master said: "Son, now you see
116|the souls of those whom anger vanquished;
117|and also I want you to believe for certain
118|that under the water are people who sigh,
119|and make this water bubble at the top,
120|as the eye tells you, wherever it turns.
121|Stuck in the slime they say: 'Sullen we were
122|in the sweet air that rejoices in the sun,
123|carrying within a slothful smoke:
124|now we are sullen in the black mire'.
125|This hymn they gurgle in their throats,
126|for they cannot say it with complete word".
127|So we circled of the filthy pool
128|a great arc, between the dry bank and the mire,
129|with the eyes turned on those who gulp down the mud.
130|We came to the foot of a tower at the last.
