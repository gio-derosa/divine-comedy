1|Who could ever even with unfettered words
2|tell of the blood and of the wounds in full
3|that I now saw, by narrating many times?
4|Every tongue surely would fall short
5|because of our speech and of our mind
6|which have little capacity to comprehend so much.
7|If there were gathered again all the people
8|who once, upon the fortune-tossed land
9|of Apulia, grieved for their blood
10|because of the Trojans and of the long war
11|which made of the rings such high spoils,
12|as Livy writes, who does not err,
13|with those who felt the pains of blows
14|for opposing Robert Guiscard;
15|and the others whose bones are still gathered
16|at Ceperano, there where was a liar
17|every Apulian, and there by Tagliacozzo,
18|where without arms old Alardo won;
19|and one his limb pierced and one cut off
20|should show, it would be nothing to equal
21|the foul manner of the ninth pouch.
22|Truly a cask, from losing middle-board or side-piece,
23|is not so holed as one I saw,
24|split from the chin to where one breaks wind.
25|Between his legs hung his entrails;
26|the vitals appeared and the sad sack
27|which makes shit of what is swallowed.
28|While I attach myself wholly to seeing him,
29|he looked at me and with his hands opened his chest,
30|saying: "Now see how I tear myself apart!
31|see how mangled is Muhammad!
32|Before me goes away weeping Ali,
33|split in the face from the chin to the forelock.
34|And all the others whom you see here,
35|sowers of scandal and of schism
36|were alive, and therefore they are split thus.
37|A devil is here behind who fixes us up
38|so cruelly, to the edge of the sword
39|putting again each of this ream,
40|when we have circled the sorrowful road;
41|because the wounds are closed again
42|before one goes again in front of him.
43|But you, who are you who muse upon the crag,
44|perhaps to delay going to the punishment
45|which is judged upon your accusations?".
46|"Neither has death reached him yet, nor does guilt lead him",
47|answered my master, "to torment him;
48|but to give him full experience,
49|for me, who am dead, it is fitting to lead him
50|through hell down here from circle to circle;
51|and this is true just as I speak to you".
52|More than a hundred there were who, when they heard him,
53|stopped in the ditch to look at me
54|in wonder, forgetting the torment.
55|"Now tell Fra Dolcino then to arm himself,
56|you who perhaps will see the sun shortly,
57|if he does not want soon to follow me here,
58|so with provisions, that the grip of snow
59|may not bring the victory to the Novarese,
60|which otherwise to gain would not be easy".
61|After he had lifted one foot to go away,
62|Muhammad said to me this word;
63|then to depart he stretched it on the ground.
64|Another, who had his throat pierced
65|and his nose cut off up to under his brows,
66|and had only one single ear,
67|having stopped to look in wonder
68|with the others, before the others opened his windpipe,
69|which was outside on every part vermilion,
70|and said: "O you whom guilt does not condemn
71|and whom I saw up on Italian soil,
72|if too much resemblance does not deceive me,
73|remember Pier da Medicina,
74|if ever you return to see the sweet plain
75|which from Vercelli to Marcabò slopes down.
76|And make known to the two best of Fano,
77|to messer Guido and also to Angiolello,
78|that, if the foresight here is not vain,
79|they will be thrown out of their vessel
80|and drowned near La Cattolica
81|through the treachery of a wicked tyrant.
82|Between the island of Cyprus and of Majorca
83|Neptune never saw so great a crime,
84|not by pirates, not by Argolic people.
85|That traitor who sees only with one,
86|and holds the city which one here with me
87|would wish to be fasting from seeing,
88|will make them come to parley with him;
89|then he will do so, that against the wind of Focara
90|they will have no need of vow or prayer".
91|And I to him: "Show me and declare,
92|if you want me to carry news of you up above,
93|who is that one of the bitter sight".
94|Then he put his hand on the jaw
95|of one of his companions and opened his mouth,
96|crying: "This is he, and he does not speak.
97|This one, banished, drowned the doubting
98|in Caesar, affirming that one prepared
99|always suffered harm from waiting".
100|Oh how dismayed he seemed to me
101|with his tongue cut off in his throat,
102|Curio, who was so bold in speaking!
103|And one who had the one and the other hand cut off,
104|lifting the stumps through the dusky air,
105|so that the blood made his face foul,
106|cried: "You will remember also Mosca,
107|who said, alas!, 'A thing done has an end',
108|which was an evil seed for the Tuscan people".
109|And I added to him: "And death of your stock";
110|whereupon he, heaping pain upon pain,
111|went away like a person sad and mad.
112|But I remained to look at the throng,
113|and saw a thing which I would be afraid,
114|without more proof, only to recount;
115|except that conscience reassures me,
116|the good companion which emboldens a man
117|under the hauberk of feeling itself pure.
118|I surely saw, and still it seems that I see it,
119|a trunk without a head going just as
120|the others of the sad flock were going;
121|and the severed head it held by the hair,
122|dangling from its hand like a lantern:
123|and that one gazed at us and said: "Oh me!".
124|Of itself it made for itself a lamp,
125|and they were two in one and one in two;
126|how it can be, he knows who so governs.
127|When it was right at the foot of the bridge,
128|it raised its arm high with the whole head
129|to bring near to us its words,
130|which were: "Now see the grievous punishment,
131|you who, breathing, go seeing the dead:
132|see if any is great like this.
133|And so that you may carry news of me,
134|know that I am Bertran de Born, the one
135|who gave to the young king the evil encouragements.
136|I made the father and the son rebels against each other;
137|Ahithophel did not do more with Absalom
138|and with David with his wicked goadings.
139|Because I divided persons so joined,
140|I carry my brain divided, alas!,
141|from its origin which is in this trunk.
142|Thus is observed in me the counter-penalty".
