1|There broke the deep sleep in my head
2|a heavy thunder, so that I started up
3|like a person who is by force awakened;
4|and the rested eye around I moved,
5|risen upright, and fixedly I looked
6|to know the place where I was.
7|True it is that on the brink I found myself
8|of the valley of the sorrowful abyss
9|which gathers the thunder of infinite wails.
10|Dark and deep it was and cloudy
11|so much that, for fixing the sight to the bottom,
12|I did not discern there anything.
13|"Now let us descend down here into the blind world",
14|began the poet all pale.
15|"I will be first, and you will be second".
16|And I, who had become aware of the color,
17|said: "How will I come, if you are afraid
18|who are accustomed to be comfort to my doubting?".
19|And he to me: "The anguish of the people
20|who are down here, on my face paints
21|that pity which you take for fear.
22|Let us go, for the long way urges us".
23|So he set out and so he made me enter
24|into the first circle that girds the abyss.
25|There, according to what one could tell by listening,
26|there was no weeping but of sighs
27|which made the eternal air tremble;
28|this arose from sorrow without torments,
29|which the crowds had, which were many and great,
30|of infants and of women and of men.
31|The good master to me: "You do not ask
32|what spirits are these that you see?
33|Now I want that you know, before you go further,
34|that they did not sin; and if they have merits,
35|it is not enough, because they did not have baptism,
36|which is the gate of the faith that you believe;
37|and if they were before Christianity,
38|they did not adore God duly:
39|and of these such am I myself.
40|For such defects, not for other guilt,
41|we are lost, and only so far harmed
42|that without hope we live in desire".
43|Great grief seized me at the heart when I heard it,
44|because people of much worth
45|I knew who in that limbo were suspended.
46|"Tell me, my master, tell me, lord",
47|I began, wanting to be certain
48|of that faith which conquers every error:
49|"did anyone ever go out from here, either by his merit
50|or by another's, who afterward was blessed?".
51|And he who understood my covert speech,
52|answered: "I was new in this state,
53|when I saw come here a mighty one,
54|with sign of victory crowned.
55|He drew out from here the shade of the first parent,
56|of Abel his son and that of Noah,
57|of Moses lawgiver and obedient;
58|Abraham patriarch and David king,
59|Israel with his father and with his children
60|and with Rachel, for whom he did so much,
61|and many others, and he made them blessed.
62|And I want you to know that, before them,
63|human spirits were not saved".
64|We did not leave off going because he was speaking,
65|but we were passing the forest all the while,
66|the forest, I say, of thick spirits.
67|Not yet long was our way
68|on this side of the sleep, when I saw a fire
69|which a hemisphere of darkness was overcoming.
70|We were still a little distant from it,
71|but not so that I did not discern in part
72|that honorable people possessed that place.
73|"O you who honor science and art,
74|who are these who have so much honor,
75|that it parts them from the manner of the others?".
76|And he to me: "The honored renown
77|that sounds of them up in your life,
78|acquires grace in heaven which so advances them".
79|Meanwhile a voice was heard by me:
80|"Honor the highest poet;
81|his shade returns, which had departed".
82|After the voice had stopped and was quiet,
83|I saw four great shades come to us:
84|they had a semblance neither sad nor glad.
85|The good master began to say:
86|"Look at him with that sword in hand,
87|who comes before the three just like a lord:
88|that one is Homer, sovereign poet;
89|the other is Horace the satirist who comes;
90|Ovid is the third, and the last Lucan.
91|Because each one agrees with me
92|in the name that the single voice sounded,
93|they do me honor, and in that they do well".
94|So I saw assemble the beautiful school
95|of that lord of the highest song
96|who above the others like an eagle flies.
97|After they had talked together somewhat,
98|they turned to me with a sign of greeting,
99|and my master smiled at so much;
100|and more of honor still by far they did me,
101|for they made me of their band,
102|so that I was sixth among so much wisdom.
103|Thus we went as far as the light,
104|speaking things about which silence is beautiful,
105|just as the speaking was there where I was.
106|We came to the foot of a noble castle,
107|seven times circled by high walls,
108|defended around by a beautiful little river.
109|This we passed as if dry land;
110|through seven gates I entered with these sages:
111|we arrived at a meadow of fresh verdure.
112|People were there with eyes slow and grave,
113|of great authority in their semblances:
114|they spoke seldom, with gentle voices.
115|We drew ourselves thus to one of the sides,
116|into a place open, luminous and high,
117|so that they could be seen all of them.
118|There straight ahead, on the green enamel,
119|were shown to me the great spirits,
120|so that from seeing them in myself I am exalted.
121|I saw Electra with many companions,
122|among whom I knew Hector and Aeneas,
123|Caesar armed with the hawk-like eyes.
124|I saw Camilla and Penthesilea;
125|on the other side I saw King Latinus
126|who with Lavinia his daughter was sitting.
127|I saw that Brutus who chased out Tarquin,
128|Lucretia, Julia, Marcia and Cornelia;
129|and alone, apart, I saw Saladin.
130|After I raised a little more my brows,
131|I saw the master of those who know
132|sitting among a philosophic family.
133|All gaze at him, all do him honor:
134|there I saw Socrates and Plato,
135|who before the others stand closest to him;
136|Democritus who ascribes the world to chance,
137|Diogenes, Anaxagoras and Thales,
138|Empedocles, Heraclitus and Zeno;
139|and I saw the good gatherer of the qualities,
140|Dioscorides I mean; and I saw Orpheus,
141|Tully and Linus and moral Seneca;
142|Euclid geometer and Ptolemy,
143|Hippocrates, Avicenna and Galen,
144|Averroes who made the great commentary.
145|I cannot portray all of them in full,
146|because so drives me the long theme,
147|that many times the telling falls short of the fact.
148|The company of six diminishes into two:
149|by another way the wise leader leads me,
150|out of the quiet, into the air that trembles.
151|And I come into a part where there is nothing that shines.
