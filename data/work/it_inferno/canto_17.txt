1|"Behold the beast with the pointed tail,
2|which passes mountains and breaks walls and weapons!
3|Behold her who makes the whole world stink!".
4|So began my leader to speak to me;
5|and he beckoned to it that it should come to shore,
6|near the end of the walked-upon marbles.
7|And that filthy image of fraud
8|came forward, and landed its head and its bust,
9|but onto the bank it did not draw its tail.
10|Its face was the face of a just man,
11|so benign did it have the skin outside,
12|and of a serpent all the rest of the trunk;
13|two paws it had, hairy up to the armpits;
14|the back and the chest and both the sides
15|it had painted with knots and with little circles.
16|With more colors, in ground and in embroidery,
17|Tartars or Turks never made cloths,
18|nor were such webs laid on the loom by Arachne.
19|As sometimes the barges stand on the shore,
20|which are partly in water and partly on land,
21|and as there among the gluttonous Germans
22|the beaver settles itself to make its war,
23|so the worst beast was staying
24|on the rim which is of stone and encloses the sand.
25|In the void all its tail was quivering,
26|twisting upward the venomous fork
27|which in the manner of a scorpion armed the tip.
28|The leader said: "Now it is fitting that should bend
29|our way a little as far as that
30|wicked beast which lies down there".
31|Therefore we descended on the right-hand side,
32|and ten steps we made upon the edge,
33|so as to avoid well the sand and the flames.
34|And when we have come to it,
35|a little further on I see upon the sand
36|people sitting close to the empty place.
37|Here the master: "So that full
38|experience of this ring you may carry away",
39|said to me, "go, and see their condition.
40|Let your speeches there be short;
41|until you return, I will speak with this one,
42|that she may grant us her strong shoulders".
43|So further along the extreme head
44|of that seventh circle all alone
45|I went, where the sad people were sitting.
46|Through their eyes out burst their pain;
47|here, there they helped themselves with their hands
48|now against the vapors, and now against the hot soil:
49|not otherwise do dogs in summer
50|now with the muzzle now with the paw, when they are bitten
51|either by fleas or by flies or by gadflies.
52|After I set my eyes on the face of some,
53|on whom the sorrowful fire falls,
54|I did not know any of them; but I perceived
55|that from the neck of each hung a pouch
56|which had a certain color and a certain sign,
57|and on that it seems that their eye feeds.
58|And as I come among them looking,
59|on a yellow purse I saw azure
60|which had the face and bearing of a lion.
61|Then, proceeding with the course of my gaze,
62|I saw another as red as blood,
63|showing a goose whiter than butter.
64|And one who with a sow azure and big
65|had marked his little white sack,
66|said to me: "What are you doing in this ditch?
67|Now go away; and since you are still alive,
68|know that my neighbor Vitaliano
69|will sit here by my left flank.
70|With these Florentines I am a Paduan:
71|many times they thunder in my ears
72|shouting: 'Let the sovereign knight come,
73|who will bring the pouch with three goats!'".
74|Here he twisted his mouth and out he stuck
75|his tongue, like an ox that licks its nose.
76|And I, fearing lest my staying longer anger
77|him who had warned me to stay little,
78|turned back from the weary souls.
79|I found my leader, who had climbed
80|already onto the rump of the fierce animal,
81|and he said to me: "Now be strong and bold.
82|From now on one descends by such stairs;
83|mount in front, for I want to be in the middle,
84|so that the tail cannot do harm".
85|As is one who has so near the shivering
86|of the quartan fever, that he has already his nails pale,
87|and trembles all over just looking at the shade,
88|such I became at the words offered;
89|but shame made its threats to me,
90|which before a good lord makes a servant strong.
91|I settled myself upon those huge shoulders;
92|I wanted to say, but the voice did not come
93|as I believed: 'See that you embrace me'.
94|But he, who another time helped me
95|in another peril, as soon as I mounted
96|with his arms clasped me and held me up;
97|and he said: "Geryon, move now:
98|the wheels wide, and let the descending be slow;
99|think of the new burden that you have".
100|As the little boat goes out from its place
101|backward, backward, so from there it drew away;
102|and when it felt itself entirely free,
103|there where its chest had been, it turned its tail,
104|and that one stretched, like an eel, it moved,
105|and with its paws it gathered the air to itself.
106|Greater fear I do not believe there was
107|when Phaethon abandoned the reins,
108|whereby the sky, as still appears, was scorched;
109|nor when wretched Icarus his loins
110|felt unfeathering because of the heated wax,
111|his father crying to him 'You hold a bad way!',
112|than was mine, when I saw that I was
113|in the air on every side, and saw quenched
114|every sight except of the beast.
115|She goes away swimming slowly, slowly;
116|she wheels and descends, but I do not perceive it
117|except that on my face and from below the wind blows on me.
118|I heard already on the right hand the whirlpool
119|make beneath us a horrible roar,
120|wherefore with my eyes downward I stretch out my head.
121|Then I was more fearful of the dismounting,
122|because I saw fires and heard laments;
123|whereupon I, trembling, all cling with my thighs.
124|And I saw then, for I had not seen it before,
125|the descending and the circling through the great evils
126|which drew near from different sides.
127|As the falcon who has been long on the wing,
128|who without seeing lure or bird
129|makes the falconer say 'Alas, you come down!',
130|descends weary where it moves off swiftly,
131|through a hundred wheels, and far away alights
132|from its master, disdainful and sullen;
133|so Geryon set us at the bottom
134|at the very foot of the sheer-cut rock,
135|and, having unloaded our persons,
136|vanished like a notch from a bowstring.
