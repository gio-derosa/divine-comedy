1|"The banners of the king of hell go forth
2|toward us; therefore look ahead",
3|said my master, "whether you discern him".
4|As when a thick fog breathes,
5|or when our hemisphere grows dark with night,
6|there appears from afar a mill which the wind turns,
7|such a structure it seemed to me I saw then;
8|then because of the wind I drew back behind
9|my leader, for there was no other shelter there.
10|I was already, and with fear I put it in verse,
11|there where the shades were all covered,
12|and showed through like a straw in glass.
13|Some are lying; others stand upright,
14|this one with the head and that one with the soles;
15|another, like a bow, bends its face to its feet.
16|When we had gone so far forward,
17|that it pleased my master to show me
18|the creature which had the beautiful semblance,
19|he took himself from before me and made me stop,
20|"Behold Dis", saying, "and behold the place
21|where it is fitting that you arm yourself with strength".
22|How I then became frozen and faint,
23|do not ask it, reader, for I do not write it,
24|because all speech would be little.
25|I did not die and I did not remain alive;
26|think now for yourself, if you have a bit of wit,
27|what I became, deprived of both one and the other.
28|The emperor of the sorrowful kingdom
29|from mid-chest came out of the ice;
30|and I more match a giant,
31|than the giants do his arms:
32|see now how great must be that whole
33|which to such a part is proportioned.
34|If he was as beautiful as he is now ugly,
35|and against his maker raised his brows,
36|well must from him proceed every grief.
37|Oh how great a marvel it seemed to me
38|when I saw three faces on his head!
39|One in front, and that was vermilion;
40|the others were two, which joined with this one
41|just above the middle of each shoulder,
42|and joined themselves at the place of the crest:
43|and the right one seemed between white and yellow;
44|the left one to see was such as are those
45|who come from there where the Nile descends into the valley.
46|Under each came out two great wings,
47|as much as was fitting for so great a bird:
48|sails of the sea I never saw such.
49|They had no feathers, but of a bat
50|was their fashion; and those he was flapping,
51|so that three winds moved from him:
52|thereby Cocytus was all frozen.
53|With six eyes he wept, and down three chins
54|dripped the weeping and bloody slaver.
55|In each mouth he was crushing with his teeth
56|a sinner, in the manner of a flax-brake,
57|so that he made three of them thus suffer.
58|To the one in front the biting was nothing
59|compared to the clawing, for at times the back
60|remained all stripped of the skin.
61|"That soul up there which has the greatest punishment",
62|said the master, "is Judas Iscariot,
63|who has his head inside and outside plies his legs.
64|Of the other two who have their head below,
65|he who hangs from the black muzzle is Brutus:
66|see how he writhes, and makes no word!;
67|and the other is Cassius, who seems so large-limbed.
68|But the night is rising again, and now
69|it is time to depart, for we have seen everything".
70|As it pleased him, I clasped his neck;
71|and he chose the time and place,
72|and when the wings were opened wide,
73|he took hold on the shaggy sides;
74|from tuft to tuft he descended down then
75|between the thick hair and the frozen crusts.
76|When we were there where the thigh
77|turns, right on the thick of the haunches,
78|the leader, with effort and with anguish,
79|turned his head where he had his shanks,
80|and grappled on the hair like a man who climbs,
81|so that I believed we were returning into hell again.
82|"Hold on well, for by such stairs",
83|said the master, panting like a weary man,
84|"we must depart from so much evil".
85|Then he came out through the opening of a rock
86|and set me upon the edge to sit;
87|then he stretched toward me his wary step.
88|I raised my eyes and believed I would see
89|Lucifer as I had left him,
90|and I saw him holding his legs upward;
91|and if I became then troubled,
92|let the dull people think it, who do not see
93|what that point is which I had passed.
94|"Get up", said the master, "on your feet:
95|the way is long and the road is bad,
96|and already the sun returns to mid-tierce".
97|It was not a palace hall
98|there where we were, but a natural dungeon
99|which had a bad floor and lack of light.
100|"Before I tear myself from the abyss,
101|my master", I said when I was upright,
102|"to draw me from error speak to me a little:
103|where is the ice? and this one, how is he fixed
104|so upside down? and how, in so short a time,
105|from evening to morning has the sun made its passage?".
106|And he to me: "You imagine still
107|that you are beyond the center, where I took hold
108|of the hair of the guilty worm which pierces the world.
109|You were beyond it as long as I descended;
110|when I turned, you passed the point
111|to which the weights are drawn from every part.
112|And you have now arrived under the hemisphere
113|which is opposite to that which the great dry land
114|covers, and under whose zenith was consumed
115|the man who was born and lived without sin;
116|you have your feet upon a little sphere
117|which makes the other face of the Judecca.
118|Here it is morning, when there it is evening;
119|and this one, who made a ladder for us with his hair,
120|is fixed still just as he was before.
121|On this side he fell down from heaven;
122|and the land, which before stood out on this side,
123|for fear of him made a veil of the sea,
124|and came to our hemisphere; and perhaps
125|to flee him left here an empty place
126|that which appears on this side, and ran back up".
127|There is a place down there from Beelzebub removed
128|as far as the tomb extends,
129|which not by sight, but by sound is known
130|of a little stream which descends there
131|through the hole of a rock, which it has gnawed,
132|with the course that it winds, and it slopes little.
133|The leader and I by that hidden road
134|entered to return into the bright world;
135|and without care to have any rest,
136|we climbed up, he first and I second,
137|so far that I saw of the beautiful things
138|which heaven bears, through a round opening.
139|And thence we came out to see again the stars.
