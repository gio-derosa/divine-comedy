1|Nessus had not yet arrived over there,
2|when we set out through a wood
3|which by no path was marked.
4|Not green foliage, but of dusky color;
5|not branches smooth, but knotted and twisted;
6|no fruits were there, but thorns with poison.
7|They do not have such harsh thickets nor so dense,
8|those wild beasts that hold in hatred
9|between Cecina and Corneto the tilled places.
10|There the ugly Harpies make their nests,
11|who chased the Trojans from the Strophades
12|with sad announcement of future harm.
13|Wings they have broad, and necks and faces human,
14|feet with talons, and feathered the great belly;
15|they make laments upon the strange trees.
16|And the good master: "Before you go further in,
17|know that you are in the second ring",
18|he began to say to me, "and will be until
19|you will come to the horrible sand.
20|Therefore look well; so you will see
21|things that would take away belief from my speech".
22|I heard from every side wails uttered
23|and did not see any person who made them;
24|wherefore I, all bewildered, stopped.
25|I believe that he believed that I believed
26|that so many voices came out, among those stumps,
27|from people who were hiding from us.
28|Therefore said the master: "If you break off
29|some little twig from one of these plants,
30|the thoughts that you have will all be made maimed".
31|Then I stretched my hand a little forward
32|and plucked a little branch from a great thornbush;
33|and its trunk cried: "Why do you break me?".
34|After it had then become dark with blood,
35|it began again to say: "Why do you tear me?
36|do you not have any spirit of pity?
37|Men we were, and now we are made into stumps:
38|surely your hand should be more merciful,
39|if we had been souls of serpents".
40|As from a green brand that is burned
41|at one of its ends, which from the other groans
42|and hisses with wind that goes away,
43|so from the broken splinter came out together
44|words and blood; whereupon I let the tip
45|fall, and stood like the man who is afraid.
46|"If he had been able to believe before",
47|answered my sage, "wounded soul,
48|what he has seen only in my verse,
49|he would not have stretched his hand against you;
50|but the incredible thing made me
51|induce him to a deed that weighs on me myself.
52|But tell him who you were, so that in place
53|of some amends he may refresh your fame
54|in the world above, where it is permitted him to return".
55|And the trunk: "So with sweet speech you lure me,
56|that I cannot be silent; and let it not weigh on you
57|if I a little am limed into speaking.
58|I am he who held both the keys
59|of the heart of Frederick, and who turned them,
60|locking and unlocking, so gently,
61|that from his secrets I kept almost every man;
62|faith I bore to the glorious office,
63|so much that I lost for it sleep and pulses.
64|The harlot who never from the dwelling
65|of Caesar turned her whorish eyes,
66|common death and vice of courts,
67|inflamed against me all minds;
68|and the inflamed ones inflamed Augustus so,
69|that the glad honors turned into sad mourning.
70|My mind, through a disdainful taste,
71|believing by dying to flee disdain,
72|made me unjust against my just self.
73|By the new roots of this wood
74|I swear to you that I never broke faith
75|with my lord, who was so worthy of honor.
76|And if one of you returns to the world,
77|let him comfort my memory, which lies still
78|from the blow that envy gave it".
79|He waited a little, and then: "Since he is silent",
80|the poet said to me, "do not lose the hour;
81|but speak, and ask him, if more pleases you".
82|Whereupon I to him: "You ask him further
83|of what you believe would satisfy me;
84|for I could not, so much pity grieves my heart".
85|Therefore he began again: "So may the man do for you
86|freely what your speech begs,
87|imprisoned spirit, may it please you still
88|to tell us how the soul is bound
89|in these knots; and tell us, if you can,
90|whether any ever frees itself from such limbs".
91|Then the trunk blew hard, and then
92|that wind was converted into such a voice:
93|"Briefly you will be answered.
94|When the fierce soul departs
95|from the body from which it has torn itself,
96|Minos sends it to the seventh gorge.
97|It falls into the forest, and no part is chosen for it;
98|but there where fortune slings it,
99|there it sprouts like a grain of spelt.
100|It rises into a sapling and into a wild plant:
101|the Harpies, feeding then on its leaves,
102|make pain, and for the pain a window.
103|Like the others we will come for our spoils,
104|but not however that any may clothe itself again with them,
105|for it is not just to have what one takes from oneself.
106|Here we will drag them, and through the mournful
107|forest will our bodies be hung,
108|each on the thornbush of its troublesome shade".
109|We were still attentive to the trunk,
110|believing that it wanted to tell us something else,
111|when we were surprised by a noise,
112|similarly to one who coming
113|hears the boar and the hunt to his post,
114|who hears the beasts, and the branches crashing.
115|And behold two on the left side,
116|naked and scratched, fleeing so hard,
117|that of the forest they broke every obstacle.
118|The one in front: "Now come, come, death!".
119|And the other, to whom it seemed he was too slow,
120|was shouting: "Lano, not so nimble were
121|your legs at the jousts of the Toppo!".
122|And then, because perhaps his breath was failing him,
123|of himself and of a bush he made one tangle.
124|Behind them the forest was full
125|of black bitches, greedy and running
126|like greyhounds that came out of their chain.
127|On that one who crouched they put their teeth,
128|and they tore him apart piece by piece;
129|then they carried away those suffering limbs.
130|My escort then took me by the hand,
131|and led me to the bush that was weeping
132|through its bleeding breaks in vain.
133|"O Iacopo", it was saying, "da Santo Andrea,
134|what has it helped you to make of me a shield?
135|what blame have I for your guilty life?".
136|When the master had stopped over it,
137|he said: "Who were you, who through so many tips
138|blow out with blood a sorrowful speech?".
139|And he to us: "O souls who have arrived
140|to see the dishonorable rending
141|which has so disjoined my leaves from me,
142|gather them at the foot of the sad bush.
143|I was of the city that for the Baptist
144|changed its first patron; whence he for this
145|always with his art will make it sad;
146|and were it not that on the crossing of the Arno
147|there remains still of him some image,
148|those citizens who later refounded it
149|upon the ash that was left from Attila,
150|would have made the work in vain.
151|I made a gibbet for myself of my own houses".
