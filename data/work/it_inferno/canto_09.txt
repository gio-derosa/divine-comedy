1|That color which cowardice painted on me outside
2|seeing my leader turn back,
3|more quickly made him press within his new one.
4|Attentive he stopped like a man who listens;
5|for the eye could not lead him far
6|through the black air and through the thick fog.
7|"Yet it will be fitting for us to win the fight",
8|he began, "if not ... Such a one offered herself to us.
9|Oh how long it seems to me until another arrives here!".
10|I saw well how he covered up
11|the beginning with the other that came after,
12|for they were words different from the first;
13|but nonetheless his speech gave me fear,
14|because I drew the broken-off word
15|perhaps to a worse meaning than it held.
16|"Into this bottom of the sad hollow
17|does anyone ever descend from the first grade,
18|which for punishment has only hope cut off?".
19|This question I made; and he: "Seldom
20|it happens", he answered me, "that of us
21|anyone makes the journey on which I go.
22|True it is that another time I was down here,
23|conjured by that cruel Erichtho
24|who called back the shades to their bodies.
25|Only a short while was my flesh bare of me,
26|when she made me enter inside that wall,
27|to draw out from there a spirit from the circle of Judas.
28|That is the lowest place and the darkest,
29|and the farthest from the heaven that turns all:
30|well do I know the way; therefore make yourself secure.
31|This marsh which breathes out the great stench
32|girds around the sorrowful city,
33|where we cannot enter now without anger".
34|And other things he said, but I do not have it in mind;
35|because the eye had drawn me wholly
36|toward the high tower with the glowing top,
37|where in an instant were risen up quickly
38|three infernal furies stained with blood,
39|who had the limbs and bearing of women,
40|and with hydras very green were girt;
41|little serpents and horned snakes they had for hair,
42|with which their fierce temples were bound.
43|And he, who well knew the handmaids
44|of the queen of the eternal lament,
45|"Look", he said to me, "at the fierce Erinyes.
46|This is Megaera on the left side;
47|she who weeps on the right is Alecto;
48|Tisiphone is in the middle"; and he fell silent at that.
49|With their nails each was tearing her breast;
50|they beat themselves with palms and cried so loud,
51|that I pressed close to the poet for fear.
52|"Let Medusa come: so we will make him into stone",
53|they were all saying, looking down;
54|"ill did we not avenge on Theseus the assault".
55|"Turn yourself backward and keep your face closed;
56|for if the Gorgon shows herself and you should see it,
57|there would be no returning ever above".
58|So said the master; and he himself
59|turned me, and did not trust to my hands,
60|so that with his own too he did not close me.
61|O you who have sound intellects,
62|gaze at the doctrine that hides itself
63|under the veil of the strange verses.
64|And already there was coming over the turbid waves
65|a crash of a sound, full of terror,
66|at which both the banks were trembling,
67|not otherwise made than of a wind
68|impetuous because of the opposing heats,
69|which strikes the forest and without any restraint
70|the branches it shatters, beats down and carries off;
71|in front dusty it goes proudly,
72|and makes the beasts and the shepherds flee.
73|He loosed my eyes and said: "Now direct the nerve
74|of sight over that ancient foam
75|there where that smoke is most bitter".
76|As the frogs before the hostile
77|snake through the water all vanish,
78|until on the ground each one squats in a heap,
79|I saw more than a thousand ruined souls
80|flee thus before one who at the crossing
81|was passing the Styx with dry soles.
82|From his face he was removing that thick air,
83|moving his left hand before him often;
84|and only of that annoyance did he seem weary.
85|Well I perceived that he was sent from heaven,
86|and I turned to the master; and he made a sign
87|that I should stay quiet and bow to him.
88|Ah how full of disdain he seemed to me!
89|He came to the gate and with a little wand
90|he opened it, for there was no resistance there.
91|"O ones chased out of heaven, despised people",
92|he began upon the horrible threshold,
93|"whence does this insolence nestle in you?
94|Why do you kick against that will
95|from which the end can never be cut off,
96|and which many times has increased your pain?
97|What does it help to butt against the fates?
98|Your Cerberus, if you remember well,
99|still bears for it his chin and throat peeled bare".
100|Then he turned back along the filthy road,
101|and made no word to us, but made the semblance
102|of a man whom other care presses and bites
103|than that of the one who is before him;
104|and we moved our feet toward the city,
105|secure after the holy words.
106|Within it we entered without any war;
107|and I, who had desire to look at
108|the condition that such a fortress locks in,
109|as soon as I was inside, send my eye around:
110|and I see on every hand a great plain,
111|full of grief and of cruel torment.
112|Just as at Arles, where the Rhone stagnates,
113|just as at Pola, near the Quarnaro
114|which closes Italy and bathes its borders,
115|the sepulchres make the whole place uneven,
116|so they did here on every side,
117|except that the manner there was more bitter;
118|for among the tombs flames were scattered,
119|by which they were so entirely heated,
120|that iron more so no craft requires.
121|All their lids were raised,
122|and out of them came such harsh laments,
123|that they well seemed of wretched and of wounded ones.
124|And I: "Master, who are those people
125|who, buried inside those chests,
126|make themselves heard with the sorrowful sighs?".
127|And he to me: "Here are the heresiarchs
128|with their followers, of every sect, and much
129|more than you believe are the tombs laden.
130|Like with like here is buried,
131|and the monuments are more and less hot".
132|And after he had turned to the right hand,
133|we passed between the torments and the high battlements.
