1|While I was fearful because of my extinguished sight,
2|from the effulgent flame that extinguished it
3|came out a breath that made me attentive,
4|saying: "While you recover the sense
5|of the sight which you have consumed on me,
6|it is well that by discoursing you compensate for it.
7|Begin then; and say where is aimed
8|your soul, and reckon that
9|the sight in you is bewildered and not dead:
10|because the lady who through this divine
11|region leads you, has in her gaze
12|the power that the hand of Ananias had".
13|I said: "At her pleasure either soon or late
14|let remedy come to my eyes, which were the doors
15|when she entered with the fire with which I always burn.
16|The good that makes this court content,
17|is Alpha and O of all the scripture
18|that Love reads to me either lightly or loudly".
19|That same voice which the fear
20|of the sudden dazzlement had taken from me,
21|put me in the concern of discoursing further;
22|and said: "Surely through a narrower sieve
23|you must clarify: you must tell
24|who directed your bow to such a target".
25|And I: "By philosophical arguments
26|and by authority that descends from here
27|such love must be imprinted in me:
28|for the good, in so far as good, as it is understood,
29|so kindles love, and so much the greater
30|as it comprehends more goodness in itself.
31|Therefore to the essence where there is such advantage,
32|that every good which is found outside of it
33|is nothing else but a light of its ray,
34|more than to any other it behooves to move
35|the mind, in loving, of everyone who discerns
36|the truth on which this proof is founded.
37|Such truth to my intellect is laid out by
38|him who demonstrates to me the first love
39|of all the everlasting substances.
40|It is laid out by the voice of the truthful author,
41|who says to Moses, speaking of himself:
42|'I will make you see all goodness'.
43|You lay it out for me also, beginning
44|the high proclamation that cries the mystery
45|of here down there above every other herald".
46|And I heard: "By human intellect
47|and by authorities concordant with it
48|of your loves the sovereign one looks to God.
49|But say further if you feel other cords
50|draw you toward him, so that you may declare
51|with how many teeth this love bites you".
52|The holy intention was not hidden
53|of the eagle of Christ, rather I perceived
54|where he wanted to lead my profession.
55|Therefore I began again: "All those bites
56|which can make the heart turn to God,
57|have concurred in my charity:
58|for the being of the world and my being,
59|the death that he endured so that I may live,
60|and that which every faithful one hopes as I do,
61|with the aforementioned living knowledge,
62|have drawn me from the sea of twisted love,
63|and of the straight have set me on the shore.
64|The leaves with which all the garden is leafed
65|of the eternal gardener, I love as much
66|as good is borne from him to them".
67|As soon as I fell silent, a most sweet song
68|resounded through the heaven, and my lady
69|said with the others: "Holy, holy, holy!".
70|And as at a sharp light one is awakened
71|because of the visual spirit that runs
72|to the splendor that goes from membrane to membrane,
73|and the awakened one shrinks from what he sees,
74|so unknowing is the sudden waking
75|until the judging faculty comes to help;
76|so from my eyes every speck
77|Beatrice chased away with the ray of hers,
78|which shone more than a thousand miles:
79|so that I saw better than before then;
80|and almost stupefied I asked
81|about a fourth light that I saw among us.
82|And my lady: "Within those rays
83|gazes with love upon his maker the first soul
84|that the first power ever created".
85|As the bough that bends its top
86|in the passing of the wind, and then lifts itself
87|by its own power that raises it up,
88|so did I, while she was speaking,
89|marveling, and then made me secure again
90|a desire to speak with which I was burning.
91|And I began: "O fruit that ripe
92|alone were produced, O ancient father
93|to whom each bride is daughter and daughter-in-law,
94|devoutly as much as I can I beseech you
95|to speak to me: you see my wish,
96|and to hear you sooner I do not say it".
97|Sometimes an animal covered stirs,
98|so that its feeling must appear
99|by the following that its wrapping makes to it;
100|and similarly the first soul
101|made appear to me through its covering
102|how gay it came to please me.
103|Then it breathed: "Without its being offered to me
104|by you, I discern your wish better
105|than you whatever thing is most certain to you;
106|because I see it in the truthful mirror
107|which makes itself the likeness of other things,
108|and nothing makes itself the likeness of it.
109|You want to hear how long it is since God placed me
110|in the lofty garden, where she
111|prepared you for so long a stair,
112|and how long it was a delight to my eyes,
113|and the proper cause of the great disdain,
114|and the idiom that I used and that I made.
115|Now, my son, not the tasting of the tree
116|was in itself the cause of so great an exile,
117|but only the overstepping of the mark.
118|There whence your lady moved Virgil,
119|four thousand three hundred and two revolutions
120|of the sun I desired this council;
121|and I saw it return to all the lights
122|of its road nine hundred thirty
123|times, while I was on earth.
124|The tongue that I spoke was all extinct
125|before to the unaccomplishable work
126|the people of Nimrod were attentive:
127|for no product ever of reason,
128|because of human pleasure which renews itself
129|following the heaven, was ever durable.
130|It is a natural work that man speaks;
131|but thus or thus, nature leaves
132|then to you to do according as it pleases you.
133|Before I descended to the infernal anguish,
134|I was called on earth the highest good
135|whence comes the gladness that swathes me;
136|and El it was called afterward: and this is fitting,
137|for the usage of mortals is like a leaf
138|on a branch, which goes away and another comes.
139|On the mountain that rises highest from the wave,
140|I was, with life pure and dishonored,
141|from the first hour to the one that follows,
142|when the sun changes quadrant, the sixth hour".
