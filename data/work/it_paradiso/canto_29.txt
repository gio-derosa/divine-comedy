1|When both the children of Latona,
2|covered by the Ram and by the Scales,
3|make of the horizon together a belt,
4|as long as it is from the moment when the zenith balances them
5|until the one and the other from that girdle,
6|changing hemisphere, unbalances itself,
7|so long, with her face painted with a smile,
8|was Beatrice silent, gazing
9|fixedly at the point that had vanquished me.
10|Then she began: "I tell, and do not ask,
11|what you want to hear, because I have seen it
12|there where every where and every when is focused.
13|Not to have for himself acquisition of good,
14|which cannot be, but so that his splendor
15|might, shining back, say "I subsist",
16|in his eternity outside of time,
17|outside of every other comprehending, as it pleased him,
18|opened itself into new loves the eternal love.
19|Nor before did he lie as if torpid;
20|for neither before nor after proceeded
21|the moving of God upon these waters.
22|Form and matter, conjoined and pure,
23|came forth into being which had no flaw,
24|as from a three-stringed bow three arrows.
25|And as in glass, in amber or in crystal
26|a ray shines so, that from its coming
27|to its being whole there is no interval,
28|so the triform effect from its lord
29|into its being rayed all together
30|without distinction in its beginning.
31|Concreated was order and constituted
32|for the substances; and those were the summit
33|in the world in which pure act was produced;
34|pure potency held the lowest part;
35|in the middle potency with act bound
36|such a bond, that it is never unbound.
37|Jerome wrote to you of a long stretch
38|of centuries of the angels created
39|before the rest of the world was made;
40|but this truth is written in many places
41|by the writers of the Holy Spirit,
42|and you will become aware of it if you look well;
43|and also reason sees it somewhat,
44|which would not concede that the movers
45|would be without their perfection so long.
46|Now you know where and when these loves
47|were created and how: so that extinguished
48|in your desire already are three ardors.
49|Nor would one reach, counting, to twenty
50|so quickly, as part of the angels
51|disturbed the substrate of your elements.
52|The other remained, and began this art
53|which you discern, with such great delight,
54|that it never departs from circling.
55|The beginning of the fall was the accursed
56|pride of him whom you saw
57|constrained by all the weights of the world.
58|Those whom you see here were modest
59|in recognizing themselves as from the goodness
60|which had made them ready for so much understanding:
61|so that their sights were exalted
62|with illuminating grace and with their merit,
63|so that they have firm and full will;
64|and I do not want you to doubt, but be certain,
65|that to receive grace is meritorious
66|according as the affection is open to it.
67|Now around this consistory
68|you can contemplate much, if my words
69|are gathered up, without other help.
70|But because on earth through your schools
71|it is taught that the angelic nature
72|is such, that it understands and remembers and wills,
73|I will speak further, so that you may see pure
74|the truth which down there is confused,
75|by equivocating in such teaching.
76|These substances, since they were joyous
77|in the face of God, have not turned their sight
78|from it, from which nothing is hidden:
79|therefore they do not have their seeing interrupted
80|by a new object, and therefore they do not need
81|to remember through divided concept;
82|so that down there, not sleeping, people dream,
83|believing and not believing they speak the truth;
84|but in the one is more guilt and more shame.
85|You down there do not go along one path
86|in philosophizing: so much does it carry you away
87|the love of appearance and the thought of it!
88|And even this up here is borne
89|with less disdain than when is set aside
90|the divine Scripture or when it is twisted.
91|They do not think there how much blood it costs
92|to sow it in the world and how much he pleases
93|who humbly draws near to it.
94|To make a show each one strives and makes
95|his own inventions; and those are gone over
96|by the preachers and the Gospel is silent.
97|One says that the moon turned back
98|in the passion of Christ and interposed itself,
99|so that the light of the sun did not reach down;
100|and he lies, for the light hid itself
101|of itself: so to the Spaniards and to the Indians
102|as to the Jews such an eclipse corresponded.
103|Florence does not have so many Lapos and Bindos
104|as there are such fables in a year
105|shouted from the pulpit on this side and that:
106|so that the little sheep, who do not know,
107|return from the pasture fed with wind,
108|and not seeing the harm does not excuse them.
109|Christ did not say to his first company:
110|'Go, and preach to the world idle chatter';
111|but he gave them a true foundation;
112|and that alone sounded in their cheeks,
113|so that to fight to kindle the faith
114|they made of the Gospel shield and lances.
115|Now one goes with jests and with buffooneries
116|to preach, and as long as there is a good laugh,
117|the hood swells and nothing more is required.
118|But such a bird nests in the tip of the hood,
119|that if the common folk saw it, they would see
120|the pardon in which they trust:
121|through which such foolishness has grown on earth,
122|that, without proof of any testimony,
123|people would run to every promise.
124|On this Saint Anthony fattens his pig,
125|and many others who are yet more pigs,
126|paying with money without stamp.
127|But since we have digressed enough, turn back
128|your eyes now toward the straight road,
129|so that the way may be shortened with the time.
130|This nature so far goes up in grades
131|in number, that there was never speech
132|nor mortal concept that goes so far;
133|and if you look at what is revealed
134|through Daniel, you will see that in his thousands
135|a determined number is hidden.
136|The first light, which irradiates it all,
137|in as many ways is received in it,
138|as are the splendors with which it pairs itself.
139|Whence, since the act that conceives
140|is followed by affection, the sweetness of love
141|diversely in it glows and is lukewarm.
142|See now the height and the breadth
143|of the eternal worth, since so many
144|mirrors it has made for itself in which it is broken,
145|remaining one in itself as before".
