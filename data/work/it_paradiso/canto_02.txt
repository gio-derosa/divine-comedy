1|O you who are in a little boat,
2|desirous to listen, following
3|behind my ship that singing crosses over,
4|turn back to see again your shores:
5|do not put yourselves out on the open sea, for perhaps,
6|losing me, you would remain lost.
7|The water that I take was never yet run;
8|Minerva breathes, and Apollo guides me,
9|and nine Muses point out to me the Bears.
10|You other few who lifted up your neck
11|in time to the bread of the angels, on which
12|one lives here but does not come away sated from it,
13|you can well put out over the deep salt
14|your vessel, keeping my furrow
15|ahead of the water that turns back equal.
16|Those glorious ones who passed over to Colchis
17|did not marvel as you will do,
18|when they saw Jason made a plowman.
19|The concreated and perpetual thirst
20|for the deiform kingdom was carrying us
21|swift almost as the heaven you see.
22|Beatrice upward, and I at her was looking;
23|and perhaps in as much time as a bolt rests
24|and flies and from the nut is unlatched,
25|arrived I saw myself where a wondrous thing
26|twisted my face to itself; and therefore she
27|from whom my care could not be hidden,
28|turned toward me, as glad as beautiful,
29|"Direct your mind to God grateful", she said to me,
30|"who has conjoined us with the first star".
31|It seemed to me that a cloud covered us
32|lucid, thick, solid and polished,
33|like adamant that the sun struck.
34|Within itself the eternal pearl
35|received us, as water receives
36|a ray of light remaining united.
37|If I was body, and here one does not conceive
38|how one dimension another endured,
39|which must be if body into body creeps,
40|it ought to kindle us more the desire
41|to see that essence in which one sees
42|how our nature and God united.
43|There will be seen what we hold by faith,
44|not demonstrated, but it will be known of itself
45|in the manner of the first truth that man believes.
46|I answered: "My lady, as devout
47|as I can most be, I thank him
48|who from the mortal world has removed me.
49|But tell me: what are the dark marks
50|of this body, which down there on earth
51|make others tell fables of Cain?".
52|She smiled somewhat, and then "If it errs
53|the opinion", she said to me, "of mortals
54|where the key of sense does not unlock,
55|surely the arrows ought not to prick you
56|of wonder now, since behind the senses
57|you see that reason has short wings.
58|But tell me what you by yourself think of it".
59|And I: "That which appears to us up here diverse
60|I believe is made by bodies rare and dense".
61|And she: "Surely enough you will see submerged
62|in the false your belief, if you listen well
63|to the arguing that I will make adverse to it.
64|The eighth sphere shows you many
65|lights, which both in the quality and in the quantity
66|can be noted as of different faces.
67|If rare and dense did this so much,
68|one single power would be in all,
69|more and less distributed and equally.
70|Different powers must be fruits
71|of formal principles, and those, except one,
72|would follow, by your reasoning, destroyed.
73|Again, if rarity were of that dark
74|the cause that you ask about, either through and through in part
75|would be of its matter so fasting
76|this planet, or, just as divides
77|the fat and the lean a body, so this
78|in its volume would change pages.
79|If the first were so, it would be manifest
80|in the eclipse of the sun, by shining through
81|the light as in other rare matter taken in.
82|This is not so: therefore one must look
83|at the other; and if it happens that I cancel the other,
84|falsified will be your opinion.
85|If it is that this rare does not pass through,
86|there must be a limit from where
87|its contrary does not let it pass further;
88|and from there the other's ray is poured back
89|just as color returns through glass
90|which behind itself hides lead.
91|Now you will say that it shows itself dark
92|there the ray more than in other parts,
93|by being there reflected farther back.
94|From this objection can deliver you
95|experience, if ever you try it,
96|which is wont to be the fount for the streams of your arts.
97|Three mirrors you will take; and two you set away
98|from you in one way, and the other, more removed,
99|between both the first your eyes find again.
100|Turned toward them, see that behind your back
101|there stands for you a light that kindles the three mirrors
102|and returns to you from all struck back.
103|Although in the quantity so much does not extend
104|the more distant sight, there you will see
105|how it must equally shine.
106|Now, as under the blows of the warm rays
107|of the snow remains bare the subject
108|both of the color and of the cold of before,
109|so you remaining so in the intellect
110|I want to inform with light so vivid,
111|that it will tremble for you in its aspect.
112|Within the heaven of the divine peace
113|turns a body in whose power
114|the being of all its content lies.
115|The following heaven, which has so many sights,
116|that being divides among diverse essences,
117|from it distinct and by it contained.
118|The other spheres by various differences
119|the distinctions that they have within themselves
120|dispose to their ends and their seeds.
121|These organs of the world thus go,
122|as you see now, from grade to grade,
123|so that from above they take and below they make.
124|Look well now at how I go
125|through this place to the truth that you desire,
126|so that afterwards you may know alone to keep the ford.
127|The motion and the power of the holy circles,
128|as from the smith the art of the hammer,
129|from the blessed movers must breathe;
130|and the heaven which so many lights make beautiful,
131|from the profound mind that turns it
132|takes the image and makes itself the seal of it.
133|And as the soul within your dust
134|through different members and conformed
135|to diverse powers resolves itself,
136|so the intelligence its goodness
137|multiplied through the stars unfolds,
138|turning itself upon its unity.
139|Different power makes different alloy
140|with the precious body that it quickens,
141|in which, just as life in you, it binds itself.
142|Through the glad nature from which it derives,
143|the power mixed through the body shines
144|as gladness through a living pupil.
145|From it comes what from light to light
146|seems different, not from dense and rare;
147|it is the formal principle that produces,
148|conformed to its goodness, the dim and the bright".
