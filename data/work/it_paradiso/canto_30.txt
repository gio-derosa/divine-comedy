1|Perhaps six thousand miles away
2|burns the sixth hour, and this world
3|already inclines its shadow almost to the level bed,
4|when the middle of the heaven, deep above us,
5|begins to become such, that some star
6|loses its appearing down to this depth;
7|and as comes the brightest handmaid
8|of the sun further on, so the heaven closes
9|from sight to sight up to the most beautiful.
10|Not otherwise the triumph that plays
11|always around the point that vanquished me,
12|seeming enclosed by that which it encloses,
13|little by little faded from my seeing:
14|so that to return with my eyes to Beatrice
15|seeing nothing and love constrained me.
16|If as much as up to here is said of her
17|were all enclosed in one praise,
18|it would be too little to furnish this turn.
19|The beauty that I saw goes beyond measure
20|not only beyond us, but certainly I believe
21|that only its maker enjoys it wholly.
22|At this pass I concede myself vanquished
23|more than ever by a point of his theme
24|was overcome a comic or tragic poet:
25|for, as the sun in the sight that most trembles,
26|so the remembering of the sweet smile
27|deprives my mind of itself.
28|From the first day when I saw her face
29|in this life, up to this sight,
30|the following has not been cut off from my singing;
31|but now my following must desist
32|further behind her beauty, in poetizing,
33|as at his utmost every artist.
34|Such as I leave her to a greater proclamation
35|than that of my trumpet, which draws
36|its arduous matter to a close,
37|with the gesture and voice of a guide whose task is done
38|she began again: "We have come forth
39|from the greatest body to the heaven which is pure light:
40|intellectual light, full of love;
41|love of true good, full of gladness;
42|gladness that transcends every sweetness.
43|Here you will see the one and the other soldiery
44|of paradise, and the one in those aspects
45|which you will see at the last judgment".
46|As a sudden flash that scatters
47|the visual spirits, so that it deprives
48|the eye of the action of stronger objects,
49|so there shone around me living light,
50|and left me swathed with such a veil
51|of its brightness, that nothing appeared to me.
52|"Always the love that quiets this heaven
53|welcomes into itself with such a greeting,
54|to make the candle ready for its flame".
55|No sooner had come within me
56|these brief words, than I understood
57|myself to be surmounting above my power;
58|and with new sight I was rekindled
59|such, that no light is so pure,
60|that my eyes could not have defended themselves from it;
61|and I saw light in the form of a river
62|glowing with brightness, between two banks
63|painted with wondrous springtime.
64|From such a stream came forth living sparks,
65|and on every side they settled in the flowers,
66|like rubies that gold encircles;
67|then, as if inebriated by the odors,
68|they plunged themselves again into the wondrous whirlpool,
69|and if one entered, another came out of it.
70|"The high desire that now inflames and urges you,
71|to have knowledge of what you see,
72|pleases me the more the more it swells;
73|but of this water you must drink
74|before so great a thirst in you is sated":
75|thus said to me the sun of my eyes.
76|She also added: "The river and the topazes
77|that enter and come out and the laughing of the grasses
78|are of their truth shadowy prefaces.
79|Not that these things are of themselves unripe;
80|but it is a defect on your part,
81|who do not yet have sight so exalted".
82|There is no infant that so suddenly rushes
83|with its face toward the milk, if it wakes
84|much later than its custom,
85|as I did, to make better mirrors
86|still of my eyes, bending down to the wave
87|which flows forth so that one may be bettered in it;
88|and as soon as of it drank the eaves
89|of my eyelids, so it seemed to me
90|from its length to have become round.
91|Then, like people who have been under masks,
92|who seem other than before, if they strip off
93|the semblance not their own in which they disappeared,
94|so there changed for me into greater festivities
95|the flowers and the sparks, so that I saw
96|both the courts of heaven manifest.
97|O splendor of God, through which I saw
98|the high triumph of the true kingdom,
99|give me power to tell how I saw it!
100|There is light up there which makes visible
101|the creator to that creature
102|which only in seeing him has its peace.
103|It extends itself in circular figure,
104|so far that its circumference
105|would be to the sun too wide a girdle.
106|All its appearance is made of a ray
107|reflected from the top of the first moving heaven,
108|which takes from it life and power.
109|And as a slope in water at its foot
110|mirrors itself, as if to see itself adorned,
111|when it is rich in green and in little flowers,
112|so, standing above the light round and round,
113|I saw mirrored in more than a thousand tiers
114|all of us that have made return up there.
115|And if the lowest step gathers in itself
116|so great a light, how great is the breadth
117|of this rose in its outermost petals!
118|My sight in the breadth and in the height
119|did not lose itself, but took in all
120|the quantity and the quality of that joy.
121|Near and far, there, neither add nor take away:
122|for where God governs without intermediary,
123|natural law has no relevance.
124|Into the yellow of the everlasting rose,
125|which rises in steps and expands and breathes forth
126|an odor of praise to the sun that makes perpetual spring,
127|like one who is silent and wants to speak,
128|Beatrice drew me, and said: "Look
129|how great is the assembly of the white robes!
130|See our city how far it circles;
131|see our seats so filled,
132|that few people more are desired here.
133|And in that great seat on which you keep your eyes
134|because of the crown that is already placed upon it,
135|before you sup at these nuptials,
136|will sit the soul, which will be imperial down there,
137|of the high Henry, who to set Italy straight
138|will come before she is ready.
139|The blind greed that bewitches you
140|has made you similar to the little child
141|who dies of hunger and drives away the nurse.
142|And there will be prefect in the divine court
143|then such a one, who openly and covertly
144|will not go with him along one road.
145|But little after will he be suffered by God
146|in the holy office; for he will be thrust down
147|there where Simon Magus is for his desert,
148|and will make him of Anagni go further down".
