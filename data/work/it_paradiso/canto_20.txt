1|When he who illumines all the world
2|from our hemisphere so descends,
3|that the day on every side is consumed,
4|the heaven, which before is kindled by him alone,
5|suddenly makes itself visible again
6|through many lights, in which one shines;
7|and this act of the heaven came to my mind,
8|when the sign of the world and of its leaders
9|in the blessed beak became silent;
10|because all those living lights,
11|shining far more, began songs
12|from my memory slipping and fallen.
13|O sweet love that cloaks yourself with a smile,
14|how burning you seemed in those flutes,
15|which had the breath only of holy thoughts!
16|After the dear and shining stones
17|with which I saw bejeweled the sixth light
18|imposed silence on the angelic chimes,
19|I seemed to hear a murmuring of a river
20|that descends clear down from stone to stone,
21|showing the abundance of its summit.
22|And as sound at the neck of the cithern
23|takes its form, and just as at the vent
24|of the bagpipe the wind that enters,
25|so, the delay of waiting removed,
26|that murmuring of the eagle rose
27|up through the neck, as if it were hollow.
28|It became a voice there, and from there it came out
29|through its beak in the form of words,
30|such as the heart awaited where I wrote them.
31|"The part in me which sees and endures the sun
32|in mortal eagles", it began to me,
33|"now must be gazed upon fixedly,
34|because of the fires of which I make my figure,
35|those with which the eye in my head sparkles,
36|they of all their grades are the highest.
37|He who shines in the middle as pupil,
38|was the singer of the Holy Spirit,
39|who carried the ark from town to town:
40|now he knows the merit of his song,
41|in so far as it was the effect of his counsel,
42|by the reward which is equal to it.
43|Of the five that make a circle for me as brow,
44|he who draws nearest to my beak,
45|consoled the poor widow for her son:
46|now he knows how dear it costs
47|not to follow Christ, by the experience
48|of this sweet life and of the opposite.
49|And he who follows in the circumference
50|of which I speak, along the upper arc,
51|delayed death through true penitence:
52|now he knows that the eternal judgment
53|is not changed, when worthy prayer
54|makes down there tomorrow of today.
55|The next who follows, with the laws and with me,
56|under a good intention that bore bad fruit,
57|to yield to the pastor made himself Greek:
58|now he knows how the evil derived
59|from his good working is not harmful to him,
60|although the world be destroyed by it.
61|And him whom you see in the downward arc,
62|was William, whom that land deplores
63|which weeps for Charles and Frederick living:
64|now he knows how enamored
65|the heaven is of a just king, and by the semblance
66|of his brightness makes it seen still.
67|Who would believe down in the erring world
68|that Ripheus the Trojan in this circle
69|was the fifth of the holy lights?
70|Now he knows much of that which the world
71|cannot see of the divine grace,
72|although his sight does not discern the bottom".
73|Like a little lark that ranges in the air
74|first singing, and then falls silent content
75|with the last sweetness that sates it,
76|such seemed to me the image of the imprint
77|of the eternal pleasure, by whose desire
78|each thing becomes what it is.
79|And although I was, with regard to my doubt,
80|there like glass to the color that clothes it,
81|to wait for time in silence it did not suffer,
82|but from my mouth, "What things are these?",
83|it pushed out with the force of its weight:
84|so that I saw great festivities of flashing.
85|Then after, with its eye more kindled,
86|the blessed sign answered me
87|so as not to keep me suspended in wonder:
88|"I see that you believe these things
89|because I say them, but you do not see how;
90|so that, if they are believed, they are hidden.
91|You do as one who the thing by name
92|apprehends well, but its quiddity
93|cannot see if another does not bring it out.
94|The kingdom of the heavens suffers violence
95|from warm love and from living hope,
96|which vanquishes the divine will:
97|not in the manner that man overpowers man,
98|but vanquishes it because it wants to be vanquished,
99|and, vanquished, vanquishes with its benevolence.
100|The first life of the brow and the fifth
101|make you marvel, because you see with them
102|the region of the angels painted.
103|From their bodies they did not go out, as you believe,
104|Gentiles, but Christians, in firm faith
105|the one of the feet to suffer and the other of the feet that had suffered.
106|For the one from hell, where one never returns
107|to good will, came back to his bones;
108|and this was the reward of living hope:
109|of living hope, which put the power
110|into the prayers made to God to raise him,
111|so that his will could be moved.
112|The glorious soul of which I speak,
113|returned into the flesh, in which it was a short time,
114|believed in him who was able to help it;
115|and believing it was kindled in such fire
116|of true love, that at the second death
117|it was worthy to come to this joy.
118|The other, through grace which from so deep
119|a fountain wells, that never did creature
120|push its eye as far as the first wave,
121|all his love down there set on righteousness:
122|so that, from grace to grace, God opened
123|his eye to our future redemption;
124|whereupon he believed in it, and did not suffer
125|from then on the stench of paganism any more;
126|and he reproved for it the perverse peoples.
127|Those three ladies served him for baptism
128|whom you saw by the right wheel,
129|more than a thousand years before baptizing.
130|O predestination, how remote
131|is your root from those sights
132|which do not see the first cause entire!
133|And you, mortals, keep yourselves restrained
134|in judging: for we, who see God,
135|do not yet know all the elect;
136|and such a lack is sweet to us,
137|because our good in this good is refined,
138|that what God wills, we also will".
139|Thus by that divine image,
140|to make clear my short sight,
141|was given to me a gentle medicine.
142|And as to a good singer a good lutenist
143|makes follow the quivering of the string,
144|whereby the song acquires more pleasure,
145|so, while it spoke, so I remember
146|that I saw the two blessed lights,
147|just as the blinking of eyes accords,
148|move their little flames with the words.
