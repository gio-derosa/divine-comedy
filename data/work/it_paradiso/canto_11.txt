1|O senseless care of mortals,
2|how defective are the syllogisms
3|that make you beat your wings downward!
4|One after law and one after aphorisms
5|was going, and one pursuing priesthood,
6|and one to reign by force or by sophistries,
7|and one to rob and one civil business,
8|one in the delight of the flesh entangled
9|was toiling and one was giving himself to idleness,
10|when, from all these things set free,
11|with Beatrice I was up in heaven
12|so gloriously welcomed.
13|After each one had returned to the
14|point of the circle in which it had been before,
15|it stopped, like a candle in a candlestick.
16|And I heard within that light
17|which first had spoken to me, smiling
18|begin, making itself more pure:
19|"Just as I shine with its ray,
20|so, gazing into the eternal light,
21|your thoughts, whence they arise, I apprehend.
22|You are in doubt, and have a wish that should be sifted again
23|in so open and in so extended language
24|my saying, so that it may be leveled to your understanding,
25|where before I said: "Where one fattens well",
26|and there where I said: "There was never born a second";
27|and here there is need that one distinguish well.
28|The providence, which governs the world
29|with that counsel in which every sight
30|created is vanquished before it goes to the bottom,
31|so that she might go toward her delight
32|the bride of him who with loud cries
33|espoused her with the blessed blood,
34|secure in herself and also more faithful to him,
35|two princes ordained in her favor,
36|who on this side and that might be her guides.
37|The one was all seraphic in ardor;
38|the other through wisdom on earth was
39|of cherubic light a splendor.
40|Of the one I will speak, because of both
41|one speaks in praising one, whichever one takes,
42|because to one end were their works.
43|Between Tupino and the water that descends
44|from the hill chosen by the blessed Ubaldo,
45|a fertile slope of a high mountain hangs,
46|whence Perugia feels cold and heat
47|from Porta Sole; and behind it weep
48|for heavy yoke Nocera with Gualdo.
49|From this slope, there where it breaks
50|most its steepness, was born to the world a sun,
51|as this one does at times from the Ganges.
52|So whoever makes words of that place,
53|let him not say Ascesi, for he would say short,
54|but Orient, if he wants to speak properly.
55|He was not yet very far from his rising,
56|when he began to make the earth feel
57|of his great virtue some comfort;
58|for for such a lady, as a youth, into war
59|with his father he ran, to whom, as to death,
60|the door of pleasure no one unlocks;
61|and before his spiritual court
62|and in the presence of his father he was united to her;
63|then from day to day he loved her more strongly.
64|She, deprived of her first husband,
65|eleven hundred years and more despised and obscure
66|until this one remained without invitation;
67|nor did it avail to hear that he found her secure
68|with Amyclas, at the sound of his voice,
69|he who made the whole world afraid;
70|nor did it avail to be constant and fierce,
71|so that, where Mary remained below,
72|she with Christ wept upon the cross.
73|But so that I do not proceed too closed,
74|Francis and Poverty for these lovers
75|take now in my extended speech.
76|Their concord and their glad looks,
77|love and marvel and sweet regard
78|made to be the cause of holy thoughts;
79|so much that the venerable Bernard
80|unshod himself first, and after so great a peace
81|ran and, running, it seemed to him he was slow.
82|Oh unknown wealth! oh fertile good!
83|Egidius unshoes himself, Sylvester unshoes himself
84|behind the bridegroom, so much the bride pleases.
85|Then goes forth that father and that master
86|with his lady and with that family
87|which already was binding the humble cord.
88|Nor did baseness of heart weigh down his brows
89|for being the son of Pietro Bernardone,
90|nor for seeming despised to a marvel;
91|but royally his hard intention
92|to Innocent he opened, and from him had
93|the first seal for his order.
94|After the poor folk increased
95|behind this one, whose wondrous life
96|would be better sung in the glory of heaven,
97|with a second crown was encircled
98|through Honorius by the Eternal Breath
99|the holy will of this archimandrite.
100|And after, through thirst for martyrdom,
101|in the proud presence of the Sultan
102|he preached Christ and the others who followed him,
103|and finding too unripe for conversion
104|the people and so as not to stay in vain,
105|he returned to the fruit of the Italian grass,
106|on the harsh rock between Tiber and Arno
107|from Christ he took the last seal,
108|which his limbs bore for two years.
109|When to him who allotted him to so much good
110|it pleased to draw him up to the reward
111|that he merited in making himself lowly,
112|to his brothers, as to rightful heirs,
113|he commended his dearest lady,
114|and commanded that they love her faithfully;
115|and from her bosom the illustrious soul
116|wished to move, returning to its kingdom,
117|and for its body wanted no other bier.
118|Think now what he was who as a worthy
119|colleague was to keep the bark
120|of Peter on the high sea on the right course;
121|and this was our patriarch;
122|so that whoever follows him, as he commands,
123|you can discern that he loads good merchandise.
124|But his flock for new food
125|has become greedy, so that it cannot be
126|that through different pastures it does not scatter;
127|and the more his sheep remote
128|and vagabond go from him,
129|the more they return to the fold empty of milk.
130|Indeed there are some of them that fear the harm
131|and press close to the shepherd; but they are so few,
132|that little cloth supplies the cowls.
133|Now, if my words are not faint,
134|if your hearing has been attentive,
135|if what is said you recall to mind,
136|in part your wish will be content,
137|because you will see the plant from which it splinters,
138|and you will see the correction that argues
139|"Where one fattens well, if one does not stray"".
