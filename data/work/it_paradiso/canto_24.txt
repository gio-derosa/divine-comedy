1|"O fellowship chosen for the great supper
2|of the blessed Lamb, who feeds you
3|so, that your wish is always full,
4|if by grace of God this man foretastes
5|of that which falls from your table,
6|before death prescribes his time,
7|give heed to his immense affection
8|and bedew him somewhat: you drink
9|always from the fountain whence comes that which he thinks".
10|Thus Beatrice; and those glad souls
11|made themselves spheres upon fixed poles,
12|flaming, turned, in the manner of comets.
13|And as wheels in the mechanism of clocks
14|turn so, that the first, to whoever pays attention,
15|seems quiet, and the last seems to fly;
16|so those carols, in different
17|ways dancing, of their richness
18|made me judge, swift and slow.
19|From that one which I noted of greatest beauty
20|I saw come out a fire so happy,
21|that it left none there of greater brightness;
22|and three times around Beatrice
23|it turned with a song so divine,
24|that my fantasy does not repeat it to me.
25|Therefore the pen leaps and I do not write it:
26|for our imagination for such folds,
27|not to mention speech, is too bright a color.
28|"O my holy sister who so pray to us
29|devoutly, by your ardent affection
30|you unbind me from that beautiful sphere".
31|Then, having stopped, the blessed fire
32|to my lady directed its breath,
33|which spoke thus as I have said.
34|And she: "O eternal light of the great man
35|to whom Our Lord left the keys,
36|which he carried down, of this wondrous joy,
37|test this man on points light and grave,
38|as it pleases you, concerning the faith,
39|by which you went upon the sea.
40|If he loves well and well hopes and believes,
41|it is not hidden from you, because you have your sight there
42|where every thing is seen depicted;
43|but because this kingdom has made citizens
44|through the true faith, to glorify it,
45|it is well that it fall to him to speak of it".
46|Just as the bachelor arms himself and does not speak
47|until the master proposes the question,
48|to argue it, not to settle it,
49|so I armed myself with every reason
50|while she was speaking, to be ready
51|for such a questioner and for such a profession.
52|"Speak, good Christian, make yourself manifest:
53|faith, what is it?". Whereupon I lifted my brow
54|toward that light from which this was breathed;
55|then I turned to Beatrice, and she prompt
56|signs made to me that I should pour
57|the water out from my inner fountain.
58|"May the Grace that grants me to confess myself",
59|I began, "to the high chief centurion,
60|make my concepts well expressed".
61|And I went on: "As the truthful pen
62|wrote of it, father, of your dear brother
63|who with you put Rome on the good path,
64|faith is the substance of things hoped for
65|and the argument of those not apparent;
66|and this seems to me its quiddity".
67|Then I heard: "Rightly you think,
68|if you understand well why he placed it
69|among the substances, and then among the arguments".
70|And I then: "The profound things
71|which grant me here their appearance,
72|to the eyes down there are so hidden,
73|that their being is there in belief alone,
74|upon which the high hope is founded;
75|and therefore it takes the meaning of substance.
76|And from this belief it behooves us
77|to syllogize, without having other sight:
78|therefore it holds the meaning of argument".
79|Then I heard: "If whatever is acquired
80|down there by doctrine, were so understood,
81|there would be no room there for the wit of the sophist".
82|Thus breathed forth that kindled love;
83|then it added: "Very well has been examined
84|of this coin already the alloy and the weight;
85|but tell me if you have it in your purse".
86|Whereupon I: "Yes, I have it, so shining and so round,
87|that in its stamp nothing is doubtful to me".
88|Next came forth from the deep light
89|that was shining there: "This dear jewel
90|upon which every virtue is founded,
91|whence did it come to you?". And I: "The copious rain
92|of the Holy Spirit, which is poured
93|upon the old and upon the new parchments,
94|is a syllogism that has concluded it for me
95|so acutely, that in comparison with it
96|every demonstration seems obtuse to me".
97|I heard then: "The old and the new
98|proposition which so concludes for you,
99|why do you hold it as divine speech?".
100|And I: "The proof that discloses the truth to me,
101|are the works that followed, for which nature
102|never heated iron nor beat anvil".
103|It was answered me: "Say, who assures you
104|that those works existed? That very same thing
105|which wants to be proved, no other, swears it to you".
106|"If the world turned itself to Christianity",
107|I said, "without miracles, this one
108|is such, that the others are not the hundredth part of it:
109|for you entered poor and fasting
110|into the field, to sow the good plant
111|which was once a vine and now has become a thorn".
112|This finished, the high holy court
113|resounded through the spheres with a 'God we praise'
114|in the melody that up there is sung.
115|And that baron who so from branch to branch,
116|examining, had already drawn me,
117|that we were approaching the last leaves,
118|began again: "The Grace, which woos
119|your mind, opened your mouth
120|up to here as it should be opened,
121|so that I approve what came forth;
122|but now you must express what you believe,
123|and whence it offered itself to your belief".
124|"O holy father, and spirit who see
125|what you believed so, that you outran
126|toward the sepulcher younger feet",
127|I began, "you want me to make manifest
128|here the form of my prompt believing,
129|and also the cause of it you asked.
130|And I answer: I believe in one God
131|alone and eternal, who moves all the heaven,
132|not moved, with love and with desire;
133|and for such belief I have not only proofs
134|physical and metaphysical, but it is given me
135|also by the truth that rains from here
136|through Moses, through prophets and through psalms,
137|through the Gospel and through you who wrote
138|after the ardent Spirit made you holy;
139|and I believe in three eternal persons, and these
140|I believe one essence so one and so triune,
141|that it allows joined 'are' and 'is'.
142|Of the profound divine condition
143|which I touch now, my mind is sealed
144|many times by the evangelical doctrine.
145|This is the beginning, this is the spark
146|that expands into a flame then vivid,
147|and like a star in heaven sparkles in me".
148|As the lord who hears what pleases him,
149|then embraces the servant, rejoicing
150|at the news, as soon as he falls silent;
151|so, blessing me as it sang,
152|three times encircled me, as soon as I fell silent,
153|the apostolic light at whose command
154|I had spoken: so much in speaking did I please it!
