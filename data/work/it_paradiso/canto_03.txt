1|That sun which first with love warmed my breast,
2|of beautiful truth had uncovered to me,
3|proving and disproving, the sweet aspect;
4|and I, to confess corrected and certain
5|myself, as much as was fitting
6|raised my head more upright to speak;
7|but a vision appeared that held
8|me to itself so tightly, to be seen,
9|that of my confession I did not remember.
10|As through glasses transparent and polished,
11|or else through waters clear and tranquil,
12|not so deep that the bottoms are lost,
13|return the outlines of our faces
14|so faint, that a pearl on a white forehead
15|comes not less strongly to our pupils;
16|such I saw many faces ready to speak;
17|so that I ran into the error contrary
18|to that which kindled love between the man and the fountain.
19|Suddenly as soon as I became aware of them,
20|judging them to be mirrored semblances,
21|to see whose they were, I turned my eyes;
22|and saw nothing, and turned them forward again
23|straight into the light of the sweet guide,
24|who, smiling, was burning in her holy eyes.
25|"Do not marvel because I smile",
26|she said to me, "following your childish thought,
27|since upon the truth your foot does not yet trust,
28|but turns you, as it is wont, to emptiness:
29|true substances are what you see,
30|relegated here for failing in a vow.
31|So speak with them and hear and believe;
32|for the true light that satisfies them
33|does not let them turn their feet from itself".
34|And I to the shade that seemed most eager
35|to talk, directed myself, and began,
36|almost like a man whom too much wanting dismays:
37|"O well-created spirit, who in the rays
38|of eternal life feel the sweetness
39|which, not tasted, is never understood,
40|gracious to me it will be if you content me
41|with your name and with your lot".
42|Whereupon she, ready and with laughing eyes:
43|"Our charity does not lock doors
44|to a just wish, any more than that one
45|which wants like itself all its court.
46|I was in the world a virgin sister;
47|and if your mind looks well at itself,
48|my being more beautiful will not hide me from you,
49|but you will recognize that I am Piccarda,
50|who, placed here with these other blessed ones,
51|am blessed in the slowest sphere.
52|Our affections, which only are inflamed
53|in the pleasure of the Holy Spirit,
54|rejoice at being formed by his order.
55|And this lot which appears so low,
56|is therefore given to us, because neglected were
57|our vows, and void in some part".
58|Whereupon I to her: "In the wondrous aspects
59|of yours shines I know not what divine
60|that transmutes you from the first concepts:
61|therefore I was not quick in remembering;
62|but now what you tell me helps me,
63|so that recognizing is easier for me.
64|But tell me: you who are here happy,
65|do you desire a higher place
66|to see more and to make yourselves more friends?".
67|With those other shades she first smiled a little;
68|thereafter she answered me so glad,
69|that she seemed to burn with love in the first fire:
70|"Brother, our will is quieted by
71|the power of charity, which makes us want
72|only what we have, and for other things does not make us thirst.
73|If we desired to be more on high,
74|our desires would be discordant
75|with the will of him who here sorts us;
76|which you will see does not fit in these circles,
77|if to be in charity is here necessary,
78|and if you look well at its nature.
79|Rather it is formal to this blessed being
80|to keep oneself within the divine will,
81|so that one our wills themselves are made;
82|so that, as we are from threshold to threshold
83|through this kingdom, to all the kingdom it is pleasing
84|as to the king who in his will makes us will.
85|And in his will is our peace:
86|it is that sea to which moves everything
87|that it creates or that nature makes".
88|Clear it was to me then how every where
89|in heaven is paradise, even if the grace
90|of the highest good does not rain there in one way.
91|But just as it happens, if one food sates
92|and for another there still remains the appetite,
93|that that is asked for and for this thanks are given,
94|so did I with gesture and with word,
95|to learn from her what was the cloth
96|through which she did not draw the shuttle to the end.
97|"Perfect life and high merit enheavens
98|a lady higher up", she said to me, "according to whose rule
99|in your world below they clothe and veil themselves,
100|so that until dying they may wake and sleep
101|with that spouse who accepts every vow
102|that charity conforms to his pleasure.
103|From the world, to follow her, a young girl
104|I fled, and in her habit I enclosed myself
105|and promised the way of her order.
106|Men then, used to evil more than to good,
107|snatched me out of the sweet cloister:
108|God knows what then my life became.
109|And this other splendor that shows itself to you
110|at my right side and that is kindled
111|with all the light of our sphere,
112|what I say of me, she understands of herself;
113|a sister she was, and thus from her was taken
114|from her head the shadow of the sacred veils.
115|But after she was indeed turned back to the world
116|against her will and against good custom,
117|she was never unbound from the veil of the heart.
118|This is the light of the great Constance
119|who from the second wind of Swabia
120|begot the third and the last power".
121|Thus she spoke to me, and then began 'Ave,
122|Maria' singing, and singing vanished
123|as through deep water a heavy thing.
124|My sight, which followed her as much
125|as was possible, after it lost her,
126|turned to the mark of greater desire,
127|and to Beatrice wholly turned itself;
128|but she flashed into my gaze
129|so that at first my sight did not endure it;
130|and this made me slower to ask.
