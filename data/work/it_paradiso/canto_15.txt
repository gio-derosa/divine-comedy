1|Benign will in which is melted
2|always the love that breathes rightly,
3|as cupidity does in the wicked will,
4|imposed silence on that sweet lyre,
5|and made quiet the holy strings
6|which the right hand of heaven loosens and draws tight.
7|How will they be to just prayers deaf,
8|those substances which, to give me the wish
9|to pray to them, were concordant in keeping silent?
10|It is well that without end he should grieve
11|who, for love of a thing that does not last
12|eternally, strips himself of that love.
13|As through the serene skies tranquil and pure
14|there runs from time to time a sudden fire,
15|moving the eyes that were at rest,
16|and it seems a star that changes place,
17|except that from the part where it is kindled
18|nothing is lost, and it lasts a short time:
19|so from the arm that extends to the right
20|to the foot of that cross ran a star
21|of the constellation that shines there;
22|nor did the gem depart from its ribbon,
23|but along the radial strip it ran,
24|so that it seemed fire behind alabaster.
25|So loving did the shade of Anchises stretch forth,
26|if our greatest muse merits faith,
27|when in Elysium he perceived his son.
28|"O my blood, O poured down from above
29|grace of God, as to you to whom
30|twice ever was the gate of heaven opened?".
31|Thus that light: so I attended to it;
32|then I turned back to my lady my face,
33|and on this side and that I was stupefied;
34|for within her eyes burned a smile
35|such, that I thought with mine to touch the bottom
36|of my grace and of my paradise.
37|Then, joyous to hear and to see,
38|the spirit added to its beginning things,
39|which I did not understand, so deeply it spoke;
40|nor did it hide itself from me by choice,
41|but by necessity, for its concept
42|set itself above the mark of mortals.
43|And when the bow of the burning affection
44|was so relaxed, that the speech descended
45|toward the mark of our intellect,
46|the first thing that was understood by me,
47|"Blessed be you", was, "three and one,
48|who toward my seed are so courteous!".
49|And it followed: "A pleasing and long fast,
50|drawn from reading in the great volume
51|where neither white nor dark is ever changed,
52|you have relieved, son, within this light
53|in which I speak to you, thanks to her
54|who for the high flight clothed you with feathers.
55|You believe that your thought flows to me
56|from that which is first, just as rays forth
57|from the one, if it is known, the five and the six;
58|and therefore who I am and why I appear
59|more joyous to you, you do not ask me,
60|than any other in this gay crowd.
61|You believe the truth; for the lesser and the great
62|of this life gaze into the mirror
63|in which, before you think, you display the thought;
64|but so that the sacred love in which I keep watch
65|with perpetual sight and which makes me thirst
66|with sweet desiring, may be better fulfilled,
67|let your voice secure, bold and glad
68|sound the will, sound the desire,
69|to which my answer is already decreed!".
70|I turned to Beatrice, and she heard
71|before I spoke, and smiled to me a sign
72|that made the wings grow to my will.
73|Then I began thus: "Affection and wisdom,
74|as soon as the first equality appeared to you,
75|became of one weight for each of you,
76|because the sun which illumined and burned you,
77|with its heat and with its light is so equal,
78|that all similitudes are insufficient.
79|But will and means in mortals,
80|for the reason which is manifest to you,
81|are differently feathered in their wings;
82|whence I, who am mortal, feel myself in this
83|inequality, and therefore I do not give thanks
84|except with my heart for the paternal welcome.
85|Indeed I beseech you, living topaz
86|who bejewel this precious jewel,
87|that you make me sated with your name".
88|"O my leaf in whom I took delight
89|even awaiting, I was your root":
90|such a beginning, answering, it made to me.
91|Then it said to me: "He from whom is named
92|your family and who a hundred years and more
93|has circled the mountain on the first cornice,
94|was my son and was your great-grandfather:
95|well it is fitting that his long toil
96|you shorten for him with your works.
97|Florence within the ancient circle of walls,
98|from which she still takes both tierce and nones,
99|stood in peace, sober and chaste.
100|She had no necklace, no crown,
101|no adorned gowns, no girdle
102|that was more to be seen than the person.
103|Not yet, at her birth, did she make afraid
104|the daughter her father, for the age and the dowry
105|did not flee on this side and that the measure.
106|She had no houses empty of family;
107|Sardanapalus had not yet arrived there
108|to show what can be done in the bedchamber.
109|Not yet was Montemalo surpassed
110|by your Uccellatoio, which, as it is surpassed
111|in the rising up, so will it be in the fall.
112|Bellincion Berti I saw go girded
113|with leather and with bone, and come from the mirror
114|his lady without her face painted;
115|and I saw him of the Nerli and him of the Vecchio
116|be content with uncovered skin,
117|and their ladies with the spindle and the distaff.
118|Oh fortunate ones! each one was certain
119|of her burial place, and as yet none
120|was because of France deserted in her bed.
121|One kept watch in care of the cradle,
122|and, consoling, used the idiom
123|that first delights fathers and mothers;
124|another, drawing from the distaff the tresses,
125|told tales with her household
126|of the Trojans, of Fiesole and of Rome.
127|Would have been held then such a marvel
128|a Cianghella, a Lapo Salterello,
129|as now would be Cincinnatus and Cornelia.
130|To so reposeful, to so beautiful
131|a living of citizens, to so faithful
132|a citizenship, to so sweet an inn,
133|Mary gave me, called upon with loud cries;
134|and in your ancient Baptistery
135|at once I became a Christian and Cacciaguida.
136|Moronto was my brother and Eliseo;
137|my wife came to me from the valley of the Po,
138|and from there your surname was made.
139|Then I followed the emperor Conrad;
140|and he girded me into his knighthood,
141|so much by good working I came into his favor.
142|After him I went against the wickedness
143|of that law whose people usurps,
144|through the fault of the pastors, your right.
145|There I was by that foul people
146|disentangled from the deceitful world,
147|the love of which defiles many souls;
148|and I came from martyrdom to this peace".
