1|After, against the present life
2|of wretched mortals, had opened the truth
3|she who imparadises my mind,
4|as in the mirror the flame of a double candle
5|sees he who is lit by it from behind,
6|before he has it in sight or in thought,
7|and turns himself to see if the glass
8|tells him the truth, and sees that it accords
9|with it as a note with its measure;
10|so my memory recalls
11|that I did, gazing into the beautiful eyes
12|with which Love made the cord to catch me.
13|And as I turned and mine were touched
14|by that which appears in that volume,
15|whenever one looks well into its circling,
16|a point I saw which rayed light
17|so sharp, that the sight which it sets on fire
18|must close because of the strong sharpness;
19|and whatever star seems from here the smallest,
20|would seem a moon, placed beside it
21|as star with star is placed.
22|Perhaps as far as appears close
23|a halo to encircle the light that paints it
24|when the vapor that bears it is thickest,
25|distant around the point a circle of fire
26|was turning so swiftly, that it would have surpassed
27|that motion which most swiftly girds the world;
28|and this was by another encircled,
29|and that by the third, and the third then by the fourth,
30|by the fifth the fourth, and then by the sixth the fifth.
31|Above followed the seventh so spread
32|already in width, that the messenger of Juno
33|entire would be narrow to contain it.
34|So the eighth and the ninth; and each one
35|moved more slowly, according as it was
36|in number more distant from the one;
37|and that one had the purest flame
38|from which the pure spark was least distant,
39|I believe, because it most partakes of its truth.
40|My lady, who saw me in concern
41|strongly suspended, said: "From that point
42|depends heaven and all nature.
43|Look at that circle which is most joined to it;
44|and know that its moving is so swift
45|because of the fiery love by which it is spurred".
46|And I to her: "If the world were placed
47|with the order that I see in those wheels,
48|what is set before me would have sated me;
49|but in the sensible world one can
50|see the revolutions so much more divine,
51|as they are more remote from the center.
52|Whence, if my desire must have an end
53|in this wondrous and angelic temple
54|which has only love and light for its boundary,
55|I must hear further how the copy
56|and the exemplar do not go in one way,
57|for I by myself contemplate this in vain".
58|"If your fingers are not for such a knot
59|sufficient, it is no marvel:
60|so much, by not being attempted, has it become hard!".
61|Thus my lady; then she said: "Take
62|what I will tell you, if you want to be sated;
63|and around it sharpen your wit.
64|The corporeal circles are wide and narrow
65|according to the more and the less of the power
66|that extends through all their parts.
67|Greater goodness wills to make greater welfare;
68|greater welfare a greater body contains,
69|if it has its parts equally complete.
70|Therefore this one which sweeps all
71|the rest of the universe with it, corresponds
72|to the circle that loves most and that knows most:
73|so that, if you around the power draw
74|your measure, not around the appearance
75|of the substances that appear round to you,
76|you will see a wondrous correspondence
77|of greater to more and of lesser to less,
78|in each heaven, to its intelligence".
79|As remains splendid and serene
80|the hemisphere of the air, when blows
81|Boreas from that cheek from which he is most gentle,
82|so that is purged and dissolved the murk
83|that before troubled it, so that the heaven laughs
84|with the beauties of its every region;
85|so did I, after my lady provided me
86|with her clear answer,
87|and like a star in heaven the truth was seen.
88|And after her words stopped,
89|not otherwise does iron throw off sparks
90|that boils, than the circles sparkled.
91|Every spark followed its fire;
92|and they were so many, that their number
93|more than the doubling of the chess squares runs into thousands.
94|I heard hosanna sung from choir to choir
95|to the fixed point that holds them in their places,
96|and will hold them always, in which they always were.
97|And she who saw the doubtful thoughts
98|in my mind, said: "The first circles
99|have shown you Seraphim and Cherubim.
100|So swiftly they follow their bonds,
101|to liken themselves to the point as much as they can;
102|and they can as much as they are sublime in seeing.
103|Those other loves that go around them,
104|are called Thrones of the divine aspect,
105|because they ended the first triad;
106|and you must know that all have delight
107|in so far as their sight penetrates
108|into the truth in which every intellect is quieted.
109|From this one can see how is founded
110|the being blessed in the act that sees,
111|not in that which loves, which follows after;
112|and of seeing merit is the measure,
113|which grace begets and good will:
114|thus from grade to grade one proceeds.
115|The other triad, which so buds
116|in this everlasting springtime
117|which the nocturnal Ram does not despoil,
118|perpetually 'Hosanna' sings out
119|with three melodies, which sound in the three
120|orders of gladness of which it is formed.
121|In that hierarchy are the other divinities:
122|first Dominations, and then Virtues;
123|the third order is of Powers.
124|Then in the two penultimate dances
125|Principalities and Archangels circle;
126|the last is all of Angelic games.
127|These orders all gaze upward,
128|and downward prevail so, that toward God
129|all are drawn and all draw.
130|And Dionysius with such great desire
131|set himself to contemplate these orders,
132|that he named them and distinguished them as I do.
133|But Gregory afterward separated himself from him;
134|so that, as soon as he opened his eyes
135|in this heaven, he laughed at himself.
136|And if so secret a truth was set forth
137|by a mortal on earth, I do not want you to marvel:
138|for he who saw it up here disclosed it to him
139|with much else of the truth of these circles".
