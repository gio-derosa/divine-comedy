1|The world used to believe in its peril
2|that the beautiful Cyprian the mad love
3|rayed down, turning in the third epicycle;
4|so that not only to her did they do honor
5|with sacrifice and with votive cry
6|the ancient peoples in the ancient error;
7|but they honored Dione and Cupid,
8|the former as her mother, the latter as her son,
9|and they said that he sat in the lap of Dido;
10|and from her from whom I take my beginning
11|they took the name of the star
12|which the sun woos now from the nape, now from the brow.
13|I did not perceive the rising into it;
14|but of being within it gave me full faith
15|my lady whom I saw become more beautiful.
16|And as in a flame a spark is seen,
17|and as in a voice a voice is discerned,
18|when one is steady and another goes and returns,
19|I saw in that light other lamps
20|moving in a circle more and less swift,
21|in the manner, I believe, of their inner sight.
22|From a cold cloud there never descended winds,
23|either visible or not, so rapid,
24|that they would not seem hindered and slow
25|to one who had those divine lights
26|seen coming to us, leaving the circling
27|first begun among the high Seraphim;
28|and within those that appeared most in front
29|sounded 'Hosanna' so, that never since
30|of hearing it again have I been without desire.
31|Then one made itself nearer to us
32|and alone began: "We are all ready
33|for your pleasure, so that you may rejoice in us.
34|We turn with the celestial princes
35|in one circle and one circling and one thirst,
36|to whom you in the world once said:
37|'You who by understanding move the third heaven';
38|and we are so full of love, that, to please you,
39|a little quiet will not be less sweet".
40|After my eyes had offered themselves
41|to my lady reverent, and she
42|had made them of herself content and certain,
43|they turned back to the light that promised
44|so much of itself, and "Ah, who are you?" was
45|my voice with great affection imprinted.
46|And how much and how I saw it become more
47|through new joy that was added,
48|when I spoke, to its joys!
49|Thus made, it said to me: "The world had me
50|down there little time; and if it had been more,
51|much evil will be, which would not be.
52|My gladness keeps me hidden from you
53|which rays around me and hides me
54|like an animal swathed in its silk.
55|Much you loved me, and you had good reason;
56|for if I had been below, I would have shown you
57|of my love more beyond than the leaves.
58|That left bank which is washed
59|by the Rhone after it is mixed with the Sorgue,
60|as its lord in time was awaiting me,
61|and that horn of Ausonia which is townshipped
62|with Bari and with Gaeta and with Catona,
63|from where Tronto and Verde into the sea pour out.
64|There shone already on my brow the crown
65|of that land which the Danube waters
66|after it abandons the German banks.
67|And the beautiful Trinacria, which is darkened
68|between Pachynus and Pelorus, over the gulf
69|that receives from Eurus the greatest vexation,
70|not because of Typhoeus but because of nascent sulfur,
71|would have awaited still its kings,
72|born through me from Charles and from Rudolf,
73|if bad lordship, which always embitters
74|the subject peoples, had not
75|moved Palermo to shout: "Death, death!".
76|And if my brother foresaw this,
77|the greedy poverty of Catalonia
78|he would already flee, so that it would not harm him;
79|for truly there is need to provide
80|by him, or by another, so that on his bark
81|laden already more of a load is not laid.
82|His nature, which from a generous one stingy
83|descended, would have need of such soldiery
84|as would not care to put into the coffer".
85|"Because I believe that the high gladness
86|which your speech pours into me, my lord,
87|there where every good ends and begins,
88|is seen by you as I see it,
89|it is more welcome to me; and also this I hold dear
90|that you discern it gazing into God.
91|You have made me glad, and so make me clear,
92|since, by speaking, you have moved me to doubt
93|how there can come, from sweet seed, bitter".
94|This I to him; and he to me: "If I can
95|show you a truth, to that which you ask
96|you will hold your face as you hold your back.
97|The good that the whole kingdom which you climb
98|turns and contents, makes to be a power
99|its providence in these great bodies.
100|And not only are the natures provided for
101|in the mind which is by itself perfect,
102|but they together with their well-being:
103|so that whatever this bow shoots
104|falls disposed to a foreseen end,
105|just as a thing directed at its mark.
106|If this were not so, the heaven that you walk
107|would produce its effects so,
108|that they would not be works of art, but ruins;
109|and this cannot be, if the intellects
110|that move these stars are not defective,
111|and defective the first, which has not made them perfect.
112|Do you want this truth to become whiter for you?".
113|And I: "No indeed; for I see it impossible
114|that nature, in what is needed, should tire".
115|Whereupon he again: "Now say: would it be worse
116|for man on earth, if he were not a citizen?".
117|"Yes", I answered; "and here I ask no reason".
118|"And can that be, if below they do not live
119|diversely in diverse offices?
120|No, if your master writes well for you".
121|So he came deducing up to here;
122|then he concluded: "Therefore diverse
123|must be the roots of your effects:
124|so that one is born Solon and another Xerxes,
125|another Melchizedek and another that one
126|who, flying through the air, lost his son.
127|The circling nature, which is the seal
128|for the mortal wax, does its art well,
129|but does not distinguish one from another dwelling.
130|Hence it happens that Esau departs
131|in seed from Jacob; and Quirinus comes
132|from so base a father, that he is ascribed to Mars.
133|Generated nature its path
134|would make always similar to the generators,
135|if divine providence did not prevail.
136|Now what was behind you is before you:
137|but so that you may know that I delight in you,
138|a corollary I want to cloak you with.
139|Always nature, if it finds fortune
140|discordant with itself, like every other seed
141|out of its region, makes a bad showing.
142|And if the world down there paid attention
143|to the foundation that nature lays,
144|following it, it would have good people.
145|But you twist to religion
146|one who was born to gird on the sword,
147|and you make king of one who is for sermons;
148|whereby your track is off the road".
