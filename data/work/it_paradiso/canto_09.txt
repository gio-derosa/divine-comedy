1|After your Charles, beautiful Clemence,
2|had enlightened me, he told me of the deceptions
3|that his seed was to receive;
4|but he said: "Be silent and let the years move";
5|so that I cannot say anything but that weeping
6|just will come behind your harms.
7|And already the life of that holy light
8|had turned back to the Sun that fills it
9|as that good which to everything is so much.
10|Ah souls deceived and impious creatures,
11|who from such a good twist your hearts,
12|directing your temples toward vanity!
13|And behold another of those splendors
14|made toward me, and its will to please me
15|it signified in brightening outwardly.
16|The eyes of Beatrice, which were fixed
17|upon me, as before, of dear assent
18|to my desire made me certain.
19|"Ah, give to my wish quick satisfaction,
20|blessed spirit", I said, "and give me proof
21|that I can reflect in you what I think!".
22|Whereupon the light that was still new to me,
23|from its depth, where before it was singing,
24|continued as one who delights in doing good:
25|"In that part of the depraved land
26|Italian which sits between Rialto
27|and the springs of Brenta and of Piave,
28|rises a hill, and it does not rise very high,
29|from where there once descended a firebrand
30|that made upon the region a great assault.
31|From one root were born both I and he:
32|Cunizza I was called, and here I shine
33|because the light of this star overcame me;
34|but gladly to myself I pardon
35|the cause of my lot, and it does not trouble me;
36|which would seem perhaps hard to your common folk.
37|Of this luminous and dear jewel
38|of our heaven which is nearest to me,
39|great fame remained; and before it dies,
40|this hundredth year will still be fivefold:
41|see if man should make himself excellent,
42|so that another life the first may leave behind.
43|And this is not thought by the present crowd
44|that Tagliamento and Adige enclose,
45|nor for being beaten does it yet repent;
46|but soon it will be that Padua at the marsh
47|will change the water that bathes Vicenza,
48|because the people are stubborn to duty;
49|and where Sile and Cagnano join,
50|one lords it and goes with his head high,
51|for whom already to catch him the web is made.
52|Feltre will weep yet the crime
53|of its impious pastor, which will be foul
54|so, that for the like no one entered into Malta.
55|Too large would be the vat
56|that would receive the Ferrarese blood,
57|and weary he who would weigh it ounce by ounce,
58|which this courteous priest will give
59|to show himself of the party; and such gifts
60|will be conformed to the living of the country.
61|Up there are mirrors, you call them Thrones,
62|from which shines on us God judging;
63|so that these words seem good to us".
64|Here she fell silent; and she made me the semblance
65|that she was turned to other things, by the wheel
66|into which she placed herself as she was before.
67|The other joy, which was already known to me
68|as a dear thing, became to my sight
69|like a fine ruby upon which the sun strikes.
70|Through rejoicing up there brightness is gained,
71|just as a smile here; but below darkens
72|the shade outwardly, as the mind is sad.
73|"God sees everything, and your seeing in-hims itself",
74|I said, "blessed spirit, so that no
75|wish of itself from you can be stolen.
76|Therefore your voice, which delights the heaven
77|always with the song of those pious fires
78|which of six wings make themselves the cowl,
79|why does it not satisfy my desires?
80|I would not now await your question,
81|if I in-you'd myself, as you in-me yourself".
82|"The greatest valley in which the water spreads",
83|then began his words,
84|"apart from that sea which garlands the earth,
85|between discordant shores against the sun
86|goes so far, that it makes meridian
87|there where before it is wont to make the horizon.
88|Of that valley I was a shore-dweller
89|between Ebro and Magra, which with a short course
90|divides the Genoese from the Tuscan.
91|With almost one sunset and one sunrise
92|sit Bougie and the land where I was from,
93|which with its blood once made the harbor warm.
94|Folco I was called by those people to whom
95|my name was known; and this heaven
96|is imprinted with me, as I was with it;
97|for no more did the daughter of Belus burn,
98|vexing both Sychaeus and Creusa,
99|than I, as long as it befitted my hair;
100|nor that Rhodopean who was deluded
101|by Demophoon, nor Alcides
102|when he had Iole enclosed in his heart.
103|Yet here one does not repent, but smiles,
104|not at the fault, which does not return to mind,
105|but at the power that ordered and provided.
106|Here one gazes into the art that adorns
107|so great an effect, and one discerns the good
108|by which the world above turns that below.
109|But so that all your wishes, full,
110|you may carry away which were born in this sphere,
111|it behooves me to proceed still further.
112|You want to know who is in this light
113|that here beside me so sparkles
114|like a ray of sun in clear water.
115|Now know that there within is at peace
116|Rahab; and joined to our order,
117|with her in the highest degree it is sealed.
118|Into this heaven, in which the shadow comes to a point
119|that your world makes, before any other soul
120|of the triumph of Christ was she taken up.
121|Well it was fitting to leave her as a palm
122|in some heaven of the high victory
123|which was won with the one and the other palm,
124|because she favored the first glory
125|of Joshua upon the Holy Land,
126|which little touches the memory of the pope.
127|Your city, which is the plant of him
128|who first turned his back on his maker
129|and whose envy is so much lamented,
130|produces and spreads the accursed flower
131|that has led astray the sheep and the lambs,
132|because it has made a wolf of the shepherd.
133|For this the Gospel and the great doctors
134|are abandoned, and only the Decretals
135|are studied, so that it shows in their margins.
136|To this the pope and the cardinals attend;
137|their thoughts do not go to Nazareth,
138|there where Gabriel opened his wings.
139|But the Vatican and the other chosen parts
140|of Rome which have been the cemetery
141|for the soldiery that followed Peter,
142|soon will be free of the adultery".
