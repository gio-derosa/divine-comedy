1|"If I flame upon you in the heat of love
2|beyond the manner that on earth is seen,
3|so that I vanquish the power of your sight,
4|do not marvel, for this proceeds
5|from perfect seeing, which, as it apprehends,
6|so in the apprehended good moves its foot.
7|I see well how already shines
8|in your intellect the eternal light,
9|which, seen, alone and always kindles love;
10|and if another thing seduces your love,
11|it is nothing but some vestige of that,
12|badly recognized, which shines through there.
13|You want to know if with other service,
14|for a broken vow, one can render so much
15|as to make the soul secure from dispute".
16|So Beatrice began this canto;
17|and like a man who does not break off his speech,
18|she continued thus the holy discourse:
19|"The greatest gift that God in his largesse
20|made in creating, and to his goodness
21|most conformed, and the one that he most prizes,
22|was the freedom of the will;
23|with which the intelligent creatures,
24|both all and only they, were and are endowed.
25|Now it will appear to you, if you argue from here,
26|the high worth of the vow, if it is so made
27|that God consents when you consent;
28|for, in sealing between God and man the pact,
29|a victim is made of this treasure,
30|such as I say; and it is made by its own act.
31|Therefore what can be rendered as restoration?
32|If you think to use well what you have offered,
33|from ill-gotten gains you want to do good work.
34|You are now certain of the greater point;
35|but because Holy Church in this gives dispensation,
36|which seems against the truth that I have uncovered to you,
37|it behooves you still to sit a little at table,
38|because the rigid food that you have taken,
39|still requires help for your digestion.
40|Open your mind to that which I reveal to you
41|and fix it within there; for it does not make knowledge,
42|without retaining it, to have understood.
43|Two things belong to the essence
44|of this sacrifice: the one is that
45|of which it is made; the other is the agreement.
46|This last is never canceled
47|unless kept; and concerning it
48|so precisely was spoken above:
49|therefore it was necessary for the Hebrews
50|still to offer, even though some offering
51|might be exchanged, as you must know.
52|The other, which as matter is open to you,
53|may well be such, that one does not err
54|if with other matter it is exchanged.
55|But let no one shift the load on his shoulder
56|by his own will, without the turning
57|both of the white key and of the yellow;
58|and every exchange believe foolish,
59|if the thing put down in the thing taken up
60|as the four in the six is not contained.
61|Therefore whatever thing weighs so much
62|by its worth that it draws down every balance,
63|cannot be satisfied with other expense.
64|Let mortals not take the vow as a trifle;
65|be faithful, and in doing so not squint-eyed,
66|as Jephthah in his first offering;
67|to whom it was more fitting to say 'I did wrong',
68|than, keeping it, to do worse; and so foolish
69|you can find the great leader of the Greeks,
70|for which Iphigenia wept for her fair face,
71|and made weep for her the fools and the wise
72|who heard speak of such a rite.
73|Be, Christians, more grave in moving yourselves:
74|do not be like a feather to every wind,
75|and do not believe that every water washes you.
76|You have the New and the Old Testament,
77|and the shepherd of the Church who guides you;
78|let this suffice you for your salvation.
79|If evil greed cries something else to you,
80|be men, and not mad sheep,
81|so that the Jew among you may not laugh at you!
82|Do not do as the lamb that leaves the milk
83|of its mother, and simple and wanton
84|with itself at its pleasure fights!".
85|Thus Beatrice to me as I write;
86|then she turned all desiring
87|to that part where the world is most alive.
88|Her silence and her changing look
89|imposed silence on my eager mind,
90|which already had new questions before it;
91|and just as an arrow that strikes the mark
92|before the bowstring is quiet,
93|so we ran into the second kingdom.
94|There I saw my lady so glad,
95|as into the light of that heaven she entered,
96|that the planet became more shining from it.
97|And if the star changed and laughed,
98|what did I become, who even by my nature
99|am transmutable in every way!
100|As in a fishpond that is tranquil and pure
101|the fish draw to what comes from outside
102|in such manner that they deem it their food,
103|so I saw well more than a thousand splendors
104|draw toward us, and in each was heard:
105|"Here is one who will increase our loves".
106|And as each one came to us,
107|one saw the shade full of gladness
108|in the clear brightness that came out of it.
109|Think, reader, if what here begins
110|did not proceed, how you would have
111|an anguished craving to know more;
112|and by yourself you will see how from these
113|I was in desire to hear their conditions,
114|as soon as to my eyes they were manifest.
115|"O well-born one to whom to see the thrones
116|of the eternal triumph grace concedes
117|before the warfare is abandoned,
118|by the light that through all the heaven spreads
119|we are kindled; and therefore, if you desire
120|to be enlightened about us, at your pleasure sate yourself".
121|Thus by one of those pious spirits
122|it was said to me; and by Beatrice: "Speak, speak
123|confidently, and believe as you would gods".
124|"I see well how you nest yourself
125|in your own light, and that you draw it from your eyes,
126|because it flashes as you laugh;
127|but I do not know who you are, nor why you have,
128|worthy soul, the rank of the sphere
129|that veils itself to mortals with another's rays".
130|This I said directly to the light
131|that first had spoken to me; whereupon it made itself
132|shining much more than what it was.
133|Just as the sun that conceals itself
134|by too much light, when the heat has gnawed away
135|the temperings of the thick vapors,
136|through more gladness so hid itself from me
137|within its ray the holy figure;
138|and thus enclosed, enclosed it answered me
139|in the manner that the following canto sings.
