1|Oppressed with stupor, to my guide
2|I turned, like a little child who runs back
3|always there where he most confides;
4|and she, like a mother who succors
5|at once her son pale and breathless
6|with her voice, which is wont to dispose him well,
7|said to me: "Do you not know that you are in heaven?
8|and do you not know that heaven is all holy,
9|and what is done here comes from good zeal?
10|How the song would have transformed you,
11|and I smiling, now you can think it,
12|since the cry has moved you so much;
13|in which, if you had understood its prayers,
14|already would be known to you the vengeance
15|which you will see before you die.
16|The sword from up here does not cut in haste
17|nor late, except in the opinion of him
18|who desiring or fearing awaits it.
19|But turn now toward the others;
20|for many illustrious spirits you will see,
21|if as I say you bring back your look".
22|As it pleased her, I turned back my eyes,
23|and saw a hundred little spheres that together
24|made themselves more beautiful with mutual rays.
25|I stood like one who in himself represses
26|the point of desire, and does not venture
27|to ask, so much he fears excess;
28|and the greatest and most luminous
29|of those pearls came forward,
30|to make my wish content concerning itself.
31|Then within it I heard: "If you saw
32|as I do the charity that burns among us,
33|your thoughts would be expressed.
34|But so that you, by waiting, may not be delayed
35|in the high goal, I will make you answer
36|only to the thought, about which you are so careful.
37|That mountain on whose slope Cassino is
38|was frequented once upon its summit
39|by the people deceived and ill-disposed;
40|and I am he who first carried up there
41|the name of him who brought to earth
42|the truth that so sublimates us;
43|and so much grace shone upon me,
44|that I drew back the surrounding towns
45|from the impious cult that seduced the world.
46|These other fires all were contemplative
47|men, kindled by that heat
48|which makes the holy flowers and fruits be born.
49|Here is Macarius, here is Romuald,
50|here are my brothers who within the cloisters
51|fixed their feet and kept their heart firm".
52|And I to him: "The affection that you show
53|in speaking with me, and the good semblance
54|that I see and note in all your ardors,
55|has so expanded my confidence,
56|as the sun does the rose when open
57|it becomes as much as it has of power.
58|Therefore I pray you, and you, father, assure me
59|if I can receive so much grace, that I
60|may see you with uncovered image".
61|Whereupon he: "Brother, your high desire
62|will be fulfilled up in the last sphere,
63|where are fulfilled all the others and mine.
64|There is perfect, ripe and whole
65|every desire; in that alone
66|is every part there where it always was,
67|because it is not in space and has no poles;
68|and our ladder reaches up to it,
69|which is why it thus steals itself from your sight.
70|All the way up there the patriarch
71|Jacob saw it stretch its upper part,
72|when it appeared to him so laden with angels.
73|But, to climb it, now no one lifts
74|his feet from earth, and my rule
75|has remained for the waste of paper.
76|The walls which used to be an abbey
77|have been made dens, and the cowls
78|are sacks full of bad flour.
79|But heavy usury is not exacted so much
80|against the pleasure of God, as that fruit
81|which makes the heart of monks so mad;
82|for whatever the Church keeps, all
83|belongs to the people who ask in God's name;
84|not to relatives nor to other more foul.
85|The flesh of mortals is so soft,
86|that down there a good beginning does not last
87|from the birth of the oak to the making of the acorn.
88|Peter began without gold and without silver,
89|and I with prayer and with fasting,
90|and Francis humbly his convent;
91|and if you look at the beginning of each,
92|then look again there where it has strayed,
93|you will see the white made dark.
94|Truly, the Jordan turned backward
95|was, and the sea fleeing, when God willed,
96|more wondrous to see than would be the succor here".
97|So he said to me, and then withdrew
98|to his college, and the college closed together;
99|then, like a whirlwind, it all wound itself upward.
100|The sweet lady pushed me behind them
101|with a single sign up along that ladder,
102|so did her power vanquish my nature;
103|nor ever down here where one mounts and descends
104|naturally, was there motion so swift
105|that it could be equaled to my wing.
106|So may I return, reader, to that devout
107|triumph for the sake of which I often weep
108|for my sins and beat my breast,
109|you would not have in so short a time drawn out and put
110|your finger into the fire, as I saw the sign
111|that follows the Bull and was within it.
112|O glorious stars, O light pregnant
113|with great power, from which I acknowledge
114|all, whatever it may be, my genius,
115|with you was born and with you was hidden
116|he who is father of every mortal life,
117|when I first felt the Tuscan air;
118|and then, when grace was bestowed on me
119|to enter into the high wheel that turns you,
120|your region was allotted to me.
121|To you devoutly now sighs
122|my soul, to acquire power
123|for the hard pass that draws it to itself.
124|"You are so near to the ultimate salvation",
125|began Beatrice, "that you must
126|have your lights clear and sharp;
127|and therefore, before you further in-her yourself,
128|look down again, and see how much world
129|I have already put beneath your feet;
130|so that your heart, as much as it can, joyous
131|may present itself to the triumphant crowd
132|that comes glad through this round ether".
133|With my sight I returned through all
134|the seven spheres, and saw this globe
135|such, that I smiled at its paltry semblance;
136|and that counsel I approve as best
137|which holds it for least; and he who thinks of other things
138|can be called truly upright.
139|I saw the daughter of Latona kindled
140|without that shadow which was the cause to me
141|for which I once believed her rare and dense.
142|The aspect of your son, Hyperion,
143|there I endured, and saw how move
144|around and near him Maia and Dione.
145|From there appeared to me the tempering of Jove
146|between the father and the son; and from there was clear to me
147|the varying that they make of their where;
148|and all seven showed themselves to me
149|how great they are and how swift they are
150|and how they are in distant dwelling.
151|The little threshing floor that makes us so fierce,
152|as I turned with the eternal Twins,
153|appeared to me all from the hills to the river mouths;
154|then I turned back my eyes to the beautiful eyes.
