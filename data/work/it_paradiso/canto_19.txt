1|There appeared before me with open wings
2|the beautiful image which in the sweet fruition
3|the interwoven souls were making glad;
4|each seemed a little ruby in which
5|a ray of sun burned so kindled,
6|that into my eyes it refracted it.
7|And that which I must now portray,
8|voice never carried, nor did ink write,
9|nor was it ever comprehended by fantasy;
10|for I saw and also heard the beak speak,
11|and sound in its voice both "I" and "my",
12|when it was in concept both 'we' and 'our'.
13|And it began: "For being just and pious
14|I am here exalted to that glory
15|which does not let itself be surpassed by desire;
16|and on earth I left my memory
17|so made, that the wicked people there
18|commend it, but do not follow the story".
19|Thus one single heat from many embers
20|makes itself felt, as from many loves
21|came out only one sound from that image.
22|Whereupon I then: "O perpetual flowers
23|of the eternal gladness, who as only one
24|make seem to me all your odors,
25|relieve me, by breathing, of the great fast
26|which for a long time has kept me in hunger,
27|not finding for it any food on earth.
28|Well I know that, if in heaven another realm
29|the divine justice makes its mirror,
30|yours does not apprehend it through a veil.
31|You know how attentively I prepare myself
32|to listen; you know what is that
33|doubt which is a fast so old for me".
34|Like a falcon that comes out of the hood,
35|moves its head and applauds itself with its wings,
36|showing its will and making itself beautiful,
37|I saw become that sign, which of praise
38|of the divine grace was woven,
39|with songs such as he knows who rejoices up there.
40|Then it began: "He who turned the compass
41|at the extremity of the world, and within it
42|distinguished so much hidden and manifest,
43|could not make his power so impressed
44|on all the universe, that his word
45|would not remain in infinite excess.
46|And this makes certain that the first proud one,
47|who was the summit of every creature,
48|for not awaiting light, fell unripe;
49|and hence it appears that every lesser nature
50|is a short receptacle for that good
51|which has no end and measures itself by itself.
52|Therefore your sight, which must
53|be one of the rays of the mind
54|with which all things are filled,
55|cannot by its nature be powerful
56|so much, that its principle does not discern
57|far beyond that which is apparent to it.
58|Therefore into the everlasting justice
59|the sight which your world receives,
60|like the eye into the sea, goes within;
61|which, although from the shore it sees the bottom,
62|in the open sea does not see it; and nonetheless
63|it is there, but its being deep conceals it.
64|There is no light, unless it comes from the serene
65|which is never troubled; rather it is darkness
66|or shadow of the flesh or its poison.
67|Now the hiding place is open enough to you
68|which hid from you the living justice,
69|of which you made question so frequent;
70|for you were saying: "A man is born on the bank
71|of the Indus, and there is no one there who discourses
72|of Christ nor who reads nor who writes;
73|and all his wishes and acts are good,
74|as far as human reason sees,
75|without sin in life or in speech.
76|He dies unbaptized and without faith:
77|where is this justice that condemns him?
78|where is his fault, if he does not believe?".
79|Now who are you, who want to sit on the bench,
80|to judge from a thousand miles away
81|with the short sight of a span?
82|Certainly for him who reasons subtly with me,
83|if the Scripture were not above you,
84|there would be cause for doubting to a marvel.
85|Oh earthly animals! oh gross minds!
86|The first will, which is of itself good,
87|from itself, which is the highest good, never moved.
88|So much is just as accords with it:
89|no created good draws it to itself,
90|but it, raying forth, causes that good".
91|As above her nest circles
92|the stork after she has fed her young,
93|and as the one who is fed looks up at her;
94|so did become, and so I raised my brows,
95|the blessed image, which its wings
96|moved impelled by so many counsels.
97|Wheeling it sang, and said: "As
98|are my notes to you, who do not understand them,
99|such is the eternal judgment to you mortals".
100|After those shining fires grew quiet
101|of the Holy Spirit still in the sign
102|which made the Romans revered by the world,
103|it began again: "To this kingdom
104|never rose anyone who did not believe in Christ,
105|neither before nor after he was nailed to the tree.
106|But see: many cry "Christ, Christ!",
107|who will be at the judgment far less near
108|to him, than some who do not know Christ;
109|and such Christians will the Ethiopian condemn,
110|when the two colleges will be parted,
111|the one eternally rich and the other destitute.
112|What will the Persians be able to say to your kings,
113|when they see that volume open
114|in which are written all their dishonors?
115|There will be seen, among the works of Albert,
116|that one which soon will move the pen,
117|by which the kingdom of Prague will be deserted.
118|There will be seen the grief that upon the Seine
119|he brings, by falsifying the coinage,
120|who will die by the blow of a boar's hide.
121|There will be seen the pride that makes thirsty,
122|which makes the Scot and the Englishman mad,
123|so that neither can suffer within his boundary.
124|There will be seen the lust and soft living
125|of him of Spain and of him of Bohemia,
126|who never knew nor wanted worth.
127|There will be seen of the Cripple of Jerusalem
128|marked with an I his goodness,
129|while the contrary an M will mark.
130|There will be seen the avarice and cowardice
131|of him who guards the island of fire,
132|where Anchises ended his long age;
133|and to give to understand how little he is,
134|his writing will be abbreviated letters,
135|which will note much in small space.
136|And there will appear to everyone the foul works
137|of the uncle and of the brother, who so illustrious
138|a nation and two crowns have made cuckolds.
139|And he of Portugal and of Norway
140|there will be known, and he of Rascia
141|who wrongly has seen the coin of Venice.
142|Oh blessed Hungary, if she does not let herself
143|be mistreated any more! and blessed Navarre,
144|if she armed herself with the mountain that swathes her!
145|And each should believe that already, as a pledge
146|of this, Nicosia and Famagusta
147|over their beast lament and rail,
148|which from the side of the others does not stray".
