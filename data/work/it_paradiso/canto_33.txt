1|"Virgin Mother, daughter of your son,
2|humble and high more than any creature,
3|fixed goal of eternal counsel,
4|you are she who human nature
5|ennobled so, that its maker
6|did not disdain to make himself its making.
7|In your womb was rekindled the love,
8|by whose warmth in the eternal peace
9|this flower has thus germinated.
10|Here you are to us a noonday torch
11|of charity, and below, among mortals,
12|you are a living fountain of hope.
13|Lady, you are so great and are worth so much,
14|that whoever wants grace and does not have recourse to you,
15|his desire wants to fly without wings.
16|Your benignity not only succors
17|him who asks, but many times
18|freely runs ahead of the asking.
19|In you mercy, in you pity,
20|in you magnificence, in you is gathered
21|whatever of goodness is in a creature.
22|Now this man, who from the lowest pit
23|of the universe up to here has seen
24|the spiritual lives one by one,
25|beseeches you, through grace, for power
26|so great, that he may with his eyes lift himself
27|higher toward the ultimate salvation.
28|And I, who never for my own seeing burned
29|more than I do for his, all my prayers
30|offer you, and pray that they may not be too little,
31|that you may unbind him from every cloud
32|of his mortality with your prayers,
33|so that the highest pleasure may unfold itself to him.
34|Further I pray you, queen, who can do
35|whatever you will, that you keep sound,
36|after so great a vision, his affections.
37|May your guardianship vanquish human impulses:
38|see Beatrice with how many blessed ones
39|for my prayers clasp their hands to you!".
40|The eyes by God loved and venerated,
41|fixed on the one praying, showed us
42|how pleasing devout prayers are to her;
43|then to the eternal light they directed themselves,
44|into which one must not believe that there is sent
45|by a creature an eye so clear.
46|And I who to the end of all desires
47|was drawing near, just as I ought,
48|brought the ardor of desire in me to its end.
49|Bernard was signaling to me, and smiling,
50|that I should look upward; but I was
51|already by myself such as he wished:
52|for my sight, becoming pure,
53|more and more was entering through the ray
54|of the high light which of itself is true.
55|From then on my seeing was greater
56|than speech shows, which yields to such sight,
57|and memory yields to such excess.
58|As is he who dreaming sees,
59|so that after the dream the impressed passion
60|remains, and the rest does not return to the mind,
61|such am I, for almost wholly ceases
62|my vision, and still there distills
63|in my heart the sweetness that was born from it.
64|Thus the snow in the sun is unsealed;
65|thus in the wind on the light leaves
66|was lost the oracle of the Sibyl.
67|O highest light who so lift yourself
68|from mortal concepts, to my mind
69|lend again a little of what you appeared,
70|and make my tongue so powerful,
71|that a single spark of your glory
72|it may leave to the future people;
73|for, by returning somewhat to my memory
74|and by sounding a little in these verses,
75|more will be conceived of your victory.
76|I believe, by the sharpness that I endured
77|of the living ray, that I would have been lost,
78|if my eyes had been turned away from it.
79|And I remember that I was more daring
80|because of this to endure, so much that I joined
81|my sight with the infinite worth.
82|Oh abundant grace by which I presumed
83|to fix my sight through the eternal light,
84|so much that I consumed my seeing there!
85|In its depth I saw that is gathered within,
86|bound with love in one volume,
87|that which through the universe is scattered like leaves:
88|substances and accidents and their relations
89|as if fused together, in such a manner
90|that what I say is a simple light.
91|The universal form of this knot
92|I believe that I saw, because more largely,
93|saying this, I feel that I rejoice.
94|A single moment is to me greater oblivion
95|than twenty-five centuries to the enterprise
96|that made Neptune marvel at the shadow of the Argo.
97|Thus my mind, all suspended,
98|was gazing fixed, immobile and attentive,
99|and always by gazing became kindled.
100|In that light one becomes such,
101|that to turn from it to another sight
102|it is impossible that one would ever consent;
103|because the good, which is the object of the will,
104|is all gathered in it, and outside of it
105|is defective that which is perfect there.
106|Now will my speech be more short,
107|even for what I remember, than that of an infant
108|who still bathes his tongue at the breast.
109|Not because more than one simple semblance
110|was in the living light at which I gazed,
111|which is always such as it was before;
112|but through my sight which was growing strong
113|in me as I looked, one single appearance,
114|as I changed, was transforming itself for me.
115|In the profound and clear subsistence
116|of the high light there appeared to me three circles
117|of three colors and of one dimension;
118|and one by the other as rainbow by rainbow
119|seemed reflected, and the third seemed fire
120|which from this one and that one equally is breathed.
121|Oh how short is speech and how faint
122|for my concept! and this, compared to what I saw,
123|is such, that it is not enough to say 'little'.
124|O eternal light that alone abide in yourself,
125|alone understand yourself, and by yourself understood
126|and understanding love and smile on yourself!
127|That circling which, so conceived,
128|appeared in you as reflected light,
129|by my eyes somewhat looked around,
130|within itself, of its own same color,
131|seemed to me painted with our image:
132|so that my sight was wholly set upon it.
133|As is the geometer who wholly applies himself
134|to measure the circle, and does not find,
135|by thinking, that principle which he needs,
136|such was I at that new sight:
137|I wanted to see how there fitted
138|the image to the circle and how it finds its place there;
139|but my own wings were not for this:
140|except that my mind was struck
141|by a flash in which its wish came.
142|To the high fantasy here power failed;
143|but already was turning my desire and my will,
144|like a wheel that is evenly moved,
145|the love that moves the sun and the other stars.
