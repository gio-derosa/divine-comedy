1|From the center to the rim, and so from the rim to the center
2|moves the water in a round vessel,
3|according as it is struck outside or inside:
4|into my mind fell suddenly
5|this which I say, as soon as fell silent
6|the glorious life of Thomas,
7|because of the similitude that was born
8|from his speaking and from that of Beatrice,
9|whom it pleased to begin thus, after him:
10|"This one has need, and he does not tell it to you
11|either with his voice or yet in thought,
12|to go to the root of another truth.
13|Tell him if the light with which flowers
14|your substance, will remain with you
15|eternally just as it is now;
16|and if it remains, tell how, after
17|you are remade visible,
18|it can be that it does not harm your seeing".
19|As, by greater gladness pushed and drawn,
20|at times those who go in a ring
21|raise their voice and gladden their gestures,
22|so, at the prayer prompt and devout,
23|the holy circles showed new joy
24|in their turning and in their wondrous note.
25|Whoever laments because here one dies
26|to live up there, has not seen there
27|the refreshment of the eternal rain.
28|That one and two and three which always lives
29|and reigns always in three and in two and in one,
30|not circumscribed, and circumscribes everything,
31|three times was sung by each one
32|of those spirits with such melody,
33|that for every merit it would be a just reward.
34|And I heard in the most divine light
35|of the smaller circle a modest voice,
36|perhaps such as was the angel's to Mary,
37|answer: "As long as will be the feast
38|of paradise, so long will our love
39|ray around itself such a garment.
40|Its brightness follows the ardor;
41|the ardor the vision, and that is as great,
42|as it has of grace above its own worth.
43|When the flesh glorious and holy
44|is put on again, our person
45|will be more pleasing for being all complete;
46|so that there will increase what is given us
47|of gratuitous light by the highest good,
48|light which enables us to see him;
49|whence the vision must increase,
50|increase the ardor that is kindled by it,
51|increase the ray that comes from that.
52|But just as a coal that gives off flame,
53|and by living whiteness surpasses it,
54|so that its appearance defends itself;
55|so this brightness which already encircles us
56|will be surpassed in appearance by the flesh
57|which all day the earth covers over;
58|nor will such light be able to tire us:
59|for the organs of the body will be strong
60|for all that can delight us".
61|So sudden and alert seemed to me
62|both the one and the other choir in saying "Amen!",
63|that well they showed desire for their dead bodies:
64|perhaps not only for themselves, but for their mothers,
65|for their fathers and for the others who were dear
66|before they were everlasting flames.
67|And behold around, of equal brightness,
68|there was born a luster above that which was there,
69|in the manner of a horizon that brightens.
70|And just as at the rising of early evening
71|there begin through the heaven new appearances,
72|so that the sight seems and does not seem true,
73|it seemed to me there new subsistences
74|I began to see, and make a circle
75|outside of the other two circumferences.
76|Oh true sparkling of the Holy Spirit!
77|how it became sudden and glowing
78|to my eyes which, vanquished, did not endure it!
79|But Beatrice so beautiful and smiling
80|showed herself to me, that among those sights
81|she must be left which the mind did not follow.
82|From this my eyes regained power
83|to lift themselves; and I saw myself translated
84|alone with my lady to a higher salvation.
85|Well I perceived that I was more lifted,
86|by the fiery smile of the star,
87|which seemed to me more red than usual.
88|With all my heart and with that speech
89|which is one in all, to God I made a holocaust,
90|such as befitted the new grace.
91|And not yet was exhausted from my breast
92|the ardor of the sacrifice, when I knew
93|this offering to have been accepted and auspicious;
94|for with such brightness and so red
95|splendors appeared to me within two rays,
96|that I said: "O Helios who so adorns them!".
97|As, distinguished by lesser and greater
98|lights, whitens between the poles of the world
99|the Galaxy so, that it makes even the very wise doubt;
100|so constellated made in the depth
101|of Mars those rays the venerable sign
102|which the junctures of quadrants in a circle make.
103|Here my memory overcomes my wit;
104|for that cross flashed forth Christ,
105|so that I do not know how to find a worthy example;
106|but whoever takes his cross and follows Christ,
107|will yet excuse me for what I leave out,
108|seeing in that whiteness flash forth Christ.
109|From arm to arm and between the top and the base
110|lights were moving, sparkling strongly
111|in joining together and in passing by:
112|so are seen here straight and slanting,
113|swift and slow, renewing their aspect,
114|the particles of bodies, long and short,
115|move through the ray with which is streaked
116|at times the shade which, for its defense,
117|people with skill and art procure.
118|And as a viol and harp, strung in harmony
119|with many strings, make a sweet tinkling
120|to one by whom the tune is not understood,
121|so from the lights that appeared to me there
122|was gathered through the cross a melody
123|that rapt me, without understanding the hymn.
124|Well I perceived that it was of high praises,
125|because there came to me "Rise again" and "Conquer"
126|as to one who does not understand and hears.
127|I was enamored so much by it,
128|that until then there was not anything
129|that bound me with such sweet bonds.
130|Perhaps my word seems too daring,
131|placing after it the pleasure of the beautiful eyes,
132|gazing into which my desire has rest;
133|but whoever perceives that the living seals
134|of every beauty do more the higher they are,
135|and that I had not turned there to those,
136|can excuse me for that of which I accuse myself
137|to excuse myself, and see me speak the truth:
138|for the holy pleasure is not here excluded,
139|because it becomes, in mounting, more pure.
