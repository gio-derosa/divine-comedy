1|Looking into his Son with the Love
2|which the one and the other eternally breathe,
3|the first and ineffable Power
4|whatever through mind and through space revolves
5|made with such order, that he cannot be
6|without tasting of him who gazes on it.
7|Lift then, reader, to the high wheels
8|with me your sight, straight to that part
9|where the one motion and the other strike each other;
10|and there begin to gaze with longing at the art
11|of that master who within himself loves it,
12|so much that never from it does he part his eye.
13|See how from there branches off
14|the oblique circle that carries the planets,
15|to satisfy the world that calls on them.
16|For if their road were not bent,
17|much power in heaven would be in vain,
18|and almost every potency down here dead;
19|and if from the straight more or less far
20|were the departure, much would be lacking
21|both below and above of the worldly order.
22|Now remain, reader, upon your bench,
23|thinking back on what is foretasted,
24|if you want to be glad much before tired.
25|I have set it before you: now by yourself feed yourself;
26|for to itself twists all my care
27|that matter of which I am made the scribe.
28|The greatest minister of nature,
29|which with the power of heaven imprints the world
30|and with its light measures time for us,
31|with that part which above is recalled
32|conjoined, was turning through the spirals
33|in which ever earlier it presents itself;
34|and I was with it; but of the rising
35|I was not aware, except as a man is aware,
36|before the first thought, of its coming.
37|It is Beatrice who so guides
38|from good to better, so suddenly
39|that her act does not extend through time.
40|How much it had to be of itself shining
41|that which was within the sun where I entered,
42|not by color, but by light apparent!
43|Though I should call on genius and art and practice,
44|I would not tell it so that it could ever be imagined;
45|but it can be believed and let one long to see it.
46|And if our fantasies are low
47|for such height, it is no marvel;
48|for above the sun there was no eye that went.
49|Such was there the fourth family
50|of the high Father, who always sates it,
51|showing how he breathes and how he begets.
52|And Beatrice began: "Thank,
53|thank the Sun of the angels, who to this
54|sensible one has lifted you by his grace".
55|Heart of mortal was never so disposed
56|to devotion and to render itself to God
57|with all its will so ready,
58|as at those words I became;
59|and so all my love set itself on him,
60|that it eclipsed Beatrice in oblivion.
61|It did not displease her; but so she smiled at it,
62|that the splendor of her laughing eyes
63|my united mind divided among more things.
64|I saw many flashes living and surpassing
65|make of us a center and of themselves make a crown,
66|more sweet in voice than in sight shining:
67|thus girded the daughter of Latona
68|we see at times, when the air is pregnant,
69|so that it retains the thread that makes the belt.
70|In the court of heaven, from where I return,
71|are found many jewels dear and beautiful
72|so much that they cannot be taken out of the kingdom;
73|and the song of those lights was of those;
74|he who does not feather himself so that he flies up there,
75|from the mute let him await news from there.
76|Then, so singing, those burning suns
77|had circled around us three times,
78|like stars near to the fixed poles,
79|ladies they seemed to me, not released from the dance,
80|but who stop silent, listening
81|until they have gathered the new notes.
82|And within one I heard begin: "Since
83|the ray of grace, by which is kindled
84|true love and which then grows by loving,
85|multiplied in you so shines,
86|that it conducts you up along that stair
87|where without climbing again no one descends;
88|whoever denied you the wine of his flask
89|for your thirst, would not be in freedom
90|except as water that does not flow down to the sea.
91|You want to know with what plants is flowered
92|this garland that around gazes lovingly at
93|the beautiful lady who strengthens you for heaven.
94|I was of the lambs of the holy flock
95|which Dominic leads along the way
96|where one fattens well if one does not stray.
97|This one who is nearest me on the right,
98|was brother and master to me, and he Albert
99|is of Cologne, and I Thomas of Aquino.
100|If thus of all the others you want to be certain,
101|behind my speech come along with your sight
102|circling up along the blessed wreath.
103|That other flaming comes out of the smile
104|of Gratian, who the one and the other court
105|helped so that it pleases in paradise.
106|The other who next adorns our choir,
107|was that Peter who with the poor widow
108|offered to Holy Church his treasure.
109|The fifth light, which is among us the most beautiful,
110|breathes with such love, that all the world
111|down there craves to know news of it:
112|within is the high mind where such deep
113|knowledge was put, that, if the truth is true,
114|to see so much there never rose a second.
115|Next see the light of that candle
116|which below in the flesh saw most deeply into
117|the angelic nature and ministry.
118|In the other little light smiles
119|that advocate of the Christian times
120|of whose Latin Augustine made use.
121|Now if you draw the eye of your mind
122|from light to light following my praises,
123|already for the eighth you remain with thirst.
124|For seeing every good within it rejoices
125|the holy soul who the deceitful world
126|makes manifest to whoever hears her well.
127|The body from which it was driven lies
128|down in Cieldauro; and it from martyrdom
129|and from exile came to this peace.
130|See beyond flaming the burning breath
131|of Isidore, of Bede and of Richard,
132|who in contemplation was more than man.
133|This one from whom your gaze returns to me,
134|is the light of a spirit to whom in thoughts
135|grave, dying seemed to come slowly:
136|it is the eternal light of Siger,
137|who, lecturing in the Street of Straw,
138|syllogized invidious truths".
139|Then, like a clock that calls us
140|in the hour when the bride of God rises
141|to sing matins to the bridegroom so that he may love her,
142|in which one part pulls and pushes the other,
143|ting ting sounding with so sweet a note,
144|that the well-disposed spirit swells with love;
145|so I saw the glorious wheel
146|move and render voice to voice in harmony
147|and in sweetness that cannot be known
148|except there where joy becomes eternal.
