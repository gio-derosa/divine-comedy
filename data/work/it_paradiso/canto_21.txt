1|Already my eyes were fixed again on the face
2|of my lady, and my mind with them,
3|and from every other intent it had withdrawn.
4|And she was not smiling; but "If I smiled",
5|she began to me, "you would become such
6|as Semele was when she became ashes:
7|for my beauty, which along the stairs
8|of the eternal palace kindles itself more,
9|as you have seen, the higher one climbs,
10|if it were not tempered, so shines,
11|that your mortal power, at its brightness,
12|would be a bough that thunder splits.
13|We are raised to the seventh splendor,
14|which beneath the breast of the burning Lion
15|rays now mixed downward with its power.
16|Fix behind your eyes your mind,
17|and make of them mirrors for the figure
18|which in this mirror will be apparent to you".
19|Whoever knew what was the pasture
20|of my sight in the blessed aspect
21|when I transferred myself to another care,
22|would know how pleasing it was to me
23|to obey my celestial escort,
24|counterweighing the one side with the other.
25|Within the crystal which bears the name,
26|circling the world, of its dear leader
27|under whom every malice lay dead,
28|of the color of gold through which a ray shines
29|I saw a ladder erected upward
30|so far, that my light did not follow it.
31|I saw also along the steps descend
32|so many splendors, that I thought every light
33|that appears in heaven, was poured from there.
34|And as, by natural custom,
35|the jackdaws together, at the beginning of the day,
36|move to warm their cold feathers;
37|then some go away without return,
38|others turn back to where they set out from,
39|and others wheeling make a stay;
40|such a manner it seemed to me was there
41|in that sparkling which came together,
42|as soon as on a certain step it struck.
43|And the one that stayed nearest to us,
44|became so bright, that I said thinking:
45|'I see well the love that you signal to me.
46|But she from whom I await the how and the when
47|of speaking and of silence, stays still; so I,
48|against desire, do well not to ask'.
49|So she, who saw my silence
50|in the seeing of him who sees everything,
51|said to me: "Release your warm desire".
52|And I began: "My merit
53|does not make me worthy of your answer;
54|but for her sake who grants me to ask,
55|blessed life who stay hidden
56|within your gladness, make known to me
57|the cause that has placed you so near me;
58|and say why is silent in this wheel
59|the sweet symphony of paradise,
60|which down through the others sounds so devout".
61|"You have mortal hearing as you have mortal sight",
62|it answered me; "so here there is no singing
63|for the reason that Beatrice has not smiled.
64|Down along the steps of the holy ladder
65|I descended so far only to make you welcome
66|with speech and with the light that cloaks me;
67|nor did more love make me more prompt,
68|for more and as much love burns from here up,
69|as the flaming manifests to you.
70|But the high charity, which makes us servants
71|ready for the counsel that governs the world,
72|allots here as you observe".
73|"I see well", I said, "sacred lamp,
74|how free love in this court
75|suffices to follow the eternal providence;
76|but this is what seems hard to me to discern,
77|why you were predestined alone
78|to this office among your companions".
79|Nor had I come to the last word,
80|before the light made a center of its middle,
81|turning itself like a swift millstone;
82|then answered the love that was within it:
83|"Divine light focuses itself upon me,
84|penetrating through this in which I en-womb myself,
85|whose power, joined with my seeing,
86|lifts me above myself so far, that I see
87|the highest essence from which it is milked.
88|From this comes the joy with which I flame;
89|so that to my sight, as much as it is clear,
90|I make equal the clarity of the flame.
91|But that soul in heaven that is most brightened,
92|that seraph who in God has his eye most fixed,
93|would not satisfy your question,
94|because so far it goes into the abyss
95|of the eternal statute, that which you ask,
96|that from every created sight it is cut off.
97|And to the mortal world, when you return,
98|report this, so that it may not presume
99|to move its feet toward such a goal any more.
100|The mind, which here shines, on earth smokes;
101|so consider how it could down there
102|do what it cannot though heaven assume it".
103|So did his words restrict me,
104|that I left the question and drew myself back
105|to ask it humbly who it was.
106|"Between the two shores of Italy rise crags,
107|and not very distant from your homeland,
108|so high that the thunders sound much lower,
109|and they make a hump that is called Catria,
110|beneath which is consecrated a hermitage,
111|which is wont to be disposed for worship alone".
112|Thus it began again to me its third speech;
113|and then, continuing, said: "There
114|in the service of God I became so firm,
115|that with just foods of the liquor of olives
116|lightly I passed heats and frosts,
117|content in contemplative thoughts.
118|That cloister used to render to these heavens
119|fertilely; and now it is made empty,
120|so that soon it must be revealed.
121|In that place I was Peter Damian,
122|and Peter the Sinner I was in the house
123|of Our Lady on the Adriatic shore.
124|Little mortal life was left to me,
125|when I was called and drawn to that hat,
126|which is ever poured from bad to worse.
127|Cephas came and there came the great vessel
128|of the Holy Spirit, lean and barefoot,
129|taking their food from whatever inn.
130|Now they want on this side and that someone to prop up
131|the modern pastors and someone to lead them,
132|so heavy are they, and someone to lift them behind.
133|They cover with their mantles their palfreys,
134|so that two beasts go under one hide:
135|oh patience that endure so much!".
136|At this voice I saw more little flames
137|from step to step descend and turn,
138|and every turn made them more beautiful.
139|Around this one they came and stopped,
140|and made a cry of such high sound,
141|that it could not be likened to anything here;
142|nor did I understand it, so the thunder overcame me.
