1|Already was enjoying alone its own word
2|that blessed mirror, and I was tasting
3|my own, tempering the bitter with the sweet;
4|and that lady who was leading me to God
5|said: "Change your thought; think that I am
6|near to him who lightens every wrong".
7|I turned myself to the loving sound
8|of my comfort; and what love I then saw
9|in the holy eyes, here I abandon it:
10|not only because I distrust my speech,
11|but because of the memory, which cannot return
12|upon itself so far, if another does not guide it.
13|So much can I retell of that moment,
14|that, gazing at her, my affection
15|was free from every other desire,
16|as long as the eternal pleasure, which directly
17|rayed on Beatrice, from her beautiful face
18|contented me with its second aspect.
19|Vanquishing me with the light of a smile,
20|she said to me: "Turn and listen;
21|for not only in my eyes is paradise".
22|As one sees here sometimes
23|the affection in the look, if it is so great,
24|that by it all the soul is taken,
25|so in the flaming of the holy brightness,
26|to which I turned, I knew the wish
27|in it to discourse with me yet somewhat.
28|It began: "In this fifth threshold
29|of the tree which lives from the top
30|and always bears fruit and never loses a leaf,
31|are blessed spirits, who below, before
32|they came to heaven, were of great renown,
33|so that every muse would be rich with them.
34|Therefore look at the arms of the cross:
35|the one that I will name, will there do the act
36|that in a cloud its swift fire does".
37|I saw along the cross a light drawn
38|by the naming of Joshua, as it was done;
39|nor was the saying known to me before the deed.
40|And at the name of the high Maccabeus
41|I saw another move itself wheeling,
42|and gladness was the whip of the top.
43|So for Charlemagne and for Roland
44|two of them my attentive gaze followed,
45|as the eye follows its falcon flying.
46|Then William and Renouard drew
47|and the duke Godfrey my sight
48|along that cross, and Robert Guiscard.
49|Then, among the other lights moving and mingled,
50|showed me the soul that had spoken to me
51|how it was among the singers of heaven an artist.
52|I turned myself to my right side
53|to see in Beatrice my duty,
54|either by speech or by gesture, signaled;
55|and I saw her lights so pure,
56|so joyous, that her semblance
57|surpassed the others and the last wont.
58|And as, by feeling more delight
59|in doing good, a man from day to day
60|perceives that his virtue advances,
61|so I perceived that my circling around
62|together with the heaven had increased its arc,
63|seeing that miracle more adorned.
64|And such as is the transformation in a brief passage
65|of time in a white lady, when her face
66|unloads itself of the load of shame,
67|such it was in my eyes, when I turned,
68|through the whiteness of the temperate star,
69|the sixth, which had gathered me within itself.
70|I saw in that jovial torch
71|the sparkling of the love that was there
72|trace to my eyes our speech.
73|And as birds risen from the riverbank,
74|as if rejoicing together at their pastures,
75|make of themselves now a round now another flock,
76|so within the lights holy creatures
77|fluttering were singing, and made themselves
78|now D, now I, now L in their figures.
79|First, singing, they moved to their own note;
80|then, becoming one of these signs,
81|they stopped a little and fell silent.
82|O divine Pegasean who makes minds
83|glorious and renders them long-lived,
84|and they with you the cities and the kingdoms,
85|illumine me with yourself, so that I may bring out
86|their figures as I have conceived them:
87|let your power appear in these brief verses!
88|They showed themselves then in five times seven
89|vowels and consonants; and I noted
90|the parts so, as they seemed spoken to me.
91|'LOVE JUSTICE', first
92|were verb and noun of all the painting;
93|'YOU WHO JUDGE THE EARTH', were the last.
94|Then in the M of the fifth word
95|they remained ordered; so that Jove
96|seemed silver there picked out with gold.
97|And I saw other lights descend where
98|was the top of the M, and there come to rest
99|singing, I believe, the good that moves them to itself.
100|Then, as at the striking of burning logs
101|rise innumerable sparks,
102|from which the foolish are wont to take auguries,
103|there seemed to rise again from there more than a thousand
104|lights and ascend, some much and some little,
105|just as the sun that kindles them allotted them;
106|and each one having come to rest in its place,
107|the head and the neck of an eagle I saw
108|represented by that distinct fire.
109|He who paints there, has no one who guides him;
110|but he himself guides, and from him derives
111|that power which is form for the nests.
112|The other beatitude, which content
113|seemed at first to en-lily itself on the M,
114|with slight motion followed the imprint.
115|O sweet star, what and how many gems
116|showed me that our justice
117|is the effect of the heaven that you bejewel!
118|Therefore I pray the mind in which begins
119|your motion and your power, that it look
120|whence comes out the smoke that vitiates your ray;
121|so that another time now it may grow angry
122|at the buying and selling within the temple
123|which was walled with signs and with martyrdoms.
124|O soldiery of heaven whom I contemplate,
125|pray for those who are on earth
126|all led astray after the bad example!
127|Once they were wont with swords to make war;
128|but now it is made by taking now here now there
129|the bread which the pious Father locks from no one.
130|But you who write only to cancel,
131|think that Peter and Paul, who died
132|for the vineyard that you lay waste, are still alive.
133|Well can you say: "I have fixed my desire
134|so on him who wanted to live alone
135|and who for dances was dragged to martyrdom,
136|that I do not know the fisherman nor Paul".
