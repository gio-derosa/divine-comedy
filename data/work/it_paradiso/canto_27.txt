1|'To the Father, to the Son, to the Holy Spirit',
2|began, 'glory!', all of paradise,
3|so that the sweet song made me drunk.
4|What I was seeing seemed to me a smile
5|of the universe; so that my inebriation
6|entered through the hearing and through the sight.
7|Oh joy! oh ineffable gladness!
8|oh life entire of love and of peace!
9|oh wealth secure without craving!
10|Before my eyes the four torches
11|stood kindled, and the one that came first
12|began to make itself more vivid,
13|and such in its semblance it became,
14|as Jove would become, if he and Mars
15|were birds and exchanged feathers.
16|The providence, which there allots
17|turn and office, in the blessed choir
18|had imposed silence on every side,
19|when I heard: "If I change color,
20|do not marvel, for, as I speak,
21|you will see all these change color.
22|He who usurps on earth my place,
23|my place, my place which is vacant
24|in the presence of the Son of God,
25|has made of my cemetery a sewer
26|of blood and of stench; whereby the perverse one
27|who fell from up here, down there is placated".
28|With that color which, because of the sun opposite,
29|paints the cloud in the evening and in the morning,
30|I saw then all the heaven overspread.
31|And as an honest lady who remains
32|sure of herself, and at another's fault,
33|only listening, becomes timid,
34|so Beatrice changed semblance;
35|and such eclipse I believe was in heaven
36|when the supreme power suffered.
37|Then his words proceeded
38|with a voice so changed from itself,
39|that his semblance did not change more:
40|"The bride of Christ was not nurtured
41|on my blood, on that of Linus, on that of Cletus,
42|to be used for the acquisition of gold;
43|but for the acquisition of this glad life
44|both Sixtus and Pius and Calixtus and Urban
45|shed their blood after much weeping.
46|It was not our intention that on the right hand
47|of our successors a part should sit,
48|a part on the other, of the Christian people;
49|nor that the keys which were granted to me,
50|should become an emblem on a banner
51|that fought against the baptized;
52|nor that I should be the figure on a seal
53|for privileges sold and lying,
54|at which I often redden and flash with fire.
55|In the clothing of shepherds rapacious wolves
56|are seen from up here through all the pastures:
57|O defense of God, why do you still lie still?
58|Of our blood Cahorsines and Gascons
59|are preparing to drink: O good beginning,
60|to what vile end must you fall!
61|But the high providence, which with Scipio
62|defended for Rome the glory of the world,
63|will soon succor, as I conceive;
64|and you, son, who because of your mortal weight
65|will return down again, open your mouth,
66|and do not hide what I do not hide".
67|Just as with frozen vapors flakes
68|downward our air, when the horn
69|of the goat of heaven is touched by the sun,
70|upward I saw so the ether adorned
71|become and flake with triumphant vapors
72|which had made a stay there with us.
73|My sight was following their semblances,
74|and followed until the medium, by its greatness,
75|took from it the passing further ahead.
76|Whereupon the lady, who saw me freed
77|from looking upward, said to me: "Lower
78|your sight and look how you have turned".
79|From the hour when I had looked before
80|I saw that I had moved through all the arc
81|which the first clime makes from the middle to the end;
82|so that I saw beyond Cadiz the passage
83|mad of Ulysses, and on this side near the shore
84|on which Europa became a sweet burden.
85|And more would have been uncovered to me the site
86|of this little threshing floor; but the sun was proceeding
87|beneath my feet a sign and more removed.
88|The enamored mind, which woos
89|my lady always, to bring back
90|to her my eyes burned more than ever;
91|and if nature or art made pastures
92|to catch eyes, so as to have the mind,
93|in human flesh or in its paintings,
94|all gathered together, they would seem nothing
95|compared to the divine pleasure that shone on me,
96|when I turned to her smiling face.
97|And the power that the look granted me,
98|from the beautiful nest of Leda tore me away,
99|and into the swiftest heaven impelled me.
100|Its parts most living and lofty
101|are so uniform, that I cannot say
102|which Beatrice chose as a place for me.
103|But she, who saw my desire,
104|began, smiling so glad,
105|that God seemed to rejoice in her face:
106|"The nature of the world, which holds quiet
107|the center and moves all the rest around,
108|begins from here as from its starting point;
109|and this heaven has no other where
110|than the divine mind, in which is kindled
111|the love that turns it and the power that it rains.
112|Light and love comprehend it with one circle,
113|just as this does the others; and that girding
114|he who girds it alone understands.
115|Its motion is not measured by another,
116|but the others are measured by this one,
117|just as ten by the half and by the fifth;
118|and how time can hold in such a pot
119|its roots and in the others its leaves,
120|now to you can be manifest.
121|Oh greed, which sinks mortals
122|so beneath you, that no one has the power
123|to draw his eyes out of your waves!
124|Well does the will blossom in men;
125|but the continual rain converts
126|into blighted fruit the true plums.
127|Faith and innocence are found
128|only in little children; then each one
129|flees before the cheeks are covered.
130|One, still lisping, fasts,
131|who later devours, with loosened tongue,
132|whatever food in whatever month;
133|and another, lisping, loves and listens to
134|his mother, who, with speech complete,
135|desires later to see her buried.
136|Thus is made the white skin black
137|at the first aspect of the beautiful daughter
138|of him who brings morning and leaves evening.
139|You, so that you may not make a marvel of it,
140|think that on earth there is no one who governs;
141|whereby the human family so goes astray.
142|But before January is wholly unwintered
143|through the hundredth part that is neglected down there,
144|these supernal circles will ray so,
145|that the fortune which is so awaited,
146|will turn the sterns where the prows are,
147|so that the fleet will run straight;
148|and true fruit will come after the flower".
