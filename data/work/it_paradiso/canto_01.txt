1|The glory of him who moves everything
2|through the universe penetrates, and shines
3|in one part more and less elsewhere.
4|In the heaven that most of his light receives
5|was I, and saw things that to retell
6|neither knows nor can he who from up there descends;
7|because, drawing itself near to its desire,
8|our intellect goes so deep,
9|that behind it the memory cannot go.
10|Truly, as much as I of the holy kingdom
11|in my mind could make treasure of,
12|will now be the matter of my song.
13|O good Apollo, for the last labor
14|make me of your worth such a vessel,
15|as you demand to give the beloved laurel.
16|Up to here the one peak of Parnassus
17|was enough for me; but now with both
18|I need to enter into the arena that remains.
19|Enter into my breast, and breathe you
20|just as when Marsyas you drew
21|out of the sheath of his limbs.
22|O divine power, if you lend yourself to me
23|so much that the shadow of the blessed kingdom
24|marked in my head I may make manifest,
25|you will see me at the foot of your beloved tree
26|come, and crown myself with the leaves
27|of which the matter and you will make me worthy.
28|So rarely, father, are any of them gathered
29|to triumph either as caesar or as poet,
30|fault and shame of human wills,
31|that to give birth to gladness in the glad
32|Delphic deity should the frond
33|Peneian, when anyone thirsts for it.
34|A small spark a great flame follows:
35|perhaps behind me with better voices
36|there will be prayer so that Cirrha may respond.
37|Rises to mortals through different outlets
38|the lamp of the world; but from that one
39|which four circles joins with three crosses,
40|with better course and with better star
41|it comes forth conjoined, and the worldly wax
42|more after its own manner tempers and seals.
43|Had made there morning and here evening
44|such an outlet, and almost all was there white
45|that hemisphere, and the other part black,
46|when Beatrice upon the left side
47|I saw turned and gazing into the sun:
48|an eagle never so fixed itself on it.
49|And just as a second ray is wont
50|to issue from the first and rise again upward,
51|just like a pilgrim who wants to return,
52|so from her act, through the eyes infused
53|into my imagination, mine was made,
54|and I fixed my eyes on the sun beyond our custom.
55|Much is permitted there, that here is not permitted
56|to our powers, thanks to the place
57|made as proper to the human species.
58|I did not endure it long, nor so little,
59|that I did not see it sparkle all around,
60|like iron that boiling comes out of the fire;
61|and suddenly it seemed that day to day
62|was added, as if he who is able
63|had the heaven with another sun adorned.
64|Beatrice wholly on the eternal wheels
65|fixed with her eyes was standing; and I on her
66|my lights fixed, from up there removed.
67|In her aspect such within I became,
68|as Glaucus became in tasting of the herb
69|that made him consort in the sea of the other gods.
70|To transhumanize signify in words
71|one could not; so let the example suffice
72|for him whom grace reserves the experience.
73|Whether I was only that of me which you created
74|newly, love that governs the heaven,
75|you know it, who with your light lifted me.
76|When the wheel that you make everlasting
77|by being desired, made me attentive to itself
78|with the harmony that you temper and distinguish,
79|there seemed to me so much then of the heaven kindled
80|by the flame of the sun, that rain or river
81|never made any lake so extended.
82|The novelty of the sound and the great light
83|about their cause kindled in me a desire
84|never felt of such sharpness.
85|Whereupon she, who saw me just as I,
86|to quiet my moved mind,
87|before I to ask, her mouth opened
88|and began: "You yourself make yourself dull
89|with false imagining, so that you do not see
90|what you would see if you had shaken it off.
91|You are not on earth, as you believe;
92|but lightning, fleeing its own site,
93|never ran as you who to it return".
94|If I was of the first doubt divested
95|by the smiled little brief words,
96|within a new one I was more ensnared
97|and said: "Already content I rested
98|from great wonder; but now I wonder
99|how I transcend these light bodies".
100|Whereupon she, after a pitying sigh,
101|directed her eyes toward me with that look
102|that a mother makes over a delirious son,
103|and began: "Things, all of them,
104|have order among themselves, and this is form
105|that makes the universe like God.
106|Here the high creatures see the imprint
107|of the eternal worth, which is the end
108|for which is made the touched-on rule.
109|In the order that I speak of are inclined
110|all natures, by different lots,
111|more near to their principle and less near;
112|so they move to different ports
113|through the great sea of being, and each one
114|with instinct given to it that may carry it.
115|This carries fire toward the moon;
116|this in mortal hearts is the mover;
117|this the earth in itself binds and gathers;
118|nor only the creatures that are outside
119|of intelligence does this bow shoot,
120|but those that have intellect and love.
121|The providence, that so much arranges,
122|with its light makes the heaven always quiet
123|within which turns that which has greatest haste;
124|and now there, as to a site decreed,
125|carries us the power of that bowstring
126|which whatever it shoots directs to a glad mark.
127|True it is that, as form does not accord
128|many times with the intention of the art,
129|because to respond the matter is deaf,
130|so from this course departs
131|at times the creature, which has power
132|to bend, thus impelled, in another direction;
133|and just as one can see fall
134|fire from a cloud, so the first impulse
135|dashes it to earth, twisted by false pleasure.
136|You must not wonder more, if I judge well,
137|at your rising, than at a stream
138|if from a high mountain it descends down to the bottom.
139|A marvel it would be in you if, deprived
140|of impediment, down there you had sat,
141|as on earth quiet in living fire".
142|Thereupon she turned toward the heaven her face.
