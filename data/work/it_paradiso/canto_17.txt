1|As came to Clymene, to make certain
2|of what he had heard against himself,
3|he who still makes fathers wary with their sons;
4|such was I, and such was I perceived
5|both by Beatrice and by the holy lamp
6|which before for my sake had changed its place.
7|So my lady "Send out the flame
8|of your desire", said to me, "so that it may come out
9|marked well with the internal stamp:
10|not because our knowledge may grow
11|through your speaking, but so that you accustom yourself
12|to tell your thirst, so that one may pour for you".
13|"O my dear root who so in-heavens yourself,
14|that, as earthly minds see
15|that two obtuse angles do not fit in a triangle,
16|so you see contingent things
17|before they are in themselves, gazing at the point
18|to which all times are present;
19|while I was joined to Virgil
20|up along the mountain that cures souls
21|and descending into the dead world,
22|there were said to me about my future life
23|grave words, although I feel myself
24|well four-square against the blows of chance;
25|so that my wish would be content
26|to understand what fortune is drawing near to me:
27|for an arrow foreseen comes more slowly".
28|Thus I said to that same light
29|which had spoken to me before; and as Beatrice
30|wished, my wish was confessed.
31|Not in riddles, in which the foolish people
32|once ensnared themselves before was slain
33|the Lamb of God that takes away sins,
34|but in clear words and with precise
35|speech answered that paternal love,
36|enclosed and visible in its own smile:
37|"Contingency, which beyond the notebook
38|of your matter does not extend,
39|is all depicted in the eternal sight;
40|necessity however it does not take from that
41|except as from the eye in which it is mirrored
42|a ship that down a torrent descends.
43|From there, just as comes to the ear
44|sweet harmony from an organ, comes to me
45|into sight the time that is being prepared for you.
46|As Hippolytus departed from Athens
47|because of his pitiless and perfidious stepmother,
48|so from Florence you must depart.
49|This is willed and this already is sought,
50|and soon it will be done by him who plans it
51|there where Christ all day is bought and sold.
52|The blame will follow the injured party
53|in outcry, as it is wont; but the vengeance
54|will be witness to the truth that dispenses it.
55|You will leave every thing loved
56|most dearly; and this is that arrow
57|which the bow of exile shoots first.
58|You will experience how it tastes of salt
59|the bread of others, and how it is a hard path
60|the going down and the going up by others' stairs.
61|And that which will most weigh upon your shoulders,
62|will be the company wicked and foolish
63|with which you will fall into this valley;
64|which all ungrateful, all mad and impious
65|will turn against you; but, shortly after,
66|it, not you, will have its temple red from it.
67|Of its bestiality its proceeding
68|will make the proof; so that for you it will be fine
69|to have made a party for yourself by yourself.
70|Your first refuge and first inn
71|will be the courtesy of the great Lombard
72|who upon the ladder bears the holy bird;
73|who will have for you such benign regard,
74|that of doing and of asking, between you two,
75|will be first that which among others is slowest.
76|With him you will see one who was imprinted,
77|at birth, so by this strong star,
78|that notable will be his works.
79|The people have not yet become aware of him
80|because of his young age, for only nine years
81|have these wheels turned around him;
82|but before the Gascon deceives the high Henry,
83|there will appear sparks of his virtue
84|in not caring for silver nor for toils.
85|His magnificences will be known
86|yet, so that his enemies
87|will not be able to keep their tongues mute about them.
88|Look to him and to his benefits;
89|through him will be transformed many people,
90|changing condition rich and beggars;
91|and you will carry it written in your mind
92|of him, and you will not tell it"; and he said things
93|incredible to those who will be present.
94|Then he added: "Son, these are the glosses
95|of what was told you; behold the snares
96|which behind a few revolutions are hidden.
97|Yet I do not want you to envy your neighbors,
98|since your life extends into the future
99|far beyond the punishment of their perfidies".
100|After, by falling silent, it showed itself finished,
101|the holy soul, with putting the woof
102|into that cloth which I held out to it warped,
103|I began, like one who longs,
104|doubting, for counsel from a person
105|who sees and wills rightly and loves:
106|"Well I see, my father, how spurs
107|time toward me, to give me a blow
108|such, that it is heavier to whoever most abandons himself;
109|so that it is good that I arm myself with foresight,
110|so that, if the dearest place is taken from me,
111|I may not lose the others through my songs.
112|Down through the world without end bitter,
113|and through the mountain from whose fair summit
114|the eyes of my lady lifted me,
115|and afterward through heaven, from light to light,
116|I have learned that which if I retell,
117|to many it will be a savor of strong sourness;
118|and if I to the truth am a timid friend,
119|I fear to lose life among those
120|who will call this time ancient".
121|The light in which was smiling my treasure
122|that I found there, first became flashing,
123|like a golden mirror in a ray of sun;
124|then it answered: "A conscience dark
125|either with its own or with another's shame
126|will indeed feel your word harsh.
127|But nonetheless, all falsehood removed,
128|make all your vision manifest;
129|and just let them scratch where the itch is.
130|For if your voice will be irksome
131|at the first taste, vital nourishment
132|it will leave afterward, when it is digested.
133|This cry of yours will do as the wind,
134|which strikes the highest peaks most;
135|and this is no small argument of honor.
136|Therefore there have been shown to you in these wheels,
137|on the mountain and in the sorrowful valley
138|only the souls that are known by fame,
139|for the mind of him who hears, does not rest
140|nor fix faith through an example that has
141|its root unknown and hidden,
142|nor through other argument that is not apparent".
