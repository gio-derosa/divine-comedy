1|Let him imagine, who wishes to understand well
2|what I now saw - and let him retain the image,
3|while I speak, like a firm rock -,
4|fifteen stars that in different regions
5|quicken the heaven with such serenity
6|that it overcomes every density of the air;
7|let him imagine that wain for which the bosom
8|of our heaven suffices both night and day,
9|so that at the turning of the pole it does not fail;
10|let him imagine the mouth of that horn
11|which begins at the point of the axle
12|around which the first wheel goes,
13|to have made of themselves two signs in heaven,
14|like the one the daughter of Minos made
15|at the time when she felt the chill of death;
16|and one to have its rays within the other,
17|and both to turn in such a manner
18|that one would go first and the other after;
19|and he will have almost the shadow of the true
20|constellation and of the double dance
21|that circled the point where I was:
22|for it is as far beyond our custom,
23|as beyond the moving of the Chiana
24|moves the heaven that surpasses all the others.
25|There they sang not Bacchus, not Paean,
26|but three persons in divine nature,
27|and in one person it and the human.
28|The singing and the turning completed their measure;
29|and those holy lights attended to us,
30|making themselves happy from care to care.
31|Broke the silence among the concordant divinities
32|then the light in which the wondrous life
33|of the little poor man of God was narrated to me,
34|and said: "When one straw is threshed,
35|when its seed is already stored away,
36|to beat the other sweet love invites me.
37|You believe that into the breast from which the rib
38|was drawn to form the beautiful cheek
39|whose palate costs the whole world dear,
40|and into that which, pierced by the lance,
41|both before and after so satisfied,
42|that it outweighs the balance of every fault,
43|whatever to human nature is permitted
44|to have of light, all was infused
45|by that power which made the one and the other;
46|and so you wonder at what I said above,
47|when I told that there never was a second to
48|the good that in the fifth light is enclosed.
49|Now open your eyes to what I answer you,
50|and you will see your belief and my saying
51|in the truth become like the center in a circle.
52|That which does not die and that which can die
53|is nothing but the splendor of that idea
54|which, loving, our Lord begets;
55|for that living light which so flows
56|from its source of light, that it is not disunited
57|from him nor from the love that makes three with them,
58|through its goodness gathers its raying,
59|as if mirrored, in nine subsistences,
60|eternally remaining one.
61|From there it descends to the last potencies
62|down from act to act, becoming such,
63|that it no longer makes anything but brief contingencies;
64|and these contingencies I mean to be
65|the generated things, which the moving heaven
66|produces with seed and without seed.
67|The wax of these and that which molds it
68|does not stand in one way; and therefore under the seal
69|ideal it then shines through more and less.
70|Whence it happens that one same tree,
71|according to species, fruits better and worse;
72|and you are born with different talents.
73|If the wax were exactly worked
74|and the heaven were in its supreme power,
75|the light of the seal would appear wholly;
76|but nature gives it always deficient,
77|working similarly to the artist
78|who has the habit of the art and a hand that trembles.
79|However, if the warm love the clear vision
80|of the first power disposes and seals,
81|all perfection is acquired there.
82|Thus was made once the earth worthy
83|of all animal perfection;
84|thus was the Virgin made pregnant;
85|so that I commend your opinion,
86|that human nature never was
87|nor will be what it was in those two persons.
88|Now if I did not proceed further,
89|'Then, how was this one without equal?'
90|your words would begin.
91|But so that what does not appear may appear well,
92|think who he was, and the cause that moved him,
93|when it was said "Ask", to ask.
94|I have not spoken so, that you cannot
95|well see that he was a king, who asked for wisdom
96|so that he might be a sufficient king;
97|not to know the number in which are
98|the movers up here, or whether necessary
99|with contingent ever made necessary;
100|not whether one must grant there is a first motion,
101|or whether in a half circle can be made
102|a triangle such that it would not have a right angle.
103|Whence, if what I said and this you note,
104|kingly prudence is that unequaled seeing
105|on which the arrow of my intention strikes;
106|and if to "rose" you direct your clear eyes,
107|you will see it has regard only
108|to kings, who are many, and the good are rare.
109|With this distinction take my saying;
110|and thus it can stand with what you believe
111|of the first father and of our Beloved.
112|And let this be always lead on your feet,
113|to make you move slowly like a weary man
114|both to the yes and to the no that you do not see:
115|for he is among the fools well down low,
116|who without distinction affirms and denies
117|in the one as in the other case;
118|because it happens that often bends
119|the hasty opinion to the false side,
120|and then affection binds the intellect.
121|Far worse than in vain he departs from shore,
122|because he does not return such as he sets out,
123|who fishes for the truth and does not have the art.
124|And of this to the world are open proofs
125|Parmenides, Melissus and Bryson and many,
126|who went and did not know where;
127|so did Sabellius and Arius and those fools
128|who were like swords to the Scriptures
129|in rendering crooked the straight faces.
130|Let the people not be, again, too secure
131|in judging, like one who values
132|the crops in the field before they are ripe;
133|for I have seen all the winter first
134|the briar show itself rigid and fierce,
135|then bear the rose upon its top;
136|and I saw once a ship straight and swift
137|run the sea through all its course,
138|perish at the end at the entering of the harbor.
139|Let not lady Bertha and sir Martin believe,
140|by seeing one steal, another make offering,
141|that they see them within the divine counsel;
142|for the one may rise, and the other may fall".
