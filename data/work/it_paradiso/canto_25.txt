1|If ever it happens that the sacred poem
2|to which both heaven and earth have set their hand,
3|so that it has made me lean for many years,
4|should overcome the cruelty that locks me out
5|of the beautiful sheepfold where I slept as a lamb,
6|enemy to the wolves that make war on it;
7|with another voice now, with another fleece
8|I will return a poet, and at the font
9|of my baptism I will take the crown;
10|because into the faith, which makes known
11|souls to God, there I entered, and then
12|Peter for its sake so circled my brow.
13|Then a light moved toward us
14|from that sphere whence came forth the first fruit
15|which Christ left of his vicars;
16|and my lady, full of gladness,
17|said to me: "Look, look: behold the baron
18|for whose sake down there Galicia is visited".
19|Just as when the dove places itself
20|near its mate, the one to the other displays,
21|circling and murmuring, its affection;
22|so I saw the one by the other great
23|glorious prince be welcomed,
24|praising the food which up there feeds them.
25|But after the rejoicing was completed,
26|silent before me each fixed itself,
27|so aflame that it vanquished my sight.
28|Smiling then Beatrice said:
29|"Illustrious life by whom the largesse
30|of our basilica was written,
31|make hope resound in this height:
32|you know, who figure it as many times,
33|as Jesus showed more fondness to the three".
34|"Lift your head and see that you reassure yourself:
35|for whatever comes up here from the mortal world,
36|must ripen in our rays".
37|This comfort from the second fire
38|came to me; whereupon I lifted my eyes to the mountains
39|which had bowed them before with too much weight.
40|"Since by grace wills that you should meet
41|our Emperor, before death,
42|in the most secret hall with his counts,
43|so that, having seen the truth of this court,
44|the hope, which down there makes to love well,
45|in you and in others you may strengthen by it,
46|say what it is, say how with it flowers
47|your mind, and say whence it came to you".
48|Thus the second light went on further.
49|And that compassionate one who guided the feathers
50|of my wings to so high a flight,
51|in the answer thus anticipated me:
52|"The Church militant has not any son
53|with more hope, as is written
54|in the Sun that rays on all our host:
55|therefore it is granted him that from Egypt
56|he come to Jerusalem to see,
57|before his soldiering is ended for him.
58|The other two points, which not for knowing
59|are asked, but so that he may report
60|how much this virtue is pleasing to you,
61|I leave to him, for they will not be hard for him
62|nor of boasting; and let him answer to this,
63|and may the grace of God grant it to him".
64|As a pupil who follows the teacher
65|prompt and willing in that in which he is expert,
66|so that his worth may be unhidden,
67|"Hope", I said, "is a certain expectation
68|of future glory, which is produced by
69|divine grace and preceding merit.
70|From many stars comes to me this light;
71|but he first distilled it into my heart
72|who was the highest singer of the highest leader.
73|'Let them hope in you', in his theody
74|he says, 'those who know your name':
75|and who does not know it, if he has my faith?
76|You distilled it into me, with his distilling,
77|in your epistle then; so that I am full,
78|and upon others I rain again your rain".
79|While I was speaking, within the living bosom
80|of that fire trembled a flash
81|sudden and frequent in the manner of lightning.
82|Then it breathed: "The love with which I blaze
83|still toward the virtue which followed me
84|up to the palm and to the leaving of the field,
85|wills that I breathe again to you who delight
86|in it; and it is pleasing to me that you tell
87|that which hope promises you".
88|And I: "The new and the old scriptures
89|set the mark, and this points it out to me,
90|of the souls that God has made his friends.
91|Isaiah says that each one clothed
92|in its own land will be with a double garment:
93|and its own land is this sweet life;
94|and your brother much more clearly set forth,
95|there where he treats of the white robes,
96|makes manifest to us this revelation".
97|And first, close upon the end of these words,
98|'Let them hope in you' above us was heard;
99|to which all the carols answered.
100|Then among them a light brightened
101|so that, if the Crab had such a crystal,
102|winter would have a month of a single day.
103|And as there rises and goes and enters the dance
104|a glad maiden, only to do honor
105|to the bride, not for any fault,
106|so I saw the brightened splendor
107|come to the two who were turning in a round
108|such as befitted their ardent love.
109|It placed itself there in the song and in the wheel;
110|and my lady kept her look upon them,
111|just like a bride silent and unmoving.
112|"This is he who lay upon the breast
113|of our pelican, and this one was
114|from upon the cross chosen for the great office".
115|My lady thus; nor however did
116|her words move her sight any more from staying attentive
117|afterward than before.
118|As is he who gazes and strives
119|to see the sun eclipsed a little,
120|who, by seeing, becomes non-seeing;
121|so I became at that last fire
122|while it was said: "Why do you dazzle yourself
123|to see a thing that here has no place?
124|On earth my body is earth, and will be there
125|with the others, until our number
126|equals the eternal purpose.
127|With the two robes in the blessed cloister
128|are the only two lights that ascended;
129|and this you will carry back into your world".
130|At this voice the inflamed circle
131|became quiet together with the sweet mingling
132|that was made in the sound of the threefold breath,
133|just as, to avoid fatigue or risk,
134|the oars, before beating in the water,
135|all rest at the sounding of a whistle.
136|Ah how much in my mind I was disturbed,
137|when I turned to see Beatrice,
138|at not being able to see, although I was
139|near her, and in the happy world!
