1|As the bird, among the beloved leaves,
2|resting on the nest of her sweet offspring
3|through the night that hides things from us,
4|who, to see the longed-for looks
5|and to find the food with which to feed them,
6|in which heavy labors are pleasing to her,
7|anticipates the time on an open bough,
8|and with ardent affection awaits the sun,
9|fixedly watching only for the dawn to be born;
10|so my lady was standing erect
11|and attentive, turned toward the region
12|beneath which the sun shows less haste:
13|so that, I seeing her suspended and longing,
14|became like one who desiring
15|would want something else, and in hoping is satisfied.
16|But short was the time between one and the other when,
17|of my waiting, I mean, and of seeing
18|the heaven become more and more brightened;
19|and Beatrice said: "Behold the hosts
20|of the triumph of Christ and all the fruit
21|gathered from the circling of these spheres!".
22|It seemed to me that her face was all burning,
23|and she had her eyes so full of gladness,
24|that I must pass it by without construction.
25|As in the serene full moons
26|Trivia smiles among the eternal nymphs
27|who paint the heaven through all its recesses,
28|I saw above thousands of lamps
29|a sun that kindled each and all of them,
30|as ours does the sights above;
31|and through the living light shone through
32|the shining substance so bright
33|into my sight, that it could not endure it.
34|Oh Beatrice, sweet guide and dear!
35|She said to me: "That which overpowers you
36|is a power from which nothing defends itself.
37|Here is the wisdom and the power
38|that opened the roads between heaven and earth,
39|for which there was once such long desire".
40|As fire from a cloud unlocks itself
41|by expanding so that it does not fit there,
42|and against its nature down to earth strikes,
43|my mind so, among those feasts
44|made greater, went out of itself,
45|and what it became it does not know how to remember.
46|"Open your eyes and look at what I am;
47|you have seen things, by which powerful
48|you are made to endure my smile".
49|I was like one who comes to himself
50|from a forgotten vision and who strives
51|in vain to bring it back to mind,
52|when I heard this offer, worthy
53|of such gratitude, that it will never be erased
54|from the book that records the past.
55|If now there sounded all those tongues
56|which Polyhymnia with her sisters made
57|most fat with their sweetest milk,
58|to help me, to the thousandth part of the truth
59|one would not come, singing the holy smile
60|and how the holy aspect it made pure;
61|and so, figuring paradise,
62|the sacred poem must leap,
63|like one who finds his path cut off.
64|But whoever thought of the ponderous theme
65|and the mortal shoulder that is loaded with it,
66|would not blame it if under it it trembles:
67|it is not a passage for a little boat
68|that which the daring prow goes cleaving,
69|nor for a helmsman who spares himself.
70|"Why does my face so enamor you,
71|that you do not turn to the beautiful garden
72|which under the rays of Christ flowers?
73|Here is the rose in which the divine word
74|became flesh; here are the lilies
75|by whose odor the good path was taken".
76|Thus Beatrice; and I, who to her counsels
77|was wholly ready, again gave myself
78|to the battle of the weak brows.
79|As under a ray of sun, which pure streams
80|through a broken cloud, once a meadow of flowers
81|my eyes have seen, covered in shade;
82|so I saw many crowds of splendors,
83|lit from above by burning rays,
84|without seeing the source of the brightness.
85|O benign power that so imprints them,
86|you exalted yourself upward, to grant room
87|there to my eyes that were not powerful enough for you.
88|The name of the beautiful flower that I always invoke
89|both morning and evening, wholly constrained
90|my mind to gaze at the greatest fire;
91|and when both my lights had painted for me
92|the quality and the quantity of the living star
93|which up there surpasses as down here it surpassed,
94|through the midst of the heaven descended a torch,
95|formed in a circle in the manner of a crown,
96|and it girded her and turned around her.
97|Whatever melody sounds most sweet
98|down here and most draws the soul to itself,
99|would seem a cloud that, torn apart, thunders,
100|compared to the sounding of that lyre
101|with which was crowned the beautiful sapphire
102|by which the brightest heaven is ensapphired.
103|"I am angelic love, who circle
104|the high gladness which breathes from the womb
105|that was the inn of our desire;
106|and I will circle, lady of heaven, as long
107|as you follow your son, and make more divine
108|the supreme sphere because you enter there".
109|Thus the circling melody
110|sealed itself, and all the other lights
111|made sound the name of Mary.
112|The royal mantle of all the volumes
113|of the world, which most burns and most quickens
114|in the breath of God and in his ways,
115|had above us its inner shore
116|so distant, that its appearance,
117|there where I was, did not yet appear:
118|therefore my eyes did not have power
119|to follow the crowned flame
120|which rose after her seed.
121|And as a little child that toward its mother
122|stretches its arms, after it has taken the milk,
123|because of the spirit that is inflamed even outwardly;
124|each of those whitenesses stretched upward
125|with its peak, so that the high affection
126|which they had for Mary was plain to me.
127|Then they remained there in my sight,
128|'Queen of heaven' singing so sweetly,
129|that never has the delight departed from me.
130|Oh how great is the abundance that is stored
131|in those most rich coffers which were
132|for sowing down here good plowlands!
133|Here they live and enjoy the treasure
134|which was acquired weeping in the exile
135|of Babylon, where gold was left behind.
136|Here triumphs, under the high Son
137|of God and of Mary, in his victory,
138|and with the ancient and with the new council,
139|he who holds the keys of such glory.
