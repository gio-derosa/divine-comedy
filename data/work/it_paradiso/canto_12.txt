1|As soon as the last word
2|the blessed flame took up to speak,
3|the holy millstone began to rotate;
4|and in its circling it had not wholly turned
5|before another with a circle enclosed it,
6|and motion to motion and song to song matched;
7|song that so much surpasses our muses,
8|our sirens in those sweet trumpets,
9|as a first splendor that which it reflects back.
10|As there curve through a tender cloud
11|two arches parallel and like in color,
12|when Juno gives command to her handmaid,
13|the one outside being born from the one within,
14|in the manner of the speech of that wandering one
15|whom love consumed as the sun vapors,
16|and make the people here foreknowing,
17|by the pact which God with Noah made,
18|of the world that never again will be flooded:
19|so of those everlasting roses
20|turned around us the two garlands,
21|and so the outer to the inner responded.
22|After the dance and the other great festivity,
23|both of the singing and of the flaming
24|light with light joyous and gentle,
25|together at one moment and with one will fell quiet,
26|just like the eyes which at the pleasure that moves them
27|must together close and lift;
28|from the heart of one of the new lights
29|a voice moved, which the needle to the star
30|made me seem in turning myself to its where;
31|and it began: "The love that makes me beautiful
32|draws me to discourse of the other leader
33|because of whom of mine here is spoken so well.
34|It is fitting that, where one is, the other be brought in:
35|so that, as they fought for one end,
36|so their glory may shine together.
37|The army of Christ, which so dear
38|cost to rearm, behind the banner
39|was moving slow, suspicious and few,
40|when the emperor who always reigns
41|provided for the soldiery, which was in danger,
42|through grace alone, not for its being worthy;
43|and, as was said, succored his bride
44|with two champions, at whose doing, at whose saying
45|the people led astray recollected themselves.
46|In that region where rises to open
47|the sweet Zephyr the new leaves
48|with which Europe is seen to reclothe itself,
49|not very far from the beating of the waves
50|behind which, because of its long course,
51|the sun at times hides itself from every man,
52|sits the fortunate Calahorra
53|under the protection of the great shield
54|on which the lion is subjected and subjugates:
55|within it was born the loving lover
56|of the Christian faith, the holy athlete
57|kind to his own and to his enemies harsh;
58|and as soon as it was created, so replete
59|was his mind with living virtue,
60|that, in his mother, it made her a prophet.
61|After the espousals were completed
62|at the sacred font between him and the Faith,
63|where they dowered each other with mutual salvation,
64|the lady who for him gave the assent,
65|saw in her sleep the wondrous fruit
66|that was to come forth from him and from his heirs;
67|and so that he might be what he was in construction,
68|from here a spirit moved to name him
69|with the possessive of him whose he wholly was.
70|Dominic he was called; and I speak of him
71|as of the husbandman whom Christ
72|chose for his garden to help him.
73|Well he seemed a messenger and familiar of Christ:
74|for the first love that in him was manifest,
75|was for the first counsel that Christ gave.
76|Many times he was silent and awake
77|found on the ground by his nurse,
78|as if he said: 'I have come for this'.
79|Oh his father truly Felix!
80|oh his mother truly Giovanna,
81|if, interpreted, it means as is said!
82|Not for the world, for which now one toils
83|after Ostiense and Taddeo,
84|but for love of the true manna
85|in a short time a great doctor he became;
86|such that he set himself to go around the vineyard
87|which soon whitens, if the vinedresser is bad.
88|And of the see which was once more kind
89|to the just poor, not through itself,
90|but through him who sits there, who degenerates,
91|not to dispense either two or three for six,
92|not the fortune of the first vacancy,
93|not the tithes, which are of the poor of God,
94|did he ask, but against the erring world
95|license to fight for the seed
96|of which twenty-four plants swathe you.
97|Then, with doctrine and with will together,
98|with the apostolic office he moved
99|like a torrent which a high vein presses;
100|and upon the heretical stumps struck
101|his impetus, most vigorously there
102|where the resistances were thickest.
103|From him were made then different streams
104|with which the catholic garden is watered,
105|so that its saplings stand more alive.
106|If such was the one wheel of the chariot
107|in which the Holy Church defended herself
108|and won in the field her civil strife,
109|well ought to be very plain to you
110|the excellence of the other, about whom Thomas
111|before my coming was so courteous.
112|But the track that the highest part
113|of its circumference made, is abandoned,
114|so that there is mold where there was crust.
115|His family, which set out straight
116|with their feet in his footprints, is so turned around,
117|that the front one throws on the one behind;
118|and soon it will be seen of the harvest
119|of the bad cultivation, when the tares
120|will complain that the bin is taken from them.
121|Truly I say, whoever searched leaf by leaf
122|our volume, would still find a page
123|where he would read "I am what I am wont to be";
124|but it will not be from Casale nor from Acquasparta,
125|from where such come to the writing,
126|that one flees it and another constricts it.
127|I am the life of Bonaventure
128|of Bagnoregio, who in the great offices
129|always put behind the left-hand care.
130|Illuminato and Augustine are here,
131|who were among the first unshod poor ones
132|who in the cord made themselves friends to God.
133|Hugh of Saint Victor is here with them,
134|and Peter the Eater and Peter of Spain,
135|who down there shines in twelve little books;
136|Nathan the prophet and the metropolitan
137|Chrysostom and Anselm and that Donatus
138|who to the first art deigned to put his hand.
139|Rabanus is here, and shines beside me
140|the Calabrian abbot Joachim
141|with prophetic spirit endowed.
142|To emulate so great a paladin
143|moved me the inflamed courtesy
144|of friar Thomas and the discerning speech;
145|and moved with me this company".
