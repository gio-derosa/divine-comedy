1|"Hosanna, holy God of hosts,
2|over-illuminating with your brightness
3|the happy fires of these kingdoms!".
4|Thus, turning itself to its own note,
5|it seemed to me that it sang, that substance,
6|upon which a double light is twinned;
7|and it and the others moved to their dance,
8|and like most swift sparks
9|veiled themselves from me with sudden distance.
10|I was doubting and saying 'Tell her, tell her!'
11|within myself, 'tell her' I was saying, 'my lady
12|who quenches my thirst with the sweet drops'.
13|But that reverence which makes itself lady
14|of all of me, even just by Be and by ice,
15|bowed me down again like a man who falls asleep.
16|Little did Beatrice suffer me thus
17|and began, beaming on me with a smile
18|such, that in the fire it would make a man happy:
19|"According to my infallible judgment,
20|how just vengeance justly
21|could be punished, has put you in thought;
22|but I will quickly loosen your mind;
23|and you listen, for my words
24|of a great doctrine will make you a present.
25|By not enduring for the power that wills
26|a curb to his own profit, that man who was not born,
27|damning himself, damned all his offspring;
28|whereby the human species lay sick
29|down there for many centuries in great error,
30|until it pleased the Word of God to descend
31|where the nature, which from its maker
32|had distanced itself, he united to himself in person
33|with the sole act of his eternal love.
34|Now direct your sight to what is now discussed:
35|this nature united to its maker,
36|as it was created, was sincere and good;
37|but by itself indeed it was banished
38|from paradise, because it turned itself
39|from the way of truth and from its life.
40|The penalty therefore that the cross offered
41|if by the nature assumed it is measured,
42|none ever so justly bit;
43|and likewise none was of such great injury,
44|looking at the person who suffered,
45|in whom such nature was contracted.
46|Thus from one act came forth diverse things:
47|for to God and to the Jews one death was pleasing;
48|because of it the earth trembled and the heaven opened.
49|It must not now seem to you more hard,
50|when it is said that just vengeance
51|was afterward avenged by a just court.
52|But I see now your mind constricted
53|from thought to thought within a knot,
54|from which with great desire it awaits to be loosened.
55|You say: "I discern well what I hear;
56|but why God willed, is hidden from me,
57|for our redemption just this way".
58|This decree, brother, stays buried
59|to the eyes of everyone whose mind
60|in the flame of love is not adult.
61|Truly, since at this mark
62|much one aims and little one discerns,
63|I will say why such a way was more worthy.
64|The divine goodness, which spurns from itself
65|all envy, burning in itself, sparkles
66|so that it unfolds the eternal beauties.
67|That which from it without intermediary distills
68|has afterward no end, because it does not move
69|its imprint when it seals.
70|That which from it without intermediary rains
71|is wholly free, because it is not subject
72|to the power of the new things.
73|The more it is conformed to it, the more it pleases it;
74|for the holy ardor that irradiates everything,
75|in the most similar is most vivid.
76|With all these endowments is advantaged
77|the human creature, and if one is lacking,
78|from its nobility it must fall.
79|Only sin is that which disenfranchises it
80|and makes it dissimilar to the highest good,
81|so that with its light it is little whitened;
82|and to its dignity it never returns,
83|unless it fills up, where fault empties,
84|against evil delight with just penalties.
85|Your nature, when it sinned wholly
86|in its seed, from these dignities,
87|as from paradise, was removed;
88|nor could they be recovered, if you consider
89|very subtly, by any way,
90|without passing through one of these fords:
91|either that God alone by his courtesy
92|had pardoned, or that man by himself
93|had given satisfaction for his folly.
94|Fix now your eye within the abyss
95|of the eternal counsel, as much as you can
96|closely fixed on my speech.
97|Man could not within his limits
98|ever give satisfaction, by not being able to go down
99|with humility obeying afterward,
100|as much as disobeying he intended to go up;
101|and this is the reason why man was
102|from being able to satisfy by himself shut out.
103|Therefore it behooved God with his ways
104|to restore man to his whole life,
105|I say with the one, or else with both.
106|But because the work is so much more pleasing
107|to the worker, the more it presents
108|of the goodness of the heart from which it came forth,
109|the divine goodness that imprints the world,
110|to proceed by all its ways,
111|to raise you up again, was content.
112|Nor between the last night and the first day
113|so high or so magnificent a proceeding,
114|either by the one or by the other, was or will be:
115|for God was more generous in giving himself
116|to make man sufficient to raise himself up,
117|than if he had only of himself pardoned;
118|and all the other ways were insufficient
119|for justice, if the Son of God
120|had not humbled himself to become incarnate.
121|Now to fill well your every desire,
122|I return to explain to you a certain place,
123|so that you may see there just as I.
124|You say: "I see water, I see fire,
125|air and earth and all their mixtures
126|come to corruption, and last little;
127|and these things too were creatures;
128|so that, if what was said has been true,
129|they should be secure from corruption".
130|The angels, brother, and the pure country
131|in which you are, can be said to be created,
132|just as they are, in their entire being;
133|but the elements that you have named
134|and those things that are made of them
135|by created power are informed.
136|Created was the matter that they have;
137|created was the informing power
138|in these stars that go around them.
139|The soul of every brute and of the plants
140|from a complexion endowed with potency draws
141|the ray and the motion of the holy lights;
142|but your life without intermediary breathes
143|the highest benevolence, and enamors it
144|of itself so that afterward it always desires it.
145|And hence you can argue furthermore
146|your resurrection, if you think again
147|how human flesh was made then
148|when the first parents both were made".
