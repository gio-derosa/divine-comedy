1|In the form then of a white rose
2|was shown to me the holy soldiery
3|which in his blood Christ made his bride;
4|but the other, which flying sees and sings
5|the glory of him who enamors it
6|and the goodness that made it so great,
7|like a swarm of bees that enflowers itself
8|at one time and at another returns
9|there where its labor is turned to savor,
10|into the great flower it descended which is adorned
11|with so many petals, and from there rose again
12|there where its love always dwells.
13|Their faces they all had of living flame
14|and their wings of gold, and the rest so white,
15|that no snow reaches that limit.
16|When they descended into the flower, from tier to tier
17|they offered of the peace and of the ardor
18|which they acquired fanning their sides.
19|Nor did the interposing between the above and the flower
20|of so great a flying multitude
21|impede the sight and the splendor:
22|for the divine light penetrates
23|through the universe according as it is worthy,
24|so that nothing can be an obstacle to it.
25|This secure and joyous kingdom,
26|thronged with ancient people and with new,
27|had its sight and love all on one mark.
28|Oh threefold light which in a single star
29|sparkling to their sight, so satisfies them!
30|look down here on our storm!
31|If the barbarians, coming from such a region
32|as each day is covered by Helice,
33|wheeling with her son of whom she is fond,
34|seeing Rome and her arduous work,
35|were stupefied, when the Lateran
36|rose above mortal things;
37|I, who to the divine from the human,
38|to the eternal from time had come,
39|and from Florence to a people just and sane,
40|with what stupor must I have been filled!
41|Certainly between it and the joy it was
42|my pleasure not to hear and to stay mute.
43|And like a pilgrim who is refreshed
44|in the temple of his vow looking around,
45|and hopes already to retell how it is,
46|so walking up through the living light,
47|I led my eyes along the tiers,
48|now up, now down and now circling around.
49|I saw faces persuasive to charity,
50|adorned with another's light and with their own smile,
51|and gestures graced with every dignity.
52|The general form of paradise
53|already all my gaze had comprehended,
54|on no part yet fixed steadily;
55|and I turned with rekindled wish
56|to ask my lady about things
57|over which my mind was in suspense.
58|One thing I intended, and another answered me:
59|I thought to see Beatrice and I saw an old man
60|clothed like the glorious people.
61|He was suffused in his eyes and in his cheeks
62|with benign gladness, in a pious bearing
63|such as befits a tender father.
64|And "Where is she?", at once I said.
65|Whereupon he: "To bring your desire to its end
66|Beatrice moved me from my place;
67|and if you look up in the third circle
68|from the highest tier, you will see her again
69|on the throne that her merits allotted her".
70|Without answering, I lifted my eyes up,
71|and saw her as she made herself a crown
72|reflecting from herself the eternal rays.
73|From that region which thunders highest up
74|no mortal eye is so distant,
75|whichever in the sea most deeply abandons itself,
76|as there from Beatrice was my sight;
77|but it did nothing to me, for her image
78|did not descend to me mixed with any medium.
79|"O lady in whom my hope is strong,
80|and who suffered for my salvation
81|to leave your footprints in hell,
82|of all the things that I have seen,
83|from your power and from your goodness
84|I acknowledge the grace and the virtue.
85|You have drawn me from servitude to liberty
86|by all those ways, by all the means
87|by which you had the power to do this.
88|Your magnificence in me guard,
89|so that my soul, which you have made healthy,
90|pleasing to you may be unknotted from the body".
91|So I prayed; and she, as far away
92|as she seemed, smiled and looked at me again;
93|then she turned back to the eternal fountain.
94|And the holy old man: "So that you may complete
95|perfectly", he said, "your journey,
96|for which prayer and holy love sent me,
97|fly with your eyes through this garden;
98|for seeing it will prepare your gaze
99|further for mounting through the divine ray.
100|And the queen of heaven, for whom I burn
101|wholly with love, will give us every grace,
102|because I am her faithful Bernard".
103|As is he who perhaps from Croatia
104|comes to see our Veronica,
105|who because of the ancient hunger is not sated by it,
106|but says in his thought, as long as it is shown:
107|'My lord Jesus Christ, true God,
108|was then your semblance made like this?';
109|such was I gazing at the living
110|charity of him who in this world,
111|contemplating, tasted of that peace.
112|"Son of grace, this joyous being",
113|he began, "will not be known to you,
114|keeping your eyes only down here at the bottom;
115|but look at the circles up to the most remote,
116|until you see seated the queen
117|to whom this kingdom is subject and devoted".
118|I lifted my eyes; and as in the morning
119|the eastern part of the horizon
120|surpasses that where the sun declines,
121|so, as if going from valley to mountain
122|with my eyes, I saw a part at the extremity
123|surpass in light all the rest of the rim.
124|And as there where is awaited the pole
125|which Phaethon guided badly, it is most inflamed,
126|and on this side and that the light becomes less,
127|so that peaceful oriflamme
128|in the middle quickened, and on every side
129|in equal manner slackened its flame;
130|and at that middle, with wings spread,
131|I saw more than a thousand angels rejoicing,
132|each distinct in brightness and in art.
133|I saw at their games there and at their songs
134|smiling a beauty, which was gladness
135|in the eyes of all the other saints;
136|and if I had in speaking as much wealth
137|as in imagining, I would not dare
138|to attempt the least of her delight.
139|Bernard, when he saw my eyes
140|fixed and attentive on the object of his warm ardor,
141|turned his own with so much affection to her,
142|that he made mine more ardent to gaze again.
